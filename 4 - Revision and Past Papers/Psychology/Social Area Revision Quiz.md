## Social Area Revision Quiz (14th Oct 2025)

### Q1) Identify one strength of Milgram's study.
**Model Answer:**
It was conducted in a highly **controlled environment** (Yale lab), which allowed Milgram to standardise the procedure (e.g., the 4 prods) and increase the **[[Internal Reliability]]**.

### Q2) Why does Bocchiaro's study have low ecological validity?
**Model Answer:**
Like Milgram, it was a **[[Lab Experiment]]** in an artificial setting (a university lab). The task-writing a persuasive letter for a sensory deprivation study-is not an everyday activity, so it may not reflect how people respond to authority in real life.

### Q3) Identify one similarity and one difference between Milgram and Bocchiaro.
**Model Answer:**
*   **Similarity:** Both studies were **Ethnocentric**. Milgram only used participants from New Haven, USA, and Bocchiaro only used students from a single university in Amsterdam. This makes it hard to generalise findings to other cultures.
*   **Difference:** The presence of authority. In Milgram, the Experimenter was in the room throughout, applying direct pressure. In Bocchiaro, participants were left **alone in a room** to make their decision, representing a more subtle form of social pressure.

### Q4) Define the "Social Area" in Psychology.
**Model Answer:**
The social area focuses on how an individual's thoughts, feelings, and behaviours are influenced by the actual, imagined, or implied presence of others and the **social context**.

### Q5) Define the "Dispositional Hypothesis".
**Model Answer:**
The theory that a person's behaviour is driven by their **internal characteristics** (e.g., personality, traits, or religion) rather than by the situation they are in.

### Q6) How did Bocchiaro test for dispositional factors?
**Model Answer:**
Participants completed standardised personality tests (e.g., **HEXACO-PI-R**) after they had decided to obey, disobey, or whistleblow. The results showed no significant link between personality and behaviour, supporting a situational explanation instead.
