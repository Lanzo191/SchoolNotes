## Research Methods - Comprehensive Revision Guide

This guide covers the essential concepts, methods, and considerations for OCR A-Level Psychology Component 1: Research Methods.

### 1. Key Terms & Concepts

*   **Aim:** A general statement of what the researcher intends to investigate; the purpose of the study.
*   **Hypothesis:** A testable statement predicting the outcome of the study.
    *   **Directional (One-tailed):** Predicts the specific nature of the relationship (e.g., "A increases B"). Used when prior research suggests a direction.
    *   **Non-directional (Two-tailed):** Predicts a relationship exists, but not its direction (e.g., "A affects B"). Used when prior research is mixed or absent.
    *   **[[Null Hypothesis|Null Hypothesis]] (H0):** States there will be no relationship or difference between variables.
*   **[[Operationalisation]]:** Clearly defining variables so they can be measured or controlled.
*   **Variables:**
    *   **[[Independent Variable|Independent Variable]]:** The variable that is manipulated by the researcher.
    *   **[[Dependent Variable]]:** The variable that is measured by the researcher; expected to change as a result of the IV.
    *   **[[Extraneous Variables|Extraneous Variables]]:** Any variable other than the IV that *could* affect the DV if not controlled (e.g., noise, temperature).
    *   **[[Confounding Variables]]:** An extraneous variable that *has* affected the DV, meaning the researcher cannot be sure if the IV caused the change.

### 2. Research Methods

#### Experiments
*   **[[Lab Experiment]]**:
    *   **Description:** Conducted in a controlled environment. IV is manipulated, DV is measured.
    *   **Strengths:** High control over EVs, high [[Internal Validity]], replicable.
    *   **Weaknesses:** Low [[Ecological Validity]], risk of [[Demand Characteristics]].
*   **[[Field Experiment]]**:
    *   **Description:** IV is manipulated in a natural, real-world setting.
    *   **Strengths:** Higher [[Ecological Validity]] than lab, less risk of [[Demand Characteristics]].
    *   **Weaknesses:** Less control over EVs, ethical issues (no consent).
*   **[[Natural Experiment]]**:
    *   **Description:** IV occurs naturally (e.g., natural disaster, policy change); researcher measures its effect on DV.
    *   **Strengths:** High [[Ecological Validity]], studies impossible/unethical IVs.
    *   **Weaknesses:** No control over IV, no random allocation, [[Confounding Variables]] likely.
*   **[[Quasi-Experiment]]**:
    *   **Description:** IV is a pre-existing difference between people (e.g., gender, age, personality trait).
    *   **Strengths:** High control (often in lab), studies individual differences.
    *   **Weaknesses:** No manipulation of IV, no random allocation, [[Confounding Variables]] possible.

#### Correlations
*   **Description:** Measures the strength and direction of a relationship between two co-variables.
*   **Strengths:** Investigates relationships not suitable for experiments, can suggest areas for future research.
*   **Weaknesses:** Cannot establish [[Cause and Effect|Cause and Effect]], third variable problem, curvilinear relationships.

#### Observations
*   **Description:** Watching and recording behavior.
*   **Types:**
    *   **Naturalistic vs. Controlled:** Natural setting vs. structured setting.
    *   **Participant vs. Non-participant:** Observer is part of group vs. separate.
    *   **Overt vs. Covert:** Participants know they are being watched vs. unaware.
*   **Strengths:** High [[Ecological Validity]] (naturalistic/covert), captures rich [[Qualitative Data|Qualitative Data]].
*   **Weaknesses:** Observer bias, lack of control, ethical issues (covert), [[Demand Characteristics]] (overt).

#### Self-Report Methods
*   **[[Questionnaires Sociology]]:**
    *   **Description:** Pre-set list of written questions (open/closed).
    *   **Strengths:** Gathers large amounts of data quickly, cost-effective, reduces interviewer bias.
    *   **Weaknesses:** Social desirability bias, acquiescence bias, response bias.
*   **[[Interviews]]:**
    *   **Description:** Face-to-face (or phone/online) verbal questioning.
    *   **Types:** Structured, semi-structured, unstructured.
    *   **Strengths:** Gathers rich [[Qualitative Data|Qualitative Data]] (unstructured), clarifies questions.
    *   **Weaknesses:** Interviewer bias, social desirability bias, time-consuming.

#### Case Studies
*   **Description:** In-depth investigation of a single individual, group, institution, or event.
*   **Strengths:** Rich, detailed [[Qualitative Data|Qualitative Data]], studies unique/rare phenomena, can generate hypotheses.
*   **Weaknesses:** Difficult to generalize, researcher bias, ethical issues (confidentiality).

### 3. Experimental Design

| Design Type | Description | Strengths | Weaknesses |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **[[Independent Measures Design]]** | Different participants in each condition of the IV. | No order effects, reduced [[Demand Characteristics]]. | [[Participant Variables]], needs more participants. |
| **[[Repeated Measures Design]]** | Same participants in all conditions of the IV. | Controls [[Participant Variables]], fewer participants needed. | Order effects (practice/fatigue), increased [[Demand Characteristics]]. |
| **[[Matched Pairs Design]]** | Different but similar participants in each condition, matched on key characteristics. | Controls [[Participant Variables]], no order effects. | Difficult/time-consuming to match, matching isn't perfect. |

### 4. Sampling Methods

*   **Random Sample:** Every member of the target population has an equal chance of being selected. (Strengths: Representative. Weaknesses: Time-consuming, difficult).
*   **Opportunity Sample:** Selecting anyone who is conveniently available at the time of the study. (Strengths: Easy, quick. Weaknesses: Unrepresentative, researcher bias).
*   **Volunteer Sample (Self-selected):** Participants put themselves forward. (Strengths: Easy, willing participants. Weaknesses: Unrepresentative, biased).
*   **Systematic Sample:** Selecting every Nth person from a list. (Strengths: Avoids researcher bias. Weaknesses: Not truly random unless list is randomized).
*   **Stratified Sample:** Population is divided into subgroups (strata), then a random sample is taken from each stratum in proportion to their occurrence in the population. (Strengths: Highly representative. Weaknesses: Time-consuming, difficult).

### 5. Ethical Considerations

*   **[[Informed Consent]]**: Participants must be aware of the aims, procedure, and their rights before agreeing.
*   **Deception**: Misleading or withholding information from participants (must be justified, debriefed).
*   **Right to Withdraw**: Participants can leave the study at any time and withdraw their data.
*   **Protection from Harm**: Participants should not be exposed to greater physical or psychological risk than in their daily lives.
*   **Confidentiality**: Personal information and data should be protected.
*   **Privacy**: Participants' personal space and behaviors should not be observed without their consent (unless in a public place).
*   **Debrief**: Post-study explanation of the true aims and to address any harm/distress.

### 6. Validity & Reliability

#### [[Validity]]
*   **Internal [[Validity]]:** Whether the observed effects are due to the manipulation of the IV, not EVs.
*   **External [[Validity]]:** Whether findings can be generalized outside the study.
    *   **Ecological [[Validity]]:** Generalize to real-life settings.
    *   **Population [[Validity]]:** Generalize to other groups of people.
    *   **Temporal [[Validity]]:** Generalize to other historical times.

#### [[Reliability]]
*   **Description:** Consistency of a measure.
*   **Types:**
    *   **Test-retest reliability:** Consistency over time (e.g., repeating a questionnaire).
    *   **Inter-rater reliability:** Consistency between different observers/researchers.

### 7. Data Analysis

#### Qualitative vs. Quantitative Data
*   **[[Qualitative Data]]**: Non-numerical, descriptive data (e.g., interview transcripts, [[Observations]]).
    *   **Strengths:** Rich in detail, high [[Ecological Validity]].
    *   **Weaknesses:** Subjective, difficult to analyze/compare, researcher bias.
*   **[[Quantitative Data]]**: Numerical data (e.g., scores, frequencies, measurements).
    *   **Strengths:** Objective, easy to analyze statistically, high reliability.
    *   **Weaknesses:** Lacks depth, may oversimplify reality.

#### Levels of Measurement
*   **[[Nominal Data]]**: Categories only (e.g., types of car).
*   **[[Ordinal Data]]**: Ranks or order, but unequal intervals (e.g., 1st, 2nd, 3rd in a race).
*   **[[Interval Data]]**: Equal intervals, no true zero (e.g., temperature in °C).
*   **[[Ratio Data]]**: Equal intervals, true zero (e.g., height, weight).

#### Descriptive Statistics
*   **Measures of Central Tendency:**
    *   **Mean:** Average (most sensitive, affected by outliers).
    *   **Median:** Middle value (unaffected by outliers).
    *   **Mode:** Most frequent value (used for [[Nominal Data|Nominal Data]]).
*   **Measures of Dispersion:**
    *   **Range:** Difference between highest and lowest value (simple, affected by outliers).
    *   **Standard Deviation:** Average spread of data from the mean (precise, not affected by outliers).

#### Inferential Statistics
*   **Description:** Used to make conclusions about a population based on a sample, and to determine if results are statistically significant (not due to chance).
*   **Significance:** Usually p ≤ 0.05 (5% chance results are random).
*   **Common Tests (Basic understanding for A-Level):** [[Sign Test]], [[Mann-Whitney U]], [[Wilcoxon T]], Spearman's Rho, Chi-Squared, [[Unrelated t-test]], [[Related t-test]].

### 8. Graphs & Tables (Data Presentation)
*   **Tables:** Organize raw data or summary statistics.
*   **Bar Charts:** Compare frequencies of [[Discrete Data|Discrete Data]].
*   **Histograms:** Show distribution of [[Continuous Data|Continuous Data]].
*   **Scattergrams:** Show relationship between two co-variables (for [[Correlations]]).
*   **Line Graphs:** Show changes over time.

### 9. The Research Process (Overview)
1.  **Aim & Hypothesis Formulation.**
2.  **Method Design:** Choose method, design, sampling, operationalize variables.
3.  **Ethical Considerations.**
4.  **[[Pilot Study|Pilot Study]] (optional):** Test procedure and materials.
5.  **Data Collection.**
6.  **Data Analysis** (Descriptive & Inferential).
7.  **Conclusion & Discussion:** Relate back to hypothesis, evaluate method, suggest improvements/future research.
8.  **Report Writing.**
