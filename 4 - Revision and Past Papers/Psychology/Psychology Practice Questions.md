# Psychology (OCR) Exam Technique & Practice

## General Exam Advice
- **Context is King**: For questions with a scenario (Section A Research Methods), **always** refer to the specific details of the story/study in your answer. Generic answers get capped marks.
- **Terminology**: Use precise terms (e.g., say "ecological [[Validity]]" not "real life").
- **Elaboration**: Don't just state a point; explain *why* it matters.

---

## Section A: Research Methods Practice

### Q1) Hypothesis Writing (3 marks)
**Scenario**: A psychologist wants to investigate if chewing gum improves concentration. They test 20 students; half chew gum, half do not, while completing a puzzle.

**Question**: Write a non-directional (two-tailed) alternate hypothesis for this study.

**Model Answer:**
> "There will be a significant difference in the time taken to complete the puzzle (in seconds) between participants who chew gum and participants who do not chew gum."

**Mark Scheme Breakdown:**
- **IV mentioned**: Chewing gum vs. no gum.
- **DV operationalised**: Time taken in *seconds* (or score on puzzle).
- **Non-directional**: "Significant difference" (not "faster" or "slower").
- *Tip: If you forgot "seconds", you'd lose a mark for lack of operationalisation.*

### Q2) Sampling Techniques (2 marks)
**Question**: Explain one weakness of using opportunity sampling in this study.

**Model Answer:**
> "One weakness is that the sample may be biased and unrepresentative of the target population. For example, the students available at the time might all be friends or study the same subject, meaning their concentration levels are not typical of all students, so results cannot be generalised."

**Mark Scheme Breakdown:**
- **Point**: Biased/Unrepresentative.
- **Context**: Mentions "students available" or "study same subject".
- **Explain**: Cannot generalise.

## Section B: Core Studies / Larger Questions

### Q3) Evaluation (15 marks)
**Question**: Discuss the ethical issues involved in Milgram's study on obedience. [15]

**How to structure this answer:**
You need to produce a balanced argument (Strengths vs Weaknesses) and stick to the "PEE" structure (Point, Evidence, Explain).

**Model Paragraph 1 (Weakness - Protection from Harm):**
> "One major ethical issue in Milgram's study is the lack of protection from harm. Participants were exposed to extreme stress, with observers noting signs of severe tension such as trembling, sweating, and even seizures. This breaches the ethical guideline that participants should leave the study in the same state they entered. However, Milgram argued this was unexpected."

**Model Paragraph 2 (Strength - Debriefing):**
> "On the other hand, Milgram showed good ethical practice through his debriefing. After the experiment, he revealed the true aim (that the shocks were fake) and reconciled the participant with the 'learner'. Follow-up questionnaires showed that 84% were glad to have participated, suggesting the harm was not long-term."

**Model Paragraph 3 (Weakness - Deception/Consent):**
> "Another issue is deception, which invalidates informed consent. Participants were told the study was about 'memory and learning' and that the shocks were real. This means they could not give fully informed consent as they didn't know the true nature of the stress they would endure."

**Conclusion:**
> "In conclusion, while Milgram's study provided groundbreaking insights, it is heavily criticised for its potential for psychological harm. The cost to participants is often debated against the benefit to society's understanding of obedience."
