# Sociology (WJEC) Exam Technique & Practice

## The "PERC" / "PEEL" Structure
To score high marks in Sociology, you must move beyond just "knowing facts". You need to construct an argument. Use this paragraph structure for essays:

1.  **Point**: State your argument clearly using sociological terminology (e.g., "Functionalists argue...").
2.  **Explain**: Elaborate on what this means. How does it work?
3.  **Evidence/Example**: Refer to a sociologist (e.g., Durkheim, Parsons) or a study/concept.
4.  **Link/Critique**: Connect it back to the question OR offer a counter-argument (Evaluation).

## Section A: Understanding Key Concepts (Short Answer)

### Q1) Define the term 'Socialisation' (5 marks)
*Don't just give a dictionary definition. Give the sociological depth.*

**Model Answer:**
> "Socialisation is the lifelong process by which individuals learn the norms, values, and culture of their society. It is divided into **primary socialisation** (occurring in the family during early childhood, e.g., learning language) and **secondary socialisation** (occurring later via institutions like education and media). Functionalists see this as essential for social solidarity, whereas Marxists argue it transmits ruling class ideology."

**Why this scores marks:**
- Defines the core concept clearly.
- Distinguishes types (Primary vs Secondary).
- Mentions theoretical perspectives (Functionalism/Marxism) - *this is the "gold standard" for full marks.*

## Section B: Essay Questions

### Q2) Outline and explain two reasons why youth subcultures form. [15 marks]

**Model Paragraph 1 (Status Frustration):**
> "One reason youth subcultures form is due to **status frustration**, a concept introduced by **Albert Cohen**.
> **Explain:** Cohen argued that working-class boys often fail to achieve status in the middle-class school system ('status deprivation').
> **Evidence:** To resolve this, they form delinquent subcultures where they can gain status among their peers by inverting mainstream values-for example, gaining respect for fighting or stealing rather than academic success.
> **Link:** Therefore, subcultures form as a psychological coping mechanism to deal with social failure."

**Model Paragraph 2 (Resistance):**
> "A second reason is to resist capitalism, as argued by **Marxist** sociologists like **Hall and Jefferson**.
> **Explain:** They suggest that youth subcultures (like Teddy Boys or Skinheads) are a magical solution to the economic problems faced by the working class.
> **Evidence:** For example, Skinheads exaggerated their working-class identity (boots, braces) to 'resist' the decline of traditional working-class communities.
> **Link:** Thus, subcultures are not just about fun, but are a political response to perceived oppression."

### Q3) Evaluate the view that the family is a positive institution. [30+ marks]
*For these big questions, you need a debate: Functionalism (Pro-family) vs. Marxism/Feminism (Anti-family).*

**Introduction Tip:**
> Start by defining the family and stating the debate. "Functionalists like Murdock argue the family is universal and beneficial, whereas critical theories suggest it oppresses women and serves capitalism."

**Main Body Tip (The "Evaluation" sandwich):**
- **Point:** Functionalists (Parsons) argue the family performs the 'warm bath theory' function.
- **Explain/Evidence:** It stabilises adult personalities by relieving stress.
- **Evaluation (The "However"):** *However*, Feminists (Ansley) criticise this, arguing that women absorb this anger ("takers of shit") and that the family is patriarchal, not a warm bath for everyone.
