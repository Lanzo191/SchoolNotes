# 1. Cell Biology

## Eukaryotic and Prokaryotic Cells
- **Eukaryotic cells** contain a [[Nucleus]], linear [[DNA]] and membrane-bound organelles. Examples include human, plant and fungal cells.  
- **[[Prokaryotic Cells]]** lack a [[Nucleus]]; instead, [[DNA]] is circular and free in the cytoplasm. These cells are smaller and simpler (e.g., bacteria).

## Organelles and Functions
- **[[Nucleus]]:** Stores [[DNA]] and controls cell activities.  
- **[[Mitochondria]]:** Site of aerobic respiration; produces [[ATP]].  
- **[[Ribosomes]]:** Site of [[Protein Synthesis]]; can be free-floating or attached to RER.  
- **Rough Endoplasmic Reticulum (RER):** [[Protein Synthesis]] and transport.  
- **Smooth ER:** Lipid synthesis and detoxification.  
- **[[Golgi Apparatus]]:** Modifies and packages [[Proteins]]/lipids into vesicles.  
- **[[Lysosomes]]:** Contain enzymes for digestion of old cell parts.  
- **Chloroplasts (plants):** Photosynthesis.  
- **Vacuole (plants):** Stores water and keeps turgor pressure.  
- **Cell membrane:** Phospholipid bilayer; selectively permeable.

## Microscopy
- **Light microscopes** allow viewing of living samples; lower resolution.  
- **Electron microscopes** give high-resolution images; cannot view living cells.

# 2. Biological Molecules

## Carbohydrates
- Built from monosaccharides (e.g., glucose).  
- Used for energy storage (starch, glycogen) and structure (cellulose).

## Lipids
- Triglycerides and phospholipids.  
- Used for energy storage, protection and insulation.  
- Phospholipids form membranes.

## Proteins
- Made of amino acids joined by peptide bonds.  
- Functions include: enzymes, transport (haemoglobin), structure (collagen), hormones.

## Enzymes
- Biological catalysts that lower activation energy.  
- Have an **active site** complementary to a specific substrate.  
- Affected by **temperature**, **pH**, **substrate concentration**, **enzyme concentration**.  
- Inhibition can be **competitive** (active site blocked) or **non-competitive** (enzyme shape changed).

---

# 3. Cellular Transport

## Diffusion
- Movement of molecules from high to low concentration.

## Osmosis
- Movement of water across a semi-permeable membrane from high to low water potential.

## Active Transport
- Movement of molecules against the gradient using ATP; uses carrier proteins.

## Facilitated Diffusion
- Passive transport through channel or carrier proteins.

---

# 4. Tissues

## Epithelial Tissue
- Covers body surfaces; protective barrier. Examples: squamous, cuboidal, ciliated.

## Connective Tissue
- Supports and connects structures. Examples: bone, blood, cartilage, adipose.

## Muscle Tissue
- **Skeletal:** Voluntary, striated, fast contractions.  
- **Smooth:** Involuntary, slow sustained contractions.  
- **Cardiac:** Found in heart; contracts rhythmically.

## Nervous Tissue
- Contains neurons and glial cells; transmits electrical impulses.

---

# 5. Digestive System

## Overview
The digestive system breaks down food into absorbable molecules.

### Major Steps
- **Mouth:** Mechanical digestion; amylase begins starch breakdown.  
- **Oesophagus:** Peristalsis moves food.  
- **Stomach:** Acidic environment; proteins digested by pepsin.  
- **Small intestine:** Chemical digestion and absorption; villi increase surface area.  
- **Large intestine:** Water absorption; formation of faeces.

## Accessory Organs
- **Liver:** Produces bile; detoxifies; stores glycogen.  
- **Gallbladder:** Stores and releases bile.  
- **Pancreas:** Produces digestive enzymes and hormones (insulin, glucagon).

---

# 6. Gas Exchange & Respiratory System

## Structure
- **Trachea → Bronchi → Bronchioles → Alveoli**  
- Alveoli provide large surface area and thin diffusion distance.

## Ventilation
- **Inhalation:** Diaphragm contracts, thoracic volume increases, pressure decreases → air moves in.  
- **Exhalation:** Opposite process.

## Gas Exchange
- Oxygen diffuses from alveoli into capillary blood; CO₂ diffuses out.  
- Maintained by ventilation and good blood supply.

---

# 7. Circulatory System

## Heart Structure
- Four chambers: left/right atria, left/right ventricles.  
- Valves prevent backflow: tricuspid, bicuspid (mitral), semilunar.

## Blood Vessels
- **Arteries:** Thick muscular walls; carry blood away from heart under high pressure.  
- **Veins:** Thinner walls; valves; return blood to heart.  
- **Capillaries:** Thin walls for diffusion.

## Blood Components
- **Red blood cells:** Carry oxygen using haemoglobin.  
- **White blood cells:** Defence against infection.  
- **Platelets:** Blood clotting.  
- **Plasma:** Transports nutrients, hormones and waste.

# 8. Homeostasis

## Thermoregulation (Temperature Control)
Controlled by the **hypothalamus**.

### When too hot:
- **Vasodilation:** Blood vessels widen → more heat loss.  
- **Sweating:** Evaporation removes heat.  
- **Erector muscles relax:** Hairs lie flat.

### When too cold:
- **Vasoconstriction:** Blood vessels narrow → reduces heat loss.  
- **Shivering:** Increases respiration → more heat.  
- **Piloerection:** Hairs trap air.

## Hyperthermia & Hypothermia
- **Hyperthermia:** Body overheats; enzymes denature; organ failure risk.  
- **Hypothermia:** Body temperature drops; slowed enzyme activity; impaired brain function.

# 9. Osmoregulation & Kidney Function

## Kidney Structure
- Cortex, medulla, pelvis.  
- Functional unit = **nephron**.

## Nephron Key Regions
- **Bowman’s capsule:** Ultrafiltration of blood.  
- **Proximal tubule:** Reabsorption of glucose, amino acids, salts.  
- **Loop of Henle:** Establishes salt gradient; water balance.  
- **Distal tubule:** Selective reabsorption.  
- **Collecting duct:** Final water reabsorption.

## ADH (Anti-Diuretic Hormone)
- Released from posterior pituitary when blood water is low.  
- Makes collecting ducts **more permeable** → more water reabsorbed → concentrated urine.

## Osmoregulation Summary
- Controlled by hypothalamus detecting blood water level.  
- ADH adjusts kidney water reabsorption.  
- Maintains stable blood volume and plasma concentration.

# 10. Endocrine System

## Hormones
- Chemical messengers released into bloodstream; slower but longer-lasting than nerves.

### Key Hormones
- **Insulin:** Lowers blood glucose by increasing uptake.  
- **Glucagon:** Raises blood glucose by releasing stored glycogen.  
- **Adrenaline:** Fight-or-flight; increases heart rate & respiration.  
- **Thyroxine:** Controls metabolic rate.  
- **ADH:** Controls water reabsorption.

---

# 11. Nervous System

## Neurons
- **Sensory neurons:** Carry impulses from receptors to CNS.  
- **Relay neurons:** Inside CNS.  
- **Motor neurons:** Carry impulses from CNS to effectors.

## Synapses
- Electrical impulse triggers neurotransmitter release.  
- Neurotransmitter diffuses across synaptic cleft and binds receptors.  
- Ensures one-way transmission.

## Reflex Arc
- Rapid, involuntary response; bypasses conscious brain → protects body.

---

# 12. Musculoskeletal System

## Bone
- Provides support, protection, stores minerals and produces blood cells.

## Joints
- **Hinge:** Elbow, knee; movement in one plane.  
- **Ball-and-socket:** Hip, shoulder; multiple directions.

## Muscle Types
- Skeletal (voluntary, fast), smooth (involuntary), cardiac (involuntary, rhythmic).

# 13. Immunity & Disease

## Innate Immunity
- Physical barriers (skin).  
- Phagocytosis: white blood cells engulf pathogens.

## Adaptive Immunity
- B lymphocytes → antibodies.  
- T lymphocytes → kill infected cells.  
- Memory cells provide long-term immunity.

## Vaccination
- Harmless antigen triggers immune response → memory cells.

# 14. Reproduction & Genetics

## DNA
- Double helix; contains genes coding for proteins.

## Protein Synthesis
- **Transcription:** DNA → mRNA.  
- **Translation:** mRNA → protein at ribosome.

## Mitosis
- Cell division producing two identical diploid cells.

## Meiosis
- Produces gametes; halves chromosome number; introduces variation.

## Fertilisation
- Fusion of sperm and egg to form zygote.
