
## Purpose
Blood tests are used to diagnose, monitor, and assess health conditions by analysing the components and chemistry of blood.

They are essential for identifying:
- Infections  
- Anaemia  
- [[Diabetes]]  
- [[Liver]] and [[Kidney]] disorders  
- Immune and hormonal imbalances

---

## Sample Type
- Venous blood (from arm vein, e.g., median cubital)
- Collected using a sterile vacutainer system
- Tubes may contain anticoagulants (EDTA, heparin) or clot activators, depending on test

## Sample Collection Procedure

### Aim
To obtain a venous blood sample safely and accurately.

### Equipment
- Tourniquet  
- Alcohol swab  
- Sterile needle and vacutainer holder  
- Collection tubes (labelled)  
- Cotton wool and plaster  
- Sharps container  
- Gloves

### Steps
1. Verify patient identity and explain the procedure.  
2. Apply a tourniquet around the upper arm to locate a suitable vein.  
3. Clean the site with an alcohol swab and allow to dry.  
4. Insert the needle at approximately 30° angle into the vein.  
5. Attach a vacutainer tube to draw the sample.  
6. When filled, remove the tube and gently invert if anticoagulant is present.  
7. Release the tourniquet and remove the needle.  
8. Apply cotton wool and a plaster to the site.  
9. Label tubes immediately with patient information, date, and test type.  
10. Dispose of the needle in the sharps bin.

## Full Blood Count (FBC)

### Aim
To measure the cellular components of blood - red blood cells (RBCs), white blood cells (WBCs), and platelets.

### Equipment
- EDTA anticoagulant tube  
- Automated haematology analyser  
- Microscope and slides (for manual check if needed)

### Steps
1. Collect 2–3 mL of blood into an EDTA tube.  
2. Mix gently to prevent clotting.  
3. Load the sample into the automated analyser.  
4. The machine counts and classifies cells using impedance or laser flow methods.  
5. Review results for:
   - RBC count  
   - WBC count  
   - Platelets  
   - Haemoglobin (Hb)  
   - Haematocrit  
6. If results are abnormal, prepare a blood smear for manual microscopic review.

### Results Interpretation
| Parameter | Low | High |
|------------|-----|------|
| RBC/Hb | Anaemia | Dehydration, polycythaemia |
| WBC | Viral infection | Bacterial infection |
| Platelets | Bleeding disorder | Inflammation |

## Blood Glucose Test

### Aim
To determine glucose concentration - essential in diagnosing diabetes mellitus.

### Equipment
- Sodium fluoride tube (prevents glycolysis)
- Centrifuge
- Glucose reagent (GOD-POD method)
- Colorimeter or spectrophotometer
- Pipette and cuvette

### Steps
1. Collect blood into a fluoride tube and mix gently.  
2. Centrifuge at 2000 rpm for 10 minutes to separate plasma.  
3. Pipette 1 mL of reagent into a test tube.  
4. Add 10–20 µL of plasma sample.  
5. Incubate at 37°C for 10 minutes.  
6. Measure absorbance at 505 nm in a spectrophotometer.  
7. Use a glucose standard to calculate concentration.

### Chemical Principle
Glucose + O₂ → (Glucose oxidase) → H₂O₂  
H₂O₂ + Chromogen → (Peroxidase) → Coloured compound

### Normal Range
- Fasting: 3.9–5.5 mmol/L  
- High: Diabetes mellitus  
- Low: Hypoglycaemia

## Liver Function Tests (LFTs)

### Aim
To assess liver health and detect hepatocellular damage or bile obstruction.

### Equipment
- Serum sample (no anticoagulant)
- Automated biochemistry analyser
- ALT, AST, Bilirubin, Albumin reagents

### Steps
1. Allow blood to clot and centrifuge to obtain serum.  
2. Load serum into the auto-analyser.  
3. Select tests required (ALT, AST, bilirubin, albumin).  
4. The analyser uses enzymatic and spectrophotometric methods to quantify results.  
5. Interpret readings using normal reference values.

### Typical Reference Ranges
| Test | Normal Range | Abnormal Indications |
|------|---------------|----------------------|
| ALT | 7–56 U/L | Liver cell damage |
| AST | 10–40 U/L | Muscle or liver injury |
| Bilirubin | <21 µmol/L | Jaundice, bile obstruction |
| Albumin | 35–50 g/L | Low = poor liver synthesis |

## Kidney Function Tests

### Aim
To evaluate the kidneys’ ability to filter waste and maintain fluid and electrolyte balance.

### Equipment
- Serum or plasma sample  
- Urea reagent, creatinine reagent  
- Colorimeter or auto-analyser

### Steps
1. Collect the sample (heparin or serum).  
2. Add urea reagent and incubate at 37°C.  
3. Measure absorbance using a colorimeter.  
4. Repeat using creatinine reagent for the second test.  
5. Compare results to standards for concentration calculation.

### Key Parameters
| Test | Normal Range | Clinical Significance |
|------|---------------|-----------------------|
| Urea | 2.5–7.1 mmol/L | High = renal failure, dehydration |
| Creatinine | 45–120 µmol/L | High = kidney impairment |
| Electrolytes (Na⁺, K⁺, Cl⁻) | Variable | Reflect balance and hydration state |

## Lipid Profile / Cholesterol

### Aim
To assess cardiovascular risk by measuring lipoproteins.

### Tests
- Total Cholesterol  
- LDL (“bad” cholesterol)  
- HDL (“good” cholesterol)  
- Triglycerides

### Procedure
1. Serum sample (after 12-hour fast).  
2. Enzymatic reaction with cholesterol oxidase/peroxidase.  
3. Measure absorbance at 500–550 nm.  
4. Interpret using reference ranges.

### Interpretation
| Result | Meaning |
|---------|----------|
| High LDL | Atherosclerosis risk |
| Low HDL | Poor cardiovascular protection |
| High Triglycerides | Obesity, metabolic syndrome |

## Serological and Immunoassay Tests (e.g., ELISA)

### Purpose
To detect antibodies or antigens (e.g., HIV, hepatitis, COVID-19).

### Principle
Antibody–antigen binding causes a colour change through enzyme-linked reactions.

### Steps (Indirect ELISA Example)
1. Wells are coated with a specific antigen.  
2. Add patient serum; if antibodies are present, they bind.  
3. Wash to remove unbound substances.  
4. Add an enzyme-linked secondary antibody.  
5. Wash again and add substrate.  
6. A colour change indicates a positive result, which is quantified spectrophotometrically.

## Common Conditions Diagnosed
| Test | Abnormal Result | Possible Disease |
|------|------------------|------------------|
| FBC | Low Hb | Anaemia |
| WBC | High | Infection |
| Glucose | High | Diabetes |
| LFTs | High ALT/AST | Liver disease |
| Urea/Creatinine | High | Kidney disease |
| Cholesterol | High LDL | Heart disease |

## Health and Safety
- Always wear gloves, lab coat, and eye protection.  
- Treat all blood as biohazardous.  
- Dispose of needles in sharps bins immediately.  
- Follow infection control and waste management procedures.
