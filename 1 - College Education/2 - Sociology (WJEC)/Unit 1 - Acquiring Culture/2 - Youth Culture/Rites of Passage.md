## Overview - 
A **Rite of Passage** is a ritual or ceremony that marks a person's transition from one social status to another, most commonly from **childhood to adulthood**. 

- These rituals serve to provide a clear boundary between life stages.
- In many traditional societies, these are formal and public. In modern Western societies, they are often less formal.

## Perspectives on Rites of Passage -

### Tim Lott - The Need for Ritual
The author **Tim Lott** argues that modern Western society suffers from a **lack of formal rites of passage**, which has led to several negative consequences:
*   **Lack of Guidance:** Young people don't know when they have truly "become adults."
*   **Negative Behaviours:** He argues that in the absence of formal rituals, young people create their own, often dangerous ones, such as **self-harm**, **self-starvation**, or **joining gangs** to prove their "toughness" or adulthood.

### Traditional Examples -
Historically, many cultures used rites of passage to separate the genders and test maturity:
*   **Men:** Often involved being "separated from the tribe" and having to survive in the wilderness or perform a feat of bravery.
*   **Women:** Often focused on preparation for marriage and motherhood; sometimes included education separate from men or, in some cultures, physical modifications like genital mutilation.

### Modern (Smaller) Rites of Passage -
In contemporary society, we have "mini" rituals that symbolise growing up:
*   **Driving Tests:** Gaining the legal right to drive.
*   **Graduations:** Marking the end of educational stages.
*   **Uniform Changes:** Moving from primary to secondary school uniform.
*   **Legal Age Milestones:** Turning 18 or 21 (voting, drinking, etc.).

## Sociological Function -
**Functionalists** (like Parsons) see these as essential for maintaining **social order**. They prevent a "gap" in identity where a young person feels lost, ensuring they understand their new roles and responsibilities in the adult world.
