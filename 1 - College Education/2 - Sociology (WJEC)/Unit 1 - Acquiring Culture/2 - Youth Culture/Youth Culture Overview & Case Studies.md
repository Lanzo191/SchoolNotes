## Overview -
Youth subcultures often form around shared music, fashion, and values. They provide a sense of belonging and "found family" for young people.

---

## Case Study 1: The Goths -
*   **Origin:** 1980s England, evolving from post-punk.
*   **Key Musicians:** Siouxsie and the Banshees, The Cure, Joy Division.
*   **Influence:** Draws from 19th-century gothic literature (Frankenstein) and German Expressionist cinema.
*   **Values:** Often explores the darker aspects of life (mortality, existentialism). Frequently overlaps with liberal or libertarian political views.

## Case Study 2: Mods vs. Rockers (1964) -
Stan Cohen used this clash to develop his theory of **Moral Panics**.

### The Context:
*   **The Mods:** Wore Italian suits, rode Vespas, and listened to Jazz/Soul. Seen as "elitist" and sophisticated.
*   **The Rockers:** Wore leather jackets, rode heavy motorbikes, and listened to 1950s American Rock 'n' Roll.

### The Media Reaction:
*   In the summer of 1964, minor scuffles broke out at Clacton pier.
*   The media used sensationalist headlines to portray both groups as **Folk Devils** (individuals or groups defined as a threat to social values).
*   **Deviancy Amplification**: By reporting exactly where the groups were, the media encouraged thousands more youths to flock to the seaside, making the problem worse, leading to a **Moral Panic**.

## Why Join a Youth Subculture? -
*   **Shared Identity:** A way to define who you are.
*   **Status:** Gaining respect from peers when you lack status in wider society.
*   **Resistance:** Symbolic rebellion against parents or the government.
*   **Community:** Finding a "found family" with shared interests and politics.
