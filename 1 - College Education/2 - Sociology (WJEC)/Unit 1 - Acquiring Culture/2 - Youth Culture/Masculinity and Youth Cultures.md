## Overview -
Research into masculinity explores how gender identity shapes the subcultural responses of young men, particularly in relation to education and the changing [[Labour]] market.

## Key Concepts -

### 1. Crisis of Masculinity -
Sociologists argue that the decline of traditional male-dominated industries (mining, manufacturing) has left some men feeling lost. Their "ego" and traditional role as "breadwinner" is under threat, leading them to reassert their masculinity through deviant subcultures.

### 2. Mac an Ghaill (1994) - "The Making of Men" -
Mac an Ghaill conducted an ethnographic study of a West Midlands school and identified four distinct male subcultures:

1.  **The Macho Lads:** Academic failures in bottom sets. They rejected school as "hostile" and valued "real" physical work. They viewed academic study as "effeminate."
2.  **The Academic Achievers:** From skilled working-class backgrounds. They were positive about school and sought upward mobility through traditional subjects (Maths, Science).
3.  **The New Enterprisers:** Pro-school but rejected the traditional curriculum. They focused on vocational subjects like Business and IT, exploiting school-industry links.
4.  **The Real Englishmen:** Middle-class students who felt superior to the school's culture. They aimed for university but tried to appear as if they achieved success "effortlessly."

### 3. Gay Students -
Mac an Ghaill was one of the first to include the experiences of gay students, who found the school environment **heterosexist and homophobic**, challenging the normalisation of the nuclear family.
