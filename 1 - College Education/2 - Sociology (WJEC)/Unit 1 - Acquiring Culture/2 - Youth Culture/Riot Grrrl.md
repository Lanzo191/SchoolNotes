## Overview -
- **Riot Grrrl** was an underground feminist punk rock movement that began in the early 1990s (Washington D.C. and Pacific Northwest).
- It emerged as a reaction against the male-dominated punk scene and broader patriarchal society.

### Key Characteristics -
- **Music:** Loud, aggressive punk music with lyrics addressing issues like rape, domestic abuse, sexuality, racism, and female empowerment.
- **Zines:** The production of "fanzines" (homemade magazines) was central to the movement, spreading political ideas and connecting girls.
- **Politics:** It was deeply political, associated with **Third-Wave [[Feminism]]**.
- **Slogan:** "Revolution Girl Style Now!"

### Connection to Sociology -
- Riot Grrrl serves as a prime example of an active, visible female subculture, challenging the idea that girls are passive or only exist in "Bedroom Cultures".
- It demonstrates how youth subcultures can be used for political resistance and social change, not just style.
