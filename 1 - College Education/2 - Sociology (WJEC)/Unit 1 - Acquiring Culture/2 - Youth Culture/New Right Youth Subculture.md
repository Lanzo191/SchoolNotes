## Overview - 
The **New Right** perspective is a political and sociological view that emphasizes traditional values, individual responsibility, and the importance of the nuclear family. They are highly critical of the welfare state.

## Charles Murray and the Underclass -
**Charles Murray** is the most prominent New Right thinker regarding subcultures. He argues that there is a distinct group at the bottom of the social structure called the **Underclass**.

*   **Characteristics of the Underclass:** They are described as "work-shy," dependent on welfare benefits, and often involved in criminal behavior.
*   **Cause of Deviant Subcultures:** Murray suggests that deviant youth subcultures are not caused by economic poverty, but by **poor [[Socialisation]]**.
*   **The Role of the Family:** He blames the rise of **single motherhood** and the absence of **father figures**. Without a male role model to provide discipline and a "breadwinner" example, young boys turn to delinquent peer groups (subcultures) for status and identity.
*   **Dependency Culture:** The New Right argues that the welfare state creates a "dependency culture" where people are rewarded for not working, leading to a cycle of poverty and deviance.

## Evaluation -
### Strengths
*   Highlights the importance of [[Socialisation]] and the family in shaping behavior.
*   Influential in shaping government policies (e.g., "tough on crime" and welfare reform).

### Weaknesses
*   **Victim Blaming:** Critics argue it blames the poor for their own situation while ignoring structural issues like unemployment and lack of investment in poor areas.
*   **Generalisation:** Many children from single-parent families grow up to be successful and law-abiding.
*   **Marxist Critique:** Marxists argue the "Underclass" is a myth used to divide the working class and distract from the failures of capitalism.
