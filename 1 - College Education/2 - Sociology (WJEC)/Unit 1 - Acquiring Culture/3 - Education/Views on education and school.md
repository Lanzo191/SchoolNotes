#Education #Sociology #WJEC

## Overview -
Sociological perspectives on education differ on whether schools benefit society as a whole or serve the interests of a dominant group.

---

## 1. Functionalist View (Positive) -
Functionalists see education as a vital part of socialisation and social order.
*   **Emile Durkheim:** Viewed schools as **"Society in Miniature."** Children learn to cooperate with people who are not their family, preparing them for wider society.
*   **Talcott Parsons:** Schools act as a **bridge** between the "particularistic" values of the family and the "universalistic" values of the adult world.
*   **Social Solidarity:** Education creates a sense of belonging to something larger than oneself.
*   **Meritocracy:** Schools are fair; they allocate people to the best roles based on their ability (**Social Selection**).

## 2. Marxist View (Negative) -
Marxists argue that schools reproduce class inequality.
*   **Legitimation of Inequality:** Schools make it seem like failing is your own fault, rather than a result of your class background.
*   **Correspondence Principle:** The structure of school mirrors the workplace (**Bowles and Gintis**).

## 3. Feminist View (Negative) -
Feminists argue that schools reinforce patriarchy.
*   **Heaton and Lawson (1996):** Argued the **Hidden Curriculum** teaches patriarchal values.
*   **Gendered Treatment:** Teachers often have different expectations for boys and girls and may tolerate more "disruptive" behaviour from boys.
*   **Subject Choice:** Girls and boys are often "steered" towards different subjects (Canalisation).
