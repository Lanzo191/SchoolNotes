**[[Interactionism]]** (also known as **Social Action Theory**) is a **micro-level perspective** that focuses on small-scale interactions between individuals rather than large-scale social structures. Unlike **[[Functionalism]]** or **[[Marxism]]**, it prioritises individual **Agency** over social structure.

---

## Core Concepts
- **Social Construction of Reality**: The idea that our world is built through our interactions and the subjective meanings we give to things.
- **Symbolic Interactionism**: We use symbols (words, gestures, clothing) to communicate and create shared meanings (e.g., see **Youth Cultures**).
- **Labelling Theory**: How being "labelled" by others (especially by those in power) can influence a person's behavior and identity (**Howard Becker**).
- **Self-Fulfilling Prophecy**: When a person's behavior changes to match the label they have been given, making the label "true."
- **The Looking-Glass Self (Cooley)**: The idea that our sense of self is a reflection of how we believe others perceive us.

---

## Key Sociologists

### 1. Howard Becker (1963) - Labelling
*   Argued that an act is not inherently "deviant" until it is labelled as such by others.
*   **Master Status:** Once a label (like "criminal") is applied, it becomes the most important part of someone's identity, affecting how they are treated.

### 2. Erving Goffman (1959) - Dramaturgy
*   **The Dramaturgical Analogy:** Goffman compared life to a theatre play. We are all "actors" performing roles on a stage.
*   **Impression Management:** We use "props" (clothes, language) to control how others see us.
*   **Front Stage vs. Back Stage:** We act differently in public (front stage) than we do when we are alone or with close family (back stage).

### 3. George Herbert Mead - The "I" and the "Me"
*   Mead argued that the "Self" consists of two parts: the **"I"** (spontaneous, creative self) and the **"Me"** (the social self that follows rules). We develop our sense of self by learning to take on the role of the "generalised other."

---

## Role of Institutions
Interactionists are interested in how people *experience* and *negotiate* institutions:
*   **Education**: Focus on teacher-pupil interactions and how teacher expectations (labels) can lead to student success or failure.
*   **Crime**: Focus on why certain acts are defined as "deviant" and how the police target specific groups (see: **[[Moral Panics and Folk Devils]]**).

## Evaluation

| Strengths                                                                                              | Weaknesses                                                                                                 |
| :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Focus on Agency**: Recognizes that individuals are active participants, not "puppets" of the system. | **Ignores Structure**: Fails to account for how class, gender, or race limit individual choices.           |
| **Rich Data**: Uses qualitative methods (e.g., observations) to get a deep understanding of meanings.  | **Un-scientific**: Critics argue that focusing on individual meanings is too subjective and hard to prove. |
| **Explains Identity**: Provides a powerful explanation for how the "self" is shaped by social labels.  | **Small-scale**: Does not explain why society is organised the way it is on a global level.                |

### Q) Explain how labelling can lead to a self-fulfilling prophecy. (10 marks)

**Model Answer:**
Interactionists like **Howard Becker** argue that labels are not just descriptions, but powerful social definitions. **Labelling** occurs when a person in authority (like a teacher or police officer) attaches a meaning to an individual (e.g., "troublemaker" or "bright student").

Once a label is applied, it can become a **Master Status**, meaning it overrides all other parts of the person's identity. If the individual accepts this label, it may lead to a **Self-Fulfilling Prophecy**. For example, if a student is labelled as "stupid," they may stop trying in class, eventually failing their exams. Their failure then "proves" the teacher's original label was correct, even though it was the label itself that caused the behavior. This process shows that reality is **socially constructed** through interaction and the power of definitions.

**Revision Tip:** When discussing Interactionism, always mention **"Meanings"** and **"Definitions."** The theory is all about how we *interpret* the world around us.
