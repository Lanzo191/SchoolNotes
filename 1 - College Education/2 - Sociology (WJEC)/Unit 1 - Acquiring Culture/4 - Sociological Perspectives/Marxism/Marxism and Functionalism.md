## Overview -
These are the two dominant **Structuralist** (Macro) theories in sociology. They both believe that the structure of society shapes the individual, but they disagree on whether this structure is beneficial or harmful.

---

## 1. Functionalism (Consensus Theory) -
**Key Thinker:** Emile Durkheim, Talcott Parsons.
*   **The Organic Analogy:** Society works like a human body. Each institution (Family, Education, Law) is an organ that must work together to keep the body healthy.
*   **Value Consensus:** Society is based on a shared agreement about norms and values, which creates **Social Solidarity**.
*   **Meritocracy:** Belief that success is based on individual talent and hard work.
*   **Anomie:** A state of "normlessness" or social instability (e.g., the 2011 London Riots).

### Evaluation:
*   **Strength:** Explains how society maintains order and stability.
*   **Weakness:** It is "rose-tinted"; it ignores conflict and inequality.

---

## 2. Marxism (Conflict Theory) -
**Key Thinker:** Karl Marx, Louis Althusser.
*   **Class Conflict:** Society is divided into the **Bourgeoisie** (owners) and the **Proletariat** (workers).
*   **Economic Base:** The economy determines everything else in society (**The Superstructure**).
*   **Ideological State Apparatus (ISA):** Institutions like school and media that control people's minds to accept capitalism.
*   **False Class Consciousness:** The workers are misled into believing the system is fair, preventing a revolution.

### Evaluation:
*   **Strength:** Exposes deep-seated inequality and the power of the rich.
*   **Weakness:** Oversimplifies class (ignores the middle class) and fails to account for other inequalities like gender.

## Theory Comparison Table -

| Feature | Functionalism | Marxism |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **View of Society** | A stable system (Body). | A site of conflict (Battlefield). |
| **Goal of Institutions** | To maintain order. | To maintain ruling-class power. |
| **Power** | Shared through consensus. | Held by the Bourgeoisie. |
| **Individual Role** | To fulfil a social function. | To be an instrument of labour. |
