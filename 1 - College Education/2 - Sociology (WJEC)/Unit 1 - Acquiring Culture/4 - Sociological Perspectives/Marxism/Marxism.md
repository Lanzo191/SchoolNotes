**[[Marxism]]** is a **conflict theory** that sees society as being divided into two competing social classes: the **Bourgeoisie** (the ruling class/owners) and the **Proletariat** (the working class). It is a **macro-structural** theory, meaning it looks at how the structure of society (the economy) shapes individual behavior.

---

## Core Concepts
- **Base and Superstructure**: The idea that the economy (the **Base**) determines everything else in society (the **Superstructure**), such as law, politics, and education.
- **[[Capitalism]]**: An economic system based on private ownership and the pursuit of profit, which Marxists believe is inherently exploitative.
- **Class Conflict**: The ongoing struggle between the ruling class (who want to maintain their wealth) and the working class (who are exploited for their **[[Labour]]**).
- **False Class Consciousness**: The ruling class uses institutions (like the media and religion) to trick workers into accepting their exploitation as "natural" or "fair."
- **Ideological State Apparatus (ISA)**: A term by **Louis Althusser** describing institutions like schools and media that control the way we think to prevent **Revolution**.

---

## Role of Institutions
*   **Education**: Reproduces class inequality by teaching working-class children to be obedient workers (see: **[[Education|Bowles & Gintis]]**).
*   **Media**: Acts as an ISA to spread ruling-class ideology and distract the public from the failures of **[[Capitalism]]**.
*   **Family**: Teaches children to accept hierarchy and socialises them into their future roles as workers or owners.

---

## Evaluation

| Strengths | Weaknesses |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Focus on Inequality**: Highlights class divisions that **[[Functionalism]]** ignores. | **Economic Reductionism**: Over-emphasizes the economy, ignoring gender (**[[Feminism]]**) or race. |
| **Structural Analysis**: Explains how institutions protect the interests of the powerful. | **Failed Predictions**: Capitalism hasn't collapsed; revolutions haven't happened in many Western societies. |
| **Critical Perspective**: Challenges the status quo and encourages social change. | **Too Negative**: Ignores the functional benefits or progress (e.g., social mobility) in capitalist societies. |

### Q) Explain the Marxist view of the relationship between the economy and social institutions. (10 marks)

**Model Answer:**
Marxists use the **Base and Superstructure** model to explain how society is organised. They argue that the **Economy (the Base)** is the most important part of society because it determines the nature of all other **Social Institutions (the Superstructure)**.

Because we live in a **[[Capitalism|Capitalist]]** system, the base is designed to generate profit for the **Bourgeoisie**. Consequently, the superstructure:including the education system, the media, and the legal system:is designed to protect that profit. For example, **Louis Althusser** argued that schools act as an **Ideological State Apparatus**, teaching children to accept authority so they become compliant workers. This creates a **False Class Consciousness**, where the **Proletariat** do not realise they are being exploited, thus preventing a **Revolution** and maintaining the power of the ruling class.
