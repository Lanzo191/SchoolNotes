## Overview
**Radical [[Feminism]]** (prominent during **Second Wave [[Feminism]]**) argues that **[[Patriarchy]]**:a system of male domination:is the primary and most fundamental form of social inequality. Unlike Liberal Feminists, they believe society is inherently biased and needs a total overhaul.

## Key Ideas
*   **[[Patriarchy]] as Systemic:** Men as a group dominate women as a group in all areas of life, including the home, workplace, and politics.
*   **Sexual Politics:** Men maintain power through the threat or use of physical force, including **rape, sexual violence**, and the objectification of women in the media/pornography (**Andrea Dworkin**).
*   **Separatism:** Some radical feminists argue that women should live separately from men to escape patriarchal control.
*   **Root and Branch Change:** They believe society requires a total restructuring, rather than just small legal changes.

## Key Thinkers
*   **Andrea Dworkin:** Argued that pornography and sexual violence are tools used by men to control women.
*   **Shulamith Firestone:** Argued that women's oppression is rooted in their biological role in reproduction.

## Evaluation
### Strengths
*   Drew attention to "private" issues like **domestic violence** and **marital rape**.
*   Challenges the very foundations of social institutions rather than just the laws.

### Weaknesses
*   **Essentialism:** Critics argue they portray men as universally "bad" and women as "good," ignoring individual differences.
*   **Misses Intersectionality:** Often ignores how race and class affect a woman's experience of **[[Patriarchy]]**.
*   **Negative Focus:** Can be criticised for focusing only on negative experiences and ignoring progress made in gender relations.

### Q) Explain the concept of "Patriarchy" as understood by Radical Feminists. (10 marks)

**Model Answer:**
For Radical Feminists, **[[Patriarchy]]** is a universal and systemic form of social organization where men dominate women. They see it as the most fundamental form of inequality, predating class or racial divisions.

One way this is expressed is through **sexual politics**. Theorists like **Andrea Dworkin** argue that men use the threat of violence (such as domestic abuse or rape) and the objectification of women in pornography to maintain their status. This ensures that women remain in a subordinate position in both public and private life.

Another key aspect is the idea of **systemic control** over women's bodies. Radical feminists believe that patriarchy is so deeply embedded in society (including marriage and the nuclear family) that equality cannot be achieved through simple law changes. Instead, they argue for **"root and branch"** change, with some even suggesting **separatism**:where women live independently of men:to truly escape patriarchal influence.

---

**Revision Tip:** When writing about Radical Feminists, use the phrase **"The personal is political."** This captures their belief that private issues like housework and childcare are actually part of a larger political system of male power.
