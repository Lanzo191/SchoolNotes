## Overview -
Gender inequality refers to the social, economic, and political disparities between men and women. Feminists argue that these differences are not biological but are **socially constructed** through [[Socialisation]] and systemic oppression (**[[Patriarchy]]**).

---

## Key Concepts in Gender Inequality -

### 1. The Motherhood Penalty -
*   **Definition:** The systematic disadvantage that women face in the workplace after having children.
*   **Impact:** Research shows a **42% loss of income** for mothers compared to non-mothers or fathers.
*   **Causes:** High costs of childcare (e.g., baby food and necessities), lack of flexible work, and the expectation that mothers should be the primary caregivers.
*   **Systemic Issue:** Women often "trade" job security for the ability to manage early motherhood, which widens the gender pay gap.

### 2. Ann Oakley (1974) - Gender Socialisation -
Oakley argued that gender roles are created through four main processes during primary socialisation:
1.  **Manipulation:** Encouraging gender-appropriate behaviour (e.g., bouncing boys more roughly than girls).
2.  **Canalisation:** Directing children towards specific toys (e.g., tools for boys, dolls for girls).
3.  **Verbal Appellations:** Using gendered language (e.g., "Good girl" vs. "Strong boy").
4.  **Domestic Activities:** Encouraging children to help with gendered chores (e.g., girls helping in the kitchen).

### 3. Harriet Bradley (2007) - Gendering -
Bradley argues that gendering is an active process that occurs at three levels:
*   **Micro Level:** Individual choices and interactions.
*   **Meso Level:** Rules within social institutions (e.g., gendered school uniforms).
*   **Macro Level:** Structural differences across the whole of society.

---

## Evaluation -
### Strengths:
*   Challenges the idea that gender roles are "natural" or "innate."
*   Provides clear evidence of economic injustice (e.g., the pay gap).

### Weaknesses:
*   Oakley has been criticised for portraying children as too **passive**; many children resist their gender socialisation.
*   Does not always account for the increasing diversity of gender identities in postmodern society.
