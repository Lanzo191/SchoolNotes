## Overview
**Liberal [[Feminism]]** is a branch of **[[Feminism]]** that focuses on achieving gender equality through **legal reform** and social policy. They believe that women’s inequality is not systemic or inherent to society but is a result of outdated laws and prejudiced attitudes.

## Key Ideas
*   **Legal Reform:** Believe that equality can be achieved by changing the law (e.g., **Sex Discrimination Act 1975**, **Equal Pay Act 1970**).
*   **Education & [[Socialisation]]:** Argue that gender stereotypes are learned through **[[Socialisation]]** and that education can be used to promote equality.
*   **Gradual Change:** They advocate for **"evolution, not revolution"**:making step-by-step progress within the existing social structure.

## Key Figures
*   **Mary Wollstonecraft:** Early pioneer who argued for women's right to education.
*   **Ann Oakley:** Studied the domestic division of **[[Labour]]** and gender [[Socialisation]].

## Evaluation
### Strengths
*   Produced tangible results in law and policy that have significantly improved women's lives.
*   More optimistic than other branches of **[[Feminism]]**.

### Weaknesses
*   **Misses Intersectionality:** Focuses primarily on the experiences of white, middle-class Western women (**Ethnocentric**).
*   **Surface Level:** Fails to consider **Structural Inequality**:critics argue that laws alone cannot fix a society built on male dominance (**[[Patriarchy]]**).

### Q) Outline and explain the Liberal Feminist view of gender inequality. (10 marks)

**Model Answer:**
Liberal Feminists argue that gender inequality stems from **legal discrimination** and biased **socialisation** rather than deep-rooted structural issues. They believe society is moving towards greater equality and that this can be achieved through **"evolution, not revolution"**.

One way they suggest achieving equality is through **legal reform**. They point to successes like the **Equal Pay Act (1970)** and the **Sex Discrimination Act (1975)** as evidence that progress is being made. By changing the rules of society, they believe women can compete on a level playing field with men.

Furthermore, they focus on **gender socialisation**. Theorists like **Ann Oakley** argue that parents and schools often teach children traditional gender roles (e.g., through **canalisation**). Liberal feminists believe that by challenging these stereotypes in schools and the media, we can change attitudes and achieve a more meritocratic society.

---

**Revision Tip:** When discussing Liberal Feminism, always mention that they are the most **optimistic** branch. Contrast them with **[[Radical Feminism]]** by noting that Liberals want to work *within* the system, while Radicals want to *overthrow* it.
