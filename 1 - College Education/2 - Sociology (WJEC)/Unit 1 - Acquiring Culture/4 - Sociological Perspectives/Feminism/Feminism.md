**[[Feminism]]** is a **conflict theory** that sees society as being dominated by men (**[[Patriarchy]]**) and aims to achieve gender equality. It challenges **consensus theories** like **[[Functionalism]]** which see traditional gender roles as beneficial.

## Main Types of Feminism

### 1. Liberal Feminism
- **Focus**: Legal and political reform.
- **View**: Believe that equality can be achieved through changing laws (e.g., **Equal Pay Act**) and challenging stereotypes.
- **Approach**: **"Evolution, not revolution."**

### 2. Radical Feminism
- **Focus**: The deep-seated, systemic nature of **[[Patriarchy]]**.
- **View**: Believe that society is fundamentally patriarchal and that men use violence or the threat of it to control women.
- **Approach**: Revolutionary; often call for **"root and branch"** change.

### 3. Marxist Feminism
- **Focus**: The intersection of **[[Capitalism]]** and **[[Patriarchy]]**.
- **View**: Believe that women's oppression serves the interests of the ruling class (e.g., providing a **"reserve army of [[Labour]]"**).
- **Approach**: Overthrowing both capitalism and [[Patriarchy]].

---

## Core Concepts
- **[[Patriarchy]]**: A social system where men hold primary power and predominate in roles of political leadership, moral authority, and social privilege.
- **[[Socialisation|Gender Socialisation]]**: The process by which we learn the norms and expectations associated with our sex (see: **Ann Oakley**).
- **The Private/Public Divide**: The idea that women are often confined to the domestic "private" sphere while men dominate the "public" world of work and politics.
- **Intersectionality**: The recognition that a woman's experience is shaped by her race, class, and sexuality as well as her gender.

## Evaluation

| Strengths | Weaknesses |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Focus on Gender**: Highlights inequality that **[[Functionalism]]** and **[[Marxism]]** often overlooked. | **Over-generalisation**: Sometimes assumes all women share the same experiences (**Essentialism**). |
| **Practical Impact**: Has led to significant improvements in law, education, and workplace equality. | **Conflict-focused**: Can be seen as ignoring areas of consensus or progress in gender relations. |

### Q) Compare and contrast two branches of Feminism. (10 marks)

**Model Answer:**
While all Feminists agree that society is **patriarchal**, they differ on the cause and the solution. **[[Liberal Feminism]]** and **[[Radical Feminism]]** provide two very different perspectives.

**Liberal Feminists** are optimistic and believe that equality can be achieved within the current system through **legal reform** (e.g., the Sex Discrimination Act). They focus on **socialisation** and education as ways to change minds. In contrast, **Radical Feminists** argue that patriarchy is so deeply embedded that laws are not enough. They believe men use **sexual politics** and physical power to control women, and therefore call for a total **"root and branch"** restructuring of society, sometimes even suggesting **separatism**.

While Liberals seek **gradual change**, Radicals seek **revolution**. However, both have been criticised for being **ethnocentric** and ignoring how race and class intersect with gender.
