**[[Functionalism]]** is a **consensus theory** that sees society as a complex system whose parts work together to promote solidarity and stability. It is a **macro-structural** theory, meaning it looks at how the structure of society shapes individual behavior.

---

## Core Concepts
- **Organic Analogy**: Functionalists (like **Émile Durkheim** and **Talcott Parsons**) compare society to a human body. Just as organs (heart, liver) work together to keep the body alive, **social institutions** (family, education, religion) work together to keep society functioning.
- **Value Consensus**: Society is held together by a shared agreement on norms and values. Without this, society would collapse into **[[Crime & Deviance|Anomie]]** (lawlessness).
- **Social Solidarity**: A sense of belonging to a wider community and sharing its goals.
- **Functional Prerequisites**: Things a society *must* have to survive (e.g., a way to produce food, a way to teach children).

---

## Role of Institutions
*   **Family**: Performs **[[Socialisation|Primary Socialisation]]**, teaching children the basic rules of society.
*   **Education**: Acts as a **"bridge"** between the family and wider society (**Parsons**). It teaches **universalistic values** and performs **role allocation** based on merit.
*   **Religion**: Provides a "collective conscience" and reinforces social bonds through shared rituals.

---

## Evaluation

| Strengths | Weaknesses |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Holistic View**: Shows how different parts of society are interconnected. | **Rose-tinted**: Ignores conflict, exploitation, and inequality (see: **[[Marxism]]**, **[[Feminism]]**). |
| **Order & Stability**: Explains how society stays together and avoids chaos. | **Deterministic**: Portrays humans as "puppets" who simply follow social rules without choice (**Agency**). |
| **Foundational**: It was the first major sociological theory and set the stage for all others. | **Conservative**: Often justifies the status quo (e.g., "poverty is functional because it motivates people"). |

### Q) Outline and explain the "Organic Analogy" in Functionalist theory. (10 marks)

**Model Answer:**
The **Organic Analogy** is a key concept used by Functionalists like **Émile Durkheim** and **Talcott Parsons** to explain how society works. They compare society to a **biological organism**, such as the human body.

In this analogy, the various **social institutions** (like the family, education system, and the law) are compared to **organs** in the body. Just as the heart, lungs, and brain must work together in harmony to keep the body alive and healthy, social institutions must work together to maintain **social order** and stability. If one institution fails:for example, if the family fails to socialise children:it affects the whole "body" of society, potentially leading to **[[Crime & Deviance|Anomie]]**. This perspective emphasizes the **interdependence** of social parts and the importance of **Value Consensus** in keeping the system functioning.

---

**Revision Tip:** When writing about Functionalism, use words like **"stability,"** **"harmony,"** and **"consensus."** Avoid talking about "conflict" or "power" unless you are evaluating the theory from a different perspective.
