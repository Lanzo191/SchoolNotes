## Overview -
**Secondary [[Socialisation]]** is the learning that takes place after early childhood. It involves acquiring the norms and values of the wider world, beyond the immediate family. It is a lifelong process.

## Main Agencies of Secondary Socialisation -

### 1. Education (School)
*   Teaches children to transition from the **particularistic standards** of the family to the **universalistic standards** of society.
*   Teaches the **[[Hidden Curriculum]]** (hierarchy, punctuality, competition).

### 2. Peer Groups
*   Become more influential during the teenage years.
*   Offer a sense of belonging and identity.
*   Can lead to the formation of **youth subcultures** and the experience of **peer pressure**.

### 3. The Media
*   Shapes our view of the world and our consumer desires.
*   Provides role models and reinforces or challenges stereotypes.
*   Contributes to **global culture** and **digital [[Socialisation]]**.

### 4. The Workplace
*   Teaches the specific codes of conduct and skills required for a career.
*   Involves learning how to behave with colleagues and authority figures.

### 5. Religion
*   Provides moral guidelines and a sense of shared identity through rituals and beliefs.

## Key Difference from Primary Socialisation -
*   **Primary:** Emotionally charged, based on love and personal relationships.
*   **Secondary:** More formal, based on rules, meritocracy, and broader social expectations.
