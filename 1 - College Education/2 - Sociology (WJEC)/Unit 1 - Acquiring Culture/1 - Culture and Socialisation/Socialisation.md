## Overview -
**Socialisation** is the lifelong process by which individuals acquire the norms, values, standards of behaviour, and skills necessary to function within their culture.

## Stages of Socialisation (Talcott Parsons) -
**Talcott Parsons**, a key Functionalist, identified two main stages:
1.  **[[Primary Socialisation]]:** Learning the basics within the family.
2.  **[[Secondary Socialisation]]:** Learning from the wider world (school, work, peers).

## Types of Socialisation -
1.  **Primary:** Ages 0-5 (Family).
2.  **Secondary:** School, peers, media, work.
3.  **Anticipatory:** Preparing for future roles (e.g., medical students learning "bedside manner").
4.  **Resocialisation:** Learning new rules for a new setting (e.g., prison, army, or a new job).
5.  **Digital:** Learning social norms through online interaction.

## Agencies of Socialisation -
These are the groups or organisations that carry out socialisation:
*   **The Family (Primary):** The first and most crucial influence.
*   **Peer Groups:** Influence during adolescence; can create subcultures.
*   **Education:** Teaches the formal and **[[Hidden Curriculum]]**.
*   **The Workplace:** Teaches professional norms and adult roles.
*   **Media:** Increasingly influential in shaping desires and identity.
*   **Religion:** Provides moral frameworks and rituals.
