## Overview -
- Sociologists, particularly **Marxists**, argue that social class is the primary factor determining the formation and nature of youth subcultures.
- Subcultures are seen as a collective solution to the shared problems faced by young people of a specific class.

# Working Class Subcultures -

### Context -
- Often formed as a reaction to economic deprivation, low status, or lack of opportunity.
- **Cohen** argued they are a reaction to "status frustration".

### Examples -
- **Skinheads:** Exaggerated working-class masculinity (boots, braces, shaved heads) to reclaim a sense of identity and community in the face of declining traditional working-class jobs.
- **Teddy Boys:** Formed by working-class youth who adopted Edwardian (upper-class) style to challenge the class system and assert status.
- **Chavs:** A derogatory label often applied to working-class youth, demonised by the media (demonisation of the working class).

# Middle Class Subcultures -

### Context -
- Often formed not out of economic necessity but as a rejection of mainstream values (counter-culture).

### Examples -
- **Hippies:** Rejected the materialism, consumerism, and career-focus of their middle-class parents. They dropped out of the "rat race" to pursue alternative lifestyles.
- **Punks (Bohemian):** While Punk had working-class roots, it also attracted middle-class art school students (e.g., Frith's analysis) who used it to critique society.

## Summary -
- **Marxists** view these subcultures as "symbolic resistance" against the class structure/Capitalism.
- **Functionalists**, however, might see them merely as a "rite of passage" bridging the gap between childhood and adulthood, regardless of class.
