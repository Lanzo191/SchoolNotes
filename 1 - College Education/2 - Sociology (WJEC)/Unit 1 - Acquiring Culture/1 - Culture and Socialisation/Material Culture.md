## Overview -
**Material Culture** refers to the physical objects, resources, and spaces that people use to define their culture. These are the "tangible" elements of a society.

## Key Components -
*   **Artifacts:** Man-made objects such as tools, weapons, and technology.
*   **Clothing & Style:** The way people dress often reflects their cultural values or subcultural affiliations (e.g., a hijab representing religious modesty, or punk leather jackets representing rebellion).
*   **Architecture:** The design of buildings and public spaces (e.g., the difference between a Gothic cathedral and a modern skyscraper).
*   **Food:** The ingredients and cooking methods unique to a society.

## Material vs Non-Material Culture -
While **Material Culture** consists of physical things, **Non-Material Culture** consists of the ideas, beliefs, and values of a society.
*   *Relationship:* The two are deeply linked. For example, a **church** (material) represents the **religious beliefs** (non-material) of a community. A **smartphone** (material) represents the value placed on **connectivity and speed** (non-material) in modern society.

## Cultural Significance -
Material objects often serve as symbols.
*   **Ascribed Status:** Objects can reflect inherited status (e.g., a crown for a monarch).
*   **Achieved Status:** Objects can reflect success (e.g., a luxury car or a university degree certificate).
*   **Cultural Capital:** Knowing how to use or "appreciate" certain material objects (e.g., fine art or classical instruments) can provide individuals with higher status in certain social groups.
