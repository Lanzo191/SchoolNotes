## Starter Quiz -

### Q1) What is Structuralism?
**Model Answer:**
Structuralism is the sociological perspective that human behaviour, culture, and identity are shaped by the overall structure of society (top-down) rather than individual choice.

### Q2) Who created Functionalism?
**Model Answer:**
**Emile Durkheim** is considered the founding father of [[Functionalism]].

### Q3) What are the two main classes in Marxism?
**Model Answer:**
The **Bourgeoisie** (Ruling Class / Owners) and the **Proletariat** (Working Class / Workers).

### Q4) What is the "Organic Analogy"?
**Model Answer:**
The Functionalist idea that society works like a human body. Each institution (Heart, [[Lungs]] / Education, Family) must function correctly for the whole body/society to remain healthy.

### Q5) What was Edward T. Hall's theory of culture?
**Model Answer:**
He proposed the **Iceberg Theory of Culture**. Surface culture (food, flags, clothes) is the visible tip, while deep culture (values, beliefs, body language) is the much larger portion hidden beneath the surface.

### Q6) Define Value Consensus.
**Model Answer:**
A state where the majority of people in a society share the same core values and beliefs, leading to social harmony.

## Key Definitions: Cultural Diversity -

*   **Cultural Diversity:** The existence of multiple different cultures or ethnic groups within a single society.
*   **Inter-cultural:** Differences *between* different cultures (e.g., comparing English culture to Chinese culture).
*   **Intra-cultural:** Differences *within* a single culture (e.g., differences between generations or regions in the UK).
*   **Ethnocentrism:** Judging or evaluating other cultures based on the standards of one's own culture, often viewing one's own as "superior" or "ideal" (e.g., extreme patriotism).
*   **Cultural Relativism:** The opposite of ethnocentrism; the practice of judging a culture by its own standards rather than applying the standards of another culture.
*   **Cultural Appropriation vs Appreciation:**
    *   **Appropriation:** Taking elements from a culture (often a marginalised one) without permission or understanding, typically reinforcing stereotypes (e.g., fashion choices that demean a culture).
    *   **Appreciation:** Respectfully learning about and celebrating another culture with genuine understanding and respect.

## Exam Practice: 5 Mark Question -

**Q: Explain what is meant by "Cultural Diversity" with examples.**
**Model Answer:**
Cultural Diversity refers to the co-existence of a variety of distinct cultures within a specific geographical area or society. This is highly visible in modern "global cities" like London, which feature a wide range of languages, religions, and traditions (e.g., the presence of China Town or Notting Hill Carnival). Modern technology and the internet have furthered cultural diversity by allowing people to consume and appreciate global cultures (e.g., the rise of Asian media and food in the Western world) without the need for physical travel.
