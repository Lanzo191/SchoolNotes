## Background -
*   **Discovered in 1970**, aged **13**, California, USA.
*   Victim of **severe abuse and isolation** since infancy.
*   Kept tied to a potty chair or confined in a cot most of her life.
*   **Almost no language or social exposure**.

## Research Aims -
*   Studied by **Susan Curtiss**, **David Rigler**, and **James Kent**.
*   Aimed to explore:
    *   Whether language can develop after **critical period** (Lenneberg, 1967).
    *   Effects of **privation** on **social and cognitive development**.

## Findings -
*   **On discovery:**
    *   Could not speak, chew solid food, or walk properly.
    *   Showed animal-like behaviour and lacked social skills.
*   **After therapy:**
    *   Learned **some vocabulary** and **basic communication**.
    *   **Never developed full grammar or syntax** → supports **critical period hypothesis**.
    *   Struggled with **social interaction and attachment**.

## Outcome -
*   Initially improved, but progress declined after **custody and funding disputes**.
*   Eventually placed in **state care**, with limited further development.

## Implications -
*   Demonstrates effects of **extreme privation** and importance of **early [[Socialisation]]**.
*   Supports the **critical period hypothesis** : language acquisition must occur before puberty.
*   Highlights **ethical issues** in psychological research.

## Evaluation -
### Strengths
*   Rare insight into **privation and development**.
*   Contributed to **language acquisition research**.

### Weaknesses
*   **Single case study** → not generalisable.
*   **Ethical issues** : potential exploitation, lack of consent.
*   **Observer bias** : researcher influence possible.
*   May have had **pre-existing disabilities**, confounding results.
