## Overview -
Identity is how we see ourselves and how others see us. **Ethnic** and **National** identities are key components of a person's overall social identity, learned through the process of [[Socialisation]].

## National Identity -
This refers to a person's sense of belonging to a specific nation or state. It is often reinforced through "Banal Nationalism" (symbols like flags, anthems, and national holidays).

### Key Concepts:
*   **Civic Nationalism:** A form of nationalism based on shared political values and citizenship within a territory, regardless of ethnicity.
*   **Extreme Nationalism (Ultranationalism):** An extreme form of nationalism that promotes the interest of one state or people above all others, often leading to conflict or exclusion.
*   **Globalisation:** Some sociologists argue that national identity is weakening because of global brands, media, and the internet (creating a "Global Culture").

## Ethnic Identity -
This is based on shared cultural heritage, including language, religion, history, and traditions.

### Key Concepts:
*   **Hybrid Identities:** Especially common among second and third-generation immigrants who may mix their ancestral culture with the culture of the country they live in (e.g., "Brasians" - British Asians).
*   **Resistance:** Sometimes ethnic groups use their identity as a form of resistance against racism or marginalisation in the host country.
*   **The Role of Agencies:**
    *   **Family:** Teaches language, food, and religious values.
    *   **Education:** May reinforce national identity through the curriculum (e.g., history) or marginalise ethnic identities (the "Ethnocentric Curriculum").

## Summary -
Both ethnic and national identities are socially constructed and can change over time. Globalisation is currently challenging traditional ideas of what it means to belong to a specific "nation" or "ethnic group."
