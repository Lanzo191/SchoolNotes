## Overview -
**Primary [[Socialisation]]** is the first and most critical stage of [[Socialisation]]. It occurs during infancy and early childhood (ages 0-5), where a child learns the foundational norms, values, and language of their culture. 

- This stage is essential for a child to develop a sense of self and the ability to function within society.
- Failure in primary [[Socialisation]] can lead to children being "feral" (e.g., the **Genie Wiley** case).

## Agencies of Primary Socialisation -
*   **The Family:** The primary and most influential agency. Parents and caregivers act as the first role models.
*   **Close Caregivers:** Extended family or nannies who provide early care.

## Key Processes -
1.  **Imitation:** Children copy the behaviour of their parents (e.g., using a toy phone).
2.  **Manipulation:** Parents encourage or discourage certain behaviours (e.g., telling a boy not to cry).
3.  **Canalisation:** Directing a child's interest into certain toys or activities based on gender (e.g., giving dolls to girls).
4.  **Verbal Appellations:** Using specific language to define a child (e.g., "Good girl" or "Naughty boy").

## Importance -
*   **Language Acquisition:** Essential for communication.
*   **Basic Norms:** Learning manners, toilet training, and basic hygiene.
*   **Internalisation of Values:** Learning the difference between right and wrong.
