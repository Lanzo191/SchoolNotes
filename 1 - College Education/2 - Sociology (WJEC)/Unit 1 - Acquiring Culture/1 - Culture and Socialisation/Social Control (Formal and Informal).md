**[[Social Control]]** refers to the methods used by society to ensure that its members follow the rules, norms, and laws. It is how society maintains order and discourages **deviance**.

## 1. Formal Social Control
This is carried out by official organizations that have the legal power to enforce rules.
- **Agencies**: The Police, the Courts, the Prison Service, and the Government.
- **Methods**: Fines, imprisonment, community service, and official warnings.
- **Purpose**: To explicitly punish law-breaking and deter others from doing the same.

## 2. Informal Social Control
This is carried out by everyday people and social groups. It is often more powerful than formal control because it happens constantly.
- **Agencies**: Family, peer groups, the media, and the workplace.
- **Methods (Sanctions)**: 
    - **Positive Sanctions**: Praise, a smile, a promotion, or a "like" on social media.
    - **Negative Sanctions**: Glaring, gossip, exclusion from a group, or being "cancelled."
- **Purpose**: To enforce **norms** (unwritten rules) and ensure people "fit in."

---

## The Relationship with Socialisation
Social control is the "enforcement" part of socialisation.
- **Functionalists** see social control as necessary for stability and value consensus.
- **Marxists** see social control (especially the police) as a way to protect the interests of the **Bourgeoisie**.
- **Feminists** see informal social control as a way to keep women within patriarchal boundaries.
