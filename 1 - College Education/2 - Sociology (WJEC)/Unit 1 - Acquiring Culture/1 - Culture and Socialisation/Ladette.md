## Jackson (2006) - "Ladettes" and Education

### Q1) What behaviour is considered "cool" for Ladettes and how does it vary from traditional feminine characteristics?
**Model Answer:**
For "Ladettes," traditional "boy-ish" or "laddish" behaviour was considered cool. This included being loud, assertive, drinking, and being disruptive in school. This directly rejects traditional feminine characteristics like being passive, quiet, and domestic (the "housewife" ideal).

### Q2) How were academic children labelled, and how did Ladettes manage their academic identity?
**Model Answer:**
Academic children were often labelled as "geeks" or "swats." Ladettes experienced high academic pressure but wanted to appear "cool." To manage this, they often adopted a "pro-school" attitude in private but an "anti-school" or "effortless" persona in public, appearing as if they did no work while still aiming for high grades.

### Q3) How does the "fear of failure" impact the behaviour of Ladettes?
**Model Answer:**
Jackson found that the "laddish" behaviour was often a **self-worth protection strategy.** By appearing not to care or not to try, they had an excuse if they failed. If they failed, it was because they didn't try; if they succeeded, it was because they were naturally brilliant.

### Q4) How can the concept of the Ladette be used to show changing gender roles in youth culture?
**Model Answer:**
It shows that traditional gender roles are becoming less restrictive. Girls are now entering into youth subcultures and behaviours that were previously "male-only," demonstrating a shift towards more fluid and less stereotyped gender identities.
