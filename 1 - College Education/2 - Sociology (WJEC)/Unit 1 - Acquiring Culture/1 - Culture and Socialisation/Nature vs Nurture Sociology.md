## Overview -
The **Nature vs Nurture** debate explores the extent to which human behaviour is a result of biological factors (nature) or environmental influences (nurture).

---

## Nurture -
**Definition:** The view that society and culture override human genetics and instincts. Our behaviour is the result of **socialisation**, initially through the family and later through education, media, and social interaction.

### Examples of Nurture Influences:
1.  **Behaviour** – Learned through social interaction.
2.  **Empathy** – Developed through emotional socialisation.
3.  **Selflessness** – Taught via moral guidance.
4.  **Personality / Speech** – Shaped by environment and upbringing.
5.  **Peer Pressure** – Influence of social groups on decision making.
6.  **Gender Role Expectations** – Learned social constructs of masculinity/femininity.
7.  **Obedience to Authority** – Taught through agents of social control.
8.  **Education** – Formal socialisation and knowledge acquisition.
9.  **Media Influence** – Shaping values and desires through consumption.
10. **Social Learning** – Mimicking observed behaviours (e.g., Bandura).
11. **Religion and Morality** – Faith-based value systems.
12. **Parenting Styles** – Affects a child's overall social acceptance.
13. **Politics** – Learned ideological beliefs.
14. **Languages** – The specific language(s) we speak are entirely cultural.
15. **Cultural Celebrations** – Traditions and rituals.
16. **Personality Development** – Heavily taught via socialisation.
17. **Abusive Environments** – Impacts learning and emotional development.
18. **Mimicry** – Subconscious imitation of observed behaviours.
19. **Creativity** – Encouraged or discouraged by social surroundings.
20. **Attachment** – How we form bonds with others.

*Key Case Study:* [[Genie Wiley Case Study]] (shows the effects of a lack of nurture/socialisation).

---

## Nature -
**Definition:** The view that humans are genetically predisposed to certain actions and instincts. This theory suggests that behaviour is entirely based on our innate biological makeup.

### Examples of Nature Influences:
1.  **Psychological Disorders** – e.g., Schizophrenia (genetic component).
2.  **Aggression Levels** – Often linked to hormones (e.g., testosterone).
3.  **Developmental Stages** – e.g., the sequence of crawling then walking.
4.  **Biological Requirements** – Food, water, and **homeostasis**.
5.  **Addiction** – Genetic predisposition to substance abuse.
6.  **Fight or Flight Response** – Innate survival instinct.
7.  **Sexual Orientation** – Biological/innate preference.
8.  **Parental Instincts** – Innate drive to protect offspring.
9.  **IQ** – Heritability of intelligence (though IQ tests are debated).
10. **Learning Ease** – Genetic cognitive capacity.
11. **Physical Traits** – Height, weight, and metabolism.
12. **Self-Protection** – The mind can develop defence mechanisms (e.g., dissociative identity disorder) in response to trauma.

*Key Case Study:* [[Jim Twins Case Study]] (shows similarities despite different upbringings).

---

## Overlap & Interaction -
1.  **Genetic Disposition:** Traits like hand-eye coordination can influence which hobbies a person chooses (social construct).
2.  **Physical Limits:** Height and weight can determine or prevent participation in certain activities or sports.
