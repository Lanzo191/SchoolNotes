#SociologyUnit1 #SociologyDefinitions #[[Socialisation]]

## Definitions - 

- [[A2 Unit 1 Key Terms|Socialisation]] is the process in which children acquire the values, standards of behaviour, attitude and skills that are viewed as appropriate to the culture in which the child resides. Three stages of [[Socialisation]] have been recognised by [[Marxism and Functionalism|Talcott Parsons]] (Man who created the social Action Theory and organic analogy)

- There are 5 total TYPES that but some aren't recognised formally these are; 

  1. [[Primary Socialisation]]
 2. [[Secondary Socialisation]]
 3. [[Anticipatory Socialisation]]
 

 - The ones covered in this will be Primary and Secondary but they will be in different areas as well. 

## [[Primary Socialisation]] - 

- [[Primary Socialisation]] is the main form of [[Socialisation]], it occurs in early childhood and consists of all the main things learnt such as behaviour, speech, manners, identity and norms. (All things taught by parents at young age) 

## [[Secondary Socialisation]] - 

- [[Secondary Socialisation]] is the second form, it occurs later in life (e.g Teens and Adulthood) but its behaviour learnt by institutions such as Schools, Peers, Work. 


## [[Agents Of Socialisation]] - 

- Agents of [[Socialisation]] is the term used to describe the social group / individual / organisation that socialises the person e.g Parents are a primary agent of [[Socialisation]]. some more examples are; 

1. Parents - Primary 
2. Peers - Secondary 
3. Role Models - Mixed 
4. Employers - Secondary 

## [[Methods of Socialisation]] - 

- There are many different methods of [[Socialisation]] but this will be in terms of parents/family - Children will imitate the actions of parents and this will make the child learn repeated actions. Sanctions CAN sometimes work but it depends on the severity, if a child did something considered disrespectful it is better to force the child to think about their actions and come to a conclusion on their own with the parents assistance rather than direct harmful discipline.
