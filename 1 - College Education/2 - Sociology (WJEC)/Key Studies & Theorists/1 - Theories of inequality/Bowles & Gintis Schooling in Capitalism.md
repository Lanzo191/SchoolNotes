## Overview

- Bowles and Gintis are economists known for their 1976 book "Schooling in Capitalist America," which argues that the education system reproduces social inequalities and prepares students for a subservient role in the capitalist workforce through a concept called the "correspondence principle." They suggest that the structure and values taught in schools mirror those found in the workplace, reinforcing existing class hierarchies.

## Critiques

- Critics argue that Bowles and Gintis oversimplify the relationship between education and the labor market.
- Some suggest that their view of students as passive recipients of education does not account for active resistance and agency among students.
