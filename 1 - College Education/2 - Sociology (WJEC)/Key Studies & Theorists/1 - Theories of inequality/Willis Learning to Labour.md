## Overview 

Paul Willis's study, "Learning to [[Labour]]," explores how working-class boys in a British secondary school develop a counter-school culture that leads them to reject mainstream educational values and ultimately accept working-class jobs. His ethnographic research highlights the boys' active choice to fail academically, reflecting their awareness of their socio-economic realities and the limitations of the education system.

It is also a marxist theory like [[Bowles & Gintis Schooling in Capitalism]]

## Criticism 
- **Participant Observation Limitations**: Critics argue that Willis's reliance on participant observation may lead to biased interpretations. The immersive nature of his research could affect objectivity, as he became part of the boys' social group.

- **Sample Size and Diversity**: The study focuses on a small group of twelve working-class boys, which raises questions about the generalizability of the findings. Critics point out that the sample lacks diversity, as it only includes white boys from a specific school.
