## Durkheim (1897) - Suicide

### 1. Theoretical Background
*   **Functionalism:** A perspective that sees society as a complex system whose parts work together to promote solidarity and stability.
*   **Social Fact:** Durkheim’s idea that there are social forces outside the individual (like religion or family) that influence their behavior.
*   **Anomie:** A state of normlessness or lack of social guidance, often occurring during periods of rapid social change.

### 2. Aim
To prove that **suicide**, which is often seen as a purely individual and psychological act, is actually a **social fact** driven by external social forces.

### 3. Methodology
*   **Method:** Comparative method using **official statistics**.
*   **Perspective:** **Functionalist** (Positivist).
*   **Sample:** National suicide statistics from several European countries (e.g. France, Denmark).
*   **Research Focus:** Comparing suicide rates across different religions, marital statuses, and economic conditions.

### 4. Results (Typology of Suicide)
Durkheim identified four types of suicide based on **social integration** and **social regulation**:
1.  **Egoistic Suicide:** Occurs when there is **low integration** (e.g. single people or Protestants, who have less community cohesion than Catholics).
2.  **Altruistic Suicide:** Occurs when there is **excessive integration** (e.g. soldiers or kamikaze pilots who sacrifice themselves for the group).
3.  **Anomic Suicide:** Occurs when there is **low regulation** during times of rapid change (e.g. economic crashes or booms).
4.  **Fatalistic Suicide:** Occurs when there is **excessive regulation** (e.g. prisoners or slaves who feel they have no future).

### 5. Conclusion
*   Suicide rates are remarkably stable within a society, proving that suicide is a **social fact**.
*   The likelihood of suicide depends on how well an individual is integrated into and regulated by society.

### 6. Detailed Evaluation & Improvements

| Feature | Evaluation & Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Research Method** | **Comparative Method**: Used large-scale **official statistics** to identify patterns. However, these statistics may be **socially constructed**. |
| **Perspective** | **Functionalist**: Provided the first scientific, sociological explanation for a seemingly individual behavior. |
| **Sample** | **National Statistics**: Covers entire populations, giving the study high **generalisability** for those countries at that time. |
| **Sampling Method** | **Total Population**: Used records of all reported suicides in the chosen countries. |
| **Data Type** | **Quantitative Data**: Numerical suicide rates allowed for objective, mathematical analysis. |
| **Ethics** | **Good**: Used secondary, anonymized data, causing no harm to individuals. |
| **Weakness** | **Interpretivist Critique**: Arguing that official statistics are unreliable because they depend on a **coroner's interpretation** of the death. |
| **Reliability Improvements** | **Consistency**: Durkheim found similar patterns across multiple different countries, increasing the **reliability** of his findings. |
| **Validity Improvements** | **Control**: By comparing similar groups (e.g. married vs. single), Durkheim could isolate **integration** as the key factor. |

### Q1) Distinguish between "egoistic" and "altruistic" suicide (2 marks)
**Model Answer:** **Egoistic suicide** occurs when an individual has **low integration** and feels detached from society, whereas **altruistic suicide** occurs when an individual is **over-integrated** and sacrifices their life for the benefit of the group.

---

### Q2) Explain why Durkheim used "official statistics" in his research (3 marks)
**Model Answer:** Durkheim used **official statistics** because as a **positivist**, he believed that sociology should be as scientific as possible. Statistics provide **quantitative, objective data** that can be used to identify broad social patterns and "social facts" that are external to the individual.

---

### Q3) Discuss the Interpretivist critique of Durkheim's study (3 marks)
**Model Answer:** **Interpretivists** criticize Durkheim's reliance on statistics, arguing that they are **socially constructed**. They suggest that a death is only recorded as a suicide if a **coroner** interprets it that way, and factors like family pressure or religious stigma may influence the coroner's decision, making the data inaccurate.
