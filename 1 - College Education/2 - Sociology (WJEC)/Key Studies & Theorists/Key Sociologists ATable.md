## 1. Sociological Perspectives & Core Theorists

### Functionalism
*   **Core Principle:** Society is an organic system based on **value consensus**, **social order**, and functional integration.
*   **Key Names:**
    *   **Émile Durkheim:** Society shapes the individual through **social facts**; deviance affirms cultural values; excessive change creates **anomie**.
    *   **Talcott Parsons:** **Organic analogy** (institutions function like biological organs); **GAIL model** (Goal attainment, Adaptation, Integration, Latency); shifts individuals from particularistic to universalistic values.
    *   **Kingsley Davis & Wilbert Moore:** **Role allocation** ensures functionally essential jobs receive highest rewards; stratification is universal and functionally necessary.
    *   **Robert K. Merton:** Introduced **manifest** (intended) and **latent** (unintended) functions; formulated **strain theory** when social structures fail to offer legitimate means.
*   **Key Evaluation:** Deterministic; ignores power distribution, conflict, and exploitation; treats institutions as naturally benign.

### Traditional & Neo-Marxism
*   **Core Principle:** Society is structured by economic exploitation where the ruling class (**Bourgeoisie**) exploits the working class (**Proletariat**).
*   **Key Names:**
    *   **Karl Marx:** Economic base determines ideological superstructure; capitalism generates **alienation** and **false class consciousness**.
    *   **Louis Althusser:** **Ideological State Apparatuses (ISAs)** reproduce capitalism quietly; **Repressive State Apparatuses (RSAs)** enforce it with coercion.
    *   **Antonio Gramsci:** **Hegemony** (cultural leadership); ruling class maintains consent through ideological compromise; workers possess dual consciousness.
    *   **Bowles & Gintis:** School mirrors capitalist employment through the **correspondence principle** and **hidden curriculum**.
    *   **Stuart Hall & CCCS:** Youth subcultures generate symbolic, magical resistance to class oppression; moral panics deflect attention from capitalist crises.
*   **Key Evaluation:** Traditional Marxism is economically reductionist; Neo-Marxists address culture and agency, but still assume economic conflict drives all social action.

### Weberian & Social Action
*   **Core Principle:** Action is guided by subjective meaning and motives; power and inequality are multidimensional.
*   **Key Names:**
    *   **Max Weber:** Stratification derives from three distinct sources: **Class** (market position), **Status** (social prestige), and **Party** (political power). Identified three authority forms: **charismatic**, **traditional**, and **rational-legal**. Advocated **Verstehen** (empathetic understanding).
    *   **Mike Savage:** Built on Weber and Bourdieu in the **Great British Class Survey**, categorizing contemporary society into 7 distinct class strata.
*   **Key Evaluation:** Provides nuanced, realistic class models; criticized by Marxists for underplaying the central unifying power of capital ownership.

### Feminism
*   **Core Principle:** Society is patriarchal, structured around male domination and the systematic oppression of women.
*   **Key Names:**
    *   **Ann Oakley (Liberal):** Socialisation constructs gender roles via **canalisation** and **manipulation**; progress achievable through legal reforms (Equal Pay Act).
    *   **Fran Ansley (Marxist):** Women act as "takers of shit", absorbing domestic stress and male anger produced by capitalist exploitation.
    *   **Sylvia Walby (Intersectional):** Identified **six patriarchal structures** (paid employment, household production, state policies, male violence, sexual relations, cultural institutions).
    *   **Angela McRobbie & Jenny Garber (Feminist Subcultural):** Uncovered **bedroom culture**; challenged male bias in subculture sociology.
    *   **bell hooks & Kimberlé Crenshaw (Black/Intersectional):** Highlighted the matrix of domination; white middle-class feminism ignores race and class intersections.
*   **Key Evaluation:** Exposed pervasive male bias in sociological research; internal disagreements between liberal reformism, radical separatism, and intersectionality.

### Interactionism & Labelling Theory
*   **Core Principle:** Society is constructed bottom-up through face-to-face interactions, symbols, and the application of labels.
*   **Key Names:**
    *   **Howard Becker:** An act is deviant only when others define it as such; labels can become a **master status** leading to a **self-fulfilling prophecy**.
    *   **Edwin Lemert:** Distinguishes **primary deviance** (unlabelled act) from **secondary deviance** (deviance caused by the social reaction to the label).
    *   **Aaron Cicourel:** Justice is negotiated based on police **typifications** and stereotypes of typical offenders.
    *   **Stanley Cohen:** Media creates **folk devils** and manufactures **moral panics**, generating a **deviance amplification spiral**.
*   **Key Evaluation:** Gives agency to actors; explains selective law enforcement and streaming; criticized for ignoring wider structural economic origins of inequality.

### Postmodernism & Late Modernity
*   **Core Principle:** Rigid social structures (class, gender) have collapsed into hyper-individualized, media-saturated consumer lifestyles.
*   **Key Names:**
    *   **Jean-François Lyotard:** Rejection of all **meta-narratives** (Marxism, religion, science); all knowledge is relative and localized.
    *   **Jean Baudrillard:** Society is saturated by **hyperreality** and **simulacra** where images and representations replace physical reality.
    *   **David Gauntlett:** Individuals actively pick and mix media representations to construct fluid, diverse identities.
    *   **Ulrich Beck:** We live in a **risk society** where manufactured hazards (pollution, financial collapse) generate universal insecurity.
    *   **Anthony Giddens:** In late modernity, identity is an ongoing **reflexive project**; relationships are negotiated purely on mutual satisfaction.
*   **Key Evaluation:** Captures modern digital, globalized life; criticized by Marxists and Feminists for ignoring stubborn, objective structural inequalities (e.g. poverty, pay gaps).

## 7. Topic Breakdown: Research Methods & Epistemology

| Sociologist & Date | Tradition | Methodological Stance | Key Study & Empirical Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Émile Durkheim** (1897) | Positivism | Quantitative comparative analysis, **social facts**, discovering causal laws | Study of **Suicide** using national statistical registers across European states; proved private acts are driven by social forces. |
| **Max Weber** (1922) | Interpretivism | Subjective meaning, qualitative depth, empathetic understanding (**Verstehen**) | **The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism**; investigated how Calvinist religious beliefs drove modern economic action. |
| **Howard Becker** (1963) | Interpretivism | Participant observation, open-ended interviewing, uncovering insider viewpoints | Studied jazz musicians and marijuana users; showed deviance is learned and socially constructed through peer interaction. |
| **Aaron Cicourel** (1968) | Interpretivism / Ethnomethodology | Deconstruction of official statistics; data is a product of social negotiation | Observed police officers and juvenile probation officers; showed statistics reflect police biases rather than criminal reality. |
| **J. Maxwell Atkinson** (1978) | Ethnomethodology | Rejection of suicide statistics as objective facts; studied coroner decision-making | Qualitative analysis of coroners' verdicts; showed coroners use common-sense assumptions (suicide notes, mental history) to label deaths. |
| **Paul Willis** (1977) | Mixed Methods (Ethnography) | Methodological triangulation: participant observation, unstructured group interviews, diaries | Studied 12 working-class boys ("the lads") in a Midlands secondary school to capture authentic counter-school attitudes. |
| **Ann Oakley** (1981) | Feminist Methodology | Critique of traditional patriarchal structured interviews; advocated collaborative, non-hierarchical interviews | Interviewed 55 women transitioning to motherhood; shared personal experiences to build mutual trust and qualitative validity. |
| **Peter Townsend** (1979) | Positivism / Mixed | Large-scale surveys, operationalisation of complex concepts via quantitative deprivation score | Nationwide survey of poverty in the UK; operationalized poverty using an objective 12-item deprivation index. |

## 8. Classic Synoptic Pairings & Essay Counter-Arguments

Use these pairing combinations to quickly score top-band evaluation marks in any essay question:

*   **Meritocracy in Education:**
    *   *Claim:* **Parsons** & **Davis & Moore** argue schools are meritocratic institutions allocating roles based on talent.
    *   *Counter:* **Bowles & Gintis** expose meritocracy as a myth designed to legitimize class reproduction; **Bourdieu** demonstrates cultural capital rigs outcomes.
*   **Student Agency & Resistance:**
    *   *Claim:* **Bowles & Gintis** treat working-class pupils as passive puppets conditioned by the hidden curriculum.
    *   *Counter:* **Paul Willis** shows working-class boys are active agents who resist school norms and mock authority.
*   **Causes of Working-Class Crime:**
    *   *Claim:* **Merton** (Strain Theory) & **Albert Cohen** (Status Frustration) argue working-class crime results from blocked opportunity and inverted values.
    *   *Counter:* **Gordon** & **Chambliss** (Marxism) argue capitalism itself is criminogenic and forces survival crime, while **Cicourel** reveals statistics only mirror selective police enforcement.
*   **Crime Prevention & Policing:**
    *   *Claim:* **Wilson & Kelling** (Right Realism) demand zero-tolerance policing and environmental target-hardening.
    *   *Counter:* **Lea & Young** (Left Realism) argue aggressive policing alienates communities; policies must tackle relative deprivation, marginalisation, and institutional bias.
*   **Gender Stratification:**
    *   *Claim:* **Oakley** (Liberal) advocates legal and policy reform (equal pay legislation, non-sexist school curricula).
    *   *Counter:* **Walby** & **Firestone** (Radical) argue patriarchy is deeply entrenched in the state, male violence, and family structures, requiring systemic transformation rather than mere legal adjustments.
*   **Youth Culture Formation:**
    *   *Claim:* **Parsons** & **Eisenstadt** (Functionalism) view youth culture as an orderly transition bridge into adult universalism.
    *   *Counter:* **Stuart Hall** & **Dick Hebdige** (CCCS) prove youth subcultures represent ideological and economic resistance against class marginalisation.
*   **Significance of Social Class Today:**
    *   *Claim:* **Marx** & **Westergaard** argue fundamental class exploitation remains the defining division in capitalist society.
    *   *Counter:* **Pakulski & Waters** / **Baudrillard** (Postmodernism) argue class is dead; consumption choices, lifestyle, and media hyperreality now construct individual identities.
*   **Objectivity of Official Statistics:**
    *   *Claim:* **Durkheim** treats official statistics as objective social facts capable of revealing causal laws.
    *   *Counter:* **Atkinson** & **Cicourel** expose official statistics as subjective social constructions assembled through human interpretation and institutional stereotyping.
