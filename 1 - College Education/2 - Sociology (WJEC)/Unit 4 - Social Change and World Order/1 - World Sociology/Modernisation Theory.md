## The Five Stages of Economic Growth

### Stage Breakdown

|Stage|Description|
|---|---|
|Traditional Society|Characterized by subsistence agriculture and low productivity, with little political authority.|
|Preconditions for Take-off|Development of commercial agriculture and urbanization, leading to increased demand for trade.|
|Take-off|A period of rapid growth driven by industrialization and economic processes overtaking traditions.|
|Drive to Maturity|Diversification of industries, investment in social infrastructure, and expansion of services.|
|Age of High Mass Consumption|A mature economy with widespread consumption and advanced technological development.|

## Key Concepts

- Linear Progression: Rostow's model suggests that all societies will eventually reach modernisation by following these stages.
- Economic Focus: The theory emphasises internal factors driving development, such as investment and societal norms, while downplaying external influences.

- Critiques: While influential, the theory has faced criticism for being Eurocentric and overly deterministic, failing to account for external factors like colonialism and global economic structures.
