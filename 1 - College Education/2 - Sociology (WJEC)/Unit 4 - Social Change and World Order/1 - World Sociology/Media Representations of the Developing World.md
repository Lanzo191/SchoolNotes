## Galtung and Ruge: News Values in Global Reporting
- Johan Galtung and Mari Holmboe Ruge (1965) identified key **news values** that determine whether international events are selected for news coverage:
  - **Proximity**: Geographical and cultural closeness to the domestic audience increases coverage.
  - **Cultural Relevance / Meaningfulness**: Events reflecting familiar cultural frameworks are prioritized.
  - **Elite Nations and Elite Persons**: Actions of powerful Western nations and prominent figures receive dominant airtime.
  - **Negativity**: Disasters, wars, and famines are reported over gradual, positive developmental progress.
  - **Simplicity / Threshold**: Complex ongoing geopolitical issues are simplified into sensational, binary narratives.

## Global Crisis Case Studies
- **Sudan Civil War (2019-Present)**:
  - Conflict between the Sudanese Armed Forces (SAF) and Rapid Support Forces (RSF) caused over 150,000 casualties, 4 million refugees, and 8.8 million internally displaced persons (IDPs).
  - Severely marginalized in Western media coverage due to geographic distance, absence of Western elite figures, and institutional neglect.
- **Nepal Flooding**:
  - Severe monsoon flooding in a landlocked nation sandwiched between India and China resulted in over 1,000 deaths.
  - Media reporting highlighted disparities in international aid allocations and transactional foreign diplomacy.

## Media Agendas and Framing
- **Agenda Setting**: News editors determine which global stories reach public consciousness, selecting issues based on political convenience and commercial viability.
- **Framing and Bias**: The perspective through which events are portrayed; refugees from the developing world are frequently framed as economic burdens or security threats rather than victims of conflict.
- **Depoliticisation of Conflict**: Media outlets frequently present African or Asian conflicts as primitive tribal rivalries or natural catastrophes, obscuring colonial legacies and international arms sales.

## Hierarchy of Suffering and Compassion Fatigue
- **Hierarchy of Suffering**: Sociological concept describing how the media values human lives unequally; casualties in Western nations receive extensive empathetic coverage, whereas casualties in the developing world are reduced to statistics.
- **Pornography of Poverty**: The practice of broadcasting graphic, degrading imagery of suffering and starvation to elicit emotional donations, stripping subjects of dignity and agency.
- **Compassion Fatigue (Shock and Yawn)**: Continuous saturation of sensational crisis reporting causes audiences to become desensitised, indifferent, and fatalistic about global poverty.

## Key Global Stratification Terminology

| Acronym | Full Form | Sociological Definition |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **LEDW** | Less Economically Developed World | Global regions characterized by lower industrialisation, lower GDP per capita, and limited infrastructure. |
| **MEDW** | More Economically Developed World | Industrialised global regions with high per-capita income, advanced healthcare, and robust infrastructure. |
| **LEDC** | Less Economically Developed Country | An individual nation experiencing severe economic constraints, poverty, and debt burdens. |
| **MEDC** | More Economically Developed Country | A sovereign state with an advanced technological economy and high Human Development Index (HDI). |
| **IDP** | Internally Displaced Person | An individual forced to flee their home due to conflict or disaster who remains within national borders. |
