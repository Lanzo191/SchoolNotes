Marxists argue that crime is caused by the structure of **Capitalism**. Laws are made by the ruling class (Bourgeoisie) to control the working class (Proletariat).

## 1. Criminogenic Capitalism (Gordon)
Capitalism is **"Criminogenic"** - by its very nature, it causes crime.
*   **Poverty:** The working class may commit crime just to survive or obtain consumer goods encouraged by advertising.
*   **Alienation:** Frustration and lack of control over work can lead to non-utilitarian crimes (violence/vandalism).
*   **Greed:** Capitalism encourages "dog eat dog" competition. Even the rich commit crime (tax evasion/fraud) because profit is the only goal.
*   **Gordon:** Argues that crime is a rational response to the capitalist system.

## 2. The State and Law Making (Chambliss)
*   Laws are made to protect the **private property** of the rich.
*   **Chambliss:** Investigated laws in Britain/East Africa and found they were primarily designed to force people to work for the colonial power and protect the owners' wealth.
*   "The law is basically the reflection of the ruling class ideology."

## 3. Selective Law Enforcement (Reiman)
*   The Criminal Justice System applies the law selectively.
*   **Working Class Crime:** Heavily policed and punished (burglary, assault).
*   **White Collar / Corporate Crime:** Often ignored or treated leniently (fines rather than prison).
*   **Ideological Function:** By focusing on "street crime", it creates the illusion that crime is a working-class problem, diverting attention from the exploitation by the rich.

## Neo-Marxism: The New Criminology (Taylor, Walton & Young)
*   Agree with Marxists that capitalism causes crime.
*   **Disagree on Determinism:** They argue criminals are not just passive victims of capitalism. Crime is a **conscious political choice** (voluntarism).
*   Example: The "Robin Hood" criminal who steals from the rich to redistribute wealth or protest injustice.

## Evaluation of Marxist Views
| Strengths | Weaknesses |
| :--- | :--- |
| Explains the link between poverty and crime. | Ignores the victims of crime (who are mostly working-class themselves). |
| Highlights how "White Collar Crime" is often ignored. | Too deterministic (Suggests all poor people will commit crime, which is false). |
| Shows how laws are not neutral. | Communism (non-capitalist states) still had crime. |
