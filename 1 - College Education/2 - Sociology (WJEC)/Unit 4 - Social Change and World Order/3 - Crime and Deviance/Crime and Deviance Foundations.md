## Core Definitions
*   **Crime:** Behaviour that breaks the formal laws of a society and is subject to legal punishment.
*   **Deviance:** Behaviour that breaks the informal norms and values of a society (e.g., weird clothing, unusual habits). 
    *   *Note:* Not all deviance is criminal (e.g., having 50 cats), and not all crime is seen as deviant (e.g., speeding).
*   **Social Construction:** The idea that crime and deviance vary over time and between cultures (e.g., homosexuality was once a crime in the UK but is now legal).

## Theoretical Perspectives on Crime

### 1. Functionalism
*   **View:** Crime is inevitable and even "functional" for society in small amounts.
*   **Functions:** 
    *   **Boundary Maintenance:** Punishing criminals reminds everyone else of the rules.
    *   **Social Change:** Deviance allows for new ideas to emerge (e.g., the Suffragettes).
*   **Anomie:** Too much crime leads to a state of normlessness.

### 2. Marxism
*   **View:** Crime is a natural result of an unequal capitalist system (**Criminogenic Capitalism**).
*   **Selective Law Enforcement:** The police focus on working-class "street crime" while ignoring "suite crime" (corporate tax evasion) committed by the rich.

### 3. Interactionism
*   **View:** No act is inherently criminal. It only becomes deviant when others **label** it as such.
*   **Focus:** How the police and courts target specific groups based on stereotypes.

---

## Evaluation of Perspectives

| Perspective | Strength | Weakness |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Functionalism** | Explains why every society has crime. | Ignores the victims of crime. |
| **Marxism** | Highlights inequality in the legal system. | Over-emphasises class; ignores gender/race. |
| **Interactionism** | Shows how definitions of crime change. | Ignores the root causes of why people commit crime. |

### Q) Explain the difference between "Crime" and "Deviance". (10 marks)

**Model Answer:**
While the terms are often used together, sociologists distinguish between **crime** and **deviance** based on the type of rule being broken. **Crime** refers to behaviour that violates the formal, written laws of a society, such as theft or assault. These acts are subject to official sanctions from the legal system, such as fines or imprisonment.

In contrast, **deviance** is a much broader concept that refers to behaviour that breaks informal social norms and values. This could include anything from unusual fashion choices to breaking social etiquette. Deviance is met with informal sanctions, such as social shaming or being stared at. Crucially, deviance is **socially constructed**: what is considered deviant in one culture (e.g., drinking alcohol) may be perfectly normal in another. Sociologists argue that while most crimes are deviant, many deviant acts are not criminal, and some crimes (like minor white-collar offences) are not always seen as deviant by the public.
