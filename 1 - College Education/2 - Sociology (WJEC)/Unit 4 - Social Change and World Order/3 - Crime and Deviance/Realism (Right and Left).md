Realism emerged in the 1980s as a reaction against "theoretical" sociology (like **[[Marxism|Marxism]]** and **[[Interactionism|Interactionism]]**). Realists believe crime is a **real problem** that affects people's lives and needs practical, immediate solutions.

---

## 1. Right Realism (Conservative/New Right View)
*   **Focus:** Personal responsibility and strict law enforcement.
*   **Causes of Crime:**
    1.  **Rational Choice Theory:** Criminals weigh the pros and cons. If the "risk" of getting caught is low and the "reward" is high, they will commit crime (**Cornish and Clarke**).
    2.  **Biological Differences:** Some people are naturally more aggressive or have lower intelligence (**Wilson and Herrnstein**).
    3.  **The Underclass:** Crime is caused by a breakdown in family values and the growth of an "underclass" dependent on welfare (**Charles Murray**).
*   **Solutions:**
    *   **Zero Tolerance:** Policing minor crimes strictly to prevent major ones (**Broken Windows Theory**).
    *   **Target Hardening:** Making it physically harder to commit crime (e.g., CCTV, alarms, gated communities).

### Evaluation of Right Realism
*   **Strength:** Provides practical solutions that have been adopted by many governments (e.g., CCTV and "Stop and Search").
*   **Weakness:** Ignores the structural causes of crime like poverty and inequality. It also leads to **[[Interactionism|Labelling]]** and potential police harassment of minority groups.

---

## 2. Left Realism (Social Reform View)
*   **Focus:** Social inequality and community-based solutions.
*   **Causes of Crime (Lea and Young):**
    1.  **Relative Deprivation:** Feeling poor compared to others (amplified by media and advertising).
    2.  **Marginalisation:** Groups (like unemployed youth) who have no political voice or power and feel frustrated.
    3.  **Subculture:** People form groups to cope with deprivation and marginalisation, which may turn to crime as a way to "get ahead."
*   **Solutions:**
    *   **Tackling Poverty:** Addressing the root causes of inequality and improving life chances.
    *   **Democratic Policing:** Police should work *with* the community to gain trust, rather than just "policing" them.

### Evaluation of Left Realism
*   **Strength:** Unlike Marxism, it takes the **victims** of crime seriously, especially those in working-class areas.
*   **Weakness:** It focuses too much on street crime and ignores **"Suite Crime"** (corporate and white-collar crime) committed by the powerful.

### Q) Compare Right and Left Realist views on the causes of crime. (10 marks)

**Model Answer:**
Right and Left Realists both agree that crime is a significant social problem, but they differ fundamentally on its causes.

**Right Realists** focus on individual choice and social breakdown. They argue that crime is a **rational choice** where criminals weigh the rewards against the risk of being caught. Furthermore, theorists like **Charles Murray** blame the rise of an "underclass" and poor **[[Socialisation|socialisation]]** in lone-parent families for creating a lack of moral values. In contrast, **Left Realists** focus on structural inequality. **Lea and Young** argue that crime is caused by **relative deprivation**-the frustration of feeling poor in a consumerist society. They also point to **marginalisation**, where groups with no political power feel excluded and turn to subcultures and crime as a response. While Right Realists blame the **individual**, Left Realists blame **society**.

**Revision Tip:** Remember that **"Realism"** means they want solutions *now*. They are not interested in waiting for a revolution or changing the entire system.
