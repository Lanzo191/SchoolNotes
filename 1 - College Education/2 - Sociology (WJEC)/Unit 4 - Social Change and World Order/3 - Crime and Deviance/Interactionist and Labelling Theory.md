#CrimeAndDeviance #Education #[[Interactionism]] #Sociology #WJEC

Interactionists focus on how society **labels** certain acts as criminal. They argue no act is inherently criminal; it only becomes so when society labels it.

## Howard Becker: Labelling Theory
*   **"Deviancy is not a quality of the act the person commits, but rather a consequence of the application by others of rules and sanctions."**
*   **Master Status:** Once a person is labelled "criminal" or "deviant", this becomes their controlling identity. People see them only as a "thief" or "junkie", ignoring other traits (father, worker, etc.).
*   **Self-Fulfilling Prophecy:** The labelled individual accepts the label, internalises it, and commits more crime because legitimate opportunities are blocked.

## Edwin Lemert: Primary vs Secondary Deviance
1.  **Primary Deviance:** Minor acts of rule-breaking that are not publicly labelled (e.g., speeding, underage drinking). The person does not see themselves as a criminal.
2.  **Secondary Deviance:** Occurs **after** the person has been caught and publicly labelled (stigmatised). The societal reaction (shame, exclusion) provokes further deviance.

## Jock Young: The Drug Takers (Case Study)
*   Studied "hippie" marijuana users in Notting Hill.
*   **Initially:** Drug use was a minor, peripheral part of their lives (Primary Deviance).
*   **Police Reaction:** Police targeted the group aggressively.
*   **Result:** The group retreated into a closed subculture, wearing distinct clothes and using more drugs as a form of defence/identity. The police reaction **amplified** the deviance.

## Cicourel: The Negotiation of Justice
*   Justice is not fixed; it is negotiable.
*   **Typifications:** Police have stereotypes of what a "typical delinquent" looks like (working class, ethnic minority, poor attitude).
*   **Middle Class Bias:** If a middle-class youth is caught, their parents can negotiate (apologise, promise it won't happen again), and they are often "counselled and released" rather than charged. Working-class youth fit the stereotype and get arrested.

## Evaluation
| Strengths | Weaknesses |
| :--- | :--- |
| Shows that the law is enforced discriminately (racism/classism in police). | Fails to explain *why* the person committed the first act (Primary Deviance). |
| Explains why prisons often increase re-offending (labelling/SFP). | Implies criminals are victims of labelling, ignoring the real harm they do to victims. |
