## Durkheim: Crime is Functional
Emile Durkheim (Functionalist) argued that crime is **inevitable** and **universal**. A certain amount of crime is healthy for society.

### Three Positive Functions of Crime:
1.  **Boundary Maintenance:** When a crime is punished, it reinforces the shared values (collective conscience) of society. It reminds everyone of what is right and wrong (e.g., public reaction to a shocking murder).
2.  **Social Change:** Deviance allows new ideas to challenge existing norms. If no one ever broke the law/norms, society would stagnate. (e.g., Suffragettes or Civil Rights protesters breaking laws to create progress).
3.  **Safety Valve:** Minor deviance can release tension and prevent serious damage to the family or society (e.g., Davis argues prostitution acts as a safety valve for men's sexual frustration, protecting the nuclear family).

### Negative Aspects:
*   **Anomie:** If there is too much crime, society suffers from 'anomie' (normlessness), where rules break down and chaos ensues.

## Robert Merton: Strain Theory
Merton argued that crime occurs when there is a strain between the **Goals** of society (e.g., the "American Dream" / Money) and the legitimate **Means** to achieve them (e.g., Education, Hard Work).

### The 5 Responses to Strain:
1.  **Conformity:** Accept goals + Accept means. (Most people).
2.  **Innovation:** Accept goals + Reject means. (Criminals - e.g., Drug dealers who want money but can't get it legally).
3.  **Ritualism:** Reject goals + Accept means. (Bureaucrats - gave up on being rich but strictly follow rules).
4.  **Retreatism:** Reject goals + Reject means. (Dropouts, addicts).
5.  **Rebellion:** New goals + New means. (Revolutionaries, terrorists).

## Evaluation of Functionalist Views
| Strengths | Weaknesses |
| :--- | :--- |
| Shows how deviance can be positive/necessary (unlike other theories that just see it as bad). | Durkheim doesn't explain *why* specific people commit crimes. |
| Merton explains why working-class crime rates might be higher (more blocked opportunities). | Ignores crimes of the wealthy (White Collar Crime) - assumes laws act for everyone's benefit. |
| | Assumes everyone shares the same goals (money/success), which isn't true. |
