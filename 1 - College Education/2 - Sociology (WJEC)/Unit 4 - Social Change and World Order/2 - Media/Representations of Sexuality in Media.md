## Heteronormativity
The media is **Heteronormative** - it assumes heterosexuality is the "norm" and superior.
*   **Batchelor (2004):** Found that being gay was often not represented at all, or when it was, it was treated as a "problem" or source of anxiety/bullying (especially in teen dramas).

## Stereotypes of Homosexuality (Dyer / Craig)
1.  **The Camp / Effeminate:** Gay men portrayed as flamboyant, fashion-obsessed, and comic relief (e.g., *Alan Carr*). This makes them "safe" and non-threatening to straight audiences.
2.  **The Macho / Butch:** Less common, but exaggerating traditional masculinity (e.g., The Village People).
3.  **The Deviant:** Historically linked to guilt, HIV/AIDS, or moral corruption.
4.  **The Dyke:** Stereotype of lesbians as angry, man-hating, or lacking femininity.

## "Sanitisation" of Gay Sexuality
*   **Gill:** Argues that to be accepted in mainstream media, gay characters must be "sanitised". They are rarely shown being intimate or sexual in the way straight couples are. They appear as the "gay best friend" - stylish, witty, but asexual.

## The Pink Pound
*   **Pluralists** argue that representation has improved because the LGBTQ+ community is a powerful consumer group (**The Pink Pound**). Media companies want their money, so they produce content that appeals to them (e.g., *RuPaul's Drag Race*, *Queer Eye*).

## Symbolic Annihilation
*   Lesbians and Bisexuals are often more invisible than gay men.
*   When lesbians are shown, it is often sexualised for the male gaze (fetishisation) rather than a realistic portrayal of relationships.
