## Overview
The **Neo-Marxist** (or **Hegemonic**) view argues that the media spreads **Dominant Ideology**, but not through direct instructions from owners. Instead, it happens because journalists and editors share the same world-view as the ruling class. This creates a state of **Hegemony**-where the ruling class rules through consent and ideas rather than force.

---

## Key Ideas

### 1. The GUMG (Glasgow University Media Group)
*   **Background:** The GUMG found that the majority of journalists are from privileged, white, middle-class backgrounds and have been socialised into ruling-class values.
*   **Self-Censorship:** Journalists don't need to be told what to write; they voluntarily spread ruling-class ideology because they genuinely believe it is "common sense."
*   **Agenda Setting:** The GUMG argue the media decides which topics are "important" (e.g., corporate tax cuts) and which are "ignored" (e.g., corporate tax evasion).

### 2. Relative Autonomy
*   Unlike traditional **[[Marxism|Marxism]]**, Neo-Marxists believe the media has **Relative Autonomy**. This means it can occasionally criticise the government or the rich.
*   **Function:** This makes the media look objective and fair, which actually makes its ruling-class bias more powerful because the audience trusts it more.

### Q) Outline and explain the Neo-Marxist view of the media. (10 marks)

**Model Answer:**
Neo-Marxists argue that the media maintains ruling-class power through **Hegemony**-the use of ideas to win the consent of the working class. Unlike traditional Marxists, they believe that **journalists and editors** are the ones who control the content, rather than the owners directly.

This is supported by the **Glasgow University Media Group (GUMG)**, who found that most journalists share the same middle-class background as the ruling class. Because of this, they engage in **Agenda Setting**, naturally filtering out ideas that challenge the capitalist system. Furthermore, the media has **Relative Autonomy**, meaning it can sometimes be critical of the powerful. Neo-Marxists argue this is a clever tactic that makes the media seem neutral, making its pro-capitalist message even more convincing to the audience and preventing **Revolution**.

---

**Revision Tip:** When comparing the two Marxist views, remember: **Traditional** = Owners (Top-down); **Neo** = Journalists (Middle-out).
