## Overview
The **[[Marxism|Marxist]]** (or **Instrumentalist**) view of the media argues that the media is a tool used directly by the **Bourgeoisie** (the ruling class) to control the population and maintain their wealth and power.

## Key Ideas

### 1. Direct Control (The Instrumentalist Approach)
*   **Ralph Miliband (1969):** Miliband argued that the media is an **"instrument"** of the ruling class. Owners (like Rupert Murdoch) use their newspapers and TV channels to spread **Dominant Ideology**.
*   **Gatekeeping:** Owners ensure that only stories which support capitalism and the "status quo" are published. Journalists who challenge this risk losing their jobs.

### 2. False Class Consciousness
*   The media distracts the **Proletariat** from their exploitation by focusing on "bread and circuses"-celebrity gossip, sport, and consumerism.
*   This prevents workers from developing **Class Consciousness**, keeping them divided and unaware of the true source of their problems.

### 3. Ideological State Apparatus (ISA)
*   **Louis Althusser:** Defined the media as an ISA. Unlike the police (who use force), the media uses **ideas** to persuade people that capitalism is fair and natural.

## Key WJEC Study: Glasgow University Media Group (GUMG)
The **GUMG** conducted a famous study called **"Bad News" (1976)**. They looked at how TV news reported on industrial strikes.
*   **Findings:** They found that the media consistently portrayed strikers as "troublemakers" and managers as "reasonable."
*   **Language:** Newsreaders used words like "demands" for workers and "offers" for employers.
*   **Visuals:** Strikers were often filmed in noisy, chaotic picket lines, while managers were interviewed in quiet, professional offices.
*   **Conclusion:** This **agenda setting** ensures that the public sides with the ruling class and views worker resistance as a threat to social order.

### Q) Discuss the Marxist view that media owners control media content. (15 marks)

**Model Answer:**
Marxists, such as **Ralph Miliband**, argue that media owners exert direct, instrumental control over media content to protect their own economic interests. They believe that because owners are part of the **Bourgeoisie**, they use their platforms to spread **Dominant Ideology** and maintain a **False Class Consciousness** among the workers.

This is supported by the **Glasgow University Media Group (GUMG)**, whose research into news reporting found that media content is often biased against those who challenge the status quo (like strikers). Through **agenda setting** and the use of biased language, the media makes ruling-class values seem like "common sense."

However, **[[Pluralist View on Media|Pluralists]]** argue that this view is too simplistic. They suggest that in a competitive market, audiences are the ones in control. Owners must provide the content that people want to buy, otherwise they will go out of business. Furthermore, the rise of **social media** has made it much harder for a small number of owners to control the flow of information, as anyone can now produce and share news independently.
