## Overview
**[[Feminism|Feminist]]** perspectives on the **Media** argue that media representations of gender reinforce **[[Patriarchy]]** and maintain the subordinate position of women in society. They focus on how women are represented (or under-represented) and how this affects their status and opportunities.

---

## Types of Feminist Views on Media

### 1. Liberal Feminism
*   **View:** Believe that media representations are slowly improving but still lag behind reality.
*   **Symbolic Annihilation (Gaye Tuchman, 1978):** Tuchman argued that women's interests are often ignored, trivialised, or condemned by the media. She called this **"symbolic annihilation"** if women are not seen in the media, they effectively do not exist in the public eye.
*   **Gatekeeping:** They argue that because media "gatekeepers" (editors, owners) are mostly men, women's issues are seen as less important.
*   **Solution:** Focus on **legal reform** and encouraging more women into senior media roles to break the "glass ceiling."

### 2. Radical Feminism
*   **View:** Argue that the media is a primary tool used by men to maintain **Patriarchy**.
*   **Focus:** They focus on the **objectification** of women, particularly in advertising and pornography.Q
*   **The Beauty Myth (Naomi Wolf):** Wolf argues that the media creates unrealistic physical standards to keep women preoccupied with their appearance, diverting their energy away from social and political activism.
*   **Male Gaze (Laura Mulvey):** The idea that the camera "eyes" women from a masculine, heterosexual point of view, presenting them as objects for male pleasure rather than active subjects.

### 3. Marxist Feminism
*   **View:** Argue that media representations of women serve the interests of **Capitalism**.
*   **Focus:** The media portrays women primarily as **consumers** to drive profit for corporations.
*   **The False Needs:** By advertising domestic products and beauty treatments, the media creates "false needs" that women feel they must satisfy, which directly benefits the capitalist economy.
*   **Reproduction of Labour:** Media images of the "perfect housewife" reinforce the idea that women should provide free domestic labour, which supports the current economic system.

## Key WJEC Study: The Glasgow University Media Group (GUMG)
While often associated with **[[Marxism]]**, the **GUMG** has also looked at how gender is used in news reporting. They found that women are often used in "soft" news stories (human interest, celebrity) while men dominate "hard" news (politics, economics, war).

### Q) Outline and explain the Feminist view that the media is "patriarchal". (10 marks)

**Model Answer:**
Feminists argue that the media is **patriarchal** because it is owned and controlled primarily by men, leading to representations that serve male interests. They believe the media acts as an agency of **[[Socialisation]]** that teaches both men and women that male dominance is natural.

One way this is achieved is through the **"Male Gaze,"** a concept developed by **Laura Mulvey**. This suggests that media content is designed to be viewed from a male perspective, reducing women to sexual objects. Furthermore, **Gaye Tuchman** identified **"Symbolic Annihilation,"** where women are either missing from the media or portrayed in narrow, stereotyped roles. **Radical Feminists** also argue that the media promotes the **"Beauty Myth,"** creating a preoccupation with appearance that keeps women subordinate. By constantly portraying women in limited roles-such as the domestic housewife or the sexualised siren-the media reinforces **Patriarchy** and limits women's aspirations.

---

**Revision Tip:** When discussing the media, always distinguish between **representation** (what we see on screen) and **ownership** (who is behind the camera). Feminists argue both are controlled by men.
