Media representations of social class are often stereotypical and reinforce class divides.

## 1. Representation of the Monarchy & Upper Class
The upper class and monarchy are generally presented in a **positive** and **sanitised** way.

*   **Nairn (1988):** Argues the media treats the Royal Family as a **"Soap Opera"** (glamour, weddings, drama) rather than focusing on their wealth or power. This creates a sense of **national identity** and distracts from inequality.
*   **"Neo-Liberal" Celebration:** The rich are often celebrated as "entrepreneurs" or "job creators" (e.g., *The Apprentice*, *Dragon's Den*). Wealth is presented as the result of hard work (Meritocracy), hiding the reality of inherited privilege.

## 2. Representation of the Middle Class
The middle class is **over-represented** in the media.

*   **Normality:** Middle-class families are presented as the "norm" or "ideal" (e.g., in sitcoms or advertisements).
*   **Values:** Their values (home ownership, nuclear family, consumerism) are presented as universal values.
*   **News Anchors:** News presenters and experts are almost predominantly middle-class, reinforcing their status as the voice of authority.

## 3. Representation of the Working Class
The working class is often **under-represented** or portrayed through negative stereotypes.

*   **Newman (2006):** Few dramas focus on the everyday lives of the working class. When they do, the focus is often on poverty or crime.
*   **Curran and Seaton:** Found that newspapers assume working-class readers are uninterested in serious political analysis, feeding them celebrity gossip and human interest stories instead.

### Key Stereotypes of the Working Class (Jones):
1.  **The "Chav":** A demonised caricature of the white working class (e.g., Vicky Pollard in *Little Britain*). Portrayed as aggressive, uneducated, and obsessed with "bling".
2.  **Benefit Scroungers:** Reality TV shows like *Benefits Street* portray the poor as lazy, undeserving, and a burden on the taxpayer ("Poverty Porn"). This justifies welfare cuts.
3.  **Dumb Buffoons:** Working-class fathers in sitcoms are often shown as well-meaning but stupid (e.g., Homer Simpson, Fred Flintstone).

## Theoretical Perspectives

### Marxist View
*   Representations serve the **Ideological State Apparatus** (Althusser).
*   Demonising the working class ("Chavs") divides the proletariat and prevents them from uniting.
*   Celebrating the upper class promotes **False Class Consciousness**.

### Pluralist View
*   Media representations simply reflect what the audience wants.
*   If "Poverty Porn" is popular, it is because the public finds it entertaining, not because of a capitalist conspiracy.
