## Overview
Media representations of masculinity have evolved significantly over time. While traditional **Hegemonic Masculinity** remains dominant, new forms of identity such as the **"New Man"** and the **"Metrosexual"** have emerged, reflecting changes in wider society and the economy.

---

## Key Representations of Men

### 1. Reinforced Hegemonic Masculinity
*   **Definition:** **R.W. Connell** defines hegemonic masculinity as the traditional, dominant form of being a man.
*   **Characteristics:** Media often portrays men as strong, aggressive, emotionally distant, and dominant (the "breadwinner" or "hero").
*   **Media Examples:** Action movie stars (e.g., James Bond, superheroes), professional athletes, and "tough guy" detectives.
*   **Purpose:** From a **[[Marxism|Marxist]]** or **[[Feminism|Feminist]]** view, this reinforces patriarchal power and social order.

### 2. The "New Man"
*   **Definition:** Emerged in the 1980s and 90s as a response to feminism.
*   **Characteristics:** This representation shows men who are **emotionally sensitive**, caring, and willing to share domestic chores and childcare. They are comfortable showing vulnerability.
*   **Media Examples:** Men in "family-friendly" sitcoms or advertisements for baby products.

### 3. The "Metrosexual"
*   **Definition:** A term coined by **Mark Simpson (1994)**.
*   **Characteristics:** Straight men who are highly concerned with their **appearance**, grooming, and fashion. This representation breaks the "taboo" of men being interested in beauty products.
*   **Media Examples:** David Beckham is often cited as the ultimate metrosexual icon.

### 4. The "New Lad"
*   **Definition:** A reaction against the "New Man."
*   **Characteristics:** Represents a return to traditional "laddish" behaviour (football, beer, sexual objectification of women) but often done with a sense of irony or "banter."
*   **Media Examples:** Men's magazines like *Loaded* or *FHM* (popular in the 90s/00s).

---

## Changes in Representation
*   **Diversity:** There is now a wider range of masculinities on screen, including more positive representations of gay, trans, and ethnic minority men.
*   **The "Crisis of Masculinity":** Some media portrays men as being "lost" or "confused" due to the decline of traditional manual industries (**McNamara**).
*   **Objectification:** Men's bodies are increasingly being objectified in the media (e.g., "six-packs" in advertisements), a phenomenon sometimes called **"The Apollo Myth."**

---

## Evaluation

| Strength of Change | Weakness/Persistence of Tradition |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Greater Choice**: Men are no longer restricted to just "the provider" role. | **Persistence of Hegemony**: Action films and sports still overwhelmingly celebrate traditional toughness. |
| **Breaks Stereotypes**: Metrosexuality allows men to care about health and hygiene without being mocked. | **Commodification**: **[[Postmodernism|Postmodernists]]** argue these "new" identities are just ways for companies to sell more products to men. |
| **Social Progress**: Reflects the influence of **[[Feminism]]** and changing laws on gender equality. | **The "New Lad" Backlash**: Shows that traditional sexism can easily re-emerge under the guise of "irony." |

### Q) Discuss the view that media representations of men have changed. (15 marks)

**Model Answer:**
Sociologists agree that representations of men in the media have shifted from a narrow focus on **Hegemonic Masculinity** to a more diverse range of identities. Traditionally, men were portrayed as "stiff upper lip" providers who used strength and aggression to solve problems.

However, since the 1980s, we have seen the rise of the **"New Man,"** who is portrayed as emotionally expressive and actively involved in family life. This was followed by the **"Metrosexual,"** identified by **Mark Simpson**, where men are encouraged to invest in their appearance and grooming. This suggests a move away from traditional stereotypes towards a more **[[Postmodernism|Postmodern]]** fluidity of identity.

Nevertheless, critics argue that these changes are superficial. **[[Feminism|Feminists]]** point out that the **"New Lad"** representation shows that traditional patriarchal attitudes still persist, often hidden behind "irony." Furthermore, the vast majority of high-budget media still reinforces hegemonic traits in its heroes and athletes. Therefore, while there is more **choice**, the traditional "tough guy" remains the most powerful and influential representation of masculinity.
