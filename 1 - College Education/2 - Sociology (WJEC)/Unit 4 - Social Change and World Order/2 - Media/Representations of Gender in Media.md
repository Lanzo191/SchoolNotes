## Theoretical Overview
*   **Symbolic Annihilation (Tuchman):** Women are "annihilated" (wiped out) from the media by being under-represented or trivialised. If they aren't seen, they aren't important.
*   **The   Male Gaze (Mulvey):** The camera "eyes" up female characters, filming them from the perspective of a heterosexual male. Women are presented as passive objects of desire.
*   **The Beauty Myth (Wolf):** The media presents an unattainable standard of beauty (thin, young, flawless) to control women and keep them insecure/consumerist.

## Common Stereotypes of Women (Ferguson)
Ferguson analysed women's magazines and found they prepared women for a "Cult of Femininity" centred on:

1.  **The WAG / Sex Object:** Valued only for appearance and relationship to a powerful man.
2.  **The Domestic Goddess:** Happy housewife, obsessed with cleaning and cooking.
3.  **The Victim:** Often found in crime dramas/news (e.g., "Missing White Woman Syndrome").

---

## Representations of Men (The "Mask of Masculinity")
*   **Hegemonic Masculinity:** The dominant ideal. Men are rational, strong, aggressive, and lack emotion.
*   **The "New Man":** Emerged in the 80s/90s. More emotional, involved in childcare, concerned with appearance (Metrosexual).
*   **Retributive Masculinity:** A backlash against feminism. Men engaging in "lads' culture" (drinking, football, objectifying women) to reclaim dominance (e.g., *Nuts* or *Zoo* magazines).

---

## Evaluation: Are things changing?
*   **Postmodernism:** Identities are now fluid. Women can be "Action Heroes" (e.g., *Wonder Woman*, *Lara Croft*), though critics argue they are still sexualised (Male Gaze).
*   **Girl Power:** 90s movement suggesting women can be assertive and sexual on their own terms.
*   **Social Media:** Has amplified the "Beauty Myth" (filters, influencers) but also provided a platform for Body Positivity movements.
