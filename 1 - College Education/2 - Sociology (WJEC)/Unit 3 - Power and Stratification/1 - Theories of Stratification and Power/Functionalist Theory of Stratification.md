Functionalists believe that social inequality (stratification) is **inevitable**, **necessary**, and **functional** for a healthy society.

## Davis and Moore (1945): Role Allocation
They argue that stratification acts as a mechanism for **Role Allocation** - ensuring the most important jobs are filled by the most talented people.

### Key Arguments:
1.  **Functional Importance:** Some jobs are more important for society's survival than others (e.g., Brain Surgeon > Cleaner).
2.  **Scarcity of Personnel:** Only a few people have the innate talent to perform these high-level roles.
3.  **Training and Sacrifice:** These roles require long, expensive training (university, medical school). People would not undertake this sacrifice without a reward.
4.  **Incentives:** High rewards (money, status) are offered as incentives to motivate talented people to compete for these positions.
5.  **Result:** Inequality is a "sorting mechanism". It is fair (Meritocratic) because everyone has an equal chance, but outcomes differ based on talent and effort.

## Evaluation of Davis and Moore

### Strengths (Supporting View)
*   **Meritocracy:** Reflects Western values that hard work should be rewarded.
*   **Motivation:** Explains why people strive for promotions and higher education.

### Weaknesses (Critique - Tumin)
*   **Importance is Subjective:** Is a surgeon really more important than a farmer or nurse? If the cleaner stops working, the hospital closes due to infection.
*   **Not Meritocratic:** The rich often get the best jobs due to inheritance and networking ("It's not what you know, but who you know"), not just talent.
*   **Blocked Opportunities:** Many talented poor people cannot afford the training (tuition fees).
*   **Dysfunction:** Inequality causes jealousy, conflict, and social unrest (crime), so it is not purely "functional".

## New Right Perspective (Saunders)
*   Agrees with Functionalists.
*   Argues that "Equality of Outcome" (Socialism) removes motivation.
*   A degree of inequality is needed to encourage innovation and economic growth (The "Trickle Down" effect).
