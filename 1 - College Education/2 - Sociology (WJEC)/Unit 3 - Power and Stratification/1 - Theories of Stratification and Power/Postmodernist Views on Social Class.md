## Postmodernist Critique of Traditional Social Class
- Postmodernist sociologists argue that traditional concepts of social class (derived from Marxist and Functionalist grand narratives) are outdated and no longer determine individual life chances or social identities.
- Industrial society, characterized by rigid occupational hierarchies and collective class solidarity, has transitioned into a post-industrial, consumer-driven culture.

## Key Tenets of the Postmodern View
- **De-Centring of Class**: Occupational position no longer dictates an individual's worldview, political choices, or social circle.
- **Proliferation of Identity Markers**: Identity is now constructed actively through a diverse combination of factors:
  - Gender and sexual orientation
  - Ethnicity and cultural heritage
  - Age and generation
  - Lifestyle choices and subcultural affiliations
  - Consumption habits and brand preferences
- **Individualisation and Pick-and-Mix Culture**: Individuals choose identities freely from global culture, rejecting inherited social roles and class boundaries.

## Traditional vs Postmodern Views on Stratification

| Dimension | Traditional Sociological View (Marxist / Functionalist) | Postmodernist View |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Basis of Identity** | Occupational social class and economic relations. | Diverse, fluid identity markers (lifestyle, ethnicity, gender, taste). |
| **Structure of Society** | Fixed, hierarchical, and structured by collective class groups. | Fragmented, fluid, decentred, and pluralistic. |
| **Life Chances** | Strictly constrained by economic class and systemic inequality. | Influenced by diverse individual choices, cultural capital, and agency. |
| **Class Consciousness** | Collective solidarity and shared class-based political struggle. | Replaced by hyper-individualism and identity politics (Pakulski and Waters). |
