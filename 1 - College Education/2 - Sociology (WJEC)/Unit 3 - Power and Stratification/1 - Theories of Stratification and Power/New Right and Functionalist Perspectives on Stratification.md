New Right 

Privatisation - Transference of a company or organisation from government control to private ownership 

Nanny State - A government perceived as having excessive interest in or control over the welfare of its citizens, especially in the enforcement of extensive public health and safety regulation

Marketisation - Marketisation refers to the introduction of market forces into the education system, encouraging competition among schools and providing parents with more choices regarding their children's education. This concept gained prominence with the 1988 Education Reform Act in the UK, promoting policies like league tables and open enrolment to foster a competitive environment in schools.

Trickle down economics - Trickle-down economics is an economic term for government policies that mainly benefit wealthy individuals and large corporations, with the claim that their gains will eventually benefit lower-income people. It’s often used by critics of supply-side economics to argue that these policies widen income inequality instead.

Dependency Culture- Dependency culture refers to a concept where individuals rely on welfare benefits, which some argue can undermine personal responsibility and create a cycle of poverty. Critics, like Charles Murray, suggest that this culture develops when welfare systems provide little incentive for claimants to seek employment.

Right to buy schemes - Introduced By Thatcher The Right to Buy scheme lets eligible council tenants in England buy their council home at a discount. You usually need a secure tenancy, live in the home as your main home, and have at least 3 years of public sector tenancy (with rules varying by property and location). 

Taxation - The act or practice of imposing taxes. ( tax is a mandatory financial charge or levy imposed on an individual or legal entity by a governmental organisation to support government spending and public expenditures collectively or to regulate economic activity)
