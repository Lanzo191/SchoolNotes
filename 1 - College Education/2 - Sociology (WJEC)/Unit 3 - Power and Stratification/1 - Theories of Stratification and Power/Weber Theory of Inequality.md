**Max Weber** argued that social inequality is more complex than just "owners vs. workers." He identified three distinct but related dimensions of inequality: **Class, Status, and Party**.

## 1. Class (Economic Dimension)
- Like Marx, Weber agreed that class was based on economic factors.
- However, Weber argued there were **multiple classes** based on "market position" (skills, qualifications, and wealth).
- *Four main classes*: The propertied upper class, the white-collar middle class, the petty bourgeoisie, and the manual working class.

## 2. Status (Social Dimension)
- Status refers to how much **social honor** or prestige a person has.
- People can have a low class but high status (e.g., an impoverished priest) or a high class but low status (e.g., a "lottery winner" or a criminal boss).
- Status groups often use **social closure** to keep others out of their privileged circles.

## 3. Party (Political Dimension)
- Party refers to a person's **political power** or influence.
- This includes membership in trade unions, pressure groups, or political parties.
- Power can be used to improve a group's class or status position.

---

## Evaluation
- **Strength**: Provides a more realistic and detailed picture of modern inequality than the simple two-class Marxist model.
- **Strength**: Explains why there is conflict *within* classes (e.g., between skilled and unskilled workers).
- **Weakness**: By splitting inequality into so many parts, it can make it harder to identify the main cause of social problems.
