## Max Weber's Definition of Power
- Max Weber defined **power** as the chance of an individual or group to realise their own will in a social action, even against the resistance of others who are participating in the action.
- Weber viewed power as an active form of agency that exists dynamically within social relationships, operating through both consent and coercion.

## Types of Power and Authority
- Weber distinguished between legitimate power (**authority**) and illegitimate power (**coercion**).

| Form of Power | Category | Sociological Mechanism | Real-World Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Charismatic Authority** | Legitimate | Derived from devotion to the extraordinary character, heroism, or exemplary qualities of an individual leader. | Martin Luther King Jr., Nelson Mandela. |
| **Traditional Authority** | Legitimate | Rooted in long-established customs, conventions, and hereditary rights handed down through generations. | Hereditary monarchies, patriarchal family structures. |
| **Rational-Legal Authority** | Legitimate | Based on a belief in the legality of enacted rules, bureaucratic procedures, and the right of officials to issue commands. | Modern civil service, elected governments, judicial courts. |
| **Coercive Power** | Illegitimate | Relies on the direct threat or use of physical force, sanctions, or intimidation without the consent of the subject. | Authoritarian state violence, military subjugation. |

## Dimensions of Stratification: Class, Status, and Party
- In contrast to Karl Marx's unidimensional economic model, Weber argued that social stratification is multidimensional, arising from the interaction of three distinct sources of power:
  - **Class (Economic Order)**: Defined by an individual's market situation and economic life chances (ownership of property, skills, education, and qualifications).
  - **Status (Social Order)**: Defined by social prestige, honour, lifestyle, and the social estimation granted by others within a community.
  - **Party (Political Order)**: Defined by the exercise of power through organised groups, political associations, trade unions, and pressure groups aimed at influencing social action.

## Weber's Four Social Classes
- Based on market situation and skills, Weber identified four main social classes in modern industrial capitalism:
  - **The Propertied Upper Class**: Owners of substantial commercial and agricultural property.
  - **The White-Collar Intelligentsia**: Educated professionals, administrators, and managers with marketable skills.
  - **The Petty Bourgeoisie**: Small business owners and independent tradespeople.
  - **The Manual Working Class**: Individuals possessing only manual labour power to sell on the market.

## Comparison: Weber vs Marx on Power and Class

| Feature | Max Weber | Karl Marx |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Basis of Power** | Multidimensional: Class, Status, and Party interact to determine power. | Unidimensional: Power is determined solely by ownership of the means of production. |
| **Class Division** | Pluralistic: Multiple classes defined by differing market situations and skills. | Polarised: Two primary conflicting classes (Bourgeoisie and Proletariat). |
| **Social Change** | Complex and open-ended; rationalisation and bureaucracy drive modern society. | Deterministic; class struggle inevitably leads to the revolutionary overthrow of capitalism. |
