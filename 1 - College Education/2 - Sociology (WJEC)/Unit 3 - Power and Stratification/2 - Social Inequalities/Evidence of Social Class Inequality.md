Social class remains one of the most significant sources of inequality in the UK. This note provides evidence of class-based inequality across different areas of social life.

## 1. Education
- **The Attainment Gap**: Children from higher social class backgrounds consistently outperform those from lower social class backgrounds in public exams (GCSEs, A-Levels).
- **Private vs. State**: A disproportionate number of top jobs in law, politics, and the media are held by people who attended independent (private) schools (~7% of the population but ~65% of senior judges).
- **Cultural Capital (Bourdieu)**: Middle-class children have the "right" language, manners, and knowledge that schools value, giving them an unfair advantage.

## 2. Health
- **Life Expectancy**: There is a significant gap in life expectancy between the wealthiest and poorest areas of the UK (up to 10 years for men).
- **The Inverse Care Law (Tudor Hart)**: Those with the greatest need for healthcare (the poor) are often the least likely to receive it, while those with the least need (the wealthy) have better access to high-quality services.
- **Morbidity Rates**: People from lower social classes are more likely to suffer from chronic illnesses and long-term disabilities.

## 3. Crime and Justice
- **The Prison Population**: A vast majority of the UK's prison population comes from working-class or deprived backgrounds.
- **White-Collar Crime**: Crimes committed by the wealthy (e.g., tax evasion, corporate fraud) are often less likely to be detected, prosecuted, or punished severely compared to "street crimes."
- **Labelling (Becker)**: The police and justice system are more likely to label working-class behavior as "criminal."

## 4. Employment and Wealth
- **The Wealth Gap**: The richest 1% of the UK population own as much wealth as the bottom 70%.
- **Zero-Hours Contracts**: Working-class individuals are more likely to be in "precarious" employment with no guaranteed hours or benefits.
- **Glass Floor**: Wealthy parents can use their connections and resources to prevent their children from "falling" down the social ladder, regardless of their ability.

## Key Evidence/Sources
- **The Great British Class Survey (2013)**: Identified 7 distinct social classes, highlighting the complexity of modern inequality.
- **Oxfam Reports**: Regularly highlight the growing gap between the ultra-wealthy and the rest of society.
- **Department for Education (DfE) Statistics**: Provide annual data on the link between Free School Meals (FSM) and exam results.
