#Education #Inequality #Sociology #WJEC

Ethnic inequality refers to the systematic disadvantage faced by certain ethnic groups in society. This is often linked to **racism** (both individual and institutional).

## 1. Education
- **Attainment Gap**: While some groups (e.g., Chinese and Indian students) statistically outperform White British students, other groups (e.g., Black Caribbean and Gypsy/Roma) consistently have lower attainment levels.
- **Institutional Racism**: Schools may have hidden biases in their curriculum (**Ethnocentric Curriculum**) or in how they discipline students.
- **Teacher Labelling**: Research suggests some teachers may have lower expectations for certain ethnic minority students (**Gillborn**).

## 2. Employment and Economy
- **The "Ethnic Penalty"**: Minority groups are more likely to be unemployed or in low-paid, insecure work compared to White British people with the same qualifications.
- **The Recruitment Gap**: [[Field Experiments]] (e.g., sending identical CVs with "White" vs "Minority" names) show that applicants with minority names receive significantly fewer [[Interviews]].
- **Poverty**: Ethnic minority households are statistically more likely to live in low-income households.

## 3. Criminal Justice System
- **Stop and Search**: Black people are statistically much more likely to be stopped and searched by the police than White people.
- **The Macpherson Report (1999)**: Investigated the murder of Stephen Lawrence and concluded that the Metropolitan Police was **institutionally racist**.
- **Prison Population**: Ethnic minorities are disproportionately represented in the UK prison system.

---

## Key Terms
- **Assimilation**: When minority groups are expected to adopt the culture of the majority.
- **Multiculturalism**: Celebrating and maintaining diverse cultural identities.
- **Institutional Racism**: When the everyday procedures of an organization (like the police or schools) discriminate against certain groups.
