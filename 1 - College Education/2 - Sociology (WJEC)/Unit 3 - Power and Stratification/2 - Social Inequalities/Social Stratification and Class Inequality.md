· Q2. Differentiate between ascribed & achieved status?
- Ascribed status refers to the status you are naturally born into e.g having parents who are politicians would give you a higher ascribed status then one born to working class parents, Achieved status is the status you achieve throughout your life via means of work, connections, education etc. 

Q3. What is social mobility?
- Social mobility is the means in which a person or family can move through one level of social hierarchy to the next or vice versa 

Q4. How would our functionalist & Marxist theorists view ‘social mobility’?
- Marxists would view social mobility as a limited system that allows for individuals or families to move through a pre-determined bracket of social hierarchy that is based around income, where as functionalists iew social mobility as a natural and necessary aspect of society, believing that it allows individuals to move up or down the social hierarchy based on their abilities and efforts. They argue that this meritocratic system helps allocate the most talented individuals to the most important roles, contributing to overall social stability and efficiency.

Question 2 - Ciphr commissioned a survey of 2,000 UK adults, including 1,188 employees, to find out how closely people’s perceptions of the gender pay gap (if any) matched reality.

The findings revealed that a quarter (25%) of men and a fifth (19%) of women don’t think there are any job roles in the UK with gender pay gaps in favour of men in 2024. The latest figures from the Office for National Statistics (ONS), however, tend to prove otherwise.

According to the ONS, across all occupations, the median gender pay gap is 13.1%. This means that women working in the UK earn £2.39* less per hour, on average, than their male peers.

Based on Ciphr’s analysis of the data, nearly three-quarters (71%) of all jobs - even those predominately held by women - and the majority (89%) of industries in the UK, have gender pay gaps (of 1% or higher) in favour of men.
