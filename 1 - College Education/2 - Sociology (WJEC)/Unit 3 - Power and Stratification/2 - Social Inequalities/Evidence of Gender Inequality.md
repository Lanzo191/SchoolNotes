Gender inequality refers to the unequal treatment or perceptions of individuals based on their gender. In the UK, while significant progress has been made, patriarchal structures still exist.

## 1. Education
- **Subject Choice**: There is still a clear gender divide in subject choices at A-Level and University (e.g., boys dominate STEM; girls dominate Humanities and Social Sciences).
- **Teacher Labelling**: Research suggests teachers may have lower expectations for boys (leading to underachievement) and may push girls toward traditional "feminine" career paths.
- **The "Glass Ceiling" in Education**: While the majority of primary school teachers are women, a disproportionate number of Headteachers are men.

## 2. Employment and the Economy
- **The Gender Pay Gap**: On average, women in the UK still earn less per hour than men (currently ~14-15% gap depending on the measure).
- **The Glass Ceiling**: An invisible barrier that prevents women from reaching the highest levels of management in corporations.
- **The Motherhood Penalty**: Women often face reduced pay or career progression after having children, while men may experience a "fatherhood bonus."
- **Horizontal Segregation**: Men and women are often clustered into different types of jobs (e.g., women in "caring" roles; men in "technical" or "manual" roles).

## 3. The Family
- **The Dual Burden / Triple Shift**: Women often do the majority of domestic housework and childcare even when working full-time. The "Triple Shift" (Duncombe and Marsden) includes paid work, housework, and emotional labor.
- **Decision Making**: Research (e.g., Edgell) suggests that men still make the most "important" financial decisions in many households.
- **Domestic Violence**: Statistically, women are far more likely to be victims of domestic abuse and sexual violence.

## 4. Media
- **Male Gaze (Mulvey)**: The media often represents women as objects for the pleasure of male viewers.
- **Stereotyping**: Women are often portrayed in limited roles (e.g., the housewife, the damsel in distress, or the "femme fatale").
- **Symbolic Annihilation (Tuchman)**: The absence or under-representation of women in powerful or diverse roles in the media.

## Key Evidence/Sources
- **Office for National Statistics (ONS)**: Provides annual data on the gender pay gap and employment rates.
- **The Fawcett Society**: A leading UK charity campaigning for gender equality; provides extensive reports on the "motherhood penalty."
- **Crime Survey for England and Wales (CSEW)**: Provides data on domestic and sexual violence.
