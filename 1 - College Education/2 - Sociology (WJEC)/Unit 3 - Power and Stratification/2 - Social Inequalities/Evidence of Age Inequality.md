Age inequality affects both the **young** and the **elderly**. It is often based on **ageism**:stereotyping or discrimination based on age.

## 1. Inequality facing the Young
- **Employment**: Young people are the most likely to be in low-paid, precarious work (e.g., zero-hours contracts). The "Minimum Wage" is also lower for younger workers.
- **Media Representation**: Young people (especially working-class youths) are often portrayed as "Folk Devils" or a threat to social order (linked to **Moral Panics**).
- **Political Power**: Young people have the lowest rates of voting and are under-represented in parliament.

## 2. Inequality facing the Elderly
- **Poverty**: Many elderly people live in "pensioner poverty," struggling with the cost of heating and food.
- **Employment**: The elderly may face discrimination in hiring or promotion, or feel forced into retirement.
- **The "Digital Divide"**: Older people are less likely to have digital skills, which can lead to social isolation and difficulty accessing services.
- **Health and Care**: While they use the NHS most, the elderly often face "rationing" of care or poor quality social care.

## 3. Key Concepts
- **The Grannying of society**: The idea that the population is aging, which puts pressure on the economy and healthcare.
- **Chronological vs. Social Age**: Chronological is your actual age; Social age is how society expects you to act at that age.
