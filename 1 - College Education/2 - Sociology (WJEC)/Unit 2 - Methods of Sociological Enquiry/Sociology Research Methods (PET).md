In WJEC Sociology, you must be able to evaluate any research method (**[[Questionnaires Sociology]]**, **[[Interviews]]**, **[[Observations]]**, etc.) using the **[[PET]]** framework. This is the foundation of the Unit 2 exam.

## The PET Framework

### 1. **P**ractical Issues
How easy or difficult is it to actually carry out the research?
*   **Time:** Does the method take years (Longitudinal) or hours (**[[Questionnaires Sociology]]**)?
*   **Cost:** Do you need a large team of researchers or expensive equipment?
*   **Access:** Is it hard to get to the group you want to study (e.g., criminal gangs or powerful "elites")?
*   **Researcher Characteristics:** Does the researcher's age, gender, or ethnicity affect their ability to gain trust?

### 2. **E**thical Issues
Does the research protect the rights and wellbeing of the participants?
*   **Informed Consent:** Do participants know what they are signing up for?
*   **Deception:** Are you lying to them (common in covert **[[Observations]]**)?
*   **Protection from Harm:** Could the research cause stress or physical danger to participants or the researcher?
*   **Right to Withdraw:** Can participants leave the study at any time?

### 3. **T**heoretical Issues
Does the method produce "good" data according to sociological theory?
*   **Reliability (Positivism):** Can the research be repeated by another researcher and get the same results? High in structured methods.
*   **[[Validity]] (Interpretivism):** Does it provide a "true," authentic picture of social reality? High in qualitative, open methods.
*   **Representativeness:** Does the sample reflect the wider target population?
*   **Generalisability:** Can you apply the findings to people outside the study based on the sample?
*   **Objectivity:** Is the researcher remaining neutral or is their bias influencing the results?

## Comparison of Major Perspectives

### 1. Positivism (Scientific Approach)
*   **Goal:** To find "social facts" and cause-and-effect relationships.
*   **Preference:** Quantitative data, large samples, and high **Reliability**.
*   **Methods:** Structured **[[Questionnaires Sociology]]**, Official Statistics, and Lab Experiments.

### 2. Interpretivism (Humanistic Approach)
*   **Goal:** To achieve **Verstehen** (empathetic understanding).
*   **Preference:** Qualitative data, small samples, and high **Validity**.
*   **Methods:** Unstructured **[[Interviews]]**, Participant Observation, and Personal Documents.

## Comparison Table

| Method | Practical Pros | Ethical Cons | Theoretical Strength |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **[[Questionnaires Sociology]]** | Cheap and fast. | Low risk of harm. | Highly Reliable. |
| **[[Interviews\|Unstructured Interviews]]** | Flexible. | High risk of over-sharing. | Highly Valid (**Verstehen**). |
| **Covert [[Observations]]** | Can study gangs. | No informed consent. | No "Hawthorne Effect." |
| **Official Statistics** | Free and easy access. | Data may be political. | High Representativeness. |
