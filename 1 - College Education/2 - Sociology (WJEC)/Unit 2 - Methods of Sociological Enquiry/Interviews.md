**Interviews** involve a face-to-face or over-the-phone conversation between a researcher and a participant.

## Types of Interviews
1. **Structured**: Pre-set questions (basically a spoken questionnaire). **Positivist**.
2. **Unstructured**: Informal, like a conversation; questions are developed during the interview. **Interpretivist**.
3. **Semi-Structured**: A mix of both; has a set of themes to cover but allows for follow-up questions.
4. **Group Interviews/Focus Groups**: Interviewing several people at once to see how they interact.

---

## PET Evaluation (Unstructured)

### 1. Practical
- **Pros**: Can clarify questions if the participant is confused.
- **Cons**: Very time-consuming and expensive. Requires a highly trained interviewer. Small sample sizes.

### 2. Ethical
- **Pros**: Good for sensitive topics as the researcher can build **rapport** (trust) and offer support.
- **Cons**: The power dynamic between interviewer and participant can be intimidating.

### 3. Theoretical
- **Pros (Validity)**: High validity; allows for deep "Verstehen" (empathetic understanding) and detailed qualitative data.
- **Cons (Reliability)**: Low reliability; every interview is unique and impossible to replicate exactly.
- **Interviewer Effect**: The presence, gender, or age of the interviewer may change how the participant responds.
