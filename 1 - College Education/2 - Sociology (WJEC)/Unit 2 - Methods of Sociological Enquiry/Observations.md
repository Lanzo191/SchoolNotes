**Observations** involve watching and recording the behavior of a group in their natural environment.

## Types of Observations
1. **Overt**: The group knows they are being watched.
2. **Covert**: The researcher's identity is hidden (they are "undercover").
3. **Participant**: The researcher joins in with the group's activities.
4. **Non-Participant**: The researcher watches from a distance.

---

## PET Evaluation (Covert Participant)

### 1. Practical
- **Pros**: Only way to study "deviant" or secret groups (e.g., gangs). High access to "hidden" behaviors.
- **Cons**: Very difficult to "get in," "stay in," and "get out" of the group. Requires great skill.

### 2. Ethical
- **Pros**: None (it is generally seen as the most unethical method).
- **Cons**: **Lack of informed consent**, deception, and potential involvement in illegal acts.

### 3. Theoretical
- **Pros (Validity)**: Extremely high validity; sees what people *actually* do rather than what they *say* they do. Avoids the **Hawthorne Effect**.
- **Cons (Reliability)**: Zero reliability; impossible to replicate. Highly subjective (researcher may "go native").

---

## Key Terms
- **Hawthorne Effect**: When people change their behavior because they know they are being studied.
- **"Going Native"**: When the researcher loses their objectivity and becomes a true member of the group.
