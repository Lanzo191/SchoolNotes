**[[Secondary Data]]** is information that has been collected by someone else (e.g., the government, media, or other sociologists). **Official Statistics** are a major form of [[Secondary Data]].

## Types of Secondary Data
- **Quantitative**: Official statistics (Census, crime rates), surveys by other researchers.
- **Qualitative**: Diaries, letters, media reports, historical documents.

---

## PET Evaluation (Official Statistics)

### 1. Practical
- **Pros**: Free and easy to access (online). Covers huge samples (the whole population). Allows for comparison over time (trends).
- **Cons**: May not exist for the specific topic you want to study.

### 2. Ethical
- **Pros**: Very ethical; data is already anonymized and public. No direct contact with participants.

### 3. Theoretical
- **Pros (Reliability)**: Highly reliable; collected in a standardized way every year.
- **Cons (Validity)**: **Interpretivists** argue statistics are "social constructs." For example, the **"Dark Figure of Crime"** refers to all the crimes that happen but aren't reported or recorded.

---

## Key Terms
- **Content Analysis**: A method used to study the content of qualitative data (like media reports) by turning it into numbers.
- **Triangulation**: Using more than one method or source of data to check the validity of findings.
