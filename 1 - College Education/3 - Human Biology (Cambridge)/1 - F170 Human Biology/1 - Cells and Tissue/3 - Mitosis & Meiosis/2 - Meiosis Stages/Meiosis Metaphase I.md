In **[[Meiosis]] Metaphase I**, the bivalents (pairs of homologous [[Chromosomes]]) align along the equator of the cell.

## Key Events
1. **Alignment**: **Bivalents** (homologous pairs) line up along the **metaphase plate** (equator).
2. **Spindle Attachment**: Spindle fibers from opposite poles attach to the **centromere** of each homologous chromosome.
3. **Independent Assortment**: The orientation of each homologous pair is random. This means the maternal or paternal chromosome can face either pole. 
    - This is the second major source of **[[Genetic Variation]]**.

---

## Significance
- **Genetic Variation**: With 23 pairs of chromosomes in humans, there are over 8 million ($2^{23}$possible combinations of chromosomes due to independent assortment alone.
