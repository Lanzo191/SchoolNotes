## Comparison of Mitosis and Meiosis

| Feature                      | [[Mitosis]]                                     | [[Meiosis]]                                                                  |
| :--------------------------- | :------------------------------------------ | :----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Purpose**                  | Growth, tissue repair, asexual reproduction | Production of gametes (sex cells) for sexual reproduction                |
| **Where it occurs**          | Somatic cells (body cells)                  | Germ cells (in gonads: testes and ovaries)                               |
| **Number of Divisions**      | One                                         | Two ([[Meiosis]] I and [[Meiosis]] II)                                           |
| **Number of Daughter Cells** | Two                                         | Four                                                                     |
| **Genetic Composition**      | Genetically **identical** clones            | Genetically **unique** (due to crossing over and independent assortment) |
| **Chromosome Number**        | **Diploid (2n)** - same as parent           | **Haploid (n)** - half of parent                                         |
| **Homologous Pairing**       | Does not occur                              | Occurs in Prophase I (forms bivalents)                                   |
| **Crossing Over**            | Does not occur                              | Occurs in Prophase I (exchange of DNA between non-sister chromatids)     |
| **Separation of Chromatids** | Anaphase                                    | Anaphase II (Homologous chromosomes separate in Anaphase I)              |

---

## Key Differences Explained

### Variation
- **[[Mitosis]]** produces consistency. It ensures every new cell has the exact same genetic information.
- **[[Meiosis]]** generates variation. Through **crossing over** (shuffling alleles) and **independent assortment** (random alignment of [[Chromosomes]]), it ensures offspring are genetically distinct from parents.

### Chromosome Count
- **[[Mitosis]]** maintains the chromosome number (e.g., 46 in humans).
- **[[Meiosis]]** reduces it by half (e.g., 23 in humans) so that when [[Fertilisation]] occurs (sperm + egg), the diploid number is restored.
