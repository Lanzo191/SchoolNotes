Metaphase is a crucial stage in the process of [[Mitosis]], which is the division of a cell into two identical daughter cells. This phase follows prophase and precedes anaphase.

### Key Features of Metaphase

- **Chromosome Alignment**: During metaphase, [[Chromosomes]] align along the equatorial plane of the cell, known as the metaphase plate. This alignment is essential for ensuring that each daughter cell receives an identical set of [[Chromosomes]].

- **Sister Chromatids**: Each chromosome consists of two identical sister chromatids, which are joined at a central point called the centromere. These chromatids are pulled by spindle fibers towards opposite poles of the cell.

- **Kinetochore Attachment**: Protein structures called kinetochores form around the centromeres. Spindle fibers, which are microtubules, attach to these kinetochores, facilitating the movement and alignment of [[Chromosomes]].


### Importance of Metaphase

- **Spindle Checkpoint**: Metaphase includes a critical checkpoint that ensures all [[Chromosomes]] are properly aligned and attached to the spindle apparatus. This prevents errors in chromosome separation, which could lead to daughter cells with an incorrect number of [[Chromosomes]].

- **Visual Analysis**: Metaphase [[Chromosomes]] are highly condensed and can be easily visualized under a microscope, making this stage important for cytogenetic studies, including karyotyping.


### Duration and Significance

Metaphase accounts for approximately 4% of the total duration of the cell cycle. Its proper execution is vital for the accurate distribution of genetic material, which is essential for cell function and organismal development.
