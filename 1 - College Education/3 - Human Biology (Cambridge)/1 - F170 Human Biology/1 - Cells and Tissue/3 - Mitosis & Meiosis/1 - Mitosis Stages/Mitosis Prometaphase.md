Prometaphase is the second stage of [[Mitosis]], occurring after prophase and before metaphase. It plays a crucial role in preparing [[Chromosomes]] for separation.

### Key Events During Prometaphase

### Breakdown of the Nuclear Envelope

- The nuclear membrane disintegrates into small vesicles.
- This breakdown allows [[Chromosomes]] to be released from the [[Nucleus]].

### Chromosome Preparation

- [[Chromosomes]] condense and form structures called kinetochores at their centromeres.
- Kinetochore microtubules extend from the centrosomes at the poles of the cell and attach to these kinetochores.

### Chromosome Movement

- The attachment of microtubules causes [[Chromosomes]] to move toward the cell's equatorial plane.
- This movement is facilitated by forces from protein motors associated with the spindle microtubules.

## Transition to Metaphase

Prometaphase concludes when all kinetochore microtubules are attached to their respective kinetochores. This alignment is critical for the next phase, metaphase, where [[Chromosomes]] line up at the cell's center. If any kinetochores remain unattached, a checkpoint signal prevents progression to anaphase, ensuring proper chromosome distribution.
