### Chromatid Separation

- The centromeres that hold the sister chromatids together divide.
- Spindle fibers, which are structures that help separate the chromatids, attach to the kinetochores at the centromeres.

### Movement of Chromatids

- The spindle fibers shorten, pulling the sister chromatids apart.
- Each chromatid is now considered an individual chromosome.

### Positioning in the Cell

- The separated [[Chromosomes]] move toward opposite ends of the cell.
- This ensures that each daughter cell will receive an identical set of [[Chromosomes]].

## Duration and Importance

- Anaphase is typically the shortest phase of [[Mitosis]].
- Proper separation of chromatids is crucial for genetic stability in the daughter cells.

## Conclusion

Anaphase is essential for ensuring that each new cell receives the correct number of [[Chromosomes]]. Any errors during this phase can lead to genetic disorders or diseases, such as cancer
