Prophase is the first stage of [[Mitosis]], which is the process of cell division that results in two identical daughter cells. This phase is crucial for preparing the cell for the separation of its genetic material.

## Key Events During Prophase

### Chromosome Condensation

- The chromatin, a complex of [[DNA]] and [[Proteins]], condenses into visible [[Chromosomes]].
- Each chromosome consists of two identical sister chromatids joined at a region called the centromere.

### Formation of the Mitotic Spindle

- Microtubules organize to form the mitotic spindle, which is essential for chromosome movement.
- Centrosomes, which are the cell's microtubule organizing centers, move to opposite poles of the cell.

### Disappearance of the Nuclear Structures

- The nucleolus, a structure within the [[Nucleus]], shrinks and eventually disappears.
- The nuclear membrane begins to break down, allowing the spindle fibers to access the [[Chromosomes]].

## Importance of Prophase

Prophase sets the stage for the subsequent phases of [[Mitosis]], ensuring that [[Chromosomes]] are properly aligned and separated. This is vital for maintaining genetic stability in the daughter cells.
