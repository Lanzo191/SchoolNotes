Telophase is the final stage of [[Mitosis]], which is the process of cell division that results in two identical daughter cells. This phase follows anaphase and is crucial for the completion of cell division.

## Key Events During Telophase

### Nuclear Envelope Formation

- A nuclear envelope forms around each set of separated [[Chromosomes]].
- This re-establishes the [[Nucleus]] in each daughter cell.

### Chromosome Decondensation

- The [[Chromosomes]] begin to unravel and decondense back into chromatin.
- This transition prepares the genetic material for the next [[Interphase]].

### Spindle Disassembly

- The mitotic spindle, which helped separate the [[Chromosomes]], disassembles.
- Remaining spindle microtubules are broken down.

## Cytokinesis

- [[Cytokinesis]] often begins during late telophase.
- This process divides the cytoplasm, resulting in two distinct daughter cells, each with a complete set of [[Chromosomes]].

## Duration and Importance

- Telophase accounts for a small portion of the cell cycle, typically around 2%.
- It is essential for ensuring that each daughter cell has the correct genetic material and is ready to enter [[Interphase]].
