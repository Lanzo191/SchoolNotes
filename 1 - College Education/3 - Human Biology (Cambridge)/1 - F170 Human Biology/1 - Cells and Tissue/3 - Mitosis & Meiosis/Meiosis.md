**[[Meiosis]]** is a specialized form of cell division that produces **four non-identical haploid gametes** (sperm and eggs) from a single **diploid** parent cell. It is essential for **sexual reproduction** and ensures [[Genetic Variation]] in offspring.

## The Process of Meiosis
[[Meiosis]] consists of two successive divisions: **[[Meiosis]] I** and **[[Meiosis]] II**.

### Meiosis I (Reduction Division)
The chromosome number is halved from diploid (2n) to haploid (n).
1. **Prophase I**: [[Chromosomes]] condense. **Homologous [[Chromosomes]]** pair up to form **bivalents**. **Crossing over** occurs at **chiasmata**, where sections of [[DNA]] are exchanged between non-sister chromatids.
2. **Metaphase I**: Bivalents line up along the equator. **Independent assortment** occurs as the orientation of each pair is random.
3. **Anaphase I**: Homologous [[Chromosomes]] are pulled to opposite poles by spindle fibers.
4. **Telophase I**: Nuclear envelopes reform and the cell undergoes [[Cytokinesis]].

### Meiosis II (Equational Division)
Similar to [[Mitosis]], where sister chromatids are separated.
1. **Prophase II**: Nuclear envelopes break down and spindles reform.
2. **Metaphase II**: Individual [[Chromosomes]] line up on the equator.
3. **Anaphase II**: Centromeres divide and **sister chromatids** are pulled to opposite poles.
4. **Telophase II**: Nuclear envelopes reform around four distinct haploid nuclei. [[Cytokinesis]] produces four daughter cells.

---

## Sources of Genetic Variation
1. **Crossing Over (Prophase I)**: Creates new combinations of alleles on a single chromosome (**recombinant chromatids**). 
2. **Independent Assortment (Metaphase I & II)**: Random distribution of maternal and paternal chromosomes into gametes.
3. **Random Fertilization**: Any sperm can fuse with any egg, creating a unique zygote.

---

## Comparison: Mitosis vs. Meiosis

| Feature | [[Mitosis]] | [[Meiosis]] |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Purpose** | Growth, repair, asexual reproduction | Production of gametes |
| **Daughter Cells** | 2 Identical cells | 4 Non-identical cells |
| **Ploidy** | Diploid (2n) $\rightarrow$ Diploid (2n) | Diploid (2n) $\rightarrow$ Haploid (n) |
| **Variation** | None (clones) | High (crossing over/assortment) |

---

## Significance
- Maintains a constant chromosome number across generations.
- Provides the **[[Genetic Variation]]** necessary for natural selection and evolution.
