Cytokinesis is the final stage of cell division, occurring after [[Mitosis]] or [[Meiosis]]. It involves the physical separation of the cytoplasm of a parental cell into two daughter cells. This process ensures that each daughter cell receives an equal share of the cytoplasmic components and organelles.

## Process of Cytokinesis

### In Animal Cells

- **Cleavage Furrow Formation**: A contractile ring made of actin and myosin filaments forms just inside the plasma membrane at the cell's equator.
- **Contraction**: The ring contracts, pulling the membrane inward and creating a cleavage furrow.
- **Completion**: The furrow deepens until the cell membrane pinches off, resulting in two separate daughter cells.

### In Plant Cells

- **Cell Plate Formation**: Instead of a cleavage furrow, plant cells form a cell plate at the center of the dividing cell.
- **Vesicle Fusion**: Golgi-derived vesicles coalesce at the metaphase plate, forming the cell plate.
- **Cell Wall Development**: The cell plate expands outward, eventually fusing with the existing cell wall, creating two daughter cells.

## Importance of Cytokinesis

Cytokinesis is crucial for maintaining the correct number of [[Chromosomes]] and ensuring that each daughter cell is functional. It is tightly regulated to occur after the separation of sister chromatids during anaphase, ensuring proper cell division and function.
