## Overview
The **reproductive tract** is divided into male and female systems, both of which host unique microbial communities that play a critical role in reproductive health and protection against infection.

---

## The Vaginal Microbiome
In healthy women of reproductive age, the **vaginal microbiome** is characterized by low diversity and is dominated by **Lactobacillus** species.

### The Role of Lactobacillus
- **Lactic Acid Production:** Fermenting glycogen (released by the epithelial cells) into **lactic acid**, which maintains a low pH (3.5 to 4.5).
- **Inhibition of Pathogens:** The acidic environment and the production of **hydrogen peroxide** inhibit the growth of **pathogens** such as *Candida albicans* and *Gardnerella vaginalis*.
- **Colonisation Resistance:** Prevents the attachment of **STIs** to the **mucous membrane**.

---

## Factors Influencing Reproductive Tract Microbiome
The microbiome of the female reproductive tract is dynamic and can be shifted by:
- **Hormonal Changes:** Variations in **oestrogen** during the menstrual cycle, pregnancy, and menopause affect glycogen levels and therefore the dominant microbial species.
- **Sexual Activity:** Can introduce new microbes and alter the pH.
- **Antibiotic Use:** Can lead to **dysbiosis**, such as the overgrowth of yeast leading to **thrush**.
- **Hygiene Practices:** Douching or using alkaline soaps can disrupt the natural acidic balance.

---

## Infections of the Reproductive Tract

| Condition | Description | Pathogen |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bacterial Vaginosis (BV)** | An imbalance where **Lactobacilli** are replaced by anaerobic bacteria. | *Gardnerella vaginalis*. |
| **Candidiasis (Thrush)** | Overgrowth of a commensal yeast. | *Candida albicans*. |
| **STIs** | Infections primarily transmitted through sexual contact. | *Chlamydia trachomatis*, *Neisseria gonorrhoeae*. |
