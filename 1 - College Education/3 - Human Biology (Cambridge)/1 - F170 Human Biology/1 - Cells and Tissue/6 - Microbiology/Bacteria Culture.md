## Overview
**Bacteria culture** is the method of multiplying microbial organisms by letting them reproduce in a predetermined **culture medium** under controlled laboratory conditions.

 Sterilisation ->Inoculation -> Incubation 

---

## Types of Culture Media

| Media Type | Description | Use |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nutrient Broth** | Liquid medium containing proteins, minerals, and water. | Growing large quantities of bacteria in suspension. |
| **Agar Plates** | Solidified medium containing **Agar** (a seaweed extract). | Isolating individual **colonies** and observing morphology. |
| **Enriched Media** | Contains extra nutrients like blood or vitamins. | Growing fastidious (fussy) bacteria. |
| **Selective Media** | Contains substances (e.g., antibiotics) that inhibit some bacteria while allowing others to grow. | Selecting for specific organisms. |
| **Differential Media** | Contains indicators (e.g., dyes) that show differences between bacterial species. | Identifying specific bacteria (e.g., by pH change). |

## The Bacterial Growth Curve
When grown in a closed environment (like a flask), the population follows four distinct phases:

1. **Lag Phase:** Cells are metabolically active but not yet dividing. They are preparing for growth by synthesising enzymes.
2. **Log (Exponential) Phase:** Cells divide at a constant, maximum rate (**binary fission**). Population doubles at regular intervals.
3. **Stationary Phase:** Rate of division equals the rate of death. Nutrients are exhausted and toxic metabolic wastes accumulate.
4. **Death (Decline) Phase:** The environment becomes toxic, and the population decreases exponentially.

## Optimal Conditions for Growth
- **Temperature:** Most human pathogens grow best at **37°C**. 
- **pH:** Most bacteria prefer neutral conditions (pH 7.0 to 7.4).
- **Oxygen:** Varies by type (**aerobes** vs. **anaerobes**).
- **Nutrients:** Source of carbon (e.g., glucose) and nitrogen (e.g., amino acids).
