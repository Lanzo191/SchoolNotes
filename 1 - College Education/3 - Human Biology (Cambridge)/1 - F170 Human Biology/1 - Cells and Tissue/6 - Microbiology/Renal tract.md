## Overview
The **renal tract** (urinary system) consists of the **kidneys**, **ureters**, **bladder**, and **urethra**. While once thought to be sterile, it is now known to host a resident **urinary microbiome**.

---

## The Urinary Microbiome
Microbial communities in the renal tract are less dense and diverse than those in the **GI tract**. 
- **Healthy Microbiome:** Commonly includes species such as *Lactobacillus*, *Streptococcus*, and *Staphylococcus*.
- **Role:** Resident microbes provide **colonisation resistance**, helping to prevent the overgrowth of **pathogens**.

---

## Barriers to Infection in the Renal Tract
The body has several mechanisms to prevent **urinary tract infections (UTIs)**:
1. **Flushing Action:** The regular flow of urine physically removes bacteria from the **urethra**.
2. **Urine Composition:** High urea concentration, high osmolarity, and varying pH (typically 4.5 to 8.0) inhibit the growth of many microbes.
3. **Secretory IgA:** An antibody present in the **mucous membrane** that traps bacteria.
4. **Tamm-Horsfall Protein:** A protein in urine that binds to the **pili** of *E. coli*, preventing them from attaching to the bladder wall.

---

## Infections of the Renal Tract

| Infection Type | Location | Common Pathogen |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Urethritis** | Inflammation of the **urethra**. | *Chlamydia trachomatis* (STI). |
| **Cystitis** | Infection of the **bladder**. | *Escherichia coli* (most common). |
| **Pyelonephritis** | Infection of the **kidney** (severe). | *E. coli* or *Proteus mirabilis*. |
