## Overview
**Sequencing** technologies allow scientists to identify and characterize the **human microbiome** without the need for traditional culture-based methods. This is essential as many microbes are "unculturable" in a laboratory setting.

---

## Key Sequencing Methods

### 1. 16S rRNA Gene Sequencing
This is the most common method for bacterial identification.
- **Target:** The **16S rRNA gene**, which is a highly conserved part of the **prokaryotic ribosome**.
- **Process:** Small regions of the gene that vary between species are sequenced.
- **Result:** Provides a taxonomic profile (who is there), but has limited resolution to the species level and gives no functional information.

### 2. Shotgun Metagenomics
This method sequences all the **DNA** present in a sample.
- **Process:** All genomic DNA is fragmented and sequenced.
- **Result:** Provides high-resolution taxonomic data (to the species or strain level) and identifies the **functional potential** (what the microbes can do, e.g., which enzymes they produce).

---

## Functional Omics
Beyond DNA sequencing, other "omics" provide insights into microbial activity:

| Method | Target | Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Metatranscriptomics** | **RNA** | Shows which genes are actively being expressed. |
| **Metaproteomics** | **Proteins** | Shows which enzymes and structural proteins are being produced. |
| **Metabolomics** | **Metabolites** | Measures small molecules (e.g., **SCFAs**) produced by the microbes. |

---

## Comparison: Culture vs. Sequencing

| Feature | Culture-Based | Sequencing-Based |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Detection** | Only viable, culturable microbes. | All microbes (living and dead). |
| **Breadth** | Limited to a few species at a time. | Thousands of species simultaneously. |
| **Functional Data** | High (can test antibiotic sensitivity). | Predicted from genetic potential. |
| **Cost/Time** | Generally cheaper; can be fast. | More expensive; requires bioinformatic analysis. |
