## Culturing Microorganisms
To study bacteria and fungi, they are grown in a **culture medium** (either liquid broth or solid agar).

### Growth Requirements
For successful growth, a medium must provide:
- **Nutrients:** Carbon, nitrogen, and minerals.
- **Oxygen:** Depending on whether the organism is an **aerobe** or **anaerobe**.
- **Moisture:** All microbes require water.
- **Optimum pH:** Most bacteria prefer a neutral pH (7.0).
- **Optimum Temperature:** Human pathogens are usually incubated at **37°C** (the body's core temperature). In school/college settings, they are often incubated at **25°C** to prevent the growth of dangerous human pathogens.

## Aseptic Technique
**Aseptic technique** is a set of practices used to prevent contamination by unwanted microorganisms.

| Practice | Purpose |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Flaming** | Passing inoculating loops and the necks of bottles through a Bunsen burner flame to kill any existing microbes. |
| **Disinfecting Surfaces** | Using 70% ethanol or bleach to remove surface microbes. |
| **Lid Handling** | Opening Petri dishes as little as possible and at an angle to block falling airborne microbes. |
| **Autoclaving** | Using high-pressure steam (121°C) to sterilise equipment and media by killing all living things, including **spores**. |
| **Personal Hygiene** | Wearing gloves, lab coats, and washing hands to prevent cross-contamination. |

## Measuring Microbial Growth
- **Total Count:** Counting all cells (living and dead) using a microscope and a specialised slide called a **haemocytometer**.
- **Viable Count:** Counting only living cells. This is usually done by performing a **serial dilution** and then plating the samples on agar. Each living cell develops into a visible **colony (CFU: Colony Forming Unit)**.
- **Turbidity:** Measuring the "cloudiness" of a liquid culture using a **colorimeter**; a cloudier culture indicates higher bacterial density.

## Controlling Pathogens: Treatment

### Antibiotics
**Antibiotics** are chemicals that kill or inhibit the growth of **bacteria**. They do NOT work on viruses.

| Mechanism | Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Cell Wall Inhibition** | Prevent the cross-linking of **murein** (e.g., Penicillin). This leads to osmotic lysis. |
| **Protein Synthesis Inhibition** | Target the **70S ribosomes** of bacteria to prevent protein production (e.g., Tetracycline). |
| **Nucleic Acid Inhibition** | Prevent DNA replication or RNA synthesis. |
| **Metabolic Inhibition** | Block specific enzymes needed for metabolism. |

### Antibiotic Resistance
Bacteria can develop resistance through **random mutations**. These resistant bacteria survive antibiotic treatment and reproduce, passing on the resistance genes via **plasmids**.
- **MRSA (Methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus):** A common "superbug" in hospitals.
- **Prevention:** Complete the full course of antibiotics; avoid over-prescribing; maintain high hygiene standards (hand-washing).

### Antivirals and Antifungals
- **Antivirals:** Target viral enzymes (e.g., Reverse Transcriptase inhibitors for HIV) or prevent viral attachment/release.
- **Antifungals:** Often target the fungal cell membrane (specifically **ergosterol**, which humans lack).
