## Overview
The **Gastrointestinal (GI) Tract** is a long, continuous tube that extends from the mouth to the anus. It is the body's largest surface area in contact with the external environment and contains the majority of the **human microbiome**.

---

## Microbiome Distribution in the GI Tract

The concentration and diversity of microorganisms increase as you move further down the tract:

| Region | Conditions | Microbial Load | Major Species |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Stomach** | High acidity (pH 2.0). | Very low. | *Helicobacter pylori* (pathobiont). |
| **Small Intestine** | Faster transit; presence of bile and enzymes. | Moderate. | *Lactobacilli*, *Streptococci*. |
| **Large Intestine (Colon)** | Slow transit; neutral pH; low oxygen. | **Highest** (billions per gram). | *Bacteroides*, *Bifidobacteria*, *Clostridia*. |

---

## Key Functions of the GI Microbiome
- **Metabolism:** Breaking down complex carbohydrates (e.g., fibre) into **Short-Chain Fatty Acids (SCFAs)** like **butyrate**.
- **Vitamin Synthesis:** Producing **vitamin K** and certain **B vitamins** (B12, biotin).
- **Immune Training:** Developing the body's **Mucosa-Associated Lymphoid Tissue (MALT)**.
- **Colonisation Resistance:** Preventing **pathogens** from attaching to the **mucous membrane**.

---

## Barriers to Infection in the GI Tract
The GI tract has multiple defense mechanisms:
1. **Gastric Acid:** The low pH of the **stomach** kills many swallowed pathogens.
2. **Mucous Layer:** A thick layer of **mucin** produced by **goblet cells** prevents direct contact with the epithelium.
3. **Peristalsis:** The movement of the tract physically flushes out potential pathogens.
4. **Digestive Enzymes:** Can break down the cell walls of certain bacteria.
