## Overview
**Aseptic technique** is a set of practices used in microbiology laboratories to prevent the contamination of samples, instruments, and work areas by unwanted microorganisms. It also protects the researcher from accidental infection by the microbes being studied.

---

## Key Aseptic Practices

| Practice | Procedure | Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Work Area Disinfection** | Wiping the bench with **70% ethanol** or disinfectant before and after work. | Removes surface microbes that could contaminate the sample. |
| **Flaming Loops** | Passing the inoculating loop through the **blue Bunsen burner flame** until it glows red. | Sterilises the tool by killing all bacteria and spores before use. |
| **Bottling** | Quickly passing the neck of a glass bottle through the flame after opening and before closing. | Creates an upward convection current to prevent falling microbes from entering the bottle. |
| **Petri Dish Handling** | Lifting the lid only at an angle and for the minimum time necessary. | Minimises exposure to airborne contaminants. |
| **Sterile Reagents** | Using media and equipment that has been **autoclaved** (at 121°C). | Ensures the starting environment is completely free of living microbes. |

---

## Personal Aseptic Practices
- **Hand Washing:** Using antibacterial soap before and after procedures.
- **Protective Gear:** Wearing a lab coat, gloves, and safety goggles.
- **Controlled Environment:** Working near a Bunsen burner (to create a sterile field) or in a **Laminar Flow Hood**.
- **Labeling:** Clearly marking all cultures with the date, organism name, and researcher's initials.

---

## Importance of Aseptic Technique
1. **Accurate Results:** Prevents the growth of "contaminants" that could lead to false-positive or misleading results.
2. **Safety:** Prevents the escape of potentially pathogenic bacteria into the environment.
3. **Purity:** Essential for maintaining a **pure culture** (a culture containing only one species of microorganism).
