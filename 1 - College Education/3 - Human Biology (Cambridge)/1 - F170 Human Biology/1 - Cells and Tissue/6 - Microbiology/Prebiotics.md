## Overview
**Prebiotics** are non-digestible food components (usually specialised plant fibres) that selectively stimulate the growth and activity of beneficial bacteria (e.g., *Bifidobacteria* and *Lactobacilli*) already residing in the human **gut**.

---

## Mechanism of Action
Prebiotics pass through the **upper gastrointestinal tract** undigested because humans lack the enzymes to break them down.
- Once they reach the **colon**, they are fermented by resident beneficial bacteria.
- This fermentation process produces **Short-Chain Fatty Acids (SCFAs)**, such as **butyrate**, **acetate**, and **propionate**.
- **SCFAs** lower the pH of the colon, creating an environment that is hostile to **pathogens** but ideal for beneficial microbes.

---

## Common Prebiotic Sources

| Prebiotic Type | Common Food Sources |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Inulin** | Chicory root, garlic, onions, asparagus. |
| **Fructooligosaccharides (FOS)** | Bananas, artichokes, leeks. |
| **Galactooligosaccharides (GOS)** | Legumes, beans, lentils. |
| **Resistant Starch** | Green bananas, cooked and cooled potatoes or rice. |

---

## Benefits of Prebiotics
1. **Improved Digestive Health:** Enhances regular bowel movements and prevents constipation.
2. **Enhanced Mineral Absorption:** Some prebiotics can improve the absorption of **calcium** and **magnesium**.
3. **Immune System Support:** By promoting a healthy microbiome, prebiotics strengthen the body's natural **colonisation resistance** against infections.
4. **Anti-inflammatory Effects:** **Butyrate** produced from prebiotics fuels the cells lining the colon (colonocytes) and helps maintain the **mucous membrane** barrier.
