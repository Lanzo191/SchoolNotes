## Fungi
**Fungi** are **eukaryotic** organisms that can be unicellular (e.g., yeast) or multicellular (e.g., moulds and mushrooms). They are **heterotrophs**, meaning they must obtain nutrients from other organisms (often by decomposition).

### Fungal Structure and Features
| Feature          | Details                                                            |
| :--------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Cell Wall**    | Made of **chitin** (not cellulose as in plants).                   |
| **Nucleus**      | Contains linear DNA associated with histones.                      |
| **Reproduction** | Primarily through **spores** (asexual or sexual).                  |
| **Hyphae**       | Long, branching filaments that form a network called **mycelium**. |

Sporangium 
- A sporangium is a structure in which spores are produced and stored, found in plants, fungi, and some algae. It can be single-celled or multicellular and plays a key role in reproduction.

Spores 
- Spores are reproductive cells produced by certain fungi, plants, and bacteria that can develop into new individuals without needing to fuse with another cell. They are typically single-celled and help organisms reproduce asexually and survive in harsh conditions

Hyphae
- A hypha is a long, branching structure of a fungus, which is essential for its growth and is part of a network called mycelium. Hyphae can be divided into cells by internal walls called septa and play a crucial role in nutrient absorption and reproduction in fungi.

Mycelium 
- Mycelium is a root-like structure of fungi made up of branching, thread-like filaments called hyphae. It plays a crucial role in nutrient absorption, decomposition, and forming symbiotic relationships with plants, enhancing their growth and health.

Septum (Not in diagram)
- A septum in fungi refers to a partition or wall that divides the hyphae, which are the thread-like structures of the fungus. Fungi can be classified as septate, having these internal walls, or nonseptate, lacking them, which affects their growth and structure.

### Pathogenic Fungi (Mycoses)
Fungi often cause opportunistic infections or superficial skin conditions:
- **Tinea Pedis (Athlete's Foot):** A fungal infection of the skin on the feet. (Trichophytum rubrum)
- **Candida Albicans (Thrush):** An opportunistic yeast infection that can affect the mouth, gut, or vagina.
- **Ringworm:** A common skin infection caused by several species of fungi.
- **Aspergillosis:** A respiratory infection caused by inhaling Aspergillus spores, often in immunocompromised individuals.

## Protozoa
**Protozoa** are unicellular **eukaryotic** organisms. They are highly diverse and can live in aquatic environments, soil, or as parasites within other organisms.

### Protozoan Features
| Feature | Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Locomotion** | Many use **flagella**, **cilia**, or **pseudopodia** (false feet) to move. |
| **Life Cycle** | Often complex, involving different stages and host organisms. |
| **Nutrition** | Most are heterotrophic, consuming organic matter or other microbes. |

---

### Pathogenic Protozoa and Vectors
Many protozoan diseases are transmitted through **vectors** (organisms that carry the pathogen between hosts).

| Pathogen | Disease | Vector / Transmission |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Plasmodium** | **Malaria** | **Anopheles Mosquito** (vector). Attacks red blood cells. |
| **Giardia Lamblia** | **Giardiasis** | Contaminated water or food (fecal-oral route). |
| **Trypanosoma** | **Sleeping Sickness** | **Tsetse Fly** (vector). Attacks the nervous system. |
| **Toxoplasma Gondii** | **Toxoplasmosis** | Contact with cat feces or undercooked meat. |
