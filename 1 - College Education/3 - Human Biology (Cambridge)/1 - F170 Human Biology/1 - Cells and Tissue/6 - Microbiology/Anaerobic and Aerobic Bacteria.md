## Overview
Bacteria can be classified based on their oxygen requirements for growth and metabolism. These requirements reflect the different ways bacteria produce energy (either through aerobic respiration, anaerobic respiration, or fermentation).

---

## Classification by Oxygen Use

| Type | Description | Key Feature | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Obligate Aerobe** | Needs oxygen to grow. | Uses oxygen as the final electron acceptor in aerobic respiration. | *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* |
| **Obligate Anaerobe** | Cannot grow in the presence of oxygen. | Oxygen is toxic to them because they lack enzymes to neutralise reactive oxygen species. | *Clostridium difficile* |
| **Facultative Anaerobe** | Can grow with or without oxygen. | They prefer oxygen for high-energy production but can switch to fermentation or anaerobic respiration when it is absent. | *Escherichia coli* |
| **Aerotolerant Anaerobe** | Can tolerate oxygen but does not use it. | They grow equally well whether oxygen is present or not, typically using fermentation. | *Streptococcus pyogenes* |
| **Microaerophile** | Requires oxygen at very low concentrations. | Too much oxygen is toxic, but a small amount is needed for growth. | *Helicobacter pylori* |

---

## Clinical Significance
Understanding the oxygen requirements of pathogens is essential for diagnosis and treatment:

- **Deep Tissue Infections:** **Obligate anaerobes** (e.g., *Clostridium*) thrive in deep wounds, the gut, or necrotic tissue where oxygen levels are extremely low. 
- **Respiratory Infections:** **Obligate aerobes** (e.g., *Mycobacterium*) often infect the **lungs** where oxygen is abundant.
- **Laboratory Culturing:** To grow anaerobes in a lab, specialised equipment such as **anaerobic jars** or chambers must be used to remove all oxygen from the environment.
