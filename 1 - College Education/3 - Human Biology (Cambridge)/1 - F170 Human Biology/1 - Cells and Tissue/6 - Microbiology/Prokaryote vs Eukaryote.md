## Overview
All living organisms are composed of either **prokaryotic** or **eukaryotic** cells. **Prokaryotes** are simpler, single-celled organisms (like bacteria), while **eukaryotes** are more complex and can be unicellular (like yeast) or multicellular (like humans).

---

## Key Differences Summary

| Feature | Prokaryotic Cells | Eukaryotic Cells |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Size** | Much smaller (**0.1 to 5 µm**). | Much larger (**10 to 100 µm**). |
| **Nucleus** | **Absent**. **DNA** is free in the cytoplasm. | **Present**. **DNA** is enclosed in a double membrane. |
| **DNA Structure** | **Circular DNA**; not associated with proteins. | **Linear DNA**; associated with **histone** proteins. |
| **Organelles** | **No membrane-bound organelles** (no mitochondria, ER, etc.). | **Many membrane-bound organelles** present. |
| **Ribosomes** | Smaller (**70S**). | Larger (**80S**). |
| **Cell Wall** | Always present; made of **murein (peptidoglycan)**. | Present in plants (**cellulose**) and fungi (**chitin**); absent in animals. |
| **Reproduction** | **Binary Fission** (asexual). | **Mitosis** and **Meiosis**. |
| **Plasmids** | Often present (extra loops of **DNA**). | Rarely present (except in some fungi or protists). |
| **Flagella** | Simple; made of the protein **flagellin**; rotates. | Complex; made of **microtubules** (9+2 arrangement); whips. |

## Detailed Structural Comparison

### Genetic Material
- **Prokaryotes:** The **DNA** is found in a region called the **nucleoid**. It consists of one main circular chromosome and often smaller loops called **plasmids**, which can carry genes for antibiotic resistance.
- **Eukaryotes:** **DNA** is organized into multiple linear chromosomes within the **nucleus**. It is tightly wrapped around **histone** proteins to form chromatin.

### Organelles and Metabolism
- **Prokaryotes:** Lack mitochondria; instead, they use infoldings of the cell membrane (once thought to be **mesosomes**) or enzymes in the cytoplasm for respiration.
- **Eukaryotes:** Have specialized organelles like **mitochondria** for aerobic respiration and **chloroplasts** (in plants or algae) for photosynthesis. This allows for compartmentalization of chemical reactions.

### Cell Walls
- **Prokaryotes:** The cell wall is a complex network of **murein (peptidoglycan)**, which provides structural support and prevents osmotic lysis.
- **Eukaryotes:** Animal cells have **no cell wall**, which allows for greater mobility and flexibility. Fungi use **chitin**, and plants use **cellulose**.

---

## Similarities (What they share)
Despite their differences, both cell types share certain fundamental features:
- **Plasma Membrane:** A phospholipid bilayer that controls the entry and exit of substances.
- **Cytoplasm:** The jelly-like substance where metabolic reactions occur.
- **Ribosomes:** Sites of protein synthesis (though they differ in size).
- **DNA:** The genetic blueprint for the cell.
