## Overview
**Pili** (singular: **pilus**) are hair-like appendages found on the surface of many **prokaryotic cells** (bacteria). They are composed of a protein called **pilin** and are primarily involved in attachment and genetic exchange.

---

## Types of Pili

### 1. Common Pili (Fimbriae)
- **Structure:** Short, thin, and numerous (up to hundreds per cell).
- **Function:** Primarily used for **attachment** to surfaces or other cells.
- **Virulence:** They are a key **virulence factor** because they allow **pathogens** to adhere to host tissues (e.g., the lining of the urinary tract), preventing them from being washed away by fluids.

### 2. Sex Pili (Conjugation Pili)
- **Structure:** Longer and thicker than fimbriae, usually only one or two per cell.
- **Function:** Facilitate **conjugation**, a form of **horizontal gene transfer**.
- **Process:** The sex pilus from a "donor" cell (F+) attaches to a "recipient" cell (F-) and pulls them together, allowing the transfer of a **plasmid** (often containing **antibiotic resistance** genes).

---

## Comparison: Pili vs. Flagella

| Feature | Pili / Fimbriae | Flagella |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Size** | Short and thin. | Long and thick. |
| **Number** | Often hundreds (fimbriae). | Usually one or a few. |
| **Protein** | **Pilin**. | **Flagellin**. |
| **Main Function** | **Attachment** and **Conjugation**. | **Motility** (movement). |
| **Movement** | Non-rotatory. | Rotatory (spinning like a propeller). |
