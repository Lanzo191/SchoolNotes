## Overview
The **mucous membrane** (or **mucosa**) is a protective lining that covers all body cavities that are open to the external environment. It is a critical component of the body's **innate immune system**, serving as both a physical and chemical barrier against **pathogens**.

---

## Structure and Features
The mucous membrane consists of several layers:
- **Epithelium:** The outermost layer, which varies in structure (e.g., squamous vs. columnar) depending on its location.
- **Goblet Cells:** Specialised cells that produce and secrete **mucin**, a thick glycoprotein that forms **mucus**.
- **Lamina Propria:** A layer of connective tissue supporting the epithelium, containing blood vessels and immune cells (e.g., **MALT**).

---

## Key Mucous Membranes by Body Site

| Location | Epithelial Type | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Oral Cavity** | Stratified squamous. | Protects against mechanical friction; secretion of saliva. |
| **Nasal Passage** | Ciliated columnar. | Warms, humidifies, and filters air; traps dust and microbes in mucus. |
| **Trachea** | Pseudo-stratified ciliated columnar. | Moves trapped debris upwards via the **mucociliary escalator** to be swallowed. |
| **GI Tract** | Simple columnar. | Secretes enzymes and absorbs nutrients; high density of **goblet cells**. |
| **Vagina** | Stratified squamous. | Protects against pathogens; maintained at a low pH by resident **Lactobacillus**. |

---

## Role in Immunity
The mucous membrane acts as a barrier to infection in several ways:
1. **Mechanical Trapping:** Pathogens are physically stuck in the sticky **mucus**.
2. **Ciliary Action:** In the respiratory tract, **cilia** (hair-like projections) wave in a coordinated fashion to sweep mucus away from the **lungs**.
3. **Chemical Defence:** Mucus contains antimicrobial substances like **lysozyme** (which breaks down bacterial cell walls) and **Secretory IgA** (an antibody).
4. **Resident Microbiome:** Beneficial microbes on the mucosa compete with pathogens for space and nutrients (**colonisation resistance**).
