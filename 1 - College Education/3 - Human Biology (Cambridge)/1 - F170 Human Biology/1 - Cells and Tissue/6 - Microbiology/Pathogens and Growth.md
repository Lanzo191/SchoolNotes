## Overview
A **pathogen** is any microorganism that can cause disease. The primary groups are **Bacteria**, **Viruses**, **Fungi**, and **Protozoa**. **Pathogenicity** describes the ability of an organism to cause disease, while **virulence** refers to the degree of damage or severity.

## Pathogen Characteristics Summary

| Type | Structure | Key Feature |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **[[Prokaryotic Cells\|Bacteria]]** | Prokaryotic; **murein** cell wall; circular **DNA**. | Produce **toxins** or damage cells by rapid reproduction. |
| **[[Viruses]]** | Acellular; genetic material (**DNA** or **RNA**) in a **capsid**. | **Obligate parasites**; hijack host machinery to replicate. |
| **[[Fungi and Protozoa\|Fungi]]** | Eukaryotic; cell walls of **chitin**. | Reproduce via spores; cause superficial or systemic infections. |
| **[[Fungi and Protozoa\|Protozoa]]** | Unicellular eukaryotes; often complex life cycles. | Often transmitted by **vectors** (e.g., mosquitoes). |

## Methods of Transmission
Pathogens can be spread in several ways:
- **Direct Contact:** Skin-to-skin contact, sexual transmission (e.g., **HIV**, **STIs**).
- **Indirect Contact:** Touching contaminated surfaces (**fomites**), such as door handles or shared equipment.
- **Aerosol (Airborne):** Droplets released through coughing, sneezing, or breathing (e.g., **Influenza**, **SARS-CoV-2**).
- **Fecal-Oral Route:** Consuming contaminated food or water (e.g., **Cholera**, **Norovirus**).
- **Vector-borne:** Transmission via animals or insects (e.g., **Malaria**, **Lyme disease**).
- **Vertical Transmission:** From mother to child during pregnancy, birth, or breastfeeding.

## Bacterial Growth Curve
Bacteria reproduce via **Binary Fission**. Under optimal conditions, their population follows a characteristic growth curve:

| Phase | Description | Why? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Lag Phase** | Slow growth as bacteria adjust to environment. | Enzymes are being synthesized for the new nutrient source. |
| **Log (Exponential) Phase** | Rapid cell division. | Abundant nutrients and space; low accumulation of waste. In theory exponential Growth. |
| **Stationary Phase** | Rate of cell division = Rate of death. | Nutrients are limited; waste products become toxic. |
| **Death (Decline) Phase** | Population decreases. | Lack of nutrients and high toxicity leads to massive cell death. |

### Bacterial Growth Calculations
- **Number of cells** = Initial population x $2^n$ (where $n$ is the number of divisions).
- **Number of divisions** = $\log_2(\text{Final Number of cells} / \text{Initial population})$.
