## Overview
**Viruses** are non-living, acellular infectious agents. They are much smaller than [[Prokaryotic Cells|bacteria]] (20 to 300 nm) and lack the machinery for metabolism and reproduction. They are **obligate intracellular parasites**, meaning they can only replicate by hijacking a host cell.

## Viral Structure
Viruses consist of genetic material surrounded by a protective protein coat.

| Structure | Description | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Genetic Material** | Either **DNA** or **RNA** (can be single or double-stranded). | Carries the instructions for making new viral particles. |
| **Capsid** | A protein coat made of subunits called **capsomeres**. | Protects the genetic material; determines the shape of the virus. |
| **Envelope** | A lipid bilayer derived from the host cell membrane (only in some viruses). | Helps the virus evade the immune system and fuse with host cells. |
| **Glycoproteins** | Spike proteins on the surface or envelope. | Allow the virus to attach to specific receptors on the host cell membrane. |
| **Enzymes** | Some viruses carry enzymes (e.g., **Reverse Transcriptase** in retroviruses). | Aids in the replication of the viral genome within the host. |

## Viral Replication
Viruses do not undergo **Binary Fission**. Instead, they replicate through a specific cycle:

1. **Attachment:** The virus binds to specific receptors on the surface of the host cell.
2. **Penetration:** The virus or its genetic material enters the host cell.
3. **Uncoating:** The viral capsid is removed, releasing the genetic material into the host's cytoplasm or nucleus.
4. **Biosynthesis:** The host cell's machinery (ribosomes, enzymes) is forced to produce viral nucleic acids and proteins.
5. **Assembly (Maturation):** New viral particles are assembled from the produced components.
6. **Release:** New viruses leave the host cell via **lysis** (bursting and killing the cell) or **budding** (pinching off the membrane, often taking an envelope).

## Retroviruses (e.g., HIV)
**Retroviruses** are a special class of RNA viruses.
- They use the enzyme **Reverse Transcriptase** to convert their RNA into DNA.
- This viral DNA is then integrated into the host cell's genome by the enzyme **Integrase**.
- Once integrated, it is known as a **provirus** and can remain dormant for years.

## Comparison: Viruses vs. Bacteria

| Feature | Viruses | Bacteria ([[Prokaryotic Cells\|Prokaryotes]]) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Size** | 20 to 300 nm (Tiny) | 1 to 5 µm (Small) |
| **Living?** | No (Non-living) | Yes (Living) |
| **Structure** | Acellular (DNA/RNA + Capsid) | Unicellular (Cytoplasm, Ribosomes, Wall) |
| **Reproduction** | Hijacks host machinery | **Binary Fission** |
| **Treatment** | **Antivirals** (Antibiotics do NOT work) | **Antibiotics** |
| **Genetic Material** | DNA or RNA | DNA (Circular) |

## Common Viral Pathogens
- **HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus):** Attacks T-helper cells, leading to **[[Immunity\|AIDS]]**.
- **Influenza:** Respiratory virus that undergoes rapid mutation (antigenic drift).
- **Hepatitis:** Attacks the [[Liver]], causing inflammation and damage.
- **Norovirus:** Highly contagious virus causing gastroenteritis (vomiting or diarrhea).
- **SARS-CoV-2:** The virus responsible for COVID-19.
