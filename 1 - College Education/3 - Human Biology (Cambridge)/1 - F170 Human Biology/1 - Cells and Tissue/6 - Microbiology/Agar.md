## Overview
**Agar** (or **agar-agar**) is a gelatinous substance used in microbiology to solidify **culture media**. It is derived from the cell walls of **red algae** (seaweed) and is highly valued for its unique physical properties.

---

## Properties of Agar

| Property | Description | Benefit for Microbiology |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Solidification** | It is a powder that forms a gel when mixed with water and cooled. | Provides a stable surface for **colony** formation. |
| **Hysteresis** | Melts at **85°C** and solidifies at **32 to 40°C**. | Allows nutrients and bacteria to be added at temperatures that won't kill them. |
| **Metabolic Inertness** | Most bacteria cannot digest or break down agar. | The medium remains solid even when colonized by dense bacterial growth. |
| **Transparency** | It is generally clear. | Allows for easy observation of bacterial colonies and morphological features. |

---

## Use of Agar in Microbiology
Agar is typically used at a concentration of **1.5%** in **Petri dishes** to create plates.
- **Inoculation:** Bacteria are spread across the surface of the agar.
- **Colony Formation:** A single bacterium divides multiple times by **binary fission** to form a visible **colony** (CFU: Colony Forming Unit).
- **Isolation:** By using a **streak plate** technique, individual colonies can be isolated from a mixed sample to create a pure culture.

---

## Common Agar Formats

- **Agar Plates:** Used for isolation and counting.
- **Agar Slants:** Agar solidified at an angle in a test tube. Used for long-term storage of cultures.
- **Agar Deeps:** Solidified in a vertical tube. Used to test for oxygen requirements (**aerobes** vs. **anaerobes**).
