## 1. Definition
- Osmosis is the **net movement of water molecules** across a **partially permeable membrane** from a region of **higher water potential (Ψ)** to a region of **lower water potential (Ψ)**.  
- Passive process → does **not** require [[ATP]].  

---

## 2. Water Potential (Ψ)
- Measured in **kilopascals (kPa)**.  
- Pure water: Ψ = 0 kPa (highest possible value).  
- Adding solutes → lowers water potential (more negative).  
- Water always moves towards the **more negative Ψ**.  

---

## 3. Membrane Permeability
- Cell membranes are **partially permeable**:  
  - Allow water molecules through (via aquaporins or directly through bilayer).  
  - Restrict many solutes.  
- Osmosis depends on solute concentrations inside vs outside the cell.  

---

## 4. Effects on Animal Cells
- **Hypotonic solution (higher Ψ outside):** Water enters → cell swells → risk of lysis (bursting).  
- **Isotonic solution (equal Ψ):** No net water movement → cell remains stable.  
- **Hypertonic solution (lower Ψ outside):** Water leaves → cell shrinks → crenation.  

---

## 5. Osmosis in Context
- Important for:  
  - **Absorption of water** in the gut.  
  - **Exchange of fluids** between blood plasma and tissue fluid.  
  - **Kidney function** (reabsorption in nephron).  

---

## 6. Experimental Investigation
- Use of **potato or beetroot cylinders** in sucrose solutions of varying concentration.  
  - Mass gain = water entered (solution less concentrated than cytoplasm).  
  - Mass loss = water left (solution more concentrated).  
- Plotting % change in mass against solution concentration → point where curve crosses 0 = isotonic solution (Ψ of tissue).  

---

## 7. Key Exam Points
- Always state **direction of water movement** relative to Ψ gradient.  
- Remember: water potential values are always **negative** unless pure water.  
- Animal vs plant responses differ → animal cells risk lysis, plant cells become turgid.
