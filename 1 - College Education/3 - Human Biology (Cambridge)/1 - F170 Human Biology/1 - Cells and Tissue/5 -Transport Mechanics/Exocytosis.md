## 1. Definition
- Exocytosis is the **bulk transport of material out of the cell** via vesicles that fuse with the plasma membrane.  
- Requires **[[ATP]]** for vesicle transport and membrane fusion.  

---

## 2. Mechanism
1. Substances are packaged into vesicles by the Golgi apparatus or endoplasmic reticulum.  
2. Vesicles move along [[Eukaryotic Cells Simplified|cytoskeleton]] tracks (microtubules) using motor proteins.  
3. Vesicle membrane fuses with plasma membrane.  
4. Contents released into extracellular space.  

---

## 3. Functions
- **Secretion of substances:**  
  - Hormones (e.g. insulin from pancreatic cells).  
  - Neurotransmitters (e.g. acetylcholine from nerve terminals).  
- **Waste removal:** Expels unwanted materials or toxins.  
- **Insertion of membrane proteins/lipids:** Vesicles add new proteins and phospholipids to plasma membrane.  

---

## 4. Types of Exocytosis
- **Constitutive exocytosis:** Continuous, e.g. secretion of antibodies from plasma cells.  
- **Regulated exocytosis:** Triggered by signals (e.g. Ca²⁺ influx triggers neurotransmitter release).  

---

## 5. Biological Examples
- **Nerve synapses:** Vesicles release neurotransmitters into synaptic cleft.  
- **Endocrine glands:** Hormones released into blood.  
- **Exocrine glands:** Enzyme secretion (e.g. pancreatic amylase).  

---

## 6. Key Exam Points
- Requires **[[ATP]]**.  
- Vesicles formed by **[[Golgi Apparatus]]** often.  
- Can be constitutive or regulated.  
- Essential for **[[Cell Signalling]], secretion, and waste removal**.
