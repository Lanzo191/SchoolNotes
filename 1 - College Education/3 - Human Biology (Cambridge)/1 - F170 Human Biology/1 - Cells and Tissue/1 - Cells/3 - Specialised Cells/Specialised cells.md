
Specialised cells are [[Eukaryotic Cells Simplified]] adapted to perform specific functions in the human body.  

---

## 1. Sperm Cell
- **Structure**: 
  - Flagellum for motility.  
  - Midpiece packed with mitochondria.  
  - Acrosome with hydrolytic enzymes.  
  - Haploid nucleus.  
- **Functions**:  
  - Fertilise egg by delivering paternal DNA.  
  - Penetrate egg membranes (zona pellucida).  
  - Activate egg metabolism after fertilisation.  

---

## 2. Egg Cell (Ovum)
- **Structure**:  
  - Large cytoplasm with nutrients.  
  - Zona pellucida protective glycoprotein layer.  
  - Cortical granules.  
- **Functions**:  
  - Female gamete.  
  - Supports early embryo development.  
  - Prevents polyspermy after fertilisation.  

---

## 3. Erythrocyte (Red Blood Cell)
- **Structure**:  
  - Biconcave disc shape.  
  - No nucleus/organelles.  
  - Packed with haemoglobin.  
- **Functions**:  
  - Transport oxygen bound to haemoglobin.  
  - Transport some CO₂.  
  - Maintain blood pH (buffering).  

---

## 4. Neutrophil
- **Structure**:  
  - Multi-lobed nucleus.  
  - Many lysosomes with hydrolytic enzymes.  
- **Functions**:  
  - Phagocytosis of bacteria/fungi.  
  - Release cytokines to recruit immune cells.  
  - First line of defence (innate immunity).  

---

## 5. Lymphocyte
- **Structure**:  
  - Large round nucleus, little cytoplasm.  
- **Functions**:  
  - B cells: produce antibodies.  
  - T helper cells: coordinate immune response.  
  - Cytotoxic T cells: destroy infected/cancerous cells.  
  - Provide immunological memory.  

---

## 6. Eosinophil
- **Structure**:  
  - Bilobed nucleus.  
  - Granules containing toxic proteins.  
- **Functions**:  
  - Defend against parasitic infections.  
  - Modulate allergic responses and inflammation.  

---

## 7. Monocyte
- **Structure**:  
  - Largest WBC.  
  - Kidney-shaped nucleus.  
  - Differentiate into macrophages in tissues.  
- **Functions**:  
  - Phagocytosis of pathogens and debris.  
  - Antigen presentation to lymphocytes.  
  - Secrete cytokines to activate immune responses.  

---

## 8. Neurone
- **Structure**:  
  - Dendrites for receiving signals.  
  - Long axon for impulse transmission.  
  - Myelin sheath (many neurones) with nodes of Ranvier.  
- **Functions**:  
  - Transmit electrical impulses.  
  - Facilitate rapid communication.  
  - Integrate sensory, motor, and cognitive functions.  

---

## 9. Hepatocyte (Liver Cell)
- **Structure**:  
  - Polygonal shape, many mitochondria.  
  - Extensive smooth ER.  
  - Glycogen granules.  
- **Functions**:  
  - Metabolism of carbohydrates, proteins, lipids.  
  - Detoxification of harmful substances.  
  - Bile production.  
  - Storage of glycogen, vitamins, and minerals.  

---

## 10. Renal Tubule Epithelial Cell
- **Structure**:  
  - Microvilli (brush border) for large surface area.  
  - Many mitochondria.  
- **Functions**:  
  - Reabsorption of water, [[Carbohydrates|glucose]], and ions.  
  - Secretion of waste into filtrate.  
  - Maintain acid–base and electrolyte balance.  

---

## 11. Rods and Cones (Photoreceptor Cells)
- **Structure**:  
  - Outer segments contain photopigments (rhodopsin in rods, iodopsins in cones).  
  - Synaptic terminals connect to bipolar cells.  
- **Functions**:  
  - Rods: sensitive to low light (black/white vision).  
  - Cones: colour vision, high visual acuity.  

---

## 12. Ciliated Epithelial Cell
- **Structure**:  
  - Lined with motile cilia.  
  - Interspersed with goblet cells (mucus secretion).  
- **Functions**:  
  - Move mucus containing dust/pathogens out of lungs.  
  - Protect respiratory tract from infection.  

---

## 13. Squamous Epithelial Cell
- **Structure**:  
  - Thin, flat cells.  
  - Form single layer (e.g., alveoli, capillaries).  
- **Functions**:  
  - Provide short diffusion path.  
  - Allow rapid gas exchange.  
  - Reduce friction in vessels (endothelium).  

---

## 14. Skeletal/Striated Muscle Cell
- **Structure**:  
  - Long multinucleate fibres.  
  - Striated with actin and myosin.  
  - Many mitochondria.  
- **Functions**:  
  - Voluntary contraction for movement.  
  - Maintain posture and joint stability.  
  - Generate heat during shivering.  

---

## 15. Gastric Pit Epithelial Cells
- **Structure**:  
  - Line [[Stomach]] mucosa.  
  - Include specialised secretory cell types.  
- **Functions**:  
  - Mucous cells: secrete mucus → protect lining.  
  - Chief cells: secrete pepsinogen.  
  - Parietal cells: secrete hydrochloric acid and intrinsic factor.
