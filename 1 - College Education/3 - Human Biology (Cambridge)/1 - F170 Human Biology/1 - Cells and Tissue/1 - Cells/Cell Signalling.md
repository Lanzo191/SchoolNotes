**Cell signalling** is the process by which cells communicate with each other to coordinate activities. This usually involves a **signalling molecule** (ligand) binding to a **receptor** on or in a target cell.

## Types of Signalling
- **Endocrine**: Signals (Hormones) travel through the bloodstream to distant cells.
- **Paracrine**: Signals affect nearby cells (e.g., neurotransmitters at a synapse).
- **Autocrine**: A cell signals to itself.

## The Signalling Process
1. **Reception**: A signalling molecule (ligand) binds to a specific, complementary **receptor**.
2. **Transduction**: The binding triggers a change in the receptor, which initiates a cascade of chemical reactions inside the cell.
3. **Response**: The cell undergoes a specific change (e.g., activating an enzyme, opening an ion channel, or changing gene expression).

---

## Cell Surface Receptors (Water-Soluble Signals)
Peptide hormones like **[[Insulin]]** or **[[Adrenaline]]** cannot pass through the lipid bilayer.
- They bind to receptors on the **cell surface membrane**.
- This activates a **G-protein** or enzyme (like adenylyl cyclase).
- A **second messenger** (e.g., **cAMP**) is produced inside the cell.
- The second messenger activates a cascade of enzymes (phosphorylation cascade) to produce a rapid response.

## Intracellular Receptors (Lipid-Soluble Signals)
Steroid hormones like **[[Cortisol]]** or **Oestrogen** can diffuse through the membrane.
- They bind to receptors in the **cytoplasm** or **[[Nucleus]]**.
- The hormone-receptor complex acts as a **[[Transcription]] factor**, binding to DNA and turning specific genes on or off.
- This causes a slower but longer-lasting response.

---

## Importance of Cell Signalling
- **Homeostasis**: Coordinating the body's response to changes (e.g., blood glucose regulation).
- **Growth and Development**: Telling cells when to divide or differentiate.
- **Immunity**: Allowing immune cells to recognize and respond to pathogens.
