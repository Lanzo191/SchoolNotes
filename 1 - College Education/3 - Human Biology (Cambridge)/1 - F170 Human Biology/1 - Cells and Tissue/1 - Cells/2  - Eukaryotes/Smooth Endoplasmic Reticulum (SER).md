#Biology #CellBiology #EukaryoticCells #Organelle  

The **smooth endoplasmic reticulum (SER)** is a network of membrane tubules lacking [[Ribosomes]]. It is involved in lipid synthesis, steroid production, detoxification, and [[Calcium]] storage. Its structure is continuous with the rough endoplasmic reticulum, forming a single, dynamic system within the cytoplasm.

## Structure
- **Tubular Network**: Interconnected membranes forming an extensive system of smooth tubules and vesicles.  
- **No [[Ribosomes]]**: The absence of [[Ribosomes]] gives it a smooth appearance and differentiates it from the RER.  
- **Lumen**: Internal compartment for enzymatic activity related to lipid and carbohydrate metabolism.  

## Function
- **Lipid and Steroid Synthesis**: Produces phospholipids, cholesterol, and steroid hormones such as testosterone and estrogen.  
- **Detoxification**: Contains enzymes that metabolise toxins, drugs, and metabolic by-products.  
- **Carbohydrate Metabolism**: In [[Liver]] cells, the SER helps regulate blood [[Glucose]] by converting glycogen to [[Glucose]] (glycogenolysis).  
- **[[Calcium]] Storage**: In muscle cells, known as the sarcoplasmic reticulum, it stores [[Calcium]] ions released during muscle contraction.  
- **Membrane Formation**: Supplies lipids to the [[Golgi Apparatus]], plasma membrane, and other organelles.
