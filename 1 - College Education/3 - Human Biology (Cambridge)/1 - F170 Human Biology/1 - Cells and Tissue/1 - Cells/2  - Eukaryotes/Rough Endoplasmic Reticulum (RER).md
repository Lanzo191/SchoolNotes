#Biology #CellBiology #EukaryoticCells #Organelle  

The **rough endoplasmic reticulum (RER)** is a network of flattened membrane sacs (cisternae) studded with [[Ribosomes]]. It plays a crucial role in [[Protein Synthesis]], folding, and transport within the cell. The RER is continuous with the nuclear envelope, allowing for efficient coordination between [[Transcription]] and [[Translation]].

## Structure
- **Cisternae**: Flattened, interconnected membrane sacs forming an extensive internal network.  
- **[[Ribosomes]]**: 80S [[Ribosomes]] are attached to the cytoplasmic surface of the membrane, giving the rough appearance.  
- **Lumen**: The internal space where newly synthesised [[Proteins]] enter for folding and modification.  
- **Continuity**: The RER membrane is continuous with the outer membrane of the [[Nucleus]], enabling direct transport of [[mRNA]] and ribosomal subunits.  

## Function
- **[[Protein Synthesis]]**: [[Ribosomes]] on the RER translate [[mRNA]] into polypeptide chains.  
- **Protein Folding and Modification**: Polypeptides enter the lumen for folding into functional 3D structures and [[Post-Translational Modifications]] such as glycosylation.  
- **Transport**: [[Proteins]] are packaged into transport vesicles that bud off the RER and move to the [[Golgi Apparatus]] for further processing.  
- **Membrane Production**: Synthesises membrane-bound [[Proteins]] and phospholipids for incorporation into cellular membranes.
