#Biology #CellBiology #EukaryoticCells #Organelle  

The **Golgi apparatus** (also called the Golgi complex or Golgi body) is an organelle responsible for modifying, sorting, and packaging macromolecules such as [[Proteins]] and lipids for secretion or use within the cell. It functions as the central hub for post-translational processing and vesicular trafficking.

## Structure
- **Cisternae**: A stack of flattened, membrane-bound sacs arranged in a curved formation consisting of cis, medial, and trans faces.  
- **Cis Face**: The receiving side, oriented toward the endoplasmic reticulum, receives vesicles containing newly synthesised [[Proteins]].  
- **Trans Face**: The shipping side, facing the plasma membrane; vesicles bud off here to transport substances to their destinations.  
- **Associated Vesicles**: Small transport vesicles carry molecules between cisternae and to other organelles.  

## Function
- **Modification of [[Proteins]]**: Adds carbohydrate chains to form glycoproteins or phosphate groups for activation.  
- **Sorting and Packaging**: Directs modified [[Proteins]] and lipids into vesicles for secretion, lysosomal targeting, or plasma membrane incorporation.  
- **Lysosome Production**: Synthesises [[Lysosomes]] containing hydrolytic enzymes.  
- **Secretion**: Packages secretory [[Proteins]] such as hormones and enzymes into vesicles that fuse with the plasma membrane.  
- **Membrane Renewal**: Provides new membrane material for [[Endocytosis]] and [[Exocytosis]].
