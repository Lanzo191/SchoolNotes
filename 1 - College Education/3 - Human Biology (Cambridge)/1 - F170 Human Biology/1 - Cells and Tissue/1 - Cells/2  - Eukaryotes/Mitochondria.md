#Biology #CellBiology #EukaryoticCells #Organelle  

**Mitochondria** are double-membraned organelles known as the powerhouses of the cell. They are the primary site of aerobic respiration, producing [[ATP]] - the universal energy currency of the cell. Mitochondria play key roles in metabolism, apoptosis, and [[Calcium]] ion regulation.

## Structure
- **Double Membrane**: The outer membrane is smooth and permeable to small molecules and ions due to embedded [[Proteins]] called porins. The inner membrane is folded into structures called cristae, increasing the surface area for enzymatic reactions involved in respiration.  
- **Intermembrane Space**: The compartment between the inner and outer membranes where hydrogen ions (H⁺) accumulate during electron transport, creating the proton gradient essential for [[ATP]] synthesis.  
- **Matrix**: The innermost compartment containing enzymes, mitochondrial [[DNA]], 70S [[Ribosomes]], and substrates for the Krebs cycle.  
- **Mitochondrial [[DNA]] (mtDNA)**: Circular [[DNA]] molecule encoding [[Proteins]] required for mitochondrial function and replication, supporting the endosymbiotic theory of their origin.

## Function
- **[[ATP]] Production**: Site of the electron transport chain and oxidative phosphorylation, converting energy from [[Glucose]] and fatty acids into [[ATP]].  
- **[[Cellular Respiration]]**: Involves the link reaction, Krebs cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation.  
- **Apoptosis Regulation**: Releases cytochrome c to activate programmed cell death pathways.  
- **[[Calcium]] Storage**: Helps maintain [[Calcium]] ion balance within the cytoplasm.  
- **Self-Replication**: Mitochondria can replicate independently of the cell due to their own [[DNA]] and [[Ribosomes]].
