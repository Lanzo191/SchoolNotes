#Biology #CellBiology #EukaryoticCells #Organelle  

The **cytoskeleton** is a dynamic network of protein filaments that provides structural support, organisation, and movement within the cell. It is essential for maintaining cell shape, intracellular transport, and cell division.

## Structure
- **Microfilaments (Actin Filaments)**: Thin filaments composed of actin, involved in cell shape and movement.  
- **Microtubules**: Hollow tubes made of tubulin that maintain cell shape and serve as tracks for vesicle and organelle movement.  
- **Intermediate Filaments**: Rope-like structures providing tensile strength and mechanical stability.  
- **Motor [[Proteins]]**:  
  - Kinesin and dynein transport vesicles along microtubules.  
  - Myosin interacts with actin for cellular contraction.  

## Function
- **Structural Support**: Maintains cell shape and resists deformation.  
- **Intracellular Transport**: Moves vesicles, organelles, and [[Chromosomes]] within the cell.  
- **Cell Division**: Forms the mitotic spindle that separates [[Chromosomes]] during [[Mitosis]].  
- **Cell Motility**: Enables movement through cilia, flagella, and actin-based extensions such as pseudopodia.  
- **Signal Transduction**: Links external signals to internal responses by anchoring receptors.
