**Lysosomes** are spherical, membrane-bound vesicles containing hydrolytic enzymes that digest macromolecules, old organelles, and pathogens. They are formed by the [[Golgi Apparatus]] and play a crucial role in intracellular digestion and waste recycling.

## Structure
- **Single Membrane**: Maintains acidic conditions (pH ~5) using proton pumps to support enzyme activity.  
- **Enzymes**: Contain over 50 types of hydrolytic enzymes, including proteases, lipases, nucleases, and glycosidases.  
- **Origin**: Derived from the trans face of the [[Golgi Apparatus]].  

## Function
- **Digestion**: Break down large biomolecules including [[Proteins]], lipids, polysaccharides, and nucleic acids.  
- **Autophagy**: Degrades damaged or obsolete organelles for recycling.  
- **Autolysis**: In some cases, release of lysosomal enzymes leads to controlled cell destruction.  
- **Pathogen Destruction**: Fuses with phagosomes containing bacteria or debris in immune cells.  
- **Waste Management**: Recycles cellular components to maintain homeostasis.
