#Biology #CellBiology #EukaryoticCells #Organelle  

**Ribosomes** are small, non-membrane-bound structures responsible for [[Protein Synthesis]]. They are composed of ribosomal [[RNA]] ([[rRNA]]) and [[Proteins]], functioning as molecular machines that translate [[mRNA]] into polypeptide chains. Ribosomes can exist freely in the cytoplasm or attached to the rough endoplasmic reticulum.

## Structure
- **Subunits**: Consist of a large and a small subunit; 80S in eukaryotes (60S + 40S) and 70S in prokaryotes and [[Mitochondria]]/chloroplasts.  
- **Composition**: Each subunit is made of [[rRNA]] and associated structural [[Proteins]].  
- **Location**:  
  - Free ribosomes synthesise [[Proteins]] used within the cytoplasm.  
  - Bound ribosomes synthesise [[Proteins]] destined for membranes or secretion.  

## Function
- **[[Protein Synthesis]]**: Translate the nucleotide sequence of [[mRNA]] into amino acid sequences through [[Translation]].  
- **Polyribosomes**: Multiple ribosomes can attach to one [[mRNA]] strand to form a polysome, increasing efficiency.  
- **Intracellular Targeting**: [[Proteins]] synthesised by ribosomes are directed to specific destinations based on signal sequences within the polypeptide.
