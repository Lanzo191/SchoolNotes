#Biology #HumanBiology #DigestiveSystem #Microvilli #OCR  

**Microvilli** are microscopic finger-like projections on the apical surface of [[Epithelial cells]] lining the villi of the [[Small Intestine]]. They form the **brush border**, further increasing the absorptive surface area.

## Structure
- **Surface area**: Each epithelial cell has thousands of microvilli, which multiply the surface area of each villus significantly.  
- **Brush border enzymes**: Embedded in the membrane are enzymes such as maltase, lactase, sucrase, and peptidases, which complete the chemical digestion of [[Carbohydrates]] and peptides at the epithelial surface.  
- **[[Cytoskeleton]] support**: Actin filaments provide structural stability to microvilli, maintaining their shape during intestinal movements.  

## Function
- Maximises the surface area for nutrient absorption.  
- Completes chemical digestion at the site of absorption, allowing monosaccharides, [[Amino Acids]], and small peptides to be absorbed immediately.  
- Facilitates rapid uptake of nutrients due to the short diffusion distance into [[Capillaries]] and lacteals.  
- Works in conjunction with villi to maintain efficient nutrient transport into the blood and lymph.
