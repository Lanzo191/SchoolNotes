
## 1. Introduction
- Cell membranes (plasma membranes) form the boundary of all living cells and many organelles.  
- Function as **selectively permeable barriers**, controlling movement of substances in and out.  
- Provide surfaces for **[[Cell Signalling]], recognition, and metabolic reactions**.  
- Average thickness: ~7–10 nm (visible only with electron microscopes).  

---

## 2. Historical Models
- **1890s – Lipid coat model:** Membranes thought to be simple lipid layers.  
- **1935 – Davson–Danielli model:** Suggested a phospholipid bilayer coated on both sides with proteins (“protein-sandwich model”).  
- **1972 – Singer & Nicolson Fluid Mosaic Model:**  
  - Proteins embedded within, not coating the bilayer.  
  - Membrane is dynamic, not rigid.  
  - Accepted today due to electron microscopy & freeze-fracture evidence.  

---

## 3. Structure (Fluid Mosaic Model)
- **Phospholipid bilayer:**  
  - Amphipathic molecules: hydrophilic phosphate heads (face aqueous environments), hydrophobic fatty acid tails (face inward).  
  - Forms a hydrophobic core → restricts polar/charged substances.  
- **Proteins:**  
  - **Intrinsic (integral):** Span the bilayer. E.g. channel proteins (hydrophilic pores), carrier proteins (change shape to transport).  
  - **Extrinsic (peripheral):** Attached to surface, often as enzymes, anchors, or receptors.  
- **Cholesterol:**  
  - Inserts between phospholipids.  
  - Stabilises membrane by reducing lateral movement.  
  - Prevents extremes: too rigid at low temps, too fluid at high temps.  
- **Carbohydrates (glycolipids, glycoproteins):**  
  - Carbohydrate chains protrude externally.  
  - Functions: receptors, cell–cell recognition, adhesion, antigens.

## 4. Key Functions
- **Selective barrier:** Controls exchange of substances.  
- **Compartmentalisation:** Separates organelles and their reactions.  
- **Transport:** Via diffusion, facilitated diffusion, osmosis, [[Active Transport]], bulk transport.  
- **Communication:** Receptor proteins detect hormones, neurotransmitters, cytokines.  
- **Recognition:** Glycoproteins act as self-markers (important in immune response).  
- **Adhesion:** Enables tissue formation.  
- **Surface for reactions:** e.g. cristae in mitochondria, thylakoids in chloroplasts.  

---

## 5. Membrane Permeability
- **Freely permeable:** small, non-polar molecules (O₂, CO₂).  
- **Limited permeability:** small polar molecules (H₂O, urea).  
- **Impermeable (without transport proteins):** ions, large polar molecules ([[Carbohydrates|glucose]], amino acids).  

---

## 6. Factors Affecting Membrane Structure & Function
- **Temperature:**  
  - Low → phospholipids pack tightly → membrane rigid.  
  - Moderate → increased fluidity → optimal function.  
  - High → denaturation of proteins → increased permeability.  
- **Solvents:**  
  - Organic solvents (alcohols, acetone) dissolve phospholipids → increased permeability → cell lysis.  
- **Cholesterol:**  
  - Acts as a buffer against temperature extremes.  

---

## 7. Practical/Experimental Evidence
- **Freeze-fracture [[Microscopy]]:** Revealed [[Proteins]] embedded in bilayer.  
- **Fluorescent tagging of membrane [[Proteins]]:** Demonstrated protein mobility → evidence for fluidity.  
- **Beetroot membrane permeability experiment:**  
  - Heat or ethanol damages membranes → pigment leakage increases.  
  - Used to study effects of temperature/solvents on permeability.
