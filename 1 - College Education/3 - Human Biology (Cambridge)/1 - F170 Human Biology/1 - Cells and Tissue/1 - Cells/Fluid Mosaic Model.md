The **Fluid Mosaic Model** describes the structure of the plasma membrane as a dynamic, flexible bilayer of phospholipids with various proteins, carbohydrates, and lipids embedded within it. It is "fluid" because molecules can move laterally, and "mosaic" because of the varied pattern of embedded proteins.

### 1. The Phospholipid Bilayer

**Structure:** Consists of two layers of phospholipids.
- **Hydrophilic Heads:** Phosphate-containing "water-loving" heads that face the aqueous environment (extracellular fluid and cytoplasm).

- **Hydrophobic Tails:** Fatty acid "water-fearing" tails that point inwards, away from water, forming a non-polar core.

- **Function:** Forms a selectively permeable barrier; allows small, non-polar molecules (e.g., $O_2$, $CO_2$) to diffuse through while blocking polar or charged substances.


### 2. Membrane Components

**Cholesterol:**
- **Structure:** Lipid molecules fitted between phospholipid tails.
- **Function:** Regulates membrane **fluidity** and stability; prevents the membrane from becoming too rigid in the cold or too fluid in the heat.

 **Proteins:**
- **Intrinsic (Integral):** Span the entire bilayer (e.g., **Channel Proteins** and **Carrier Proteins** for facilitated diffusion and active transport).
- **Extrinsic (Peripheral):** Found on the surface; act as enzymes or receptors.
   
**Glycoproteins & Glycolipids:**
- **Structure:** Carbohydrate chains attached to proteins or lipids.
- **Function:** Act as **antigens** for cell recognition ("self" vs "non-self") and receptors for cell signalling (e.g., hormone binding).

### 3. Transport Across the Bilayer

- **Simple Diffusion:** Passive movement of small, non-polar molecules directly through the bilayer down a concentration gradient.

- **Facilitated Diffusion:** Passive movement of polar or charged molecules through specific **channel** or **carrier proteins**.

- **Active Transport:** Movement of substances _against_ a concentration gradient using **ATP** and specific carrier proteins.

- **Osmosis:** Passive movement of water from a high water potential to a low water potential through the bilayer or via **aquaporins**.


### Function Summary Table

|**Component**|**Primary Function**|
|---|---|
|**Phospholipid Bilayer**|Selectively permeable barrier; keeps cell contents contained.|
|**Cholesterol**|Regulates fluidity and increases mechanical strength.|
|**Channel Proteins**|Allows specific ions/water-soluble molecules to diffuse through.|
|**Carrier Proteins**|Changes shape to transport molecules (Active or Passive).|
|**Glycoproteins**|Cell recognition (antigens) and cell-to-cell adhesion.|
|**Glycolipids**|Cell signalling and maintaining membrane stability.|

### Comparison of Transport Mechanisms

| **Feature**      | **Simple Diffusion**     | **Facilitated Diffusion** | **Active Transport**               |
| ---------------- | ------------------------ | ------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| **Gradient**     | Down ($H \rightarrow L$) | Down ($H \rightarrow L$)  | Against ($L \rightarrow H$)        |
| **Energy (ATP)** | No                       | No                        | **Yes**                            |
| **Protein Req.** | No                       | Yes (Channel/Carrier)     | Yes (Carrier/Pump)                 |
| **Substances**   | $O_2$, $CO_2$, Lipids    | Glucose, Ions, $H_2O$     | Ions ($Na^+/K^+$), Large molecules |
