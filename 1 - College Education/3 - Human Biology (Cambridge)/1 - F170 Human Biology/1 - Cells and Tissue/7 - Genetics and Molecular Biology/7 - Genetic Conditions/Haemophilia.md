**Haemophilia** is an inherited genetic disorder that impairs the body's ability to make blood clots, a process needed to stop bleeding.

## Genetics
- **Inheritance**: **X-Linked Recessive**.
- **Gene**: Mutations in the [[Genes]] coding for clotting factors.
    - **Haemophilia A**: Deficiency in **Factor VIII** (most common).
    - **Haemophilia B**: Deficiency in **Factor IX**.
- **Population**: Primarily affects males (XY) because they have only one X chromosome. Females (XX) must be homozygous recessive to have the disease, which is rare.

## Symptoms
- Prolonged bleeding from cuts or injuries.
- Spontaneous bleeding into joints (knees, elbows) causing pain and swelling.
- Bruising easily.

## Treatment
- **Replacement Therapy**: Regular injections of the missing clotting factor (Factor VIII for Type A).
- **Desmopressin**: A hormone that can stimulate the release of stored Factor VIII in mild cases.
- **Gene Therapy**: Research is ongoing to introduce functional [[Genes]] into patients' hepatocytes ([[Liver]] cells) to produce clotting factors endogenously.

### Q) A woman who is a carrier for Haemophilia A has a child with a healthy man. What is the probability that their son will have haemophilia?
**Model Answer:**
The mother's genotype is $X^H X^h$ (carrier) and the father's is $X^H Y$ (healthy).
- They can produce sons with genotypes $X^H Y$ (healthy) or $X^h Y$ (affected).
- Therefore, there is a **50% probability** that any son born to them will have haemophilia. (Note: The probability for *any* child being an affected son is 25%).
