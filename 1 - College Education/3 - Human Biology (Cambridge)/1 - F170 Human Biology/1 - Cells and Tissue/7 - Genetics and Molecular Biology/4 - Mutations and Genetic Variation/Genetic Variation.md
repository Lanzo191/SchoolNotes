**Genetic variation** refers to the differences in [[DNA]] sequences between individuals within a population.

## Structure: Sources of Variation

### 1. Meiotic Recombination
- **Crossing Over**: Exchange of genetic material between homologous [[Chromosomes]] during Prophase I.
- **Independent Assortment**: Random alignment of maternal and paternal [[Chromosomes]] during Metaphase I.

### 2. Random Fertilisation
- Any one of millions of sperm can fuse with any one egg, creating a unique zygotic combination.

### 3. Mutations
- The only source of *new* alleles in a population.

## Function
- Increases the phenotypic diversity of a population, ensuring that some individuals may survive if environmental conditions change (Biological Fitness).

## Clinical Relevance
- **Pharmacogenomics**: Genetic variation explains why two patients might respond differently to the same dose of a drug (e.g., Warfarin sensitivity).

## Summary
Genetic variation is driven by the shuffling of existing alleles during sexual reproduction and the creation of new ones via mutation.
