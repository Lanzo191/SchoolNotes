The **effects of mutations** describe the phenotypic consequences of genetic alterations, ranging from neutral to lethal.

## Structure: Categorisation of Effects

### Neutral Mutations
- Have no effect on the organism’s survival or reproduction. 
- Often occur in non-coding regions or result in "silent" substitutions.

### Beneficial Mutations
- Confer a selective advantage.
- **Example**: Lactase persistence in humans or antibiotic resistance in bacteria.

### Deleterious (Harmful) Mutations
- Reduce fitness or cause genetic disorders.
- **Lethal Mutations**: Cause the death of the organism, often during embryonic development.

## Function
- Drives the process of evolution by altering the "fitness landscape" of a species.

## Clinical Relevance
- **Inborn Errors of Metabolism**: Mutations that disable a single enzyme, leading to toxic buildup (e.g., Phenylketonuria/PKU).

## Summary
The impact of a mutation depends heavily on the environment and the specific location of the [[DNA]] alteration.
