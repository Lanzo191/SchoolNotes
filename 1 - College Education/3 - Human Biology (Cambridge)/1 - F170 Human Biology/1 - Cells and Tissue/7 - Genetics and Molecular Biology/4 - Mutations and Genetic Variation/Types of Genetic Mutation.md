**Mutations** are permanent alterations in the [[DNA]] sequence that makes up a gene. They can range in size from a single [[DNA]] base to a large segment of a chromosome.

## Structure: Classification of Mutations

### Point Mutations (Substitution)
- **Silent**: Base change results in the same amino acid (due to the degeneracy of the [[Genetic Code]]).
- **Missense**: Base change results in a different amino acid (e.g., Sickle Cell Anemia).
- **Nonsense**: Base change creates a premature **Stop [[Codon]]**, leading to a truncated, usually non-functional protein.

### Frameshift Mutations
- **Inversions/Deletions**: If the number of bases added or removed is not a multiple of three, the entire "reading frame" of the gene is shifted.
- **Result**: Every amino acid downstream of the mutation is altered.

### Chromosomal Mutations
- **Translocation**: A segment of one chromosome breaks off and attaches to another.
- **Inversion**: A segment of [[DNA]] is reversed end-to-end.
- **Duplication**: Producing extra copies of parts of a chromosome.

## Function
- Mutations are the ultimate source of **[[Genetic Variation]]**, providing the raw material upon which natural selection acts.

## Clinical Relevance
- **[[Cystic Fibrosis]]**: Often caused by a 3-base deletion ($\Delta$F508) in the CFTR gene.
- **Cancer**: Accumulation of mutations in proto-oncogenes and tumor suppressor [[Genes]] leads to malignancy.

## Summary
Mutations range from single base changes to massive chromosomal rearrangements, often resulting in altered protein function or disease.
