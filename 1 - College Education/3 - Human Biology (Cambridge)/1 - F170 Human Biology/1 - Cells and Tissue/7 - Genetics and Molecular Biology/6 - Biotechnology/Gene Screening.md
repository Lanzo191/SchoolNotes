**Gene Screening** involves testing individuals to identify those who have or carry a genetic disorder. It allows for early diagnosis, treatment, or reproductive decisions.

## Types of Genetic Screening

### 1. Pre-implantation Genetic Diagnosis (PGD)
- Used during **IVF**. Embryos are tested for specific genetic conditions before being implanted into the [[Uterus]].
- Only healthy embryos are selected.

### 2. Prenatal Screening
Testing the fetus during pregnancy.
- **Amniocentesis** (15-20 weeks): A needle extracts amniotic fluid containing fetal cells. Slight risk of miscarriage (1%).
- **Chorionic Villus Sampling (CVS)** (10-13 weeks): A sample of cells is taken from the [[Placenta]]. Slightly higher risk of miscarriage than amniocentesis.
- **Non-Invasive Prenatal Testing (NIPT)**: Blood test from the mother analyzing cell-free fetal [[DNA]]. Safe but less diagnostic.

### 3. Newborn Screening
- "Heel prick" test shortly after birth to check for metabolic disorders like **Phenylketonuria (PKU)** and [[Cystic Fibrosis]].

## Ethical Issues
- **Abortion**: Prenatal diagnosis of a defect may lead to termination of pregnancy.
- **"Designer Babies"**: Concerns that PGD could be used to select for non-medical traits (intelligence, eye color).
- **False Results**: False positives can cause unnecessary stress; false negatives can delay treatment.
## Genetic Counselling
- A communication process designed to help families understand and adapt to the medical, psychological, and familial implications of genetic contributions to disease.
- Counsellors calculate recurrence risks and explain available testing options.
- **Non-Directive**: Counsellors provide information but do not tell patients what decision to make.

### Q) Discuss the ethical implications of genetic screening for late-onset diseases like Huntingdon's.
**Model Answer:**
Screening for late-onset conditions raises complex ethical issues.
1.  **Right to Know vs. Not Know**: Some individuals may prefer not to know their fate to avoid psychological distress ("ticking time bomb" effect).
2.  **Reproductive Choices**: Positive results may influence decisions to have children or use PGD, raising concerns about eugenics.
3.  **Insurance & Employment**: Fear of genetic discrimination where insurance premiums could rise or employment opportunities could be limited based on genetic risk (though laws like GINA in the US protect against this, protections vary globally).
