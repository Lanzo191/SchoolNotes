**Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR)** is a laboratory technique used to rapidly produce (amplify) millions to billions of copies of a specific segment of [[DNA]].

## Structure: The Thermal Cycle

1. **Denaturation (95°C)**:
   - Heat is used to break the hydrogen bonds between the two [[DNA]] strands, separating them.
2. **Annealing (55°C - 65°C)**:
   - Temperature is lowered to allow **[[DNA]] Primers** to bind to the specific target sequences on the single-stranded [[DNA]].
3. **Extension (72°C)**:
   - **Taq Polymerase** (a heat-stable enzyme) adds [[Nucleotides]] to the primers, synthesizing a new complementary strand.

## Function
- To provide enough [[DNA]] material for analysis from a very small initial sample (e.g., a single drop of blood or a hair follicle).

## Clinical Relevance
- **Forensics**: Creating [[DNA]] profiles from crime scene evidence.
- **Pathogen Detection**: PCR is the "gold standard" for detecting viral [[DNA]]/[[RNA]] (e.g., HIV or COVID-19).

## Summary
PCR mimics the natural process of [[DNA]] replication but uses temperature cycling to automate the process in a test tube.
