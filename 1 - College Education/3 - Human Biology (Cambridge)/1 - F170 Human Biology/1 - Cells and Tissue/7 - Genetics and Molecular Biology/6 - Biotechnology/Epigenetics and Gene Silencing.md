## Overview
**Epigenetics** is the study of heritable changes in gene expression that do not involve alterations to the underlying **[[DNA]]** sequence. It acts as a bridge between the **Genotype** and the **Phenotype**, determining which [[Genes]] are "switched on" (expressed) or "switched off" (silenced) in response to internal and external signals.

---

## The "On/Off" Switches: Molecular Mechanisms

### 1. [[DNA]] Methylation (The "Off" Switch)
*   **Process:** The addition of a methyl group (-CH₃) to the cytosine bases of **[[DNA]]**. This typically occurs at **CpG sites** (where a cytosine is followed by a guanine).
*   **Enzyme:** Catalysed by **DNA methyltransferases (DNMTs)**.
*   **Mechanism of Silencing:** 
    1.  The methyl group physically blocks the binding of **[[Transcription Factors]]**.
    2.  It recruits proteins that further condense the chromatin, making the **[[DNA]]** inaccessible.
*   **Result:** The gene is **switched off** and cannot be transcribed into **[[mRNA]]**.

### 2. Histone Modification (The Volume Control)
**[[DNA]]** is wrapped around proteins called **Histones**. The tightness of this wrapping determines if a gene can be read.

#### A. Acetylation (Switching ON)
*   **Process:** Adding an acetyl group (-COCH₃) to the histone tails.
*   **Enzyme:** **Histone Acetyltransferase (HAT)**.
*   **Effect:** Neutralises the positive charge on histones, weakening their attraction to the negatively charged **[[DNA]]**. 
*   **Result:** The chromatin "relaxes" (becomes **Euchromatin**), allowing **[[RNA]] polymerase** and **[[Transcription Factors]]** to bind. The gene is **switched on**.

#### B. Deacetylation (Switching OFF)
*   **Process:** Removing acetyl groups from histones.
*   **Enzyme:** **Histone Deacetylase (HDAC)**.
*   **Effect:** Increases the positive charge on histones, causing them to bind more tightly to **[[DNA]]**.
*   **Result:** The chromatin condenses (becomes **Heterochromatin**). The gene is **switched off**.

## Environmental Factors and the Epigenome
Unlike the **[[DNA]]** sequence, the **Epigenome** is dynamic and can be influenced by the environment throughout an organism's life:
*   **Diet:** Nutrients like folic acid and B12 are methyl donors; a lack of these can alter methylation patterns.
*   **Stress & Trauma:** Early life experiences can leave "epigenetic marks" on genes regulating the stress response (e.g., the glucocorticoid receptor).
*   **Toxins:** Exposure to pollution or cigarette smoke can trigger deacetylation or hypermethylation.
*   **Exercise:** Physical activity has been shown to "reprogramme" the epigenetics of muscle and fat cells.

---

## Epigenetic Inheritance and Memory
While most epigenetic marks are "wiped clean" during the formation of gametes (reprogramming), some marks persist.
*   **Transgenerational Epigenetics:** The idea that environmental impacts on a grandparent's epigenome can affect the health of their grandchildren.
*   **Twin Studies:** Identical twins have the same **[[DNA]]** but different epigenomes as they age, explaining why one twin might develop a disease while the other remains healthy.

### Key Case Study: The Dutch Hunger Winter (1944)
Children born to mothers who experienced famine during WWII were found to have distinct epigenetic marks. Decades later, these individuals had higher rates of obesity and heart disease, showing that the "memory" of the famine was stored in their epigenome.

## Role in Development and Cancer

### 1. Cell Differentiation
Epigenetics is why a skin cell and a heart cell look different despite having identical **[[DNA]]**. Specific sets of genes are permanently silenced in each cell type during development to ensure specialised function.

### 2. Cancer
*   **Hypomethylation:** "Switching on" genes that promote cell division (oncogenes).
*   **Hypermethylation:** "Switching off" genes that repair **[[DNA]]** or stop tumours (Tumour Suppressor Genes).

### Q) Compare the effects of histone acetylation and DNA methylation on gene expression. (10 marks)

**Model Answer:**
Both histone acetylation and **[[DNA]]** methylation are epigenetic mechanisms that regulate gene expression without changing the base sequence. However, they generally have opposite effects.

**Histone acetylation** involves the addition of acetyl groups to histones via the enzyme **HAT**. This process neutralises the positive charge of the histones, reducing their affinity for the negatively charged **[[DNA]]**. This results in a more open chromatin structure known as **euchromatin**, which allows **[[Transcription Factors]]** to bind and **"switches on"** the gene.

In contrast, **[[DNA]] methylation** is the addition of methyl groups directly to cytosine bases. This **"switches off"** genes by physically blocking the binding of **[[Transcription Factors]]** and encouraging the formation of tightly packed **heterochromatin**. While acetylation is often reversible and dynamic, methylation is a more stable form of long-term gene silencing used in processes like cell differentiation.
