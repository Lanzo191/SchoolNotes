**[[DNA]] Replication** is a semi-conservative process by which a cell duplicates its genome. It occurs during the **S-phase** of [[Interphase]] and is characterized by its extreme speed and near-perfect accuracy.

## The Enzymatic Machinery

### 1. The "Unzipping" 
- **[[DNA]] Helicase**: Uses [[ATP]] to break hydrogen bonds and separate the strands at the **Replication Fork**.
- **Topoisomerase ([[DNA]] Gyrase)**: Moves ahead of helicase to snip and rejoin the backbone, relieving the torsional strain (supercoiling) caused by unwinding.
- **SSB [[Proteins]]**: Bind to single strands to prevent them from snapping back together or being degraded by enzymes.

### 2. Synthesis
- **[[RNA]] Primase**: Lays down a short [[RNA]] primer (approx. 10 [[Nucleotides]]) because [[DNA]] Polymerase cannot start a chain from scratch; it can only extend an existing 3'-OH end.
- **[[DNA]] Polymerase III**: The primary builder. It adds [[Nucleotides]] in the **5' to 3' direction**.
- **Sliding Clamp**: A ring-shaped protein that holds [[DNA]] Polymerase III onto the strand, increasing "processivity" (the number of [[Nucleotides]] added before falling off).

### 3. Cleanup
- **[[DNA]] Polymerase I**: Removes the [[RNA]] primers and replaces them with [[DNA]].
- **[[DNA]] Ligase**: The "molecular glue" that heals the nicks in the sugar-phosphate backbone, particularly on the lagging strand.



## Mechanism: Leading vs. Lagging Strands
Because [[DNA]] Polymerase only works 5'→3':
- **Leading Strand**: Synthesized continuously toward the replication fork.
- **Lagging Strand**: Synthesized discontinuously in short stretches called **Okazaki Fragments**. Each fragment requires its own [[RNA]] primer.

## Function
- To provide a complete, error-free genome for [[Mitosis]] or [[Meiosis]].

## Clinical Relevance
- **Antiviral Drugs (Acyclovir)**: Act as "chain terminators." They mimic [[Nucleotides]] but lack a 3'-OH group, so when [[DNA]] polymerase adds them to a viral genome, synthesis stops dead.

## Summary
[[DNA]] replication is a highly coordinated, multi-enzyme process that utilizes a "continuous" and "discontinuous" strategy to overcome the directional constraints of [[DNA]] Polymerase.
