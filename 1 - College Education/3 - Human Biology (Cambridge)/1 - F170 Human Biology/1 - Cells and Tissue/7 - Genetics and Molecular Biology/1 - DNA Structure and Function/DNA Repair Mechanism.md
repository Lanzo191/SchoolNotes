**[[DNA]] Repair Mechanisms** are a collection of processes by which a cell identifies and corrects damage to the [[DNA]] molecules that encode its genome.

## Structure: Types of Repair

### Base Excision Repair (BER)
- Corrects small, non-distorting lesions (e.g., oxidized bases).
- **Enzyme**: [[DNA]] glycosylases remove the damaged base.

### Nucleotide Excision Repair (NER)
- Corrects bulky, helix-distorting lesions (e.g., Thymine dimers from UV light).
- **Enzyme**: Nucleases cut out a segment of the [[DNA]] strand, and [[DNA]] polymerase fills the gap.

### Mismatch Repair (MMR)
- Corrects errors missed during [[DNA]] replication (e.g., A-G pairing).
- Occurs immediately after replication.

### Double-Strand Break (DSB) Repair
- **Non-Homologous End Joining (NHEJ)**: "Glues" broken ends together (error-prone).
- **Homologous Recombination (HR)**: Uses the sister chromatid as a template for perfect repair.

## Function
- **Genomic Integrity**: Maintains the constancy of the [[Genetic Code]] across generations.
- **Cancer Prevention**: Prevents the accumulation of mutations that lead to uncontrolled cell growth.

## Clinical Relevance
- **Xeroderma Pigmentosum**: A genetic defect in [[Nucleotides]] Excision Repair; patients are extremely sensitive to sunlight and have high skin cancer risk.
- **BRCA1/2 Mutations**: Defects in homologous recombination repair, significantly increasing breast and ovarian cancer risk.

## Summary
The cell possesses highly specialized enzymatic pathways to recognize and fix various types of [[DNA]] damage, preserving the survival of the organism.
