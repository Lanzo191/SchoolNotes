**[[Chromosomes]] and Chromatin** represent the organized structural forms of [[DNA]] within the [[Nucleus]], allowing for massive compaction and regulation of the genome.

## Structure

### Levels of Compaction
- **[[DNA]] Double Helix**: The basic 2nm wide strand.
- **Nucleosomes**: [[DNA]] wrapped around a core of eight **Histone [[Proteins]]** (H2A, H2B, H3, H4). Often described as "beads on a string."
- **Chromatin Fiber**: Nucleosomes fold into a 30nm fiber.
- **Looped Domains**: Fibers attached to a scaffolding of non-histone [[Proteins]].
- **Metaphase Chromosome**: The most condensed form, visible during cell division.

### Chromatin States
- **Euchromatin**: Less condensed, transcriptionally active ("open" [[DNA]]).
- **Heterochromatin**: Highly condensed, transcriptionally inactive ("closed" [[DNA]]). Found at centromeres and *telomeres*.

### Chromosome Anatomy
- **Centromere**: The constriction point where sister chromatids join and spindle fibers attach.
- **Telomeres**: Repetitive [[DNA]] sequences at the ends that protect against degradation.
- **Sister Chromatids**: Two identical copies of a chromosome produced during [[DNA]] replication.

## Function
- **Compaction**: Fits 2 meters of [[DNA]] into a [[Nucleus]] only a few micrometers wide.
- **Protection**: Prevents [[DNA]] damage during the physical stresses of cell division.
- **Regulation**: Controls access to [[Genes]] by shifting between euchromatin and heterochromatin states.

## Clinical Relevance
- **Aneuploidy**: Abnormal chromosome numbers (e.g., Trisomy 21 / [[Down Syndrome]]).
- **Karyotyping**: A clinical technique used to visualize [[Chromosomes]] to detect genetic disorders.

## Summary
Chromatin provides the structural framework for [[DNA]] packaging, utilizing histones to condense genetic material into functional [[Chromosomes]].
