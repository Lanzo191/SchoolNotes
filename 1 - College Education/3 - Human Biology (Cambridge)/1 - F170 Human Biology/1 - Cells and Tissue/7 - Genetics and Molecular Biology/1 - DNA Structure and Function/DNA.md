**Deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA)** is a double-stranded, helical polymer of deoxyribonucleotides. It serves as the definitive chemical repository for the genetic information of all cellular life and many viruses.

## Structure

### The Nucleotide Monomer
Each [[Nucleotides]] consists of three covalently bonded components:
1.  **Pentose Sugar**: Deoxyribose (lacking a hydroxyl group at the 2' position, which increases chemical stability compared to [[RNA]]).
2.  **Phosphate Group**: Attached to the 5' carbon of the sugar, providing a strong negative charge to the molecule.
3.  **Nitrogenous Base**: Attached to the 1' carbon.
    - **Purines (Double-ring)**: Adenine (A) and Guanine (G).
    - **Pyrimidines (Single-ring)**: Thymine (T) and Cytosine (C).



### The Double Helix Geometry
- **Sugar-Phosphate Backbone**: Formed by **3'-5' phosphodiester bonds**. This creates a "directionality" to the strand, with a 5' end (phosphate) and a 3' end (hydroxyl).
- **Antiparallel Alignment**: The two strands run in opposite directions; one 5'→3' and the other 3'→5'.
- **Complementary Base Pairing**: Strands are held together by hydrogen bonds:
    - **A=T**: 2 hydrogen bonds.
    - **G≡C**: 3 hydrogen bonds (making G-C rich regions more thermally stable).
- **Spiral Parameters**: The helix completes one full turn every **10.5 base pairs** (approx. 3.4 nm).
- **Major and Minor Grooves**: The twisting creates unequal spacing. The **Major Groove** is the primary site for DNA-binding [[Proteins]] (like [[Transcription]] factors) to recognize specific sequences without unwinding the helix.



## Function
- **Information Storage**: The linear sequence of bases encodes the primary structure of [[Proteins]].
- **High Fidelity Replication**: Complementary base pairing ensures that an exact copy can be synthesized for daughter cells.
- **Chemical Stability**: The double-helix structure and the use of thymine instead of uracil protect the code from oxidative damage and spontaneous mutation.

## Clinical Relevance
- **Chemotherapy (Cisplatin)**: Forms cross-links between DNA strands, preventing replication and triggering apoptosis in rapidly dividing cancer cells.
- **Ethidium Bromide**: An intercalating agent used in labs that slides between base pairs; it is a potent mutagen because it distorts the helix.

## Summary
The structure of DNA is a masterpiece of biological engineering, balancing the strength of covalent backbones with the "unzippable" nature of hydrogen-bonded bases to allow for both storage and access.
