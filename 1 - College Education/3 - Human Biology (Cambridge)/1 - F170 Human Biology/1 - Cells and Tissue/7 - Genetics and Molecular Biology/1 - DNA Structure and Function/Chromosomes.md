## Overview of Chromosomes

[[Chromosomes]] are thread-like structures located in the [[Nucleus]] of cells. They are made of [[DNA]] tightly coiled around [[Proteins]] called histones. This packaging allows [[DNA]] to fit within the cell and protects it during cell division.

### Structure of Chromosomes

- **Composition**: Each chromosome consists of [[DNA]] and histone [[Proteins]].
- **Shape**: [[Chromosomes]] have a characteristic shape due to the centromere, which divides them into two arms: the short arm (p arm) and the long arm (q arm).
- **Visibility**: [[Chromosomes]] are not visible during most of the cell's life cycle but become visible during cell division when they condense.

## Types of Chromosomes

Humans have 23 pairs of chromosomes, totaling 46. These are categorized into two types:

|Type|Description|Number of Pairs|
|---|---|---|
|Autosomes|Non-sex chromosomes, responsible for most traits|22|
|Sex [[Chromosomes]]|Determine an individual's sex|1|

### Autosomes and Sex Chromosomes

- **Autosomes**: Carry [[Genes]] that determine various traits and functions. Each pair consists of one chromosome from each parent.
- **Sex  [[Chromosomes]]**: In humans, females have two X chromosomes (XX), while males have one X and one Y chromosome (XY). The Y chromosome is smaller and contains fewer [[Genes]]. 
- **Homologous chromosomes:** are pairs of chromosomes in a diploid organism, one inherited from each parent.
## Function of Chromosomes

[[Chromosomes]] play a crucial role in:

- **Genetic Information Storage**: They contain [[Genes]] that provide instructions for the body's functions.
- **Cell Division**: During [[Mitosis]], chromosomes ensure that [[DNA]] is accurately copied and distributed to daughter cells.
- **Genetic Diversity**: Chromosomal recombination during [[Meiosis]] contributes to [[Genetic Variation]] in offspring.

## Chromosomal Abnormalities

Changes in chromosome number or structure can lead to genetic disorders. For example:

- **[[Down Syndrome]]**: Caused by an extra copy of chromosome 21.
- **Cancer**: Some cancers are linked to chromosomal abnormalities, such as translocations or deletions.

Understanding chromosomes is essential for genetics, biology, and medicine, as they are fundamental to heredity and the functioning of living organisms.
