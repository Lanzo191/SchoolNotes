**Sex Linkage** refers to the phenotypic expression of an allele that is dependent on the gender of the individual and is directly tied to the sex [[Chromosomes]] (X and Y).

## Structure

### X-Linked Inheritance
- [[Genes]] located on the X chromosome.
- **Males (XY)** are hemizygous; they only have one copy. If they inherit a mutant X from their mother, they will express the trait.
- **Females (XX)** can be carriers.

### Y-Linked Inheritance
- [[Genes]] located on the Y chromosome (e.g., SRY gene).
- Only passed from father to son.

## Function
- Explains why certain genetic conditions are significantly more common in one sex than the other.

## Clinical Relevance
- **[[Haemophilia]] A**: A blood-clotting disorder caused by an X-linked recessive mutation.
- **Red-Green Color Blindness**: The most common X-linked recessive condition.

## Summary
Sex-linked traits do not follow standard Mendelian ratios because males and females possess different combinations of sex [[Chromosomes]].

## X-Inactivation (Lyonization)
- In female mammals (XX), one of the two X [[Chromosomes]] in each cell is randomly inactivated during early embryonic development.
- The inactive X condenses into a compact structure called a **Barr Body**.
- This ensures females have the same dose of X-linked gene products as males (Dosage Compensation).
- **Example**: Tortoiseshell cats (mosaic fur color pattern).

---
### Q) Why are men more likely to be color blind than women?
**Model Answer:**
Color blindness is an **X-linked recessive** trait. Men (XY) only need to inherit one mutant allele on their single X chromosome to be affected. Women (XX) must inherit two mutant alleles (one from each parent) to express the condition. If a woman has one mutant allele, her healthy allele usually masks it, making her a carrier.
