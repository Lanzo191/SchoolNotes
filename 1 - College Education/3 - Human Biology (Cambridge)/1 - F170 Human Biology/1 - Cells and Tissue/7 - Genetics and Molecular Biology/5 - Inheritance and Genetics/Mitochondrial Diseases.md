**Mitochondrial diseases** are a group of disorders caused by mutations in **mitochondrial [[DNA]] (mtDNA)** or in nuclear [[Genes]] that code for mitochondrial [[Proteins]]. These conditions primarily affect the function of the **[[Mitochondria]]**, leading to a failure in [[ATP]] production.

## Cause
- Mutations in **[[Mitochondria|mtDNA]]** which disrupt the electron transport chain or [[ATP]] synthase.
- Can also be caused by nuclear gene mutations affecting mitochondrial maintenance.

## Inheritance
- Strictly **Maternal Inheritance**: Only mothers pass [[Mitochondria]] to their children (via the egg cell cytoplasm). Fathers do not transmit [[Mitochondria]].
- See [[Mitochondrial Inheritance]].

## Specific Examples
- **Leber's Hereditary Optic Neuropathy (LHON)**: Causes sudden vision loss in young adults due to optic nerve death.
- **MELAS Syndrome**: Mitochondrial Encephalomyopathy, Lactic Acidosis, and Stroke-like episodes.

## Affected Tissues
Tissues with high energy demands are most severely affected:
- **Nervous System** (Seizures, developmental delays)
- **Muscles** (Weakness, cramping)
- **Heart** (Cardiomyopathy)
- **[[Liver]]** & **Kidneys**

## Diagnosis & Treatment
- Diagnosis via **[[Pedigree Charts]]** (maternal pattern), muscle biopsy (ragged red fibers), and genetic testing.
- No cure; treatment focuses on managing symptoms (e.g., cofactors, physiotherapy).

---
### Q) Why are muscle and nerve tissues most susceptible to mitochondrial diseases?
**Model Answer:**
[[Mitochondria]] are the site of aerobic respiration and [[ATP]] production. Muscle and nerve tissues have very high metabolic rates and energy demands to function (contraction, impulse transmission). Therefore, any defect in mitochondrial [[ATP]] production will disproportionately compromise the function of these tissues compared to less active ones.
