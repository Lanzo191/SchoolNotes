**Cystic Fibrosis (CF)** is a genetic condition affecting the respiratory and digestive systems.

## Genetics
- **Inheritance**: **Autosomal Recessive**.
- **Gene**: Mutation in the **CFTR gene** on chromosome 7.
- **Protein**: The CFTR protein functions as a channel for **chloride ions** across [[Cell Membranes]].
- **Mechanism**:
    - The mutation (most commonly $\Delta$F508) causes the CFTR protein to malfunction or not reach the membrane.
    - This disrupts the transport of chloride and water, leading to the production of **thick, sticky mucus**.

## Symptoms
- **[[Lungs]]**: Mucus clogs airways, trapping bacteria and leading to frequent lung infections and reduced lung function.
- **[[Pancreas]]**: Mucus blocks ducts, preventing digestive enzymes from reaching the intestines (malnutrition).
- **Reproductive**: Infertility (blocked [[Vas Deferens]] in males).

## Inheritance Pattern
Cystic Fibrosis Is a disease where the normal allele (CF) is dominant and the abnormal recessive allele (cF). 
- Carriers (heterozygous) are healthy.
- Two carrier parents have a **25% chance** of having a child with CF.

## Treatment & Management
- **Physiotherapy**: Daily chest physiotherapy to clear mucus.
- **Medication**: Bronchodilators, antibiotics for infections, and DNAse enzymes to thin mucus.
- **Diet**: High-calorie diet and **pancreatic enzyme replacement** supplements.
- **Gene Therapy**: Potential for future cures targeting the CFTR gene directly.

---
### Q) Why do males with Cystic Fibrosis often suffer from infertility?
**Model Answer:** 
The thick, sticky mucus produced due to the CFTR mutation blocks the **[[Vas Deferens]]** (the tube carrying sperm from the [[Testes]]). This physical blockage prevents sperm from being ejaculated, resulting in infertility, although sperm production itself is often normal.
