**Dihybrid Cross**: A genetic cross between individuals that are heterozygous for **two different traits** (e.g., $RrYy 	imes RrYy$). This experiment is the foundation for understanding how different [[Genes]] behave relative to one another.

---

## 1. Decoding the Notation (Capital vs. Lowercase)
Before calculating, you must understand what the letters represent:
- **Capital Letter (e.g., $R$, $Y$):** Represents the **Dominant Allele**. Biologically, this often codes for a **functional protein** (e.g., an enzyme that produces yellow pigment). It only takes one copy to see the trait.
- **Lowercase Letter (e.g., $r$, $y$):** Represents the **Recessive Allele**. This often codes for a **non-functional** or "broken" version of the protein. The trait only appears if there is no dominant "working" version present (i.e., homozygous recessive $rr$).

## 2. The Core Concept: Independent Assortment
**Mendel's Second Law** states that the inheritance of one trait does not affect the inheritance of another. 

*   **Why?** During Metaphase I of Meiosis, homologous chromosome pairs line up randomly at the equator. 
*   **Implication:** Being "Yellow" doesn't force a seed to be "Round". The "Yellow" allele ($Y$) can end up in a gamete with either the "Round" ($R$) or "Wrinkled" ($r$) allele with equal probability.

---

## 2. The Method: How to find the Gametes (FOIL)
The biggest challenge in Dihybrid crosses is determining the gametes. You cannot just put "Rr" on one side. You must find the **haploid combinations**. 

**Use the FOIL Method:** (First, Outside, Inside, Last)
For a parent **YyRr**:
1.  **F**irst: **Y** and **R** $\rightarrow$ **YR**
2.  **O**utside: **Y** and **r** $\rightarrow$ **Yr**
3.  **I**nside: **y** and **R** $\rightarrow$ **yR**
4.  **L**ast: **y** and **r** $\rightarrow$ **yr**

> **Result:** A 4x4 Punnett Square (16 boxes).

---

## 3. The Golden Ratio: 9 : 3 : 3 : 1
In a standard dihybrid cross ($AaBb 	imes AaBb$), the phenotypic ratio of offspring is always:

| Count | Phenotype | Genotype Logic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **9** | **Dominant / Dominant** | ($A\_ B\_$) |
| **3** | **Dominant / Recessive** | ($A\_ bb$) |
| **3** | **Recessive / Dominant** | ($aa B\_$) |
| **1** | **Recessive / Recessive** | ($aa bb$) |

## 5. The "Shortcut" (Probability Method)
Drawing a 16-box grid is slow. You can treat a Dihybrid cross as **two separate Monohybrid crosses** occurring at the same time.

**Question:** What is the chance of getting **Attached Earlobes ($yy$)** AND **Dimples ($R\_$)**?

1.  **Solve Earlobes ($Yy 	imes Yy$):** Chance of $yy$ = **1/4**
2.  **Solve Dimples ($Rr 	imes Rr$):** Chance of Dimples ($RR$ or $Rr$) = **3/4**
3.  **Multiply:** $1/4 	imes 3/4 = 3/16$.

**Result:** Matches the 9:3:3:1 ratio without drawing the grid.

### Q) In a dihybrid cross of two heterozygotes ($AaBb 	imes AaBb$), what is the probability of an offspring being homozygous recessive for both traits ($aabb$)?
**Model Answer:**
Using the probability method:
- Probability of inheriting **aa** = 1/4.
- Probability of inheriting **bb** = 1/4.
- Since the [[Genes]] assort independently, multiply: $1/4 	imes 1/4 = \mathbf{1/16}$ (or 6.25%).
