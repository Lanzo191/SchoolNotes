Inheritance of ABO blood groups in humans is an example of **Multiple Alleles** and **Co-dominance**.

## The Alleles
The gene for blood type (Gene I) has three possible alleles:
1.  **$I^A$:** Codes for Antigen A (Dominant).
2.  **$I^B$:** Codes for Antigen B (Dominant).
3.  **$i$:** Codes for no antigen (Recessive).

### Dominance Relationships
*   $I^A$ and $I^B$ are **Co-dominant** (if both are present, the person is Type AB).
*   Both $I^A$ and $I^B$ are **Dominant over $i$**.

---

## Phenotypes and Genotypes

| Blood Type (Phenotype) | Possible Genotypes                               |
| :--------------------- | :----------------------------------------------- |
| **Type A**             | $I^A I^A$ (Homozygous) or $I^A i$ (Heterozygous) |
| **Type B**             | $I^B I^B$ (Homozygous) or $I^B i$ (Heterozygous) |
| **Type AB**            | $I^A I^B$ (Co-dominant)                          |
| **Type O**             | $ii$ (Homozygous Recessive)                      |

---

## Example: Blood Group Punnett Square
**Scenario:** A mother with Type A blood (Heterozygous: $I^A i$) and a father with Type B blood (Heterozygous: $I^B i$) have a child.

| | **$I^A$** | **$i$** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **$I^B$** | **$I^A I^B$** (Type AB) | **$I^B i$** (Type B) |
| **$i$** | **$I^A i$** (Type A) | **$ii$** (Type O) |

**Offspring Probabilities:**
*   25% chance of **Type AB**
*   25% chance of **Type B**
*   25% chance of **Type A**
*   25% chance of **Type O**

> **Exam Tip:** This specific cross (Heterozygous A x Heterozygous B) is a common exam question because it is the only way two parents can have a child with Type O blood if neither parent is Type O themselves.

## The Rhesus Factor (Rh)
Blood is also classified by the **Rhesus (D) antigen**. This is a separate gene that follows simple Mendelian dominance.
*   **Rh+ (Positive):** Dominant (Genotype: *RR* or *Rr*).
*   **Rh- (Negative):** Recessive (Genotype: *rr*).

A person's full blood type is a combination of ABO and Rh (e.g., A+ or O-).
