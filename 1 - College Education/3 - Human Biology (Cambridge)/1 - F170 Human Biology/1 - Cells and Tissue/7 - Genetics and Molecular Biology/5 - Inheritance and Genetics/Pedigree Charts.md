**Pedigree Charts** are diagrams that show the occurrence and appearance or phenotypes of a particular gene or organism and its ancestors from one generation to the next.

## Symbols
- **Square**: Male
- **Circle**: Female
- **Shaded Shape**: Affected individual (expresses the trait).
- **Unshaded Shape**: Unaffected individual.
- **Horizontal Line**: Mating/Marriage line.
- **Vertical Line**: Offspring line.
- **Roman Numerals (I, II, III)**: Generations.

## Identifying Inheritance Patterns
### Autosomal Recessive
- Trait often skips generations.
- Affected offspring can have unaffected parents (carriers).
- Males and females affected equally.

### Autosomal Dominant
- Trait appears in every generation (does not skip).
- Affected individuals must have at least one affected parent.
- Males and females affected equally.

### X-Linked Recessive
- More males affected than females.
- No father-to-son transmission.
- Mothers of affected sons are often carriers.

### Y-Linked (Holandric)
- Only males are affected.
- Passed from father to all sons.
- Does not skip generations.

---
### Q) How can you distinguish between Autosomal Dominant and X-Linked Dominant on a pedigree chart?
**Model Answer:**
In **X-Linked Dominant** inheritance, an affected father will pass the trait to **all** of his daughters (because they all receive his only X chromosome) but **none** of his sons (who receive his Y). In **Autosomal Dominant** inheritance, an affected father can pass the trait to both sons and daughters with equal probability (50%).
