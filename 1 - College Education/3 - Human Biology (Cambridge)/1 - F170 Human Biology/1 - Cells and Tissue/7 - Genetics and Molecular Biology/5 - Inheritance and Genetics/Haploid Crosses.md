## Overview
A **Haploid Cross** refers to a genetic cross between organisms that spend most of their life cycle in a **haploid (n)** state (containing only one set of [[Chromosomes]]). This is common in many fungi, algae, and bacteria.

---

## Key Differences from Diploid Crosses

| Feature | Diploid Cross (2n) | Haploid Cross (n) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Alleles per Gene** | Two alleles (one from each parent). | Only **one** allele present in each individual. |
| **Dominance** | Dominant alleles can hide recessive ones. | No dominance/recessive interaction in the vegetative state. |
| **Expression** | Phenotype depends on the interaction of two alleles. | The single allele is **immediately expressed** in the phenotype. |
| **Punnett Square** | Predicts zygote genotypes (2n). | Predicts possible recombinant genotypes in offspring. |

## The Process
In haploid organisms (like the bread mould *Neurospora*):
1.  Two haploid cells of different "mating types" fuse to form a temporary **diploid zygote**.
2.  The zygote immediately undergoes **[[Meiosis]]** to produce new haploid spores.
3.  These spores grow into new haploid individuals.

---

## Significance in Research
Haploid organisms are used in genetic research because:
*   **Ease of Observation:** Since there is only one allele, any **[[Mutation Effects|mutation]]** is immediately visible in the phenotype.
*   **Speed:** They typically have much shorter generation times than diploid organisms.

---

### Q) Why is it easier to study the effects of mutations in haploid organisms compared to diploid organisms?

**Model Answer:**
In **haploid organisms**, there is only one copy of each gene. This means that if a **[[Mutation Effects|mutation]]** occurs, it is **immediately expressed** in the phenotype. In contrast, in **diploid organisms**, a mutated allele is often **recessive** and can be "masked" by a dominant wild-type allele in a heterozygote. Therefore, recessive mutations can remain hidden for many generations in diploid populations, making them much harder to detect and study.
