**Monohybrid Cross** involves the study of the inheritance of a single trait (gene). It demonstrates **Mendel's Law of Segregation**.

---

## 1. Genetic Notation
In genetic crosses, we use letters to represent alleles:
- **Capital Letter (A):** Represents the **Dominant** allele. This allele is "stronger" and masks the recessive one. Biologically, it usually codes for a **functional protein**.
- **Lowercase Letter (a):** Represents the **Recessive** allele. This allele is "hidden" unless it is paired with another recessive allele. Biologically, it often codes for a **non-functional** or alternative protein.

## 2. Mendel's First Law
- **Law of Segregation**: A diploid organism passes a randomly selected allele for a trait to its offspring, such that the offspring receives one allele from each parent.

## Standard Cross
Cross between two heterozygous individuals (**Bb** x **Bb**).
- **B** = Brown eyes (Dominant)
- **b** = Blue eyes (Recessive)

### Punnett Square
| | **B** | **b** |
|---|---|---|
| **B** | **BB** (Homozygous Dominant) | **Bb** (Heterozygous) |
| **b** | **Bb** (Heterozygous) | **bb** (Homozygous Recessive) |

### Ratios
- **Genotypic Ratio**: 1:2:1 (1 BB : 2 Bb : 1 bb)
- **Phenotypic Ratio**: 3:1 (3 Brown Eyes : 1 Blue Eye)

## The Test Cross
Used to determine the genotype of an individual with a dominant phenotype (unknown if **BB** or **Bb**).
- **Method**: Cross the unknown individual with a **Homozygous Recessive (bb)** individual.
- **Result A**: If all offspring are dominant phenotype -> Unknown parent was **BB**.
- **Result B**: If 50% offspring are recessive -> Unknown parent was **Bb**.

---
### Q) Why is the phenotypic ratio 3:1 in a heterozygous cross?
**Model Answer:**
Because the dominant allele (**B**) masks the presence of the recessive allele (**b**) in heterozygotes (**Bb**). Therefore, both homozygous dominant (**BB**) and heterozygous (**Bb**) individuals express the dominant phenotype. Only homozygous recessive (**bb**) individuals show the recessive trait. This results in 3 dominant types for every 1 recessive type.
