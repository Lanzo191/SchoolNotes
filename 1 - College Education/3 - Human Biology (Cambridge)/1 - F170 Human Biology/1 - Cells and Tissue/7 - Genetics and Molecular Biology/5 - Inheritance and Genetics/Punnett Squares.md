## Overview
A **Punnett Square** is a simple graphical tool used to predict the possible **genotypes** and **phenotypes** of offspring from a particular genetic cross. It illustrates how alleles segregate and recombine according to Mendelian laws.

---

## How to Construct a Punnett Square

1.  **Identify Parent Genotypes:** Determine the alleles of both parents (e.g., *Bb* and *Bb*).
2.  **Determine Gametes:** Based on the **Law of Segregation**, each parent produces haploid gametes. (Parent *Bb* produces *B* and *b* gametes).
3.  **Set up the Grid:** Place one parent's gametes along the top and the other parent's along the side.
4.  **Fill the Squares:** Combine the gametes in each box to show the possible diploid zygotes.

### Example: Monohybrid Cross (One Gene)
**Scenario:** Crossing two heterozygous tall pea plants (*Tt* x *Tt*), where *T* (Tall) is dominant and *t* (short) is recessive.

| | **T** | **t** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **T** | **TT** (Tall) | **Tt** (Tall) |
| **t** | **Tt** (Tall) | **tt** (short) |

**Results:**
*   **Genotypic Ratio:** 1 *TT* : 2 *Tt* : 1 *tt* (1:2:1)
*   **Phenotypic Ratio:** 3 Tall : 1 short (3:1)

---

## Probability in Genetics
Punnett squares provide the **probability** of an outcome, not a guaranteed number of offspring.
*   Each "box" in a 2x2 grid represents a **25% (1/4)** chance.
*   In the example above, there is a 75% chance of a Tall plant and a 25% chance of a short plant.

### Q) A homozygous dominant brown-eyed parent (BB) crosses with a blue-eyed parent (bb). Use a Punnett square to predict the phenotype of the F1 generation.

**Model Answer:**
All offspring in the F1 generation will have the genotype **Bb**. Because the brown-eye allele (**B**) is dominant over the blue-eye allele (**b**), **100% of the offspring will have the brown-eyed phenotype**.

| | **B** | **B** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **b** | **Bb** | **Bb** |
| **b** | **Bb** | **Bb** |
