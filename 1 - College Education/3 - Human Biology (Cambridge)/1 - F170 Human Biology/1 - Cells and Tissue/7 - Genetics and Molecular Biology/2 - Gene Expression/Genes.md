## Overview
A **gene** is a specific sequence of **[[Nucleotides]]** in a **[[DNA]]** molecule that codes for a specific sequence of **[[Amino Acids]]** in a polypeptide chain (a **[[Proteins|protein]]**) or a functional **[[RNA]]** molecule. It is the fundamental unit of inheritance.

---

## Structure and Location
*   **Locus:** The specific fixed position of a gene on a **[[Chromosomes|chromosome]]**.
*   **Alleles:** Different versions of the same gene (e.g., coding for different eye colours).
*   **Exons:** The coding sequences of a gene that remain in the final **[[mRNA]]**.
*   **Introns:** Non-coding sequences that are removed during **[[Transcription|splicing]]**.

## How Genes Work
Genes act as instructions. The **[[Genetic Code]]** within the gene is read in triplets (codons) to determine the order of amino acids during **[[Protein Synthesis]]**.

| Term | Definition |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Genome** | The complete set of genes or genetic material present in a cell or organism. |
| **Genotype** | The genetic constitution of an individual organism. |
| **Phenotype** | The observable characteristics of an individual resulting from the interaction of its genotype with the environment. |

### Q) Define a "gene" and explain the significance of its "locus".

**Model Answer:**
A **gene** is a sequence of **[[DNA]]** bases that occupies a specific position, or **locus**, on a **[[Chromosomes|chromosome]]**. Each gene codes for a specific polypeptide or functional **[[RNA]]**. 

The significance of the **locus** is that it is consistent across individuals of the same species. This allows for homologous chromosomes to pair up correctly during **[[Meiosis]]**, ensuring that corresponding alleles are aligned for potential crossing over and accurate inheritance.
