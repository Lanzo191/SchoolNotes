**Messenger [[RNA]] (mRNA)** is a single-stranded molecule of [[RNA]] that corresponds to the genetic sequence of a gene and is read by a ribosome in the process of synthesizing a protein.

## Structure

### Molecular Architecture
- **Linear Polynucleotide**: A single strand of ribonucleotides.
- **[[Codon]]**: A sequence of three [[Nucleotides]] that together form a unit of [[Genetic Code]].
- **5' Cap**: A modified guanine [[Nucleotides]] added to the "front" to prevent degradation.
- **Poly-A Tail**: A long chain of adenine [[Nucleotides]] added to the 3' end to increase stability.
- **Exons**: The coding regions that remain after splicing.

### Characteristics
- **Short-lived**: mRNA is typically degraded quickly by the cell after [[Translation]] to ensure protein production stops when not needed.
- **Variable Length**: The length of the mRNA is directly proportional to the size of the protein it encodes.

## Function
- **Information Transfer**: Acts as the intermediate "messenger" that carries the genetic blueprint from the [[DNA]] in the [[Nucleus]] to the [[Ribosomes]] in the cytoplasm.
- **Template for [[Translation]]**: Provides the specific sequence that determines the primary structure (amino acid sequence) of a protein.

## Clinical Relevance
- **mRNA Vaccines**: (e.g., COVID-19 vaccines) use synthetic mRNA to instruct cells to produce a viral protein, triggering an immune response without using the actual virus.
- **Degradation Defects**: If mRNA is not degraded properly, it can lead to the overproduction of certain [[Proteins]], potentially contributing to cancer.

## Summary
mRNA is the transient, mobile copy of a gene that bridges the gap between the storage of genetic info ([[DNA]]) and the execution of biological function (Protein).
