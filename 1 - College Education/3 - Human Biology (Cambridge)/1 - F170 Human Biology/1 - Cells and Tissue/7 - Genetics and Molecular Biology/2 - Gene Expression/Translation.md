**Translation** is the decoding of an [[mRNA]] message into a polypeptide chain, occurring at the [[Ribosomes]] in the cytoplasm or on the Rough ER.

## Structure: The Ribosomal Complex
- **Small Subunit (40S)**: Binds the [[mRNA]] transcript first.
- **Large Subunit (60S)**: Contains three functional sites:
    - **A (Aminoacyl) site**: Entry point for charged [[tRNA]].
    - **P (Peptidyl) site**: Holds the growing polypeptide chain.
    - **E (Exit) site**: Where empty tRNAs depart.

## Mechanism
1. **Initiation**: The start [[Codon]] (AUG) is recognized by the initiator [[tRNA]] carrying Methionine.
2. **Elongation**: tRNAs bring [[Amino Acids]] to the ribosome. **Peptidyl transferase** (an [[rRNA]] ribozyme) forms peptide bonds between [[Amino Acids]].
3. **Termination**: A stop [[Codon]] (UAA, UAG, UGA) enters the A site. No [[tRNA]] matches; a **Release Factor** enters, causing the complex to dissociate.



## Function
- To translate the 4-letter [[Nucleotides]] "language" into the 20-letter amino acid "language," resulting in a primary protein structure.

## Clinical Relevance
- **Antibiotics**: Erythromycin and Tetracycline work by binding to bacterial [[Ribosomes]], preventing translation and killing the bacteria without affecting human cells.

## Summary
Translation is the energy-intensive process of protein assembly, where the [[Genetic Code]] is physically realized as a functional polypeptide.
