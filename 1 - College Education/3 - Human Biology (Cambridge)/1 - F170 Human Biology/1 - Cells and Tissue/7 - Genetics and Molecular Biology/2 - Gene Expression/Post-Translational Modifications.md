**Post-Translational Modifications** are the chemical changes a protein undergoes after its synthesis is complete. These modifications define the final 3D shape and biological activity of the protein.

## Structure: Common Modifications

### 1. Folding and Cleavage (Proteolysis)
- **Chaperone [[Proteins]]**: Assist the polypeptide in folding into its tertiary structure.
- **Zymogen Activation**: Many [[Proteins]] are synthesized in an inactive "pro-" form and must be cleaved to become active (e.g., **Proinsulin → [[Insulin]]**).

### 2. Addition of Chemical Groups
- **Phosphorylation**: Addition of a phosphate group (via Kinases). This acts as a molecular "on/off" switch for enzyme activity.
- **Glycosylation**: Addition of carbohydrate chains (in the [[Golgi Apparatus]]). Essential for cell-surface receptors and protection from proteases.

### 3. Lipidation
- Attaching lipid molecules to anchor the protein to the cell membrane.



## Function
- To increase the functional diversity of the proteome (the "one gene, many [[Proteins]]" concept).
- To regulate protein half-life and cellular localization.

## Clinical Relevance
- **Alzheimer’s Disease**: Hyperphosphorylation of the **Tau protein** leads to the formation of neurofibrillary tangles and cell death.

## Summary
PTMs are the final layer of control in gene expression, ensuring that [[Proteins]] are activated and targeted to the correct cellular location.
