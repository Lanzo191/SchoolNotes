A **codon** is a sequence of three **[[Nucleotides]]** in [[DNA]] or **[[mRNA]]** that corresponds to a specific **amino acid** or a signal to stop **[[Protein Synthesis]]**.

## Characteristics of the Genetic Code
1. **Triplet Code**: Three bases code for one amino acid.
2. **Degenerate**: Most [[Amino Acids]] are coded for by more than one codon (e.g., there are 6 different codons for Leucine). This helps protect against some mutations.
3. **Non-overlapping**: Each base is part of only one triplet.
4. **Universal**: The same codons code for the same [[Amino Acids]] in almost all living organisms.

---

## Important Codons
- **Start Codon (AUG)**: Signals the beginning of translation and codes for the amino acid **Methionine**.
- **Stop Codons (UAA, UAG, UGA)**: Do not code for amino acids; they signal the termination of the polypeptide chain.

---

## Codons vs. Anti-codons
| Feature | Codon | Anti-codon |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Location** | **mRNA** (and [[DNA]]) | **tRNA** |
| **Function** | Carries the genetic code from DNA. | Matches and binds to the codon. |
| **Base Pairing** | Complementary to anti-codon. | Complementary to codon. |
