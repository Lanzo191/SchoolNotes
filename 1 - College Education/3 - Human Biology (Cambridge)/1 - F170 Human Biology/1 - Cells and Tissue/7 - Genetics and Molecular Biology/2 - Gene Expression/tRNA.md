**Transfer [[RNA]] (tRNA)** is a small [[RNA]] molecule (typically 76 to 90 [[Nucleotides]] in length) that serves as the physical link between the [[mRNA]] and the amino acid sequence of [[Proteins]].

## Structure

### Cloverleaf Secondary Structure
- **Anticodon Loop**: A triplet of bases that is complementary to a specific [[mRNA]] [[Codon]].
- **Acceptor Stem**: The 3' end (always ends in the sequence CCA) where a specific amino acid is covalently attached.
- **D-Loop and T-Loop**: Specialized loops that assist in ribosome binding and recognition by enzymes.

### 3D L-Shape
- While the "cloverleaf" is the 2D representation, the molecule folds into a compact **L-shaped 3D structure** to fit into the A and P sites of the ribosome.

## Function
- **[[Translation]] / Decoding**: It "reads" the [[mRNA]] [[Codon]] and brings the corresponding amino acid to the ribosome.
- **Aminoacylation**: The process where an enzyme (aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase) "charges" the tRNA by attaching the correct amino acid.

## Clinical Relevance
- **Mitochondrial tRNA Mutations**: Mutations in tRNA [[Genes]] within the [[Mitochondria]] are linked to metabolic diseases like MELAS syndrome.
- **Antibiotic Interference**: Some antibiotics work by mimicking tRNA or blocking the tRNA binding sites on bacterial [[Ribosomes]].

## Summary
tRNA is the molecular "adapter" that translates the language of nucleic acids into the language of [[Proteins]].
