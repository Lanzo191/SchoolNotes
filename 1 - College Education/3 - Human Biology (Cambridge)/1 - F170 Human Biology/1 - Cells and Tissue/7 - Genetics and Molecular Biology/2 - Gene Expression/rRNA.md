**Ribosomal [[RNA]] (rRNA)** is the [[RNA]] component of the ribosome, which is essential for [[Protein Synthesis]] in all living organisms.

## Structure

### Ribosomal Composition
- **Large Subunit (LSU)**: Contains the active site where peptide bonds are formed.
- **Small Subunit (SSU)**: Responsible for binding the [[mRNA]] and ensuring correct base pairing with [[tRNA]].
- **Complex Folding**: rRNA folds into highly complex, stable three-dimensional shapes that provide the structural "scaffold" for ribosomal [[Proteins]].

### Location
- Synthesized and assembled with [[Proteins]] in the **Nucleolus** (a dense region within the [[Nucleus]]).

## Function
- **Catalysis (Ribozyme)**: Unlike most enzymes which are [[Proteins]], the catalytic heart of the ribosome is actually the rRNA. It catalyzes the **peptidyl transferase** reaction (forming the bond between [[Amino Acids]]).
- **Structural Support**: Holds the [[mRNA]] and tRNAs in the correct orientation so the code can be read accurately.

## Clinical Relevance
- **Ribosomopathies**: Diseases caused by defects in rRNA biogenesis, such as Diamond-Blackfan anemia.
- **Taxonomy**: Because rRNA sequences change very slowly over evolutionary time, the **16S rRNA** (in bacteria) is used by scientists to identify and classify different species of bacteria.

## Summary
rRNA is the most abundant type of [[RNA]] in the cell, acting as both the structural framework and the catalytic engine of the ribosome.
