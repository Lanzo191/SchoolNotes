## Overview
**[[Transcription]] factors** are [[Proteins]] that bind to specific **[[DNA]]** sequences to control the rate of **[[Transcription]]** from **[[DNA]]** to **[[mRNA]]**. They are the primary "switches" that regulate gene expression.

---

## Types of Factors
1.  **Activators:** Proteins that bind to **[[DNA]]** and *increase* the rate of transcription (e.g., by helping **[[RNA]] polymerase** bind).
2.  **Repressors:** Proteins that bind to **[[DNA]]** and *decrease* the rate of transcription (e.g., by blocking **[[RNA]] polymerase**).

## Regulatory Regions
*   **Promoter:** A region of **[[DNA]]** located just "upstream" of a gene where transcription factors and **[[RNA]] polymerase** bind to initiate transcription.
*   **Enhancer/Silencer:** Distant sequences of **[[DNA]]** that can increase (enhancer) or decrease (silencer) the activity of a promoter.

## Coordination of Genes
Transcription factors allow for the **coordination** of gene expression. Multiple genes can have the same regulatory sequence, allowing one transcription factor to "switch on" a whole suite of genes simultaneously (e.g., during a stress response).

---

### Q) Describe the difference between an activator and a repressor.

**Model Answer:**
An **activator** is a transcription factor that stimulates gene expression. It binds to regulatory regions of **[[DNA]]** and makes it easier for **[[RNA]] polymerase** to bind to the promoter, thus increasing the production of **[[mRNA]]**. 

A **repressor**, on the other hand, inhibits gene expression. It binds to the **[[DNA]]** in a way that physically blocks **[[RNA]] polymerase** from attaching to the promoter or moving along the gene, thus preventing **[[Transcription]]**.
