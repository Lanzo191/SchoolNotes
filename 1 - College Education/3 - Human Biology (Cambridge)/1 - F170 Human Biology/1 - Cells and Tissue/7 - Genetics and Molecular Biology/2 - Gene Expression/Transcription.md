**Transcription** is the enzymatic process of creating a complementary [[RNA]] copy from a [[DNA]] template. It is the first stage of gene expression, occurring within the [[Nucleus]] of eukaryotes.

## Structure: The Transcriptional Unit
- **Promoter**: A specific [[DNA]] sequence (e.g., TATA box) that signals the start site for [[RNA]] polymerase.
- **[[Transcription Factors]]**: [[Proteins]] that bind to the promoter to facilitate or inhibit the binding of [[RNA]] polymerase.
- **[[RNA]] Polymerase II**: The primary enzyme responsible for synthesizing [[mRNA]].

## Mechanism
1. **Initiation**: [[RNA]] polymerase unwinds the [[DNA]] double helix, creating a "transcription bubble."
2. **Elongation**: The enzyme reads the **Template (Antisense) Strand** in a 3' to 5' direction, adding free [[RNA]] [[Nucleotides]] to form a strand in the 5' to 3' direction.
3. **Termination**: The enzyme reaches a termination sequence and releases the newly formed **pre-[[mRNA]]** transcript.



## Function
- To produce a mobile "working copy" of the [[Genetic Code]] that can exit the nuclear pores while the original [[DNA]] remains protected within the [[Nucleus]].

## Clinical Relevance
- **Mushroom Poisoning**: $\alpha$-amanitin binds to [[RNA]] polymerase II, stopping transcription entirely and leading to rapid organ failure.

## Summary
Transcription is the high-fidelity transfer of information from [[DNA]] to [[RNA]], regulated by complex protein-[[DNA]] interactions at the promoter site.
