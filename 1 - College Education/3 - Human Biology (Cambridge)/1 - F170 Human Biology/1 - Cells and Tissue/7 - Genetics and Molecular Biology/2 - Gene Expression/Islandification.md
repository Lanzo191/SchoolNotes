**Islandification**, specifically referred to as **Foster's Rule** or the **Island Rule**, describes a macroevolutionary pattern where members of a species get smaller or larger depending on the resources available in the environment.

## Structure: The Two Directions

### Insular Dwarfism
- Large species become smaller over generations when their population's gene pool is limited to a small environment (island).
- **Causes**: Limited food resources, lack of large predators (which removes the selective advantage of being large).
- **Examples**: Pygmy elephants, *Homo floresiensis* (the "Hobbit" hominid).

### Insular Gigantism
- Small species become significantly larger.
- **Causes**: Lack of competition from other species, abundance of niche space, or "island safety" allowing for slower metabolic rates and larger body mass.
- **Examples**: The Dodo (giant pigeon), Galapagos Tortoises, Komodo Dragons.

## Function
- This represents **directional selection** acting on a population's size to optimize metabolic efficiency and reproductive success within a confined geographical area.

## Clinical/Biological Relevance
- **Founder Effect**: Island populations often start from a very small number of individuals, leading to a restricted gene pool and an increased frequency of specific mutations.

## Summary
Islandification proves that "size" is a plastic trait in evolution, heavily dictated by the balance between caloric availability and predator-prey dynamics.
