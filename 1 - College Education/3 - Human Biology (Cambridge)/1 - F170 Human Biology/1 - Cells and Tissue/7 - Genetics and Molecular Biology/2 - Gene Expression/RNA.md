**Ribonucleic Acid (RNA)** is a versatile nucleic acid that serves various roles in the cell, from information carrier to biological catalyst.

## Structure: Chemical Properties
- **Sugar**: Ribose (contains an -OH group on the 2' carbon, making it more reactive than [[DNA]]).
- **Bases**: Adenine (A), Guanine (G), Cytosine (C), and **Uracil (U)** (replaces Thymine).
- **Secondary Structure**: Single-stranded but capable of complex folding (hairpins and loops) via intramolecular base pairing.

## The Three Primary Types

### 1. Messenger RNA (mRNA)
- **Structure**: Long, linear strand with a 5' Methyl-Guanosine cap and a 3' Poly-A tail.
- **Function**: Carries the genetic blueprint from the [[Nucleus]] to the ribosome.

### 2. Transfer RNA (tRNA)
- **Structure**: Cloverleaf 2D shape, L-shaped 3D structure. Contains an **Anticodon** and a **3' Amino Acid attachment site**.
- **Function**: Transfers specific [[Amino Acids]] to the ribosome during [[Translation]].

### 3. Ribosomal RNA (rRNA)
- **Structure**: Complex, globular folding integrated with [[Proteins]] to form [[Ribosomes]].
- **Function**: Provides structural support and catalyzes peptide bond formation (ribozyme activity).



## Summary
While [[DNA]] stores information, the different forms of RNA are the active machinery that executes the genetic program.
