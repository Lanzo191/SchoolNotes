## Overview
**Homeobox [[Genes]]** are a group of [[Genes]] that contain a specific **180-base-pair** sequence called the **homeobox**. They play a vital role in controlling the development of the body plan in eukaryotes, ensuring that body parts develop in the correct locations.

## Key Features
*   **Homeodomain:** The homeobox sequence codes for a **60-amino-acid** polypeptide called the **homeodomain**.
*   **[[Transcription]] Factors:** The homeodomain acts as a **[[Transcription Factors|transcription factor]]**. It binds to specific regions of **[[DNA]]** to "switch on" or "switch off" other [[Genes]].
*   **Highly Conserved:** These [[Genes]] are **highly conserved**, meaning they have remained virtually unchanged across millions of years of evolution. They are found in animals, plants, and fungi.

## Hox Genes
**Hox [[Genes]]** are a specific subset of homeobox [[Genes]] found only in animals.
*   They are arranged in clusters on **[[Chromosomes]]**.
*   The order of the [[Genes]] on the chromosome matches the order of the body segments they control (colinearity).
*   **Apoptosis:** Hox [[Genes]] also regulate **[[Apoptosis]]** (programmed cell death) to refine the body shape during development (e.g., removing the webbing between fingers).

### Q) Why are homeobox genes described as "highly conserved"?

**Model Answer:**
Homeobox genes are described as **highly conserved** because their sequences are very similar across widely different species, from fruit flies to humans. This suggests they are fundamental to life and that any significant **[[Mutation Effects|mutation]]** in these genes would likely be lethal or severely disadvantageous, causing them to be eliminated by natural selection. Their consistency across millions of years highlights their critical role in early embryonic development and body plan organization.
