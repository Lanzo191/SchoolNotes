## Overview
**Oestrogen** is a steroid hormone that plays a key role in regulating gene expression. Because it is lipid-soluble, it can cross [[Cell Membranes]] via simple **[[Osmosis|diffusion]]** to reach its receptors inside the cell.

---

## The Mechanism of Action
1.  **Diffusion:** Oestrogen diffuses through the phospholipid bilayer of the cell-surface membrane.
2.  **Binding:** Inside the cytoplasm, oestrogen binds to a specific **oestrogen receptor (ER)**.
3.  **Conformational Change:** When oestrogen binds, the receptor changes shape and releases an inhibitor molecule.
4.  **Hormone-Receptor Complex:** the complex moves into the nucleus through a nuclear pore.
5.  **Transcription Factor:** The complex binds to specific **[[DNA]]** sequences (promoters) near the target gene.
6.  **Gene Activation:** This binding allows **[[RNA]] polymerase** to bind and begin **[[Transcription]]**, effectively switching the gene "on".

## Examples of Regulation
*   **Oestrogen** regulates genes involved in the **[[1 - Menstrual Cycle Index|menstrual cycle]]** and the development of female secondary sexual characteristics.

### Q) Explain how oestrogen acts as a transcription factor.

**Model Answer:**
Oestrogen acts as a **[[Transcription Factors|transcription factor]]** by binding to a receptor in the cytoplasm. Once the **hormone-receptor complex** is formed, it migrates into the nucleus. Here, it binds to a specific region of **[[DNA]]** called a promoter. This binding facilitates the recruitment of **[[RNA]] polymerase**, which then initiates the **[[Transcription]]** of the target gene into **[[mRNA]]**. Without the hormone-receptor complex, the gene would remain inactive.
