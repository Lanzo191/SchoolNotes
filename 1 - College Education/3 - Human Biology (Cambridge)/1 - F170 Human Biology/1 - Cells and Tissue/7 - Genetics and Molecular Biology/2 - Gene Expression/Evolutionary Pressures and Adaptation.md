**Evolutionary pressures** are external factors that affect an organism's ability to survive in a given environment and influence the frequency of specific alleles within a population.

## Structure: Types of Pressures

### Abiotic Pressures
- Non-living factors such as climate change, temperature shifts, water availability, and geographical barriers (e.g., mountains, oceans).

### Biotic Pressures
- Living factors including **Predation**, **Competition** for resources/mates, and **Disease** (pathogenic pressure).

### Types of Selection
1. **Stabilising Selection**: Favors intermediate phenotypes (e.g., human birth weight).
2. **Directional Selection**: Favors one extreme (e.g., antibiotic resistance in bacteria).
3. **Disruptive Selection**: Favors both extremes, potentially leading to speciation.

## Function
- These pressures act as the "filter" for mutations. While mutations are random, the survival and inheritance of those mutations are determined by these pressures.

## Clinical Relevance
- **Antibiotic Resistance**: The presence of antibiotics acts as a powerful directional pressure, selecting for bacteria with mutations that confer resistance.

## Summary
Evolution is not a random walk; it is a structured response to environmental pressures that prune the gene pool of less "fit" alleles.
