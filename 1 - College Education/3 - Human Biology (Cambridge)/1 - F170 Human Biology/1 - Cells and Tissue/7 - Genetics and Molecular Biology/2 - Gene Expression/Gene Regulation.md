**Gene regulation** refers to the wide range of mechanisms used by cells to increase or decrease the production of specific gene products (protein or [[RNA]]).

## Structure: Levels of Control

### Transcriptional Control (Primary)
- **[[Transcription Factors]]**: [[Proteins]] like **Activators** and **Repressors** that control the rate of production.
- **Promoters**: [[DNA]] sequences where [[RNA]] polymerase binds.
- **Enhancers and Silencers**: Distant [[DNA]] sequences that increase or decrease [[Transcription]].
- **[[Oestrogen and Gene Regulation|Role of Hormones]]**: How lipid-soluble signals like **Oestrogen** trigger gene expression.
- **[[Homeobox Genes]]**: Master switches that control the anatomical development of the body plan.
- **Operons (Prokaryotes)**: Clusters of [[Genes]] under the control of a single promoter (e.g., the *lac* operon).

### Epigenetic Control
- **[[DNA]] Methylation**: Adding methyl groups to cytosine bases usually "switches off" [[Genes]].
- **Histone Acetylation**: Relaxes chromatin structure, making [[DNA]] more accessible for [[Transcription]].

### Post-Transcriptional Control
- **[[RNA]] Interference (RNAi)**: Small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) can lead to the degradation of specific [[mRNA]] transcripts.

## Function
- Allows for **cell differentiation** (all cells have the same [[DNA]] but express different [[Proteins]])
- Enables cells to respond to environmental changes (e.g., [[Glucose]] vs. lactose availability).

## Clinical Relevance
- **Epigenetic Therapy**: Drugs that inhibit [[DNA]] methyltransferase are being used to "reactivate" tumor suppressor [[Genes]] in cancer patients.
- **Huntington’s Disease**: Involves dysregulation of gene expression at the transcriptional level.

## Summary
Gene regulation is a multi-layered system that ensures [[Proteins]] are produced at the right time, in the right place, and in the right amounts.
