# Mechanisms of Hormone Action

## Overview
Hormones are chemical messengers that act on target cells. Their mechanism depends on their chemical structure.

## Peptide vs. Steroid Hormones
| Feature | **Peptide / Protein** | **Steroid** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Solubility** | Water-soluble (Lipid-insoluble) | Lipid-soluble (Water-insoluble) |
| **Example** | **Insulin**, ADH, Glucagon. | **Oestrogen**, Testosterone, Cortisol. |
| **Mechanism** | Binds to **cell-surface receptors**; triggers a **second messenger** (e.g., cAMP). | Crosses the cell membrane; binds to an **intracellular receptor**. |
| **Response** | Rapid, short-lived effect (e.g., enzyme activation). | Slower, long-lived effect (e.g., gene transcription). |

## Second Messenger System (Peptide)
1. Hormone (1st messenger) binds to receptor.
2. Receptor activates **G-protein**.
3. G-protein activates **adenylyl cyclase**, which converts ATP to **cAMP** (2nd messenger).
4. cAMP triggers a protein kinase cascade, causing the cellular response.

## Intracellular Action (Steroid)
1. Hormone crosses the cell membrane by simple diffusion.
2. Binds to a receptor in the cytoplasm or nucleus.
3. The **hormone-receptor complex** acts as a **transcription factor**, binding to DNA and switching genes on or off.
