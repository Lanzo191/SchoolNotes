**Skeletal muscle** is a voluntary, striated [[Muscle Tissue]] responsible for movement, posture, and heat production. It is under conscious control and attaches to bones via tendons.

## Structure
- Composed of **muscle fibres**, which are long, multinucleated cells surrounded by a **sarcolemma** (plasma membrane).  
- Each muscle fibre contains numerous **myofibrils**, which are cylindrical structures made up of **actin (thin)** and **myosin (thick)** filaments.  
- **Sarcomeres** are the repeating contractile units within myofibrils, delineated by Z-lines, giving the muscle its striated appearance.  
- The **sarcoplasmic reticulum (SR)** stores and releases [[Calcium]] ions required for contraction.  
- **[[Mitochondria]]** are abundant to meet high [[ATP]] demands during contraction.  
- The muscle is organised into three [[Connective Tissue]] layers:  
  - **Epimysium**: Surrounds the entire muscle.  
  - **Perimysium**: Encloses bundles of fibres called fascicles.  
  - **Endomysium**: Surrounds individual muscle fibres.  

## Function
- Contraction occurs via the **sliding filament theory**, where actin and myosin filaments slide past each other, shortening the sarcomere.  
- **[[ATP]]** and **[[Calcium]] ions** are required for cross-bridge formation and detachment.  
- Controlled by the **somatic nervous system** through **neuromuscular junctions**, where **acetylcholine** triggers depolarisation and contraction.  
- Responsible for voluntary body movement, maintaining posture, and generating heat through shivering thermogenesis.  

## Adaptations
- High density of [[Mitochondria]] for aerobic respiration.  
- Rich blood supply for oxygen and nutrient delivery.  
- Presence of **myoglobin** to store oxygen.  
- Fibres can be **slow-twitch (type I)** for endurance or **fast-twitch (type II)** for rapid, powerful contractions.
