# Sliding Filament Theory

## Overview
The **Sliding Filament Theory** explains how skeletal muscle contracts as thin **actin** filaments slide over thick **myosin** filaments, shortening the **sarcomere**.

## The Process
1. **Ca2+ Release**: An action potential triggers the sarcoplasmic reticulum to release **calcium ions (Ca2+)**.
2. **Binding Sites Revealed**: Ca2+ binds to **troponin**, causing **tropomyosin** to move and expose binding sites on the actin.
3. **Cross-Bridge Formation**: Myosin heads bind to actin, forming cross-bridges.
4. **The Power Stroke**: Myosin heads tilt, pulling actin filaments toward the **M-line**.
5. **ATP Attachment**: **ATP** binds to the myosin head, causing it to detach from actin.
6. **Hydrolysis**: ATP is broken down into ADP and Pi, providing energy to "cock" the myosin head back to its high-energy position.

## Key Structures
| Structure | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Actin** | Thin protein filament with binding sites for myosin. |
| **Myosin** | Thick protein filament with "heads" that pull on actin. |
| **Sarcomere** | The unit of contraction between two **Z-lines**. |
| **Troponin/Tropomyosin** | Regulatory proteins that control myosin-actin binding. |

## Key Point
The filaments themselves **do not shorten**; they slide past each other, causing the overall muscle fiber to shorten.
