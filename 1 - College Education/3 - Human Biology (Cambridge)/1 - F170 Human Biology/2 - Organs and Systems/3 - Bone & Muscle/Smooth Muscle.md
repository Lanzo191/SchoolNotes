**Smooth muscle** is an involuntary, non-striated muscle found in the walls of hollow organs such as the intestines, blood vessels, and bladder.

## Structure
- Composed of **spindle-shaped cells** with a single central [[Nucleus]].  
- Lacks sarcomeres; actin and myosin filaments are arranged irregularly, producing smooth contractions.  
- Contains **dense bodies** that anchor actin filaments, functioning similarly to Z-lines in [[Skeletal Muscle]].  
- Surrounded by a thin layer of [[Connective Tissue]] to support and coordinate contractions.  

## Function
- Controlled by the **autonomic nervous system**, **hormones**, and **local factors** rather than conscious thought.  
- Contracts slowly and can maintain tension for long periods with low energy expenditure.  
- Found in the **digestive tract** (peristalsis), **[[Arteries]]** ([[Vasoconstriction]]/[[Vasodilation]]), **respiratory system**, and **reproductive organs**.  
- Uses **[[Calcium]]–calmodulin** instead of troponin to initiate contraction.  

## Adaptations
- Extremely **fatigue-resistant** due to efficient [[ATP]] use.  
- Capable of **myogenic activity**, meaning it can contract without neural stimulation.  
- Can stretch and contract over a wide range, vital for organ function such as bladder filling.
