Peristaltic contractions are rhythmic, wave-like movements of [[Smooth Muscle]] in the walls of tubular organs that propel contents along the tract.

## Definition
Peristaltic contractions are coordinated sequences of muscle contraction and relaxation that move luminal contents in one direction through hollow organs.

## Mechanism
- Peristalsis involves alternating activity of circular and longitudinal muscle layers.  
- Circular muscle contracts behind the bolus or chyme to constrict the lumen and push the content forward.  
- Longitudinal muscle contracts ahead of the bolus to shorten and widen the tract, reducing resistance to movement.  
- The coordinated sequence produces a travelling wave of contraction which moves material progressively along the organ.

## Control
- Peristaltic activity is controlled involuntarily by the enteric nervous system and modulated by the autonomic nervous system.  
- Local reflexes initiated by stretch receptors in the gut wall can trigger peristalsis without central input.  
- Hormones, paracrine factors, and local chemical environment can increase or decrease peristaltic frequency and strength.

## Locations and Examples
- In the [[Oesophagus]], peristalsis propels the swallowed bolus into the [[Stomach]].  
- In the small and large intestines, peristalsis mixes luminal contents and moves chyme or faeces toward the next segment.  
- In the ureters and urinary bladder, similar peristaltic waves move urine from the kidneys to the bladder and expel it.

## Purpose
- Ensures unidirectional transport of contents, preventing stagnation.  
- Promotes mixing with digestive secretions and enhances exposure of substrates to enzymes.  
- Helps maintain fluid movement and prevents bacterial overgrowth by continuous transit.
