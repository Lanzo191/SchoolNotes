Muscle contractions can be classified by how muscle length and tension change, and by the pattern of neural stimulation. The major types are described below.

## Isotonic Contraction
- In an isotonic contraction, the muscle changes length while the tension remains approximately constant.  
- Concentric contractions occur when the muscle shortens while generating force, for example when lifting a weight.  
- Eccentric contractions occur when the muscle lengthens under tension, for example when lowering a weight in a controlled manner.  
- Isotonic contractions produce movement of body segments and perform mechanical work.

## Isometric Contraction
- In an isometric contraction, muscle tension increases but the muscle length does not change.  
- Examples include holding a heavy object steady or maintaining posture against gravity.  
- Isometric contractions stabilise joints and allow the body to resist external forces without changing position.

## Twitch Contraction
- A twitch is a single, brief contraction and relaxation cycle of a muscle fibre in response to a single [[Action Potential]].  
- The twitch has three phases: latent period, contraction phase, and relaxation phase.  
- Twitches are most often observed in isolated muscle studies and rarely occur alone during voluntary activity.

## Tetanic Contraction
- Tetanic contraction occurs when repeated stimuli arrive in rapid succession so that the muscle cannot relax between stimuli.  
- Temporal [[Summation]] produces a sustained and smooth contraction, which can be partial (incomplete tetanus) or fused (complete tetanus).  
- Tetanic contractions are used during sustained activities that require steady force.

## Smooth Muscle Specific Patterns
- [[Smooth Muscle]] exhibits slow, sustained contractions and can display rhythmic contractile patterns such as peristalsis or segmentation in the gut.  
- [[Smooth Muscle]] contractions are often graded and can be maintained for long periods with low energy cost.

## Molecular Basis and Energy
- All contractions involve cycling interactions between actin and myosin powered by [[ATP]] hydrolysis.  
- [[Calcium]] ions released from intracellular stores or entering from extracellular fluid regulate cross-bridge cycling in different muscle types.  
- Different contraction types vary in [[ATP]] demand and fatigue resistance; for example, isometric holds and slow [[Smooth Muscle]] contractions require relatively low [[ATP]] turnover compared with repeated fast isotonic contractions.
