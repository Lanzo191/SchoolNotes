**Bone tissue** is a specialised [[Connective Tissue]] that provides structural support, protection, and a site for mineral storage and blood cell formation.

## Structure
- Composed of a **calcified matrix** containing **collagen fibres**, which provide flexibility and tensile strength.  
- The matrix is impregnated with **[[Calcium]] phosphate crystals (hydroxyapatite)**, giving the bone rigidity and compressive strength.  
- Contains **osteocytes** within small cavities called **lacunae**, connected by microscopic canals known as **canaliculi** for nutrient and waste exchange.  
- The **periosteum** covers the outer surface, containing blood vessels, nerves, and osteoprogenitor cells.  
- The **extracellular matrix (ECM)** consists of organic (collagen, [[Proteins]]) and inorganic (minerals) components.  

## Cell Types
- **[[Osteoblasts]]**: Bone-forming cells responsible for secreting the organic matrix and initiating mineralisation.  
- **Osteocytes**: Mature bone cells derived from [[Osteoblasts]] that maintain the matrix.  
- **[[Osteoclasts]]**: Large, multinucleated cells that resorb and remodel bone tissue.  

## Function
- Provides mechanical support for muscles and organs.  
- Protects vital structures such as the brain, heart, and [[Lungs]].  
- Acts as a **mineral reservoir**, especially for [[Calcium]] and phosphate.  
- Contains **bone marrow**, which produces blood cells (hematopoiesis).  
- Serves as a lever system for movement with skeletal muscles.
