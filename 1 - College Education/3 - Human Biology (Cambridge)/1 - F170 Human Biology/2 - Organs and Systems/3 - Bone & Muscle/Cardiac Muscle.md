**Cardiac muscle** is a specialised, involuntary, striated muscle found only in the heart. It contracts rhythmically and continuously throughout life.

## Structure
- Composed of **branched cells** joined end-to-end by **intercalated discs**, which contain **gap junctions** allowing electrical impulses to spread rapidly between cells.  
- Each cell has a single central [[Nucleus]] and abundant **[[Mitochondria]]** for continuous [[ATP]] production.  
- Organised into **sarcomeres**, giving it a striated appearance similar to [[Skeletal Muscle]].  
- Richly supplied with **blood vessels** to provide oxygen and nutrients.  

## Function
- Contracts rhythmically and involuntarily, pumping blood through the [[Circulatory System]].  
- **Myogenic** the heartbeat originates within the muscle itself (sinoatrial node).  
- **[[Calcium]] ions** enter the cell through both the sarcoplasmic reticulum and extracellular fluid, sustaining longer contractions than [[Skeletal Muscle]].  
- Resistant to fatigue due to continuous aerobic metabolism.  

## Adaptations
- High mitochondrial density to meet constant energy demand.  
- Intercalated discs synchronise contraction for efficient pumping.  
- Rich capillary network for oxygen supply.  
- Maintains **homeostasis** through self-regulated rhythmic contraction.
