**Villi** are finger-like projections of the [[Small Intestine]] mucosa that dramatically increase surface area to maximise nutrient absorption.

## Structure
- **Large surface area**: Thousands of villi line each square centimetre of the [[Ileum]] wall, and each epithelial cell contains [[Microvilli]], further multiplying the absorptive surface.  

- **Epithelial layer**: One cell thick, forming a short diffusion pathway for nutrients to move quickly from the lumen into the blood or lymph.  
- **[[Capillaries]] and lacteals**: Each villus contains a network of [[Capillaries]] for water-soluble nutrients (e.g., [[Glucose]] and [[Amino Acids]]) and a central lacteal for lipid absorption.

- **Blood supply**: Rich capillary network with continuous blood flow maintains a steep concentration gradient for efficient nutrient uptake.  

- **Drainage**: Blood carrying absorbed nutrients drains into the [[Hepatic Portal Vein]], transporting nutrients directly to the [[Liver]] for processing and storage.

## Function
- Maximises the surface area for efficient absorption of [[Carbohydrates]], [[Proteins]], lipids, vitamins, and minerals.  
- Ensures nutrients can pass rapidly into the circulatory or [[Lymphatic System]] due to short diffusion distances.  
- Maintains steep concentration gradients to sustain efficient absorption.  
- Facilitates transport of nutrients to the [[Liver]] for metabolism and storage.
