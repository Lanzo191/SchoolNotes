The **gallbladder** is a small, muscular, pear-shaped organ located beneath the [[Liver|Liver]]. It stores and concentrates bile produced by the [[Liver|Liver]] and releases it into the [[Small Intestine]] during digestion.

## Structure

- The gallbladder lies in a depression on the underside of the right lobe of the [[Liver|Liver]].
- It is divided into three regions: **fundus**, **body**, and **neck**, which connects to the **cystic duct**.
- The cystic duct joins the **common hepatic duct** (from the [[Liver|Liver]]) to form the **common bile duct**, which empties into the [[Duodenum]].
- Its walls contain **[[Smooth Muscle]]** and are lined with **mucosal folds** that allow expansion.


## Function

- **Bile storage:** The [[Liver|Liver]] produces bile continuously, but the gallbladder stores it between meals.

- **Bile concentration:** The gallbladder absorbs water and electrolytes from bile, making it more concentrated.

- **Bile release:** When fatty food enters the [[Duodenum]], the hormone **cholecystokinin (CCK)** is released, triggering contraction of the gallbladder and relaxation of the bile duct sphincter (**sphincter of Oddi**).

- The concentrated bile then enters the [[Duodenum]] to emulsify fats.


## Composition of Bile

- **Bile salts:** Derived from cholesterol, these emulsify fats to increase surface area for enzyme action.

- **Bilirubin:** A waste pigment formed from the breakdown of haemoglobin.

- **Cholesterol and phospholipids:** Help form micelles for fat digestion.

- **Electrolytes and water:** Maintain bile fluidity.


## Regulation

- **Hormonal control:**

- **CCK** stimulates gallbladder contraction and bile release.
- **Secretin** from the [[Duodenum]] increases bile production in the [[Liver|Liver]].

- **Neural control:**

- Parasympathetic stimulation (via the vagus nerve) promotes gallbladder contraction.
- Sympathetic activity inhibits it.

## Clinical Relevance

- **Gallstones (cholelithiasis):** Hardened deposits of cholesterol or bile pigments can block the bile ducts, causing pain and jaundice.

- **Cholecystitis:** Inflammation of the gallbladder, often due to gallstones obstructing bile flow.

- **Bile duct obstruction:** Can lead to fat malabsorption and pale stools due to lack of bile in the intestine.

- **Gallbladder removal (cholecystectomy):** After removal, bile flows directly from the [[Liver|Liver]] to the [[Small Intestine]].


## Summary

The gallbladder is essential for storing and concentrating bile and coordinating its release into the [[Small Intestine]]. It works in close cooperation with the **[[Liver|Liver]]** and **[[Pancreas]]** to ensure efficient digestion of fats.
