The route of food through the human digestive system involves both **mechanical** and **chemical digestion**, allowing complex macromolecules to be broken down into smaller, absorbable units. Each organ has a specialised role in digestion, absorption, or transport.

## 1. Mouth (Oral Cavity)
- **Mechanical digestion** begins in the [[Mouth]] as food is chewed by the teeth, increasing surface area for enzymes to act on.  
- **Saliva**, produced by the **salivary glands**, contains **amylase**, which starts the chemical breakdown of starch into maltose.  
- The **tongue** mixes food with saliva and shapes it into a **bolus** for swallowing.  
- The [[Mouth]] maintains a slightly **alkaline pH (7.0–7.4)**, optimal for salivary amylase activity.

## 2. Pharynx and Oesophagus
- The **[[Pharynx]]** serves as a passageway for both food and air. During swallowing, the **epiglottis** closes over the [[Trachea]] to prevent food from entering the airways.  
- The **[[Oesophagus]]** is a muscular tube that uses **[[Peristaltic Contractions]]** to move the bolus toward the [[Stomach]].  
- No digestion occurs here; the process is mechanical transport only.  
- The **cardiac sphincter** prevents acidic contents of the [[Stomach]] from refluxing into the [[Oesophagus]].

## 3. Stomach
- The **[[Stomach]]** temporarily stores food and begins **protein digestion**.  
- Gastric glands secrete **gastric juice** containing:  
  - **Hydrochloric acid (HCl):** Maintains a highly acidic pH (around 1.5–2.0), denatures [[Proteins]], and destroys pathogens.  
  - **Pepsinogen:** Inactive form of pepsin, activated by HCl to break down [[Proteins]] into shorter peptide chains.  
  - **Mucus:** Protects the [[Stomach]] lining from acid corrosion.  
- The [[Stomach]]’s muscular walls perform **churning movements**, mixing food with digestive juices to form a semi-liquid called **chyme**.  
- The **pyloric sphincter** controls the slow release of chyme into the [[Duodenum]].

## 4. Duodenum
- The **[[Duodenum]]** is the first section of the [[Small Intestine]] and the main site for **chemical digestion**.  
- It receives **bile** from the **[[Gallbladder]]** and **pancreatic juice** from the **[[Pancreas]]**.  
- **Bile** emulsifies lipids into smaller droplets, increasing the surface area for enzyme action.  
- **Pancreatic enzymes** include:  
  - **Amylase:** Continues the breakdown of starch into maltose.  
  - **Lipase:** Hydrolyses lipids into fatty acids and glycerol.  
  - **Trypsin and chymotrypsin:** Continue the breakdown of polypeptides into shorter chains.  
- The [[Duodenum]] maintains an **alkaline pH (7.5–8.0)** as **[[Sodium]] hydrogen carbonate** neutralises the acidic chyme entering from the [[Stomach]].

## 5. Jejunum
- The **jejunum** is the middle section of the [[Small Intestine]] where most **nutrient absorption** occurs.  
- Its inner lining is covered with **villi** and **[[Microvilli]]**, dramatically increasing the surface area for absorption.  
- Enzymes embedded in the **brush border membrane** complete digestion:  
  - **Maltase, sucrase, and lactase** convert disaccharides into monosaccharides.  
  - **Peptidases** break down peptides into [[Amino Acids]].  
- Nutrients such as [[Glucose]] and [[Amino Acids]] enter **blood [[Capillaries]]**, while fatty acids and glycerol are absorbed into **lacteals** of the [[Lymphatic System]].

## 6. Ileum
- The **[[Ileum]]** is the final part of the [[Small Intestine]].  
- It absorbs **vitamin B₁₂**, **bile salts**, and any remaining nutrients not absorbed earlier.  
- Contains **Peyer’s patches**, part of the **immune system**, which monitor intestinal bacteria and prevent infection.  
- Absorbed nutrients enter the **[[Hepatic Portal Vein]]**, which transports them directly to the **[[Liver]]** for processing, detoxification, and storage.

## 7. Large Intestine (Colon)
- The **large intestine** absorbs **water and electrolytes** from the remaining indigestible material, forming solid **faeces**.  
- **Beneficial bacteria** in the colon synthesise **vitamins B and K**.  
- The **rectum** stores faeces until defecation through the **anus**, completing the digestive process.

## Summary
The digestive system efficiently breaks down food into absorbable molecules, absorbs nutrients through the [[Small Intestine]], and eliminates waste. This process ensures energy and essential molecules are available for growth, repair, and metabolic function throughout the body.
