
| Feature                    | [[Duodenum]]                                                                                                 | [[Ileum]]                                                                                                     | How the Structure Supports Function                                                                                                                                              |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Position**               | First part of the [[Small Intestine]], directly after the [[Stomach]]                                        | Last part of the [[Small Intestine]], before the large intestine.                                             | The [[Duodenum]] receives chyme from the [[Stomach]] and secretions from the [[Pancreas]] and [[Liver]]; the [[Ileum]]absorbs remaining nutrients before waste enters the colon. |
| **Main Function**          | Chemical digestion of food using enzymes and bile.                                                           | Absorption of nutrients, especially vitamin B12 and bile salts.                                               | The [[Duodenum]] role in digestion is supported by its connection to digestive glands; the [[Ileum]]’s long length and [[Vili]] increase absorption.                             |
| **pH**                     | Slightly alkaline (around 7–8).                                                                              | Slightly alkaline (around 7–8).                                                                               | The alkaline pH neutralizes acidic chyme, creating optimal conditions for enzyme activity throughout the [[Small Intestine]].                                                    |
| **Internal Surface**       | Has large circular folds (plicae circulares) and many villi.                                                 | Has fewer folds but very long villi and more Peyer’s patches (lymphoid tissue).                               | The [[Duodenum]]’s folds slow chyme for digestion; the [[Ileum]]’s villi maximize surface area for nutrient absorption and immune protection.                                    |
| **Secretions**             | Receives bile from the [[Liver]]/[[Gallbladder]] and enzymes from the [[Pancreas]].                          | Produces intestinal juices (mucus and enzymes).                                                               | Duodenal secretions aid digestion; ileal secretions protect the lining and continue absorption.                                                                                  |
| **Enzymes Active**         | Pancreatic amylase, lipase, proteases (e.g., trypsin).                                                       | Brush-border enzymes (e.g., maltase, peptidase).                                                              | Duodenal enzymes break down macronutrients; ileal enzymes finish digestion into absorbable forms.                                                                                |
| **Blood and Lymph Supply** | Rich arterial blood supply from branches of the celiac trunk; lymph drains to pancreatic and duodenal nodes. | Supplied by branches of the superior mesenteric artery; lymph drains via Peyer’s patches to mesenteric nodes. | Both have extensive vascular and lymphatic networks to efficiently absorb and transport nutrients.                                                                               |
| **Wall Thickness**         | Thicker and more muscular.                                                                                   | Thinner wall.                                                                                                 | The thicker duodenal wall handles strong [[Peristaltic Contractions]] and mixing; the thinner [[Ileum]] wall is optimized for absorption efficiency.                                 |
