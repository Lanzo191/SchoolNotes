**The stomach** is a muscular, J-shaped organ that continues digestion after the [[Mouth]] and [[Oesophagus]], initiating protein breakdown and regulating the flow of chyme into the [[Small Intestine]].

## Structure
- **Muscular wall**: Three layers of [[Smooth Muscle]] (longitudinal, circular, and oblique) allow mechanical mixing and churning of food.  
- **Gastric mucosa**: Lined with [[Epithelial cells]] containing gastric pits and glands.  
- **Gastric glands**: Produce hydrochloric acid (HCl), pepsinogen, mucus, and intrinsic factor.  
- **Rugae**: Folds in the mucosa allow the stomach to expand as it fills.  
- **pH**: Maintains a highly acidic environment (~pH 1.5–3.5).  

## Secretions and Factors
- **Hydrochloric acid (HCl)**: Secreted by parietal cells; denatures [[Proteins]], kills pathogens, and creates an acidic environment for enzyme activation.  
- **Pepsinogen**: Secreted by chief cells; converted into pepsin in acidic conditions to hydrolyse [[Proteins]] into peptides.  
- **Mucus**: Secreted by goblet cells; protects the stomach lining from acid and mechanical damage.  
- **Intrinsic factor**: Secreted by parietal cells; essential for vitamin B12 absorption in the [[Ileum]].  
- **Gastrin**: Hormone that stimulates HCl production and gastric motility.  

## Function
- Mechanically churns food to form chyme.  
- Chemically digests [[Proteins]] using pepsin and acid.  
- Protects the body by killing microorganisms ingested with food.  
- Regulates chyme release into the [[Duodenum]] via the pyloric sphincter.  
- Supports absorption of vitamin B12 for red blood cell production.
