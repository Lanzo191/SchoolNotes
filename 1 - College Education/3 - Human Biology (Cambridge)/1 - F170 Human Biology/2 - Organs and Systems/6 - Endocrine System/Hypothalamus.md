The hypothalamus is a small region of the brain that acts as the **master regulator** of the endocrine system. It links the nervous and endocrine systems via the [[Pituitary Gland]].

## Structure
- Located in the **diencephalon**, below the [[Thalamus]].  
- Composed of various nuclei that produce different hormones.  
- Connected to the [[Pituitary Gland]] via the **infundibulum (pituitary stalk)**.

## Function
- Produces **releasing and inhibiting hormones** that control the anterior pituitary.  
- Synthesises **ADH and oxytocin** for release via the posterior pituitary.  
- Regulates **homeostasis**, including temperature, hunger, thirst, and circadian rhythms.

## Hormones
- **Corticotropin-releasing hormone (CRH)** → stimulates ACTH from anterior pituitary.  
- **Thyrotropin-releasing hormone (TRH)** → stimulates TSH.  
- **Gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH)** → stimulates LH and FSH.  
- **ADH (Vasopressin)** → water reabsorption in kidneys.  
- **Oxytocin** → uterine contractions, milk ejection.

## Clinical Relevance
- Tumours can disrupt hormone balance → Cushing’s disease, [[Diabetes]] insipidus.
