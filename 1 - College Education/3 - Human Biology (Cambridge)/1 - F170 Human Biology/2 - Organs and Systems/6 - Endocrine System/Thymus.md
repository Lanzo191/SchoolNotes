The thymus is a primary lymphoid and endocrine organ located in the upper anterior mediastinum, just behind the sternum. It plays a critical role in the development of the immune system, particularly during childhood.

## Structure
- Bilobed gland with each lobe divided into **cortex** and **medulla**.  
- Comprised mainly of **thymocytes (immature T cells)** and supporting [[Epithelial cells]].  
- In adults, the thymus undergoes **involution**, gradually being replaced by fatty tissue.

## Function
- Site of **T [[Lymphocyte]] maturation**:
  - Immature T cells from bone marrow migrate to the thymus.  
  - Positive selection in cortex ensures T cells can recognise self-MHC molecules.  
  - Negative selection in medulla removes T cells that react strongly to self-antigens (prevents autoimmunity).  
- Produces **thymic hormones** such as thymosin, thymulin, and thymopoietin which stimulate T cell differentiation and proliferation.  

## Clinical Relevance
- **DiGeorge Syndrome**: congenital thymic hypoplasia → immunodeficiency.  
- **Thymoma**: tumor of thymic [[Epithelial cells]] → can be associated with myasthenia gravis.  
- Important for establishing **adaptive [[Immunity]]** in early life; less critical in adults after involution.
