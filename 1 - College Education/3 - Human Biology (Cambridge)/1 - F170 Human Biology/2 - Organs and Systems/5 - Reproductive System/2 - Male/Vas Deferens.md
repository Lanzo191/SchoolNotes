The **vas deferens** (also called ductus deferens) is a muscular tube that transports mature sperm from the [[Epididymis]] to the ejaculatory duct during ejaculation.

## Structure

### Location and Path

- Begins at the tail of the [[Epididymis]]
- Ascends through the **spermatic cord** alongside blood vessels and nerves
- Passes through the **inguinal canal** into the pelvic cavity
- Travels over the urinary bladder
- Joins with the seminal vesicle duct to form the **ejaculatory duct**
- Total length: approximately **30-35 cm**

### Layers of the Wall

- **Mucosa**: Inner epithelial lining with pseudostratified columnar epithelium containing stereocilia
- **Lamina propria**: Loose [[Connective Tissue]] layer
- **Muscularis**: Thick layer of [[Smooth Muscle]] (inner longitudinal, middle circular, outer longitudinal)
- **Adventitia**: Outer [[Connective Tissue]] layer with blood vessels and nerves

### Spermatic Cord

Contains:

- Vas deferens
- Testicular artery
- Pampiniform plexus of [[Veins]]
- Autonomic nerves
- Lymphatic vessels
- Cremaster muscle fibres
- Surrounded by fascia layers

### Ampulla

- Dilated terminal portion of vas deferens (near seminal vesicle)
- **5-7 cm long**
- Increased diameter for temporary sperm storage
- Glandular epithelium secretes nutritive fluid

## Function

### Sperm Transport

- Conducts sperm from [[Epididymis]] to ejaculatory duct during ejaculation
- **[[Peristaltic Contractions]]** of [[Smooth Muscle]] propel sperm forward
- Transit time: approximately 30 seconds during ejaculation
- Can store sperm temporarily in the ampulla

### Sperm Maturation Support

- Epithelial secretions provide nutrients
- Maintains optimal environment for sperm survival
- Stereocilia absorb excess fluid, concentrating sperm

### Role in Ejaculation

- Rhythmic contractions during orgasm propel sperm
- Coordinated with contractions of:
    - [[Epididymis]]
    - Seminal vesicles
    - [[Prostate Gland]]
    - Bulbourethral glands
- Controlled by sympathetic nervous system

## Spermatic Cord Function

- **Vascular supply** to [[Testes]] via testicular artery
- **Venous drainage** through pampiniform plexus
- **[[Thermoregulation]]** via countercurrent heat exchange
- **Nerve supply** for sensation and autonomic control
- **Lymphatic drainage** removes waste products

## Clinical Relevance

### Vasectomy

- Surgical male sterilisation procedure
- Vas deferens is cut and sealed on both sides
- Prevents sperm from entering ejaculatory fluid
- **99.9% effective** contraceptive method
- Does not affect:
    - Testosterone production
    - Sexual function
    - Ejaculation volume (sperm constitute <5% of semen volume)
- Reversible via vasovasostomy (success varies)

### Vasitis

- Inflammation of vas deferens
- Causes: bacterial infection, trauma, autoimmune reaction
- Symptoms: pain, swelling in groin/scrotum, painful ejaculation
- Treatment: antibiotics, anti-inflammatory medications

### Congenital Bilateral Absence of Vas Deferens (CBAVD)

- Genetic condition often associated with [[Cystic Fibrosis]] gene mutations
- Vas deferens fails to develop
- Causes obstructive azoospermia (no sperm in ejaculate)
- Fertility possible through surgical sperm extraction and IVF

### Inguinal Hernia

- Weakness in inguinal canal allows abdominal contents to protrude
- Can compress spermatic cord and vas deferens
- May impair blood supply to testis
- Requires surgical repair

### Obstructive Azoospermia

- Blockage of vas deferens prevents sperm release
- Causes: infection, trauma, congenital absence, post-surgical scarring
- Diagnosed via semen analysis and imaging
- Treatment: surgical reconstruction or sperm extraction for assisted reproduction

## Hormonal Influence

- **Testosterone** maintains structure and function of vas deferens
- Androgen receptors present in epithelial and [[Smooth Muscle]] cells
- Low testosterone can impair contractility and secretory function

## Embryological Development

- Develops from **mesonephric (Wolffian) duct**
- Differentiation stimulated by testosterone from fetal [[Testes]]
- Anti-Müllerian hormone prevents development of female structures
- Descent with [[Testes]] through inguinal canal during fetal development

## Adaptations for Function

- **Thick [[Smooth Muscle]] layer** enables powerful [[Peristaltic Contractions]]
- **Pseudostratified epithelium** with stereocilia maximises absorption
- **Long length** allows for sperm storage and maturation
- **Protected within spermatic cord** during passage through inguinal canal

## Interaction with Accessory Glands

- Receives secretions from:
    - **Seminal vesicles** (60-70% of semen volume)
    - **[[Prostate Gland]]** (20-30% of semen volume)
    - **Bulbourethral glands** (pre-ejaculatory fluid)
- These secretions mix with sperm to form complete semen

## Summary

The vas deferens is a critical component of the male reproductive tract, serving as the primary conduit for sperm transport from the [[Epididymis]] to the urethra. Its thick muscular walls and epithelial specialisations support both sperm storage and rapid ejaculation, while its accessibility makes it a target for surgical [[Contraception]].
