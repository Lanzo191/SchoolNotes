The **seminal vesicles** are paired accessory glands that secrete a significant portion of the seminal fluid, providing energy substrates and factors that support sperm survival and motility.

## Structure

### Location and Size

- Located **posterior to the bladder** and superior to the prostate
- Lateral to the ampulla of the [[Vas Deferens]]
- Approximately **5-7 cm long** when uncoiled
- Highly coiled tubular structure (appears lobulated externally)
- Each gland weighs approximately 2-3 grams

### Microscopic Structure

- **Mucosa**: highly folded pseudostratified columnar epithelium
- **Muscularis**: inner circular and outer longitudinal [[Smooth Muscle]] layers
- **Adventitia**: outer [[Connective Tissue]] layer
- Secretory epithelium produces viscous, alkaline fluid

### Seminal Vesicle Duct

- Each seminal vesicle has a **single excretory duct** (~2 cm long)
- Joins with the ampulla of the [[Vas Deferens]]
- Forms the **ejaculatory duct** which pierces through the prostate
- Ejaculatory ducts open into prostatic urethra

## Function

### Seminal Fluid Production

- Contributes approximately **60-70% of total semen volume**
- Produces approximately 2-4 mL of fluid per ejaculation
- Viscous, yellowish, alkaline fluid (pH 7.2-7.8)
- Secreted during sexual arousal and ejaculation

### Composition of Seminal Vesicle Fluid

**Fructose**:

- Primary energy source for sperm (15-20 mg/mL)
- Metabolised by sperm to produce [[ATP]] for motility
- Absence of fructose in semen suggests seminal vesicle dysfunction or absence

**Prostaglandins**:

- [[Smooth Muscle]] stimulants
- Induce contractions in female reproductive tract, aiding sperm transport
- Dilate cervical mucus
- May suppress female immune response to sperm

**Fibrinogen and Clotting Factors**:

- Semen coagulates immediately after ejaculation
- Forms temporary gel trapping sperm near [[Cervix]]
- Later liquefied by PSA from prostate

**Alkaline pH**:

- Neutralises acidic vaginal environment (pH 3.5-4.5)
- Protects sperm from acidic damage
- Optimises sperm motility

**Other Components**:

- **[[Amino Acids]]**: nutrition for sperm
- **Ascorbic acid (Vitamin C)**: antioxidant
- **Flavins**: yellow pigmentation of semen
- **Citric acid**: chelates [[Calcium]]
- **Enzymes**: various metabolic enzymes

### Role in Reproduction

- **Energy provision**: fructose fuels sperm metabolism
- **Sperm protection**: alkaline fluid neutralises vaginal acidity
- **Enhanced transport**: prostaglandins facilitate sperm movement through female tract
- **Coagulation-liquefaction**: initial coagulation holds semen in place; subsequent liquefaction releases motile sperm

## Hormonal Regulation

### Androgen Dependence

- Structure and secretory function depend on **testosterone**
- Androgen receptors present in [[Epithelial cells]]
- **Dihydrotestosterone (DHT)** also stimulates secretion
- Castration or androgen deficiency causes atrophy
- Androgen replacement restores function

### Age-Related Changes

- Gradual decrease in secretory function with aging
- Reduced testosterone levels correlate with reduced seminal vesicle secretions
- Size may decrease in elderly men

## Development

- Develops from **mesonephric (Wolffian) duct** during fetal development
- Differentiation induced by testosterone from fetal [[Testes]]
- Begins developing around 10-13 weeks of gestation
- Functional maturity reached at puberty

## Clinical Relevance

### Seminal Vesicle Dysfunction

- **Hypoplasia or agenesis**: congenital absence or underdevelopment
- Often associated with congenital absence of [[Vas Deferens]] (CAVD)
- Linked to [[Cystic Fibrosis]] gene mutations
- Results in low semen volume and infertility

### Obstruction of Ejaculatory Ducts

- Blockage prevents seminal vesicle fluid from entering ejaculate
- **Causes**: congenital cysts, calculi, post-inflammatory scarring
- **Symptoms**: low semen volume (<1.5 mL), infertility, painful ejaculation
- **Diagnosis**: transrectal ultrasound, MRI
- **Treatment**: surgical resection of obstruction (transurethral resection of ejaculatory ducts - TURED)

### Seminal Vesiculitis

- Inflammation of seminal vesicles, often secondary to prostatitis or epididymitis
- **Symptoms**: pelvic pain, painful ejaculation (hemospermia - blood in semen), urinary symptoms
- **Causes**: bacterial infection (E. coli, sexually transmitted infections)
- **Treatment**: antibiotics, anti-inflammatory medications

### Hemospermia (Blood in Semen)

- Often originates from seminal vesicles
- **Causes**:
    - Seminal vesiculitis (most common)
    - Seminal vesicle stones
    - Tumors (rare)
    - Vascular malformations
    - Trauma
- Usually benign and self-limiting in young men
- Persistent hemospermia in men >40 requires investigation

### Tumors

- **Seminal vesicle cancer** is rare
- More commonly invaded by prostate or bladder cancer
- **Benign cysts** occasionally occur
- **Diagnosis**: [[MRI]], CT, biopsy
- **Treatment**: surgical excision if symptomatic

## Semen Analysis and Seminal Vesicle Function

### Fructose Testing

- Absence of fructose in semen indicates:
    - Seminal vesicle agenesis
    - Ejaculatory duct obstruction
    - Seminal vesicle dysfunction
- Part of evaluation for low semen volume or azoospermia

### Semen Volume

- Normal: 1.5-5.0 mL
- Low volume (<1.5 mL) may indicate:
    - Seminal vesicle hypofunction
    - Ejaculatory duct obstruction
    - Retrograde ejaculation
    - Androgen deficiency

## Imaging

### Transrectal Ultrasound (TRUS)

- Visualises seminal vesicles posterior to bladder
- Assesses size, symmetry, cysts, calcifications
- Guides diagnostic aspiration or biopsy

### MRI

- Superior soft tissue resolution
- Best for detecting tumors, cysts, hemorrhage
- Used when TRUS is inconclusive

## Adaptations for Function

- **Highly coiled structure** increases secretory surface area
- **Folded mucosa** maximises fluid production
- **[[Smooth Muscle]]** enables forceful expulsion during ejaculation
- **Strategic location** near ampulla of [[Vas Deferens]] ensures mixing of sperm and seminal fluid
- **Large contribution to semen volume** ensures sperm dilution and optimal environment

## Interaction with Other Glands

Semen is a mixture from:

- **Seminal vesicles** (60-70%): fructose, prostaglandins, coagulating factors
- **Prostate** (20-30%): PSA, citric acid, zinc, liquefying enzymes
- **Bulbourethral glands** (<1%): pre-ejaculatory fluid, mucus
- **[[Testes]] and [[Epididymis]]** (~5%): sperm and testicular fluid

## Summary

The seminal vesicles are major contributors to semen volume and composition, providing essential nutrients, buffering capacity, and biochemical factors that enhance sperm survival and transport in the female reproductive tract. Dysfunction or absence of seminal vesicles significantly impairs fertility despite normal sperm production.
