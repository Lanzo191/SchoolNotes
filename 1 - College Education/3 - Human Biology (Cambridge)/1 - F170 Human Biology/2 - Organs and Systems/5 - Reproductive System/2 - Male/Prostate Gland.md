The **prostate gland** is a walnut-sized accessory reproductive gland that surrounds the urethra just below the urinary bladder. It produces prostatic fluid, a major component of semen.

## Structure

### Location and Size

- Located **inferior to the bladder**, surrounding the proximal urethra (prostatic urethra)
- Sits **anterior to the rectum** (allowing digital rectal examination)
- Approximately **3-4 cm wide, 2-3 cm tall, 3 cm deep**
- Weighs approximately **20-25 grams** in young adults
- Increases in size with age (benign prostatic hyperplasia common after 50)

### Anatomical Zones

- **Peripheral zone** (70% of glandular tissue):
    - Posterior and lateral portions
    - Most common site for prostate cancer
    - Palpable during digital rectal examination
- **Central zone** (25%):
    - Surrounds ejaculatory ducts
    - Rarely affected by disease
- **Transition zone** (5%):
    - Surrounds urethra
    - Site of benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH)
- **Anterior fibromuscular stroma** (non-glandular):
    - Muscle and fibrous tissue
    - No glandular function

### Microscopic Structure

- Composed of 30-50 tubuloalveolar glands
- Glandular epithelium: columnar and cuboidal cells
- [[Smooth Muscle]] and fibrous stroma between glands
- **Prostatic ducts** (15-30) drain into prostatic urethra
- **Prostatic utricle**: small blind pouch (embryological remnant)

### Blood Supply

- **Inferior vesical artery** (main supply)
- **Middle rectal artery**
- **Internal pudendal artery**
- **Prostatic venous plexus** drains to internal iliac [[Veins]]

### Nerve Supply

- **Inferior hypogastric plexus** (autonomic)
- Sympathetic fibres stimulate secretion and [[Smooth Muscle]] contraction
- Parasympathetic fibres involved in erection
- **Prostatic plexus** contains nerves controlling erectile function (risk during prostate surgery)

## Function

### Prostatic Fluid Production

- Contributes approximately **20-30% of seminal fluid volume**
- Secreted during ejaculation via prostatic ducts into urethra
- Alkaline pH (7.2-7.8) neutralises acidic vaginal environment
- Volume: approximately 0.5-1.0 mL per ejaculation

### Composition of Prostatic Secretions

- **Citric acid**: provides energy for sperm
- **Acid phosphatase**: enzyme marker for prostate function
- **Prostate-specific antigen (PSA)**: liquefies semen after ejaculation
- **Zinc**: antimicrobial properties, sperm motility
- **[[Calcium]]**: stabilises sperm chromatin
- **Spermine and spermidine**: contribute to semen odour, antioxidant
- **Fibrinolysin**: breaks down fibrin clots
- **Immunoglobulins**: immune protection

### Role in Reproduction

- **Semen liquefaction**: PSA breaks down seminal coagulum formed by seminal vesicle [[Proteins]]
- **Sperm activation**: prostatic fluid activates sperm motility
- **pH buffering**: protects sperm from acidic vaginal environment
- **Antimicrobial activity**: zinc and other factors protect against urinary tract pathogens
- **Sperm nutrition**: citric acid serves as metabolic substrate

### Smooth Muscle Contraction

- During ejaculation, [[Smooth Muscle]] contracts rhythmically
- Propels prostatic fluid into urethra
- Closes bladder neck to prevent retrograde ejaculation into bladder
- Coordinated with [[Vas Deferens]] and seminal vesicle contractions

## Hormonal Regulation

### Androgen Dependence

- **Testosterone** essential for prostate development and function
- Converted to **dihydrotestosterone (DHT)** by 5α-reductase enzyme
- DHT is more potent androgen, binds to androgen receptors in prostate cells
- Stimulates:
    - Growth and maintenance of prostatic tissue
    - Secretory activity
    - Cell proliferation

### Age-Related Changes

- Gradual enlargement (BPH) due to DHT stimulation
- Increased estrogen:androgen ratio with aging may contribute to BPH
- PSA levels increase with age and prostate size

## Development

- Develops from **urogenital sinus** during fetal development (10-12 weeks)
- Androgen-dependent differentiation
- Undergoes two growth phases:
    - Puberty: rapid growth due to testosterone surge
    - After age 50: gradual enlargement (BPH in most men)

## Clinical Relevance

### Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (BPH)

- Non-cancerous enlargement of prostate, mainly in transition zone
- Affects >50% of men over 60, >90% over 80
- **Symptoms (Lower Urinary Tract Symptoms - LUTS)**:
    - Weak urine stream
    - Difficulty starting urination
    - Incomplete bladder emptying
    - Frequent urination, especially at night (nocturia)
    - Urgency
- **Causes**: urethral compression by enlarged prostate
- **Treatment**:
    - Medications: α-blockers (relax [[Smooth Muscle]]), 5α-reductase inhibitors (reduce prostate size)
    - Minimally invasive procedures: TURP (transurethral resection), laser therapy
    - Surgery for severe cases

### Prostate Cancer

- Most common cancer in men (excluding skin cancer)
- Usually arises in peripheral zone
- Often asymptomatic in early stages
- **Risk factors**: age (>65), family history, ethnicity (higher in African descent)
- **Diagnosis**:
    - **Digital rectal examination (DRE)**: palpable hard nodule
    - **PSA blood test**: elevated levels (>4 ng/mL suspicious, though BPH also raises PSA)
    - **Prostate biopsy**: definitive diagnosis
    - **[[MRI]] scanning**: detects suspicious areas
- **Gleason score**: grading system (2-10, higher = more aggressive)
- **Treatment**: depends on stage and grade
    - Active surveillance for low-risk
    - Surgery (radical prostatectomy)
    - Radiation therapy
    - Hormone therapy (androgen deprivation)
    - Chemotherapy for advanced disease
- **Prognosis**: excellent if detected early (95% 5-year survival for localised)

### Prostatitis

- Inflammation of prostate gland
- **Types**:
    - Acute bacterial: sudden infection, severe symptoms (fever, pain, dysuria)
    - Chronic bacterial: recurrent infections
    - Chronic pelvic pain syndrome: non-bacterial inflammation
- **Symptoms**: pelvic/perineal pain, urinary symptoms, painful ejaculation
- **Treatment**: antibiotics (bacterial), anti-inflammatories, α-blockers

### PSA Testing Controversy

- **Prostate-Specific Antigen (PSA)** screening for prostate cancer is debated
- **Advantages**: early detection improves outcomes
- **Disadvantages**:
    - False positives (BPH, prostatitis also raise PSA)
    - Overdiagnosis and overtreatment of slow-growing cancers
    - Anxiety from uncertain results
- **Current guidelines**: shared decision-making between doctor and patient

### Surgical Complications

- **Radical prostatectomy** risks:
    - Erectile dysfunction (damage to prostatic nerve plexus)
    - Urinary incontinence (damage to urethral sphincter)
    - Bladder neck stenosis
- **Nerve-sparing techniques** minimise erectile dysfunction risk
- **Robotic-assisted surgery** improves precision

## Examination and Diagnosis

### Digital Rectal Examination (DRE)

- Finger inserted into rectum palpates posterior prostate
- Assesses:
    - Size and symmetry
    - Consistency (normal = rubbery; cancer = hard nodular)
    - Tenderness (prostatitis)
- Part of routine screening in men >50 or with symptoms

### PSA Blood Test

- Normal: <4 ng/mL (varies with age and ethnicity)
- Elevated in: prostate cancer, BPH, prostatitis, after ejaculation, prostate biopsy
- **PSA velocity**: rate of increase over time
- **PSA density**: PSA level relative to prostate volume
- **Free vs total PSA ratio**: lower ratio suggests cancer

### Imaging

- **Transrectal [[Ultrasound]] (TRUS)**: guides prostate biopsy
- **[[MRI]]**: detects and stages prostate cancer
- **Bone scan**: checks for metastases

## Adaptations for Function

- **Strategic location** around urethra allows secretions to mix with sperm during ejaculation
- **Multiple glandular ducts** maximise secretion into urethra
- **[[Smooth Muscle]]** ensures forceful expulsion of prostatic fluid
- **Rich vascularisation** supports high metabolic activity
- **Androgen receptors** enable hormonal regulation of function

## Summary

The prostate gland is essential for male fertility, producing prostatic fluid that nourishes, protects, and activates sperm. Its location surrounding the urethra makes it clinically significant, as enlargement (BPH) or cancer can obstruct urinary flow. Regular screening and awareness of urinary symptoms are important for early detection of prostate disorders.
