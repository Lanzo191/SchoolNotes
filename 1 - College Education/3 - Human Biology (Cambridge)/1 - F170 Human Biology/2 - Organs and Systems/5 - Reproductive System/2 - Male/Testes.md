The **testes** (singular: testis) are the primary male reproductive organs responsible for producing sperm and testosterone. They are housed in the scrotum outside the body cavity.

## Structure

### External Location

- Located in the **scrotum**, a skin-covered sac suspended outside the pelvic cavity
- Temperature regulation: maintained at **2-3°C below core body temperature** (~35°C)
- Essential for viable sperm production (spermatogenesis)
- Cremaster muscle and dartos muscle contract/relax to adjust testicular temperature

### Internal Anatomy

- Each testis is oval-shaped, approximately **4-5 cm long, 2-3 cm wide**
- Covered by three protective layers:
    - **Tunica vaginalis** (outer serous membrane)
    - **Tunica albuginea** (tough fibrous capsule)
    - **Tunica vasculosa** (vascular layer)
- Divided into 200-300 lobules by fibrous septa

### Seminiferous Tubules

- Highly coiled tubules (400-600 per testis)
- Site of **spermatogenesis** (sperm production)
- Total length: approximately 250 meters per testis
- Lined with two cell types:
    - **Sertoli cells** (supporting/nurse cells)
    - **Spermatogenic cells** (at various stages of development)

### Interstitial Tissue

- Spaces between seminiferous tubules
- Contains **Leydig cells** (interstitial cells)
- Produce **testosterone** in response to luteinising hormone (LH)
- Rich blood supply and lymphatic vessels

### Rete Testis and Efferent Ductules

- Seminiferous tubules converge into **rete testis** (network of ducts)
- **Efferent ductules** (10-20 small tubes) connect rete testis to [[Epididymis]]
- Transport immature sperm from testes to [[Epididymis]]

## Blood Supply

- **Testicular [[Arteries]]** branch from abdominal aorta
- **Pampiniform plexus** of [[Veins]] surrounds testicular artery
- Countercurrent heat exchange system helps maintain cooler testicular temperature
- **Testicular [[Veins]]** drain into inferior vena cava (right) and renal vein (left)

## Function

### Spermatogenesis

- Continuous production of sperm from puberty onward
- Takes approximately **74 days** to produce mature sperm
- Produces approximately **200-300 million sperm per day**
- Controlled by follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) and testosterone

### Testosterone Production

- Leydig cells synthesise testosterone from cholesterol
- Stimulated by luteinising hormone (LH) from anterior pituitary
- Testosterone functions:
    - Promotes spermatogenesis
    - Develops and maintains male secondary sexual characteristics
    - Maintains libido
    - Stimulates [[Protein Synthesis]] and muscle growth
    - Maintains bone density

### Sertoli Cell Functions

- **Physical support** for developing [[Sperm Cells]]
- **Nutritional support** via secretion of androgen-binding protein
- Form **blood-testis barrier** protecting developing sperm from immune system
- **Phagocytosis** of degenerating germ cells
- Secrete **inhibin** (provides negative feedback to FSH secretion)
- Secrete **anti-Müllerian hormone** during fetal development

## Hormonal Regulation

### Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Gonadal Axis

- **[[Hypothalamus]]** secretes gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH)
- GnRH stimulates **anterior pituitary** to release:
    - **FSH** → stimulates Sertoli cells and spermatogenesis
    - **LH** → stimulates Leydig cells to produce testosterone
- **Negative feedback**:
    - Testosterone inhibits GnRH and LH secretion
    - Inhibin (from Sertoli cells) inhibits FSH secretion

## Descent of Testes

- Testes develop in the abdominal cavity during fetal development
- Descend through **inguinal canal** into scrotum during 7th-8th month of gestation
- Guided by **gubernaculum** (fibrous cord)
- **Cryptorchidism** (undescended testis) requires medical intervention to preserve fertility

## Clinical Relevance

### Testicular Cancer

- Most common cancer in young men (15-35 years)
- Usually affects one testis
- High cure rate if detected early
- Self-examination recommended monthly

### Varicocele

- Enlargement of pampiniform plexus [[Veins]]
- Can impair sperm production due to increased temperature
- May cause testicular atrophy

### Orchitis

- Inflammation of testes, often due to infection (mumps virus, bacteria)
- Can impair fertility if severe

### Hypogonadism

- Insufficient testosterone production
- Causes: Klinefelter syndrome, pituitary disorders, aging
- Symptoms: reduced libido, erectile dysfunction, decreased muscle mass, infertility

### Testicular Torsion

- Twisting of spermatic cord cuts off blood supply
- Medical emergency requiring immediate surgery
- Most common in adolescents

## Adaptations for Function

- **External location** maintains optimal temperature for spermatogenesis
- **Large surface area** of seminiferous tubules maximises sperm production
- **Continuous production** ensures constant sperm availability
- **Blood-testis barrier** protects developing sperm from autoimmune attack
- **Rich vascularisation** supplies nutrients and removes waste efficiently

## Summary

The testes are highly specialised organs with dual endocrine (testosterone production) and exocrine (sperm production) functions. Their external location, complex internal structure, and precise hormonal regulation ensure continuous production of viable sperm and maintenance of male reproductive physiology throughout adult life.
