**Contraception** refers to methods used to prevent pregnancy. Methods vary in effectiveness, mechanism of action, duration, reversibility, and side effects. Effectiveness is measured by the **Pearl Index** (number of pregnancies per 100 women per year) or **failure rate**.

## Hormonal Methods

### Combined Oral Contraceptive Pill (COCP / "The Pill")

**Mechanism**: Contains **estrogen + progesterone** (synthetic versions)

- Suppresses **FSH/LH** (prevents [[Ovulation]])
- Thickens cervical mucus (impedes sperm)
- Thins endometrium (reduces implantation likelihood)

**Effectiveness**: **>99%** with perfect use, ~91% typical use

**Advantages**:

- Highly effective
- Regulates menstrual cycles
- Reduces menstrual pain, heavy bleeding
- Reduces ovarian and endometrial cancer risk
- Treats acne, hirsutism (PCOS)

**Disadvantages**:

- Requires daily compliance
- Increased risk: blood clots (VTE), stroke (rare)
- Contraindicated: smokers >35, history of blood clots, certain cancers
- Side effects: nausea, headaches, mood changes, breast tenderness

**Types**: monophasic (same dose daily), triphasic (varies throughout cycle)

### Progesterone-Only Pill (POP / "Mini-Pill")

**Mechanism**: **Progesterone only** (no estrogen)

- Thickens cervical mucus (primary effect)
- Sometimes suppresses [[Ovulation]] (not always)
- Thins endometrium

**Effectiveness**: **>99%** with perfect use, ~91% typical use

**Advantages**:

- Suitable for breastfeeding women
- Suitable for women who can't take estrogen
- Fewer VTE risks than COCP

**Disadvantages**:

- **Must take at same time daily** (within 3-hour window)
- Irregular bleeding common
- Less effective than COCP

### Injectable Contraceptive (Depo-Provera)

**Mechanism**: **Progesterone injection** (medroxyprogesterone acetate) every **12-13 weeks**

- Suppresses [[Ovulation]]
- Thickens cervical mucus
- Thins endometrium

**Effectiveness**: **>99%**

**Advantages**:

- Long-acting (only 4 injections/year)
- No daily compliance needed
- Reduces endometrial cancer risk
- Reduces menstrual pain

**Disadvantages**:

- **Delayed return to fertility** (up to 12-18 months)
- Weight gain common
- Irregular bleeding initially, then amenorrhea (no periods)
- **Bone density concerns** (not recommended >2 years without medical review)
- Not immediately reversible

### Contraceptive Implant (Nexplanon)

**Mechanism**: Small rod inserted under skin of upper arm, releases **progesterone** (etonogestrel)

- Suppresses [[Ovulation]]
- Thickens cervical mucus

**Effectiveness**: **>99%** (most effective reversible method)

**Duration**: **3 years**

**Advantages**:

- Long-acting, highly effective
- No daily compliance
- Rapidly reversible (remove anytime)

**Disadvantages**:

- Irregular bleeding (most common side effect)
- Insertion/removal requires minor procedure
- Visible/palpable under skin

### Contraceptive Patch (Evra)

**Mechanism**: Transdermal patch releasing **estrogen + progesterone**

- Applied weekly for 3 weeks, 1 week patch-free (withdrawal bleed)

**Effectiveness**: ~91% typical use

**Advantages**: Similar to COCP, no daily pill

**Disadvantages**: May detach, visible, skin irritation, less effective in women >90 kg

### Vaginal Ring (NuvaRing)

**Mechanism**: Flexible ring inserted into [[Vagina]], releases **estrogen + progesterone**

- Kept in for 3 weeks, removed for 1 week (withdrawal bleed)

**Effectiveness**: ~91% typical use

**Advantages**: Monthly insertion, no daily pill

**Disadvantages**: Vaginal discomfort, discharge, must remember monthly change

## Barrier Methods

### Male Condom

**Mechanism**: Latex/polyurethane sheath worn over penis

- Physical barrier prevents sperm entering [[Vagina]]

**Effectiveness**: ~85% typical use, ~98% perfect use

**Advantages**:

- **Only method protecting against STIs** (including HIV)
- No hormones
- Widely available, inexpensive
- No prescription needed

**Disadvantages**:

- Requires use with every act of intercourse
- Can break or slip off
- Reduces sensation (some users)
- Latex allergies (use polyurethane alternatives)

### Female Condom

**Mechanism**: Polyurethane pouch inserted into [[Vagina]]

**Effectiveness**: ~79% typical use

**Advantages**: STI protection, can be inserted hours before intercourse

**Disadvantages**: More expensive, less available, difficult to use correctly

### Diaphragm / Cervical Cap

**Mechanism**: Dome-shaped silicone device inserted into [[Vagina]] to cover [[Cervix]]

- Used with **spermicide gel**

**Effectiveness**: ~88% typical use

**Advantages**: Reusable, no hormones

**Disadvantages**:

- Requires fitting by healthcare provider
- Must insert before intercourse, leave in 6+ hours after
- Increased UTI risk
- No STI protection

## Intrauterine Devices (IUDs)

### Copper IUD (ParaGard)

**Mechanism**: T-shaped device with copper wire, inserted into [[Uterus]]

- **Copper toxic to sperm** (reduces motility, [[Fertilisation]] capacity)
- Triggers inflammatory response (hostile to sperm and embryo)

**Effectiveness**: **>99%**

**Duration**: **5-10 years** (depending on type)

**Advantages**:

- Long-acting, highly effective
- Non-hormonal
- Rapidly reversible (remove anytime, immediate fertility return)
- **Emergency contraception** (can insert within 5 days post-intercourse)

**Disadvantages**:

- **Heavier, more painful periods** (especially first 3-6 months)
- Insertion uncomfortable
- Small risk: expulsion, uterine perforation, infection (rare)

### Hormonal IUD (Mirena, Kyleena, Skyla)

**Mechanism**: T-shaped device releasing **progesterone** (levonorgestrel)

- Thickens cervical mucus
- Thins endometrium
- Partially suppresses [[Ovulation]]

**Effectiveness**: **>99%**

**Duration**: **3-8 years** (depending on type)

**Advantages**:

- Long-acting, highly effective
- **Lighter periods** (many women become amenorrheic)
- Reduces menstrual pain
- Treats heavy menstrual bleeding

**Disadvantages**:

- Irregular bleeding initially (first 3-6 months)
- Insertion uncomfortable
- Hormonal side effects possible (acne, mood changes, though localized effect minimizes systemic side effects)

## Permanent Methods (Sterilisation)

### Tubal Ligation (Female Sterilisation)

**Mechanism**: Fallopian tubes cut, tied, clipped, or sealed

- Prevents sperm meeting ovum

**Effectiveness**: **>99%** (permanent)

**Procedure**: Laparoscopic surgery (general anaesthesia)

**Advantages**: Permanent, highly effective, no ongoing compliance

**Disadvantages**:

- **Surgical risks** (anesthesia, bleeding, infection)
- **Difficult to reverse** (reversal surgery not always successful)
- Regret possible (especially if done at young age)
- Slightly increased ectopic pregnancy risk if fails

### Vasectomy (Male Sterilisation)

**Mechanism**: [[Vas Deferens]] cut and sealed

- Sperm cannot enter ejaculate

**Effectiveness**: **>99%** (permanent)

**Procedure**: Local anaesthesia, outpatient (~20 minutes)

**Advantages**:

- Simpler, safer, cheaper than tubal ligation
- Permanent, highly effective
- No effect on sexual function, testosterone, ejaculation

**Disadvantages**:

- **Not immediately effective** (sperm remain in tubes for ~12 weeks / 20 ejaculations; need another contraceptive method until semen analysis confirms azoospermia)
- **Difficult to reverse** (reversal success varies)
- Small surgical risks (infection, hematoma)
- Regret possible

## Emergency Contraception

### Emergency Contraceptive Pill (ECP / "Morning-After Pill")

**Levonorgestrel (Plan B)**:

- **Progesterone-only** pill
- Delays/inhibits [[Ovulation]]
- **Effectiveness**: ~89% if taken within 72 hours (most effective within 24 hours)
- **Timing**: Take as soon as possible after unprotected intercourse
- Available over-the-counter

**Ulipristal Acetate (Ella)**:

- **Progesterone receptor modulator**
- Delays [[Ovulation]]
- **Effectiveness**: ~85% if taken within 120 hours (5 days)
- **Timing**: Effective for longer period than levonorgestrel
- Prescription required

**Mechanism**: Primarily delays [[Ovulation]] (if [[Ovulation]] already occurred, less effective)

**Disadvantages**:

- Not 100% effective
- May cause nausea, irregular bleeding
- **Not for routine use** (less effective than regular contraception)

### Copper IUD

- Can be inserted within **5 days** of unprotected intercourse
- **>99% effective** as emergency contraception
- Provides ongoing contraception once inserted

## Natural Methods (Fertility Awareness-Based Methods)

### Calendar Method

- Track menstrual cycles, avoid intercourse during estimated fertile window (~days 8-19 of cycle)

**Effectiveness**: ~76% typical use (high failure rate)

### Basal Body Temperature Method

- Daily temperature measurement; slight rise after [[Ovulation]]
- Avoid intercourse on fertile days

### Cervical Mucus Method (Billings Method)

- Monitor changes in cervical mucus throughout cycle
- Fertile: clear, stretchy, "egg-white" consistency

### Symptothermal Method

- Combines temperature, mucus, other signs (cervical position)

**Advantages** (all natural methods):

- No hormones or devices
- No cost
- Can help achieve pregnancy when desired

**Disadvantages**:

- **High failure rate** (~76-88% typical use)
- Requires diligent tracking, cycle regularity
- Abstinence during fertile window (~10 days/month)
- No STI protection
- Difficult with irregular cycles

## Lactational Amenorrhea Method (LAM)

**Mechanism**: Exclusive breastfeeding suppresses [[Ovulation]] (high prolactin)

**Criteria for effectiveness**:

- **<6 months postpartum**
- **Exclusively breastfeeding** (no formula, nursing day and night)
- **Amenorrhea** (no menstrual periods)

**Effectiveness**: ~98% if all criteria met

**Disadvantages**: Only short-term, requires strict adherence, no STI protection

## Withdrawal Method (Coitus Interruptus)

**Mechanism**: Penis withdrawn from vagina before ejaculation

**Effectiveness**: ~78% typical use (high failure rate)

**Disadvantages**:

- Requires perfect timing, self-control
- Pre-ejaculatory fluid may contain sperm
- No STI protection

## Spermicides

**Mechanism**: Chemical (nonoxynol-9) kills or immobilizes sperm

**Forms**: Gel, foam, suppository, film

**Effectiveness**: ~72% typical use (used alone)

**Advantages**: No prescription, can be used with condoms

**Disadvantages**:

- High failure rate alone
- May cause irritation
- Must insert before each act
- No STI protection

## Choosing Contraception

### Factors to Consider

- **Effectiveness**: failure rate, typical vs perfect use
- **Duration**: short-acting vs long-acting
- **Reversibility**: immediately reversible vs permanent
- **Hormones**: hormonal vs non-hormonal
- **Frequency**: daily, weekly, monthly, yearly
- **STI protection**: condoms only
- **Medical history**: contraindications
- **Lifestyle**: compliance, preference
- **Cost and access**
- **Side effects**: tolerance varies individually

### Effectiveness Ranking (Typical Use)

1. **Implant, IUD, sterilisation**: >99%
2. **Injectable, pill, patch, ring**: 91-94%
3. **Condom**: 85-88%
4. **[[Diaphragm]], fertility awareness**: 76-88%
5. **Spermicide alone, withdrawal**: 72-78%

## Summary

Contraceptive methods range from highly effective long-acting options (IUDs, implants) to user-dependent methods (pills, condoms). Choice depends on individual circumstances, health factors, and preferences. Only barrier methods (condoms) protect against STIs. Dual protection (hormonal method + condom) offers both pregnancy and STI prevention. Access to effective contraception is essential for reproductive autonomy and family planning.
