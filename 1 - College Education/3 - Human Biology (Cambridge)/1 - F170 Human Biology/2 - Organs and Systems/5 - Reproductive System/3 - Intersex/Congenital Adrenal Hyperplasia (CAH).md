**[[Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia]] (CAH)** is a group of autosomal recessive genetic disorders affecting adrenal steroid hormone synthesis. The most common form (95% of cases) is **21-hydroxylase deficiency**, which causes impaired [[Cortisol]] and aldosterone production, leading to excess androgen production.

## Cause

### Genetic Basis

- **Autosomal recessive** inheritance
- Most common form: **CYP21A2 gene mutation** (encodes 21-hydroxylase enzyme)
- Both parents must be carriers for child to be affected
- 1 in 4 chance of affected child if both parents carriers

### Biochemical Defect (21-Hydroxylase Deficiency)

**Normal adrenal hormone synthesis pathway**:

- Cholesterol → Pregnenolone → Progesterone → **[21-hydroxylase]** → 11-Deoxycorticosterone → Corticosterone → **Aldosterone**
- Progesterone → **[21-hydroxylase]** → 11-Deoxycortisol → **[[Cortisol]]**

**In 21-hydroxylase deficiency**:

- **Block** at 21-hydroxylase step
- Cannot produce [[Cortisol]] or aldosterone efficiently
- **[[Cortisol]] deficiency** → loss of negative feedback → **ACTH rises**
- High ACTH drives adrenal gland growth (**hyperplasia**)
- Precursors diverted to **androgen pathway**
- **Excess androgens** (testosterone, androstenedione) produced

## Forms of CAH

### Classic CAH (Severe Enzyme Deficiency)

**Salt-Wasting Form (~75% of classic CAH)**:

- **Severe deficiency** of 21-hydroxylase (~0% enzyme activity)
- **Cannot produce [[Cortisol]] OR aldosterone**
- **Life-threatening**: presents in first weeks of life
- **Symptoms**:
    - Vomiting, diarrhea, dehydration
    - **Adrenal crisis**: hyponatremia (low [[Sodium]]), hyperkalemia (high [[Potassium]]), hypoglycemia, shock
    - Can be fatal if untreated

**Simple Virilizing Form (~25% of classic CAH)**:

- **Partial deficiency** of 21-hydroxylase (~1-2% enzyme activity)
- **Cannot produce [[Cortisol]]** efficiently, but aldosterone production adequate
- **No salt-wasting crisis**
- Excess androgen effects present

### Non-Classic CAH (Mild Enzyme Deficiency)

- **Mild deficiency** of 21-hydroxylase (~20-60% enzyme activity)
- **Symptoms mild or absent**
- May present later in life with:
    - Hirsutism (excess body hair)
    - Acne
    - Irregular periods
    - Infertility
    - Early pubic hair development (childhood)
- More common (~1 in 200 in some populations)

## Clinical Features

### XX Individuals (Genetically Female)

**At Birth**:

- **Ambiguous genitalia** (virilization in utero):
    - **Clitoral enlargement** (clitoromegaly)
    - **Labial fusion** (variable degree)
    - May resemble male genitalia
- Internal reproductive organs **normal** ([[Ovaries]], [[Uterus]], fallopian tubes)
- Urethra and [[Vagina]] may share common opening (urogenital sinus)

**Untreated**:

- Continued virilization
- **Precocious puberty** (early)
- Rapid growth initially, but **short final adult height** (early growth plate closure)
- Irregular or absent periods
- Fertility issues

### XY Individuals (Genetically Male)

**At Birth**:

- **Normal male external genitalia** OR
- **Hyperpigmented genitalia** (high ACTH stimulates melanocytes)
- **May have enlarged penis** (androgen excess)
- **Salt-wasting form**: presents with adrenal crisis (vomiting, dehydration, shock) in first weeks
- **Often missed at birth** unless newborn screening detects biochemical abnormality

**Untreated**:

- Precocious puberty (early)
- Rapid growth, short final adult height
- **Infertility** (excess androgens suppress gonadotropins → impaired spermatogenesis)

## Diagnosis

### Newborn Screening

- Heel-prick blood test at birth
- Measures **17-hydroxyprogesterone (17-OHP)** levels
- **Elevated 17-OHP** suggests CAH (precursor accumulates before block)
- Routine in many countries (allows early treatment, prevents crises)

### Prenatal Diagnosis

- Possible if family history known
- Chorionic villus sampling or amniocentesis (genetic testing)
- Prenatal treatment with dexamethasone (crosses [[Placenta]], reduces fetal androgen exposure) controversial

### Biochemical Tests

- **17-OHP**: markedly elevated
- **Androgens**: elevated (testosterone, androstenedione)
- **[[Cortisol]]**: low
- **Aldosterone**: low (salt-wasting form)
- **ACTH**: elevated
- **Electrolytes**: hyponatremia, hyperkalemia (salt-wasting)

### ACTH Stimulation Test

- Administer synthetic ACTH, measure hormone response
- Confirms adrenal insufficiency
- Useful for non-classic CAH diagnosis

### Genetic Testing

- Confirms CYP21A2 mutations
- Helps predict severity
- Useful for family planning, prenatal diagnosis

## Management

### Medical Treatment

**Glucocorticoid Replacement**:

- **Hydrocortisone** (children) or **prednisone/dexamethasone** (adults)
- Replaces missing [[Cortisol]]
- **Suppresses ACTH** → reduces androgen production
- Dose adjusted to balance:
    - Too low → inadequate [[Cortisol]], excess androgens
    - Too high → Cushing's syndrome (weight gain, growth suppression)
- **Stress dosing**: increase dose during illness, surgery, injury (prevent adrenal crisis)

**Mineralocorticoid Replacement** (salt-wasting form):

- **Fludrocortisone** (synthetic aldosterone)
- Maintains [[Sodium]]/[[Potassium]] balance
- **Salt supplementation** (infants)

**Monitoring**:

- Regular [[Blood Tests]] (17-OHP, androgens, electrolytes)
- Growth monitoring (children)
- Bone density monitoring (long-term glucocorticoid use)

### Surgical Management (XX Individuals with Ambiguous Genitalia)

**Feminizing Genitoplasty** (timing controversial):

- **Clitoral reduction/recession** (preserve sensation)
- **Vaginoplasty** (create separate vaginal opening, enlarge vaginal cavity)
- **Timing debated**:
    - Traditionally: infancy
    - Current debate: delay until patient can consent (concerns about unnecessary surgery, sexual function, patient autonomy)
    - Decision made with multidisciplinary team and family

### Psychosocial Support

- Diagnosis disclosure (age-appropriate)
- Gender identity support (most XX individuals with CAH identify as female)
- Fertility counseling
- Psychological support for coping with chronic condition, surgery, gender issues
- Peer support groups

## Fertility

### XX Individuals

- **Can be fertile** with proper treatment
- May require:
    - Optimized glucocorticoid dosing
    - [[Ovulation]] induction
    - Assisted reproductive technologies

### XY Individuals

- **Often infertile** (excess androgens suppress spermatogenesis)
- May improve with treatment optimization
- Sperm extraction + IVF/ICSI possible

## Adrenal Crisis

### Triggers

- Illness, infection
- Surgery, trauma
- Stress
- Inadequate medication

### Symptoms

- Severe vomiting, diarrhea
- Extreme weakness, fatigue
- Confusion, loss of consciousness
- Hypotension, shock
- **Life-threatening emergency**

### Treatment

- **Immediate IV hydrocortisone**
- **IV fluids** (saline)
- **Treat underlying cause** (infection, etc.)
- **Emergency medical alert bracelet** essential

## Long-Term Outcomes

With proper treatment:

- Normal life expectancy
- Normal or near-normal growth (if treatment started early)
- Fertility possible
- Adrenal crises preventable with stress dosing

Challenges:

- Lifelong medication compliance
- Balancing treatment (avoid over/under-treatment)
- Surgical outcomes variable
- Psychosocial adjustment

## Prevalence

- **Classic CAH**: ~1 in 10,000-15,000 births
- **Non-classic CAH**: ~1 in 200 (varies by ethnicity; higher in Ashkenazi Jews, Hispanics)
- **Carrier frequency**: ~1 in 50-60

## Other Forms of CAH (Rare)

- **11β-hydroxylase deficiency** (~5% of CAH): virilization + hypertension
- **3β-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase deficiency**: virilization in XX, undervirilization in XY
- **17α-hydroxylase deficiency**: undervirilization, hypertension, hypokalemia

## Summary

[[Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia]] demonstrates how a single enzyme deficiency can have profound effects on sexual development and overall health. The most common form (21-hydroxylase deficiency) causes [[Cortisol]] and aldosterone deficiency with excess androgen production, leading to ambiguous genitalia in XX individuals and potentially life-threatening salt-wasting crises. Early diagnosis through newborn screening, prompt treatment with hormone replacement, and individualized multidisciplinary care are essential for optimal outcomes. CAH highlights the importance of understanding hormonal pathways in sexual differentiation and the need for sensitive, patient-centered care for individuals with differences of sex development.
