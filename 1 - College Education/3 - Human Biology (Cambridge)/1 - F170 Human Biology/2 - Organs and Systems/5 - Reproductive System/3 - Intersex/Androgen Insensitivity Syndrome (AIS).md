**[[Androgen Insensitivity Syndrome]] (AIS)** is an X-linked genetic condition in which individuals with XY [[Chromosomes]] (genetically male) have partial or complete resistance to androgens (male hormones), resulting in feminized or ambiguous physical appearance despite having [[Testes]].

## Cause

### Genetic Basis

- Mutation in **AR gene** on **X chromosome**
- Codes for **androgen receptor** protein
- X-linked recessive inheritance (affects XY individuals)
- Mother usually carrier (XY parent cannot pass to XY children, only XX)

### Mechanism

- **[[Testes]] form normally** (Y chromosome SRY gene functional)
- [[Testes]] produce **testosterone and anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH)**
- **AMH functions normally** → Müllerian structures ([[Uterus]], fallopian tubes, upper [[Vagina]]) regress
- **Androgen receptors non-functional or absent** → testosterone cannot act on target tissues
- Body develops along **female pathway** despite XY [[Chromosomes]] and testosterone production

## Types

### Complete Androgen Insensitivity Syndrome (CAIS)

- **Complete resistance** to androgens
- External genitalia appear **fully female**
- [[Testes]] present (often undescended, in abdomen or inguinal canal)
- **Blind-ending [[Vagina]]** (shorter than typical)
- **No [[Uterus]], fallopian tubes, [[Cervix]]** (AMH caused regression)
- Breast development at puberty (testosterone converted to estrogen)
- **No pubic/axillary hair** (androgens required for this)
- **Primary amenorrhea** (no menstruation - no [[Uterus]])

### Partial Androgen Insensitivity Syndrome (PAIS)

- **Partial resistance** to androgens
- **Ambiguous genitalia** at birth (variable presentation)
- Range: predominantly female to predominantly male appearance
- May have **enlarged clitoris/small penis**, partial labial fusion
- [[Testes]] may be palpable
- Some virilization at puberty but incomplete
- Gender assignment depends on individual presentation

### Mild Androgen Insensitivity Syndrome (MAIS)

- **Mild resistance** to androgens
- **Male external genitalia** but may have:
    - Hypospadias (urethral opening not at tip of penis)
    - Micropenis
    - Gynecomastia (breast development) at puberty
    - Infertility (impaired spermatogenesis)

## Diagnosis

### Clinical Presentation

- **CAIS**: Usually diagnosed at puberty due to primary amenorrhea in otherwise normally-developed girl
- **PAIS**: Often identified at birth due to ambiguous genitalia
- **MAIS**: May be diagnosed during infertility investigation

### Investigations

- **Karyotype**: reveals **46,XY** (surprise for apparent females)
- **Hormone levels**:
    - Testosterone: **normal to high male range**
    - LH: **elevated** (feedback loop disrupted)
    - Estrogen: **normal female range** (from testosterone conversion)
- **Pelvic [[Ultrasound]]/[[MRI]]**: absent [[Uterus]], presence of [[Testes]]
- **Genetic testing**: AR gene mutation confirmation

## Management

### CAIS

- **Gender identity**: typically **female** (raised as female, identify as female)
- **Gonadectomy** (removal of [[Testes]]):
    - Recommended after puberty (allows natural breast development)
    - **Cancer risk**: [[Testes]] have ~5% malignancy risk (germ cell tumors) if left in situ
    - Timing debated: some recommend late teens/early twenties
- **Hormone replacement therapy (HRT)** after gonadectomy:
    - **Estrogen** replacement ([[Testes]] were providing estrogen via testosterone conversion)
    - Maintains bone density, sexual function, general health
- **Vaginal dilation** or surgery if [[Vagina]] too short for comfortable intercourse
- **Psychological support**: coming to terms with diagnosis, infertility
- **Fertility**: not possible (no [[Uterus]], non-functional [[Testes]])

### PAIS

- **Gender assignment**: individualized decision
    - Based on degree of virilization, predicted adult phenotype, cultural factors
    - Input from multidisciplinary team and family
- **Surgery**: may include genital reconstruction (timing controversial)
- **Hormone therapy**: depends on gender of rearing
- **Psychological support**: essential throughout development

### MAIS

- **Gender identity**: typically **male**
- **Fertility treatment**: often infertile due to impaired spermatogenesis
    - May attempt sperm extraction + IVF/ICSI
- **Gynecomastia management**: surgery if distressing
- **Psychological support**

## Psychosocial Considerations

- **Disclosure**: how and when to tell patient (often diagnosed in adolescence)
- **Gender identity**: almost all CAIS individuals identify as female
- **Infertility**: grief and support important
- **Peer support groups**: connecting with others with AIS
- **Terminology**: preferred terms vary (differences of sex development, intersex, disorder of sex development)

## Clinical Features Summary (CAIS)

- Phenotype: **female**
- External genitalia: **female**
- Internal genitalia: **[[Testes]]** (no [[Uterus]], [[Cervix]], fallopian tubes, upper [[Vagina]])
- [[Chromosomes]]: **46,XY**
- Gonads: **[[Testes]]**
- Hormone levels: **testosterone high** (male range), **estrogen normal** (female range)
- Puberty: **breast development, female body shape**, **no menstruation**, **no pubic/axillary hair**
- Fertility: **infertile**

## Prevalence

- **CAIS**: ~1 in 20,000-64,000 XY births
- **All forms of AIS**: ~1 in 20,000 XY births

## Summary

[[Androgen Insensitivity Syndrome]] demonstrates that biological sex is not simply binary. Despite having XY [[Chromosomes]] and [[Testes]], individuals with CAIS develop female external appearance due to non-functional androgen receptors. Understanding AIS highlights the complex interplay between genetics, hormones, and physical development in sexual differentiation. Management requires a sensitive, individualized, multidisciplinary approach that respects the person's gender identity and psychological wellbeing.
