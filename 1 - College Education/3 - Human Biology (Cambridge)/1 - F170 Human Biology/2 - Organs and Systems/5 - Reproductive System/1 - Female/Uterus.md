The **uterus** is a hollow, muscular organ where implantation occurs and fetal development takes place during pregnancy. It is also responsible for menstruation when pregnancy does not occur.

## Structure

### Location and Orientation

- Located in the **pelvic cavity** between bladder (anterior) and rectum (posterior)
- **Anteversion**: entire uterus tilts forward over bladder
- **Anteflexion**: body of uterus bends forward relative to [[Cervix]] (~90-100° angle)
- **Retroversion/Retroflexion**: tilts/bends backward (10-15% of women, usually normal variant)

### Size

**Non-pregnant uterus**:

- ~7-8 cm long, 5 cm wide, 2-3 cm thick
- Weighs ~50-70 grams
- Pear-shaped, inverted

**Pregnant uterus** (full-term):

- Expands to accommodate fetus (~35 cm long)
- Can weigh >1000 grams
- Returns to near-normal size by ~6 weeks postpartum (involution)

### Anatomical Regions

**1. Fundus**:

- Dome-shaped upper portion above openings of fallopian tubes
- Palpable abdominally in pregnant women

**2. Body (Corpus)**:

- Largest part, central region
- Site of implantation and fetal development
- Thick muscular walls

**3. Isthmus**:

- Narrow region between body and [[Cervix]] (~5 mm)
- Lower uterine segment during pregnancy

**4. [[Cervix]]**:

- Lower cylindrical portion
- Protrudes into [[Vagina]] (see separate [[Cervix]] note)

### Uterine Wall Layers

**1. Perimetrium (Serosa)**:

- Outer peritoneal covering
- Continuous with broad ligament
- Incomplete anteriorly (bladder attachment area)

**2. Myometrium** (middle muscular layer):

- **Thickest layer** (~12-15 mm in non-pregnant state)
- Three layers of [[Smooth Muscle]]:
    - **Outer longitudinal**: aids expulsion during [[Labour]]
    - **Middle circular**: thickest layer, figure-8 pattern
    - **Inner oblique**: aids in compression of vessels
- Rich vascular supply
- **Hypertrophy during pregnancy**: muscle fibres increase ~10-fold in size
- **Oxytocin receptors** increase at term (enables [[Labour]] contractions)
- **Gap junctions** form between muscle cells for coordinated contractions

**3. Endometrium** (inner mucosal lining):

- Lines uterine cavity
- **Two layers**:
    - **Stratum functionalis** (superficial): shed during menstruation, regenerates each cycle
    - **Stratum basalis** (deep): permanent, regenerates functionalis after menstruation
- Richly vascularised by **spiral [[Arteries]]**
- Contains **endometrial glands**
- **Cyclical changes** in response to hormones:
    - **Proliferative phase** (days 5-14): estrogen stimulates thickening (3-5 mm)
    - **Secretory phase** (days 15-28): progesterone stimulates glandular secretion and increased vascularity (5-6 mm)
    - **Menstruation** (days 1-5): progesterone withdrawal → spiral artery constriction → ischemia → shedding

### Uterine Cavity

- **Triangular** when viewed in coronal section
- Continuous with:
    - Fallopian tube openings (ostia) at superior angles
    - Cervical canal at inferior angle (internal os)
- Potential space (walls touch except during pregnancy)

### Ligaments and Support

**Broad Ligament**:

- Double fold of peritoneum extending from sides of uterus to pelvic walls
- Contains:
    - Fallopian tubes (within mesosalpinx)
    - Ovarian ligament
    - Round ligament
    - Uterine blood vessels
    - Nerves and lymphatics

**Round Ligament**:

- Extends from uterine fundus through inguinal canal to labia majora
- Fibromuscular cord
- Helps maintain anteversion (though mainly uterosacral and cardinal ligaments)

**Uterosacral Ligaments**:

- From [[Cervix]]/upper [[Vagina]] to sacrum
- **Major support**, prevent uterine prolapse
- Contain autonomic nerve fibres (uterine pain sensation)

**Cardinal (Transverse Cervical) Ligaments**:

- From [[Cervix]] and lateral vaginal fornices to pelvic side walls
- **Primary support** for uterus
- Contain uterine vessels

**Pubocervical Ligaments**:

- From [[Cervix]] to pubic symphysis
- Support anterior uterus

## Blood Supply

### Arterial Supply

**Uterine Artery** (main supply):

- Branch of internal iliac artery
- Travels in cardinal ligament
- At uterus: divides into ascending (to fundus) and descending (to [[Cervix]]) branches
- **Spiral [[Arteries]]** branch from radial [[Arteries]] to supply endometrium
- Anastomoses with ovarian artery at fundus

**Ovarian Artery**:

- Minor supply to uterine fundus

### Venous Drainage

- **Uterine venous plexus** drains to uterine [[Veins]]
- Uterine [[Veins]] → internal iliac [[Veins]] → inferior vena cava
- Extensive plexus (can lead to significant bleeding with uterine trauma)

### Lymphatic Drainage

- Fundus → para-aortic nodes
- Body → external iliac and internal iliac nodes
- [[Cervix]] → internal iliac, external iliac, and sacral nodes

## Nerve Supply

- **Inferior hypogastric plexus** (autonomic)
- **Sympathetic** (T10-L2): uterine contraction (labor), [[Vasoconstriction]]
- **Parasympathetic** (S2-S4): [[Vasodilation]], relaxation
- **Sensory fibres**: via uterosacral ligaments and pelvic splanchnic nerves
- **Uterine contractions** can be painful (dysmenorrhea) if prostaglandins cause excessive contractions

## Function

### Menstruation

- Cyclical shedding of endometrium (stratum functionalis) when pregnancy doesn't occur
- ~30-80 mL blood loss per cycle (average 35 mL)
- Lasts 3-7 days
- Triggered by progesterone and estrogen withdrawal from corpus luteum degeneration
- Spiral [[Arteries]] constrict → endometrial ischemia → tissue breakdown
- Prostaglandins stimulate myometrial contractions (expel menstrual blood)

### Implantation Site

- **Blastocyst** implants in endometrium (~6-10 days post-[[Fertilisation]])
- Usually implants in upper posterior body wall (ideal location)
- Trophoblast cells invade endometrium and form [[Placenta]]
- Requires:
    - Properly developed endometrium (secretory phase)
    - Synchronised embryo development (blastocyst stage)
    - Molecular signalling (integrins, selectins)

### Pregnancy Functions

- **Accommodates growing fetus** (expands ~500-fold in volume)
- **Nourishes fetus** via [[Placenta]] anchored to endometrium
- **Protects fetus** within muscular, fluid-filled cavity
- **Maintains pregnancy**: myometrium remains relaxed (progesterone inhibits contractions)
- **[[Labour]]**: powerful rhythmic contractions expel fetus
- **Involution**: returns to near-normal size postpartum (~6 weeks)

## Hormonal Regulation

### Estrogen Effects

- **Proliferative phase**: stimulates endometrial growth
- Increases myometrial contractility
- Upregulates progesterone receptors
- Increases uterine blood flow

### Progesterone Effects

- **Secretory phase**: causes endometrial glands to secrete glycogen-rich fluid
- Maintains endometrium for implantation
- Inhibits myometrial contractions (maintains pregnancy)
- Decidualisation of endometrium (pregnancy-specific changes)

### Oxytocin

- Stimulates myometrial contractions during [[Labour]]
- Receptors upregulated at term
- Positive feedback loop (contractions → more oxytocin → stronger contractions)

### Prostaglandins

- PGF2α and PGE2 stimulate myometrial contractions
- Involved in menstruation and [[Labour]]
- NSAIDs (reduce prostaglandin synthesis) can relieve menstrual cramps

## Clinical Relevance

### Menstrual Disorders

**Amenorrhea** (absence of menstruation):

- **Primary**: no menarche by age 15
- **Secondary**: previously menstruated, now absent >3 months
- Causes: pregnancy, PCOS, thyroid disorders, excessive exercise, stress, eating disorders

**Dysmenorrhea** (painful periods):

- **Primary**: prostaglandin-mediated, no underlying pathology
- **Secondary**: due to endometriosis, fibroids, PID
- Treatment: NSAIDs, hormonal contraceptives, heat therapy

**Menorrhagia** (heavy menstrual bleeding):

- > 80 mL blood loss or >7 days duration
    
- Causes: hormonal imbalances, fibroids, polyps, bleeding disorders, IUD
- Can lead to anemia
- Treatment: hormonal therapy, NSAIDs, surgical (endometrial ablation, hysterectomy)

**Metrorrhagia** (irregular bleeding):

- Bleeding between periods
- Causes: hormonal imbalances, cervical/endometrial pathology, pregnancy complications

### Fibroids (Leiomyomas)

- **Benign [[Smooth Muscle]] tumors** of myometrium
- Very common (~70% of women by age 50)
- Often asymptomatic, but can cause:
    - Heavy menstrual bleeding
    - Pelvic pain/pressure
    - Urinary frequency (if large)
    - Infertility or pregnancy complications
- Estrogen-dependent (grow during reproductive years, shrink after [[Menopause]])
- **Types** by location:
    - **Submucosal**: protrude into uterine cavity (most symptomatic)
    - **Intramural**: within myometrium (most common)
    - **Subserosal**: project outward from uterus
- **Treatment**: observation, hormonal therapy, uterine artery embolization, myomectomy (surgical removal), hysterectomy

### Endometrial Cancer

- Most common gynecological malignancy in developed countries
- Usually postmenopausal women
- **Risk factors**: unopposed estrogen (obesity, PCOS, estrogen-only HRT), nulliparity, [[Diabetes]], tamoxifen use
- **Protective factors**: combined oral contraceptives, pregnancy, progesterone therapy
- **Symptoms**: abnormal vaginal bleeding (postmenopausal bleeding always requires investigation)
- **Diagnosis**: endometrial biopsy, transvaginal [[Ultrasound]] (measures endometrial thickness)
- **Treatment**: hysterectomy with bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy, radiation, chemotherapy, hormone therapy

### Endometriosis

- Endometrial tissue grows outside uterus ([[Ovaries]], fallopian tubes, peritoneum)
- Responds to hormonal cycles → cyclical bleeding and inflammation → scarring and adhesions
- **Symptoms**: pelvic pain (especially during menstruation), dyspareunia (painful intercourse), infertility
- **Diagnosis**: laparoscopy (gold standard), imaging
- **Treatment**: NSAIDs, hormonal suppression (contraceptives, GnRH agonists), surgery (ablation/excision of lesions)

### Adenomyosis

- Endometrial tissue grows into myometrium
- **Symptoms**: heavy painful periods, enlarged uterus
- Often coexists with fibroids
- **Diagnosis**: [[MRI]], transvaginal [[Ultrasound]]
- **Treatment**: NSAIDs, hormonal therapy, hysterectomy (definitive)

### Uterine Prolapse

- Descent of uterus into/through [[Vagina]] due to weakened pelvic floor support
- **Risk factors**: multiple vaginal deliveries, [[Menopause]], obesity, chronic cough, heavy lifting
- **Grades**:
    - Grade 1: [[Cervix]] descends into [[Vagina]]
    - Grade 2: [[Cervix]] reaches vaginal opening
    - Grade 3: uterus protrudes outside [[Vagina]] (procidentia)
- **Symptoms**: pelvic pressure, "bulge" sensation, urinary/bowel symptoms
- **Treatment**: pelvic floor exercises, pessary (supportive device), surgical repair, hysterectomy

### Asherman's Syndrome

- Intrauterine adhesions (scarring) from trauma (D&C, infection, surgery)
- Causes menstrual abnormalities, infertility, recurrent miscarriage
- **Treatment**: hysteroscopic adhesiolysis

### Congenital Anomalies

Due to abnormal Müllerian duct development:

- **Septate uterus**: partition divides cavity
- **Bicornuate uterus**: two uterine horns
- **Didelphys**: complete duplication (two uteri, two cervices)
- **Unicornuate**: one-sided uterus
- Can affect fertility and pregnancy outcomes

## Pregnancy-Related Changes

### First Trimester

- Endometrium transforms into **decidua** (pregnancy-specific lining)
- Implantation site forms [[Placenta]]
- Uterus begins to enlarge (still pelvic organ)

### Second Trimester

- Uterus rises into abdomen (palpable at umbilicus ~20 weeks)
- Myometrium stretches and thins
- Braxton Hicks contractions begin (irregular, painless)

### Third Trimester

- Fundus reaches xiphoid process (~36 weeks)
- Increased blood flow (~500-700 mL/min at term)
- Oxytocin receptors upregulated
- Prostaglandin production increases

### Labour

- Coordinated, rhythmic myometrial contractions
- [[Cervix]] dilates and effaces
- Three stages: dilation, expulsion, placental delivery

### Postpartum

- **Involution**: uterus shrinks back toward normal size (~6 weeks)
- **Lochia**: vaginal discharge (blood, tissue) for several weeks
- Breastfeeding stimulates oxytocin → enhances involution

## Adaptations for Function

- **Thick myometrium** capable of powerful contractions for [[Labour]]
- **Cyclical endometrial changes** prepare for potential implantation each cycle
- **Rich vascular supply** supports pregnancy
- **Expandable cavity** accommodates growing fetus
- **Ligamentous support** maintains position despite pregnancy weight
- **Hormonal responsiveness** coordinates menstrual cycle and pregnancy

## Summary

The uterus is a remarkably adaptable organ, undergoing cyclical changes each month to prepare for potential pregnancy. Its thick muscular walls expand dramatically during gestation, provide protection and nourishment to the fetus, and generate powerful contractions during [[Labour]]. When pregnancy does not occur, the endometrium is shed during menstruation, and the cycle begins anew. Disorders of the uterus can significantly affect menstruation, fertility, and pregnancy outcomes.
