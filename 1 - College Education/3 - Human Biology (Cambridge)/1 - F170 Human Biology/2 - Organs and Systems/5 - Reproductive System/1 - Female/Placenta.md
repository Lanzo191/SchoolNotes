The **placenta** is a temporary fetal organ that develops during pregnancy to provide oxygen and nutrients to the fetus while removing waste products. It also functions as an endocrine organ, producing hormones essential for maintaining pregnancy.

## Formation and Development

### Implantation (Week 1-2)

- Blastocyst (5-6 days post-[[Fertilisation]]) adheres to endometrium
- **Trophoblast** (outer cell layer) invades endometrium
- Differentiates into:
    - **Cytotrophoblast**: inner cellular layer
    - **Syncytiotrophoblast**: outer multinucleated layer that invades maternal tissue

### Early Placental Development (Weeks 2-12)

- Syncytiotrophoblast erodes maternal blood vessels
- **Lacunae** (spaces) form, fill with maternal blood
- **Primary chorionic villi** develop (week 2): cytotrophoblast projections
- **Secondary villi** (week 3): mesoderm invades villi core
- **Tertiary villi** (week 3): fetal blood vessels develop within villi
- Villi branch extensively, creating large surface area

### Mature Placenta (Week 12+)

- Fully functional by **12-14 weeks**
- Continues growing throughout pregnancy
- At term: **15-20 cm diameter, 2-3 cm thick, weighs ~500-600 g** (1/6 of fetal weight)

## Structure

### Fetal Side

- **Chorionic plate**: smooth, covered by amnion
- Umbilical cord attaches centrally (usually)
- Branching **chorionic villi** project into intervillous spaces
- **Fetal blood vessels** within villi

### Maternal Side

- **Basal plate**: rough, lobulated appearance
- **Cotyledons**: 15-20 lobes visible (functional units)
- **Decidua basalis**: modified endometrium at implantation site

### Intervillous Space

- Large blood-filled space between villi
- Contains **maternal blood** (150-200 mL at any time)
- Maternal blood bathes fetal villi (no direct mixing of blood)

### Placental Barrier (Blood-Placental Interface)

**Layers separating maternal and fetal blood**:

1. **Syncytiotrophoblast**: outermost layer in contact with maternal blood
2. **Cytotrophoblast**: present early pregnancy, sparse later
3. **Basement membrane**
4. **[[Connective Tissue]]** of villus core
5. **Basement membrane** of fetal capillary
6. **Endothelium** of fetal capillary

**By late pregnancy**: barrier thins (syncytiotrophoblast + endothelium only in many areas)

- Facilitates exchange
- Prevents direct mixing of maternal and fetal blood
- **Selectively permeable**

### Umbilical Cord

- Connects fetus to placenta
- **50-60 cm long, 1.5-2 cm diameter** at term
- Contains:
    - **Two umbilical [[Arteries]]**: carry deoxygenated blood from fetus to placenta
    - **One umbilical vein**: carries oxygenated blood from placenta to fetus
    - **Wharton's jelly**: protective gelatinous [[Connective Tissue]]
- Covered by amnion
- Spirals (helical twist) provide flexibility

## Function

### 1. Gas Exchange

- **Oxygen**: diffuses from maternal blood → fetal blood
- **Carbon dioxide**: diffuses from fetal blood → maternal blood
- **Mechanisms**:
    - [[Simple Diffusion]] (concentration gradient)
    - Fetal hemoglobin has **higher oxygen affinity** than maternal (facilitates transfer)
    - **Bohr effect**: maternal CO₂ promotes oxygen release
    - **Double Bohr effect**: fetal CO₂ pickup enhances maternal oxygen release

### 2. Nutrient Transfer

**Passive diffusion**: water, electrolytes, fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) **[[Facilitated Diffusion]]**: [[Glucose]] (main fetal energy source) **[[Active Transport]]**: [[Amino Acids]], water-soluble vitamins (B, C), [[Calcium]], iron **Pinocytosis**: large [[Proteins]] (immunoglobulins - mainly IgG)

### 3. Waste Removal

- **Urea, creatinine**: diffuse from fetal to maternal blood
- **Bilirubin**: transferred for maternal [[Liver]] processing
- Maternal kidneys excrete fetal waste

### 4. Immunological Function

- **IgG antibodies** cross placenta → provide **passive [[Immunity]]** to fetus
- Protects newborn for first 3-6 months
- **Antibodies transferred**:
    - Protection against infections (tetanus, measles, mumps, rubella)
    - Unfortunately, also harmful antibodies (Rh incompatibility)
- **IgM, IgA** do not cross placenta (too large)

### 5. Endocrine (Hormone Production)

**Human Chorionic Gonadotropin (hCG)**:

- Produced by syncytiotrophoblast from implantation
- **Maintains corpus luteum** (progesterone production) in early pregnancy
- Detectable in blood/urine (pregnancy tests)
- Peaks at 8-10 weeks, then declines

**Progesterone**:

- Initially from corpus luteum
- **Placenta takes over production** by 10-12 weeks
- Essential for:
    - Maintaining endometrium
    - Reducing uterine contractility (prevents premature [[Labour]])
    - Breast development
- Levels rise throughout pregnancy

**Estrogens** (mainly estriol):

- Produced in large amounts
- Increase blood flow to [[Uterus]]
- Stimulate uterine growth
- Promote breast development
- Soften connective tissues (prepare pelvis for birth)

**Human Placental Lactogen (hPL) / Human Chorionic Somatomammotropin**:

- Protein hormone structurally similar to growth hormone
- Functions:
    - Promotes maternal [[Insulin]] resistance (ensures [[Glucose]] availability for fetus)
    - Stimulates breast development
    - Mobilises maternal fat stores
- Levels rise steadily throughout pregnancy

**Other hormones**: relaxin, corticotropin-releasing hormone (CRH)

### 6. Protection (Limited)

- Physical barrier to some pathogens
- **Many substances cross**: alcohol, drugs, viruses (HIV, Zika, rubella, cytomegalovirus), some bacteria (syphilis, Listeria)
- **Selective permeability** provides some protection
- NOT an absolute barrier (teratogens can harm fetus)

## Blood Flow

### Maternal Blood Flow

- **Spiral [[Arteries]]** from [[Uterus]] deliver maternal blood into intervillous space
- Blood bathes chorionic villi
- Returns via **uterine [[Veins]]**
- **Flow rate**: ~500-700 mL/min at term (10-15% of maternal cardiac output)

### Fetal Blood Flow

- **Umbilical [[Arteries]]** (from fetal iliac [[Arteries]]) carry deoxygenated blood to placenta
- Blood enters fetal [[Capillaries]] in chorionic villi
- Gas and nutrient exchange occurs
- **Umbilical vein** returns oxygenated, nutrient-rich blood to fetus
- Most blood bypasses fetal [[Liver]] via **ductus venosus** → inferior vena cava → heart

## Clinical Relevance

### Placental Abruption

- **Premature separation** of placenta from uterine wall
- **Causes**: trauma, hypertension, cocaine use, smoking
- **Symptoms**: vaginal bleeding, abdominal pain, uterine tenderness, fetal distress
- **Complications**: maternal hemorrhage, fetal hypoxia, death
- **Treatment**: immediate delivery if severe

### Placenta Previa

- Placenta partially or completely **covers cervical os**
- **Types**: complete, partial, marginal, low-lying
- **Risk factors**: previous cesarean, multiparity, advanced maternal age, smoking
- **Symptoms**: painless vaginal bleeding (usually late 2nd/3rd trimester)
- **Diagnosis**: [[Ultrasound]]
- **Complications**: hemorrhage, preterm birth
- **Treatment**: cesarean delivery (vaginal delivery would cause catastrophic bleeding)

### Placenta Accreta, Increta, Percreta

- **Abnormally adherent placenta** invades uterine wall
- **Accreta**: attaches to myometrium (most common)
- **Increta**: invades into myometrium
- **Percreta**: penetrates through myometrium (may reach bladder, bowel)
- **Risk factors**: previous cesarean, placenta previa, advanced maternal age
- **Complications**: retained placenta, severe postpartum hemorrhage
- **Treatment**: often requires hysterectomy

### Placental Insufficiency

- Placenta unable to deliver adequate oxygen/nutrients to fetus
- **Causes**: maternal hypertension, [[Diabetes]], smoking, autoimmune disease
- **Consequences**: **intrauterine growth restriction (IUGR)**, fetal distress
- **Diagnosis**: [[Ultrasound]] (small fetal size), Doppler (abnormal blood flow)
- **Management**: close monitoring, early delivery if severe

### Retained Placenta

- Placenta not delivered within 30 minutes after birth
- **Types**:
    - **Adherent**: [[Uterus]] not contracting effectively
    - **Trapped**: [[Cervix]] closes before placental expulsion
    - **Morbidly adherent**: accreta/increta/percreta
- **Complications**: postpartum hemorrhage, infection
- **Treatment**: manual removal, medication (oxytocin), surgery

### Rh Incompatibility

- Maternal Rh-negative, fetal Rh-positive (from Rh-positive father)
- Maternal exposure to fetal blood (delivery, miscarriage, trauma) → maternal antibodies form
- **Hemolytic disease of newborn** in subsequent Rh-positive pregnancies
- Maternal IgG antibodies cross placenta → destroy fetal red blood cells
- **Prevention**: **Rh immunoglobulin (RhoGAM)** given to Rh-negative mothers at 28 weeks and after delivery

### Infections Crossing Placenta

- **TORCH infections**:
    - **T**oxoplasmosis
    - **O**ther (syphilis, varicella, parvovirus B19)
    - **R**ubella
    - **C**ytomegalovirus (CMV)
    - **H**erpes simplex virus
- Can cause congenital anomalies, growth restriction, stillbirth
- **Zika virus**: microcephaly, brain abnormalities
- **HIV**: can be transmitted, but antiretroviral therapy greatly reduces risk

### Drugs and Teratogens

- **Alcohol**: fetal alcohol syndrome (facial abnormalities, intellectual disability)
- **Tobacco**: IUGR, preterm birth, placental abruption
- **Medications**: isotretinoin (severe birth defects), warfarin (skeletal abnormalities), ACE inhibitors ([[Kidney]] damage)
- **Illicit drugs**: cocaine (abruption, IUGR), opioids (withdrawal in newborn)

## Examination of Placenta

### Gross Examination

- **Size, weight**: small placenta suggests insufficiency
- **Umbilical cord**: insertion site, number of vessels (normal = 2 [[Arteries]] + 1 vein; single umbilical artery associated with anomalies)
- **Membranes**: color, completeness
- **Cotyledons**: number, appearance
- **Infarctions, calcifications**: common in post-term pregnancies

### Histological Examination

- Microscopic analysis if:
    - Stillbirth, neonatal death
    - Growth restriction
    - Multiple gestation
    - Maternal/fetal infection suspected
- Assess villous structure, blood vessels, inflammatory changes

## Delivery of Placenta (Third Stage of Labour)

- **Physiological management**: await spontaneous placental separation and delivery
- **Active management**: oxytocin given after birth, controlled cord traction
- **Signs of separation**: gush of blood, cord lengthening, [[Uterus]] rises and becomes firm
- **Normally delivered within 30 minutes**
- **Inspection**: ensure placenta and membranes complete (retained fragments → hemorrhage, infection)

## Adaptations for Function

- **Large surface area** (~11-14 m²): extensive villous branching
- **Thin barrier** (late pregnancy): facilitates exchange
- **Rich vascularisation**: maternal and fetal sides
- **Countercurrent exchange**: enhances efficiency
- **Selective permeability**: protects fetus while allowing necessary transfer
- **Endocrine function**: produces hormones locally for pregnancy maintenance

## Summary

The placenta is a remarkable temporary organ that performs the functions of multiple organ systems for the developing fetus ([[Lungs]], kidneys, [[Liver]], endocrine glands, immune system). Its structure allows efficient exchange of gases, nutrients, and waste while preventing direct mixing of maternal and fetal blood. Placental dysfunction or abnormalities can have serious consequences for both mother and baby, highlighting the importance of prenatal monitoring and care.
