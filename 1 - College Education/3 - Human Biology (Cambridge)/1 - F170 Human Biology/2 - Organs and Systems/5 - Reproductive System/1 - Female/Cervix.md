The **cervix** is the lower cylindrical portion of the [[Uterus]] that connects the uterine cavity to the [[Vagina]]. It acts as a protective barrier and gateway between the external environment and the sterile uterine cavity.

## Structure

### Location and Size

- Lower portion of [[Uterus]], projecting into upper [[Vagina]]
- Approximately **2.5-3 cm long, 2.5 cm diameter**
- **Supravaginal portion**: above vaginal attachment
- **Vaginal portion**: protrudes into [[Vagina]] (visible during speculum examination)

### Cervical Canal

- Connects uterine cavity to [[Vagina]]
- **Internal os**: opening into uterine cavity
- **External os**: opening into [[Vagina]]
- **Nulliparous** (never given birth): external os small and circular
- **Parous** (given birth vaginally): external os slit-like, transverse

### Microscopic Structure

**Endocervix** (cervical canal lining):

- **Simple columnar epithelium**
- **Mucus-secreting glands** (cervical crypts)
- Secretes cervical mucus

**Ectocervix** (vaginal portion):

- **Stratified squamous epithelium** (non-keratinised)
- Resistant to acidic vaginal environment and friction

**Transformation Zone**:

- Junction between columnar and squamous epithelium
- Location changes with age, pregnancy, hormones
- **Clinically significant**: most cervical cancers arise here
- Site targeted during **Pap smear** screening

**Stroma**:

- Dense fibrous [[Connective Tissue]] (mostly collagen)
- Provides structural strength
- Less muscular than uterine body (~15% [[Smooth Muscle]])

## Function

### Cervical Mucus Production

- Secreted by endocervical glands
- Composition and consistency **vary with menstrual cycle**:

**[[Follicular Phase]] (Estrogen-dominant)**:

- Thin, watery, alkaline mucus
- **"Spinnbarkeit"**: stretchy (can stretch 10+ cm)
- **Ferning pattern** when dried (crystallisation due to salt content)
- Facilitates sperm passage through cervix

**[[Luteal Phase]] (Progesterone-dominant)**:

- Thick, viscous, acidic mucus
- Forms **mucus plug**
- Hostile to sperm penetration
- Protective barrier against infection

**Pregnancy**:

- Thick mucus plug seals cervical canal
- Protects fetus from ascending infections

### Sperm Transport

- Cervical mucus filters out abnormal/dead sperm
- Provides energy ([[Glucose]]) for sperm
- **Sperm reservoir**: crypts store viable sperm (up to 5 days)
- Capacitation begins here

### Protective Barrier

- Mucus plug prevents bacterial ascent
- Acidic environment (pH ~4.5) from [[Vagina]] less hostile in cervical canal
- Immune cells (neutrophils, macrophages) patrol cervical mucus

### Structural Support

- Supports [[Uterus]] (along with ligaments)
- Maintains pregnancy (cervix remains closed)
- Dilates during [[Labour]] to allow fetal passage

### Menstruation

- During menstruation, cervix slightly dilates
- Facilitates passage of menstrual blood from [[Uterus]]

## Hormonal Regulation

### Estrogen Effects

- Increases mucus production
- Makes mucus thin, watery, sperm-friendly
- Increases alkalinity
- Promotes ferning

### Progesterone Effects

- Decreases mucus production
- Makes mucus thick, viscous, sperm-hostile
- Forms protective plug
- Increases acidity

## Changes During Pregnancy

### Early Pregnancy

- **Chadwick's sign**: bluish discoloration (increased vascularity)
- **Goodell's sign**: softening of cervix
- Mucus plug forms (**operculum**)

### Throughout Pregnancy

- Cervix remains **firm and closed** (cervical competence)
- Length typically >2.5 cm

### Late Pregnancy (Weeks 37-40)

- **Cervical ripening**: softening and shortening
- Increased prostaglandin and relaxin production
- Collagen breakdown

### Labour

- **Effacement**: thinning and shortening (measured as %)
- **Dilation**: opening of external os (measured in cm, 0-10 cm)
- Full dilation (10 cm) allows fetal head to pass

### Postpartum

- External os remains slightly open (parous os)
- Cervix never returns to pre-pregnancy state fully

## Clinical Relevance

### Cervical Cancer

- **7th most common cancer in women** globally
- Almost all cases caused by **human papillomavirus (HPV)**
- **High-risk HPV types**: 16, 18 (cause 70% of cervical cancers)
- **Precancerous changes**: cervical intraepithelial neoplasia (CIN 1, 2, 3)

**Risk Factors**:

- HPV infection (most important)
- Smoking
- Multiple sexual partners
- Early sexual activity
- Immunosuppression (HIV)
- Long-term oral contraceptive use

**Screening**:

- **Pap smear (cervical cytology)**: detects abnormal cells
- **HPV testing**: detects high-risk HPV types
- **Guidelines** (vary by country):
    - Age 25-65: every 3-5 years
    - Co-testing (Pap + HPV) or HPV primary screening

**Prevention**:

- **HPV vaccination**: protects against HPV 16, 18 (and others)
- Recommended for girls and boys age 11-12 (catch-up to age 26)
- Safe sex practices

**Treatment** (depends on stage):

- **CIN (precancerous)**: excision (LLETZ/LEEP, cone biopsy), cryotherapy, laser
- **Early invasive cancer**: surgery (trachelectomy, hysterectomy)
- **Advanced cancer**: radiation, chemotherapy, surgery

### Cervical Incompetence (Cervical Insufficiency)

- Cervix weakens and dilates prematurely during pregnancy
- Leads to **second-trimester miscarriage or preterm birth**
- **Risk factors**: previous cervical trauma (cone biopsy, D&C), DES exposure, congenital abnormalities
- **Diagnosis**: short cervix on [[Ultrasound]] (<2.5 cm before 24 weeks), history of painless dilation
- **Treatment**: **cervical cerclage** (stitch placed to hold cervix closed), progesterone, bed rest
- Cerclage removed ~37 weeks or if [[Labour]] starts

### Cervicitis

- Inflammation of cervix
- **Causes**: sexually transmitted infections (chlamydia, gonorrhea), bacterial overgrowth, allergic reaction
- **Symptoms**: abnormal vaginal discharge, bleeding after intercourse, pelvic pain
- **Complications**: pelvic inflammatory disease, infertility
- **Treatment**: antibiotics for bacterial infections

### Cervical Polyps

- Benign growths protruding from cervical canal
- Often asymptomatic, may cause bleeding
- **Treatment**: removal (polypectomy) if symptomatic or suspicious

### Nabothian Cysts

- Benign mucus-filled cysts on cervical surface
- Occur when squamous epithelium blocks mucus glands
- Very common, usually asymptomatic, no treatment needed

### Cervical Stenosis

- Narrowing or closure of cervical canal
- **Causes**: surgery, radiation, [[Menopause]], infection
- **Symptoms**: painful periods (dysmenorrhea), difficulty with menstruation, infertility
- **Treatment**: cervical dilation

## Examination and Diagnosis

### Speculum Examination

- Visualises cervix and vaginal walls
- Assesses cervical appearance (colour, discharge, lesions)
- Collects samples for Pap smear, cultures

### Pap Smear (Cervical Cytology)

- Cells scraped from transformation zone
- Examined microscopically for abnormalities
- Detects precancerous and cancerous cells

### Colposcopy

- Magnified examination of cervix
- Performed if Pap smear abnormal
- Allows biopsy of suspicious areas
- Acetic acid (vinegar) applied → abnormal areas turn white (acetowhite)

### Cervical Biopsy

- Small tissue sample removed for histology
- Confirms diagnosis of CIN or cancer
- **Types**: punch biopsy, cone biopsy (LLETZ/LEEP)

### Transvaginal Ultrasound

- Measures cervical length during pregnancy
- Short cervix (<2.5 cm) indicates increased preterm birth risk

## Cervical Mucus and Fertility

### Fertile Window

- ~6 days before [[Ovulation]] to day of [[Ovulation]]
- Cervical mucus most receptive to sperm
- Clear, stretchy, resembles raw egg white

### Infertility Evaluation

- **Postcoital test**: examines cervical mucus after intercourse (now rarely performed)
- Hostile cervical mucus can impair sperm penetration
- Antisperm antibodies in mucus

### Fertility Awareness Methods

- Monitoring cervical mucus changes
- Can help identify fertile days
- "Billings [[Ovulation]] method"

## Adaptations for Function

- **Dual epithelium**: columnar (mucus production) and squamous (protection)
- **Transformation zone**: allows tissue remodelling in response to hormones and pH
- **Cervical crypts**: store sperm, act as reservoir
- **Cyclical mucus changes**: control sperm access based on fertility
- **Strong fibrous stroma**: maintains pregnancy despite uterine weight
- **Capacity to dilate**: allows passage of fetus during birth

## Summary

The cervix serves multiple critical functions: acting as a selective barrier between the [[Vagina]] and [[Uterus]], facilitating sperm transport during fertile periods, maintaining pregnancy, and dilating during [[Labour]]. Its unique structure, with cyclical mucus changes and dual epithelium, reflects these diverse roles. Cervical cancer screening and HPV vaccination have dramatically reduced cervical cancer incidence, highlighting the importance of preventive healthcare for this organ.
