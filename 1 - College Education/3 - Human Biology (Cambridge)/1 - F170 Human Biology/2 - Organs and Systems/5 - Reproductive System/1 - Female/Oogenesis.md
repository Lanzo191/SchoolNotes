**Oogenesis** is the process of producing mature female gametes (ova or eggs) from primordial germ cells. Unlike spermatogenesis, oogenesis begins during fetal development, arrests at specific stages, and resumes cyclically after puberty, producing typically one ovum per menstrual cycle.

## Timeline Overview
- **Fetal life**: Begins, arrests in prophase I
- **Puberty to [[Menopause]]**: Resumes cyclically, one oocyte per month ovulates
- **At [[Fertilisation]]**: [[Meiosis]] II completes

## Location
- Occurs in **ovarian follicles** within the ovarian cortex
- Supported by **granulosa cells and theca cells**

## Stages of Oogenesis

### 1. Fetal Development (Weeks 5-7 of Gestation)
**Primordial Germ Cells**:
- Migrate from yolk sac to developing gonads (genital ridges)
- Differentiate into **oogonia** (diploid, 46 [[Chromosomes]])

**Oogonial Proliferation** (Months 2-5):
- Oogonia undergo **rapid mitotic divisions**
- Peak population: ~6-7 million oogonia at 5 months gestation
- From ~7 months, **atresia** (cell death) begins

**Primary Oocyte Formation** (Months 5-9):
- Oogonia enter **[[Meiosis]] I**
- Become **primary oocytes** (diploid, 46 [[Chromosomes]], 4n [[DNA]])
- **Arrest in prophase I** (diplotene stage) before birth
- Surrounded by single layer of flat follicular cells → **primordial follicle**
- Remain arrested for years to decades until [[Ovulation]]

**At Birth**:
- ~1-2 million primordial follicles
- No new oocytes formed after birth (fixed pool)

### 2. Childhood
- Continued **atresia**: oocytes progressively lost
- Follicles remain dormant in primordial stage
- By **puberty**: ~300,000-400,000 remain

### 3. Reproductive Years (Menarche to Menopause)

**Each Menstrual Cycle**:

**[[Follicular Phase]] (Days 1-14)**:
- **FSH** stimulates cohort of primordial follicles to begin developing
- **Primary follicle** formation:
  - Follicular cells become cuboidal (granulosa cells)
  - **Zona pellucida** (glycoprotein layer) forms around oocyte
  - Oocyte grows in size (accumulates organelles, [[mRNA]], [[Proteins]])
  - Primary oocyte still arrested in prophase I

**Secondary follicle** formation:
- **Antrum** (fluid-filled cavity) develops among granulosa cells
- **Theca interna and externa** layers form outside granulosa cells
- Granulosa cells proliferate
- Oocyte surrounded by **corona radiata** (innermost granulosa cells)
- Usually **one dominant follicle** selected (largest, most FSH receptors)

**Preovulatory (Graafian) follicle**:
- Large (15-25 mm diameter)
- Oocyte positioned eccentrically on **cumulus oophorus**
- High estrogen production by granulosa cells
- Ready for [[Ovulation]]

**Completion of [[Meiosis]] I** (~36 hours before [[Ovulation]]):
- **LH surge** triggers resumption of [[Meiosis]] I
- Primary oocyte completes **[[Meiosis]] I**
- **Asymmetric division**:
  - **Secondary oocyte** (haploid, 23 [[Chromosomes]], 2n [[DNA]]): receives nearly all cytoplasm
  - **First polar body**: tiny cell with minimal cytoplasm, degenerates
- Secondary oocyte begins **[[Meiosis]] II**
- **Arrests in metaphase II** (held by cytostatic factor CSF)

**[[Ovulation]] (Day ~14)**:
- LH surge triggers [[Ovulation]]
- Mature follicle ruptures
- **Secondary oocyte** (arrested in metaphase II) released
- Surrounded by zona pellucida and corona radiata
- Captured by fimbriae of fallopian tube

### 4. Meiosis II Completion (Only if Fertilisation Occurs)
- Secondary oocyte remains arrested in **metaphase II** until [[Fertilisation]]
- If fertilised:
  - Sperm penetration triggers **completion of [[Meiosis]] II**
  - **Asymmetric division**:
    - **Mature ovum** (haploid, 23 [[Chromosomes]], 1n [[DNA]]): retains nearly all cytoplasm
    - **Second polar body**: tiny cell, degenerates
- **First polar body** may also divide → 3 polar bodies total (all degenerate)

- If **not fertilised** within ~24 hours:
  - Secondary oocyte degenerates
  - No ovum formed

### 5. Fertilisation
- Sperm and ovum nuclei (now called **pronuclei**) fuse
- Forms diploid **zygote** (46 [[Chromosomes]])

## Follicle Development Summary
- **Primordial follicle**: dormant, primary oocyte + flat follicular cells
- **Primary follicle**: primary oocyte + cuboidal granulosa cells + zona pellucida
- **Secondary follicle**: primary oocyte + granulosa cells + antrum + theca layers
- **Mature (Graafian) follicle**: secondary oocyte (metaphase II) + large antrum + cumulus oophorus
- **Corpus luteum**: post-[[Ovulation]], secretes progesterone

## Key Differences from Spermatogenesis

### Timing
- **Oogenesis**: begins fetal life, pauses, resumes cyclically at puberty
- **Spermatogenesis**: begins at puberty, continuous

### Number Produced
- **Oogenesis**: typically 1 per month, ~400 total in lifetime
- **Spermatogenesis**: ~200-300 million per day

### Meiotic Products
- **Oogenesis**: 1 functional ovum + 3 polar bodies per oogonium
- **Spermatogenesis**: 4 functional sperm per spermatogonium

### Cell Size
- **Ovum**: large (~120 µm diameter), nutrient-rich cytoplasm
- **Sperm**: small (~60 µm total), minimal cytoplasm

### Meiotic Arrests
- **Oogenesis**: arrests twice (prophase I and metaphase II)
- **Spermatogenesis**: no arrests, continuous through both divisions

### Asymmetric vs Symmetric Division
- **Oogenesis**: unequal cytoplasm distribution (ovum gets almost all)
- **Spermatogenesis**: equal cytoplasm distribution

## Hormonal Regulation

### Follicular Phase
**FSH**:
- Stimulates follicle growth
- Promotes granulosa cell proliferation and estrogen synthesis

**LH** (low levels):
- Stimulates theca cells to produce androgens
- Androgens converted to estrogen by granulosa cells (aromatase enzyme)

**Estrogen** (rising):
- Stimulates endometrial proliferation
- Negative feedback on FSH at low-moderate levels
- Positive feedback triggers LH surge at high levels (mid-cycle)

### Ovulation
**LH surge** (~36 hours before [[Ovulation]]):
- Completes [[Meiosis]] I (primary → secondary oocyte)
- Triggers [[Ovulation]] (follicle rupture)
- Initiates corpus luteum formation

### Luteal Phase
**Progesterone** (from corpus luteum):
- Maintains secretory endometrium
- Inhibits FSH and LH (negative feedback)
- Supports early pregnancy if [[Fertilisation]] occurs

**Estrogen** (from corpus luteum):
- Lower levels than [[Follicular Phase]]
- Also inhibits FSH and LH

**hCG** (if pregnant):
- Produced by blastocyst/[[Placenta]]
- Maintains corpus luteum beyond usual 14-day lifespan
- Corpus luteum continues progesterone production until [[Placenta]] takes over (~10-12 weeks)

## Mature Ovum Structure

### Plasma Membrane
- Selectively permeable
- Contains **receptors** for sperm binding
- **Cortical granules** just beneath membrane (release contents after [[Fertilisation]] to prevent polyspermy)

### Zona Pellucida
- Thick **glycoprotein layer** (~15 µm) surrounding oocyte
- Contains **ZP1, ZP2, ZP3** [[Proteins]]
- Functions:
  - Sperm binding (ZP3)
  - Induces acrosome reaction
  - Prevents polyspermy after [[Fertilisation]] (hardens → zona reaction)
- Protects oocyte/early embryo

### Corona Radiata
- Layer of granulosa cells surrounding zona pellucida
- Expelled with oocyte at [[Ovulation]]
- Provides nutrients
- Facilitates oocyte capture by fallopian tube

### Cytoplasm
- **Nutrient-rich**: yolk [[Proteins]], lipids, glycogen
- **Organelles**: abundant [[Mitochondria]], [[Ribosomes]], Golgi, ER
- **[[mRNA]] and [[Proteins]]** stored for early embryonic development (before embryonic genome activation ~day 3)
- **Cortical granules**: vesicles containing enzymes that modify zona pellucida post-[[Fertilisation]]

### Nucleus (until fertilisation)
- Arrested in **metaphase II**
- [[Chromosomes]] aligned on metaphase plate
- Completes division only if sperm penetrates

## Oocyte Maturation

### Cytoplasmic Maturation
- Accumulation of:
  - **[[mRNA]]**: for early embryo development
  - **[[Proteins]]**: enzymes, [[Transcription]] factors
  - **Organelles**: [[Mitochondria]], [[Ribosomes]], Golgi
  - **Nutrients**: lipids, [[Proteins]]
- Reorganisation of cytoplasm

### Nuclear Maturation
- Chromosome condensation
- Spindle formation
- Progression through [[Meiosis]] (triggered by LH surge)

## Atresia
- **99.9%** of oocytes undergo atresia (never ovulate)
- Continuous process from fetal life through [[Menopause]]
- Mechanisms:
  - Apoptosis (programmed cell death)
  - Triggered by insufficient gonadotropin support, [[DNA]] damage, oxidative stress
- Follicles can undergo atresia at any stage of development

## Ovarian Reserve
- Total number of remaining oocytes/follicles
- Declines with age:
  - **Birth**: ~1-2 million
  - **Puberty**: ~300,000-400,000
  - **Age 30**: ~100,000
  - **Age 40**: ~25,000
  - **[[Menopause]]** (~50): <1,000
- **Fertility declines** significantly after age 35 (fewer, lower-quality oocytes)

## Age and Oocyte Quality
- Oocytes age along with the woman (arrested in prophase I for years/decades)
- **Risks increase with maternal age**:
  - **Aneuploidy** (abnormal chromosome number, e.g., [[Down Syndrome]] - trisomy 21)
  - Miscarriage
  - Infertility
- Chromosomal errors mostly occur during [[Meiosis]] I (due to prolonged arrest)
- **Spindle checkpoint** weakens with age

## Clinical Relevance

### Anovulation
- Absence of [[Ovulation]]
- **Causes**: PCOS, hypothalamic amenorrhea, hyperprolactinemia, thyroid disorders, premature ovarian insufficiency
- **Symptoms**: irregular/absent periods, infertility
- **Treatment**: depends on cause; [[Ovulation]] induction (clomiphene, letrozole, gonadotropins), lifestyle modification

### Diminished Ovarian Reserve (DOR)
- Reduced quantity and/or quality of remaining oocytes
- Can occur **before [[Menopause]]**
- **Causes**:
  - Age-related decline
  - Genetic factors
  - Chemotherapy/radiotherapy
  - Ovarian surgery
  - Autoimmune disorders
- **Indicators**:
  - Low **AMH** (anti-Müllerian hormone)
  - High **FSH** in early [[Follicular Phase]]
  - Reduced antral follicle count on [[Ultrasound]]
- **Consequences**:
  - Reduced fertility
  - Poor response to ovarian stimulation
- **Management**:
  - Assisted reproductive technologies (e.g. IVF)
  - Oocyte or embryo donation (if severe)

### Premature Ovarian Insufficiency (POI)
- Loss of normal ovarian function **before age 40**
- Characterised by:
  - Amenorrhea or oligomenorrhea
  - Low estrogen
  - High FSH and LH
- **Causes**:
  - Genetic (e.g. Turner syndrome)
  - Autoimmune destruction of follicles
  - Iatrogenic (chemotherapy, radiotherapy)
- **Effects**:
  - Infertility
  - Osteoporosis risk (low estrogen)
  - Cardiovascular risk
- **Treatment**:
  - Hormone replacement therapy (HRT)
  - Fertility treatment (egg donation if pregnancy desired)

### Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS)
- Common endocrine disorder affecting [[Ovulation]]
- **Pathophysiology**:
  - Increased LH relative to FSH
  - Excess androgen production
  - Arrested follicle development → anovulation
- **Ovarian features**:
  - Many small immature follicles
  - Thickened ovarian cortex
- **Symptoms**:
  - Irregular periods
  - Infertility
  - Hirsutism, acne
- **Management**:
  - Weight management
  - [[Ovulation]] induction (letrozole first-line)
  - Hormonal [[Contraception]] for symptom control

## Menopause
- Permanent cessation of [[Ovulation]] and menstruation
- Occurs at **~50–51 years**
- Defined as **12 consecutive months without menstruation**
- Caused by:
  - Depletion of ovarian follicles
  - Loss of estrogen and progesterone production

### Hormonal Changes
- ↓ Estrogen
- ↓ Progesterone
- ↑ FSH and LH (loss of negative feedback)

### Effects
- End of oogenesis
- Vasomotor symptoms (hot flushes)
- Vaginal dryness
- Osteoporosis risk
- Cardiovascular risk increases

## Summary: Key Exam Points (OCR)
- Oogenesis **begins before birth**
- Primary oocytes are **arrested in prophase I** for decades
- One primary oocyte → **one ovum + polar bodies**
- **[[Meiosis]] II completes only if [[Fertilisation]] occurs**
- Ovarian reserve is **finite and declines with age**
- Errors increase with maternal age → **aneuploidy**
- Hormonal control involves **FSH, LH, estrogen, progesterone**
- Atresia occurs throughout life
