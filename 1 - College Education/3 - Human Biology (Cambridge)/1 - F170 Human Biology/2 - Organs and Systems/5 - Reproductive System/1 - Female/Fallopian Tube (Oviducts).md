The **fallopian tubes** (also called oviducts or uterine tubes) are paired muscular tubes that transport the oocyte from the ovary to the [[Uterus]] and are the usual site of [[Fertilisation]].

## Structure

### Location and Length

- Extend laterally from the superior angles of the [[Uterus]]
- Approximately **10-12 cm long**
- Lie within the upper margin of the **broad ligament** (mesosalpinx)
- One tube for each ovary
- Not directly attached to [[Ovaries]] (small gap allows oocyte release into peritoneal cavity)

### Anatomical Regions

**1. Infundibulum** (funnel-shaped opening):

- **Fimbriae**: finger-like projections that sweep oocyte into tube
- **Ostium**: opening into peritoneal cavity
- Richest in cilia
- Moves actively at [[Ovulation]] to capture oocyte

**2. Ampulla** (widest and longest section):

- ~5-8 cm long
- Thin-walled and highly muscular
- **Usual site of [[Fertilisation]]**
- Accounts for majority of tube length

**3. Isthmus** (narrow segment):

- ~2-3 cm long
- Thick muscular wall
- Narrow lumen (~1 mm diameter)
- Connects to [[Uterus]]

**4. Intramural (interstitial) portion**:

- Passes through uterine wall
- ~1 cm long
- Narrowest part of tube

### Microscopic Structure

**Wall Layers**:

- **Mucosa**: highly folded epithelium (increases surface area)
    - Simple columnar epithelium with two cell types:
        - **Ciliated cells** (~60%): propel oocyte/embryo toward [[Uterus]]
        - **Secretory (peg) cells** (~40%): produce nutrient-rich fluid
    - No glands in mucosa
    - Folds most complex in ampulla, decrease toward isthmus
- **Muscularis**: inner circular and outer longitudinal [[Smooth Muscle]] layers
- **Serosa**: outer peritoneal covering (part of broad ligament)

## Blood Supply

- **Ovarian artery** (supplies lateral portion)
- **Uterine artery** (supplies medial portion)
- Both anastomose along tube length
- Venous drainage parallels arterial supply

## Nerve Supply

- Autonomic innervation from **ovarian and uterine plexuses**
- Sympathetic and parasympathetic fibres
- Sensory fibres detect pain (important in ectopic pregnancy)

## Function

### Oocyte Transport

- **Capture**: Fimbriae sweep oocyte into infundibulum at [[Ovulation]]
- **Transport**: Combination of ciliary action and [[Peristaltic Contractions]] move oocyte toward [[Uterus]]
- **Transit time**: ~3-4 days from ovary to [[Uterus]] (varies with hormonal state)
- Ciliary beating creates current that draws oocyte into tube

### Site of Fertilisation

- Sperm must swim through [[Uterus]] and into fallopian tube
- **[[Fertilisation]]** usually occurs in ampulla
- Sperm capacitation completed in female reproductive tract
- Only ~100-200 sperm reach ampulla (from millions ejaculated)
- Fertilised oocyte (now zygote) completes [[Meiosis]] II

### Early Embryo Support

- **Tubal fluid** provides nutrients for oocyte and early embryo
- Secretions from peg cells nourish dividing embryo
- Embryo undergoes cleavage divisions during transit to [[Uterus]]
- By time embryo reaches [[Uterus]], it's at **blastocyst stage** (~5-6 days post-[[Fertilisation]])

### Embryo Transport to Uterus

- Muscular contractions propel embryo toward [[Uterus]]
- Hormonal regulation ensures proper timing:
    - Estrogen increases motility
    - Progesterone relaxes [[Smooth Muscle]] (prevents premature expulsion into [[Uterus]])
- Arrival in [[Uterus]] must coincide with proper endometrial receptivity for implantation

## Hormonal Regulation

### Estrogen Effects

- Increases:
    - Ciliary beat frequency
    - Epithelial cell height
    - Secretory activity
    - [[Smooth Muscle]] contractility
    - Blood flow to tube

### Progesterone Effects

- Increases:
    - Secretory activity of peg cells
    - Viscosity of secretions
- Decreases:
    - [[Smooth Muscle]] contractility (important to prevent premature embryo expulsion)
    - Ciliary activity

### Cyclical Changes

- **[[Follicular Phase]]**: increased ciliary activity, higher motility
- **[[Luteal Phase]]**: reduced motility, increased secretory activity (supports embryo)

## Clinical Relevance

### Ectopic Pregnancy

- **Definition**: Implantation of embryo outside [[Uterus]]
- ~95% of ectopic pregnancies occur in fallopian tube (usually ampulla)
- **Risk factors**:
    - Previous ectopic pregnancy
    - Pelvic inflammatory disease (PID)
    - Prior tubal surgery
    - Endometriosis
    - Smoking
    - IVF/fertility treatments
- **Symptoms**: abdominal pain, vaginal bleeding, shoulder pain (if ruptured)
- **Diagnosis**: β-hCG levels, transvaginal [[Ultrasound]]
- **Complications**: tubal rupture → life-threatening hemorrhage
- **Treatment**:
    - Medical: methotrexate (if detected early)
    - Surgical: laparoscopic salpingectomy (remove affected tube) or salpingotomy (preserve tube)
- Reduces fertility (one tube lost or damaged)

### Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID)

- Infection of female reproductive organs, commonly affecting fallopian tubes (**salpingitis**)
- **Causes**: sexually transmitted infections (chlamydia, gonorrhea), bacterial spread from [[Vagina]]/[[Cervix]]
- **Symptoms**: pelvic pain, fever, abnormal vaginal discharge, painful intercourse
- **Complications**:
    - **Tubal scarring and adhesions** → chronic pelvic pain
    - **Tubal occlusion** → infertility
    - Increased ectopic pregnancy risk
    - Abscess formation
- **Treatment**: antibiotics (combination therapy)
- **Prevention**: safe sex practices, STI screening, prompt treatment of infections

### Tubal Occlusion/Blockage

- **Causes**: PID, endometriosis, adhesions from previous surgery, congenital abnormalities
- Prevents sperm from reaching oocyte or embryo from reaching [[Uterus]]
- **Diagnosis**: hysterosalpingography (HSG), laparoscopy with dye test
- **Treatment**:
    - Surgical: tubal cannulation, salpingostomy (open blocked tube)
    - IVF bypasses tubes entirely (often preferred)

### Hydrosalpinx

- Fluid-filled, blocked fallopian tube (often result of chronic PID)
- Dilated tube with thin walls
- Impairs fertility (fluid toxic to embryos, physically prevents implantation)
- **Treatment**: surgical removal (salpingectomy) before IVF recommended (improves success rates)

### Tubal Ligation (Female Sterilisation)

- Surgical [[Contraception]] method
- Tubes are cut, tied, or blocked (clips, rings, cauterisation)
- **~99% effective**
- Prevents sperm from reaching oocyte
- Does not affect menstrual cycles or hormone production
- **Reversal**: tubal anastomosis possible but success variable (better with younger age, less tube damage)

### Tubal Cancer

- Rare (most pelvic cancers are ovarian or endometrial)
- Often detected incidentally or late-stage
- Symptoms similar to ovarian cancer (vague abdominal symptoms)
- Treatment: salpingectomy, chemotherapy

## Infertility and Assisted Reproduction

### Tubal Factor Infertility

- Accounts for ~30% of female infertility
- Caused by tubal blockage, adhesions, or damage
- Diagnosed via HSG or laparoscopy

### In Vitro Fertilisation (IVF)

- Bypasses fallopian tubes entirely
- Oocytes retrieved directly from [[Ovaries]]
- [[Fertilisation]] in laboratory
- Embryos transferred directly into [[Uterus]]
- Successful option for tubal factor infertility

## Development

- Develop from **paramesonephric (Müllerian) ducts** during fetal development (8-10 weeks)
- Cranial ends remain open → become fimbriated ends of tubes
- Middle portions fuse → become [[Uterus]] and upper [[Vagina]]
- Abnormal development can result in:
    - Absent or underdeveloped tubes
    - Uterine anomalies (often accompanied by tubal abnormalities)

## Adaptations for Function

- **Ciliated epithelium** efficiently transports oocyte/embryo
- **Highly folded mucosa** increases surface area for secretion and transport
- **Muscular walls** provide [[Peristaltic Contractions]] for movement
- **Strategic location** between ovary and [[Uterus]] optimises gamete meeting
- **Nutrient-rich secretions** support oocyte and early embryo before implantation
- **Hormonal responsiveness** ensures proper timing of embryo arrival in [[Uterus]]

## Summary

The fallopian tubes serve as the critical conduit between the [[Ovaries]] and [[Uterus]], providing the site for [[Fertilisation]] and supporting early embryonic development. Their ciliated epithelium and muscular contractions ensure proper transport of the oocyte and embryo, while their secretory cells provide essential nutrition. Damage or blockage to the fallopian tubes is a significant cause of infertility, though assisted reproductive technologies can overcome this barrier.
