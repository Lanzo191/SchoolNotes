The **vagina** is a fibromuscular tube that serves as the female copulatory organ, the passageway for menstrual flow, and the birth canal. It connects the external genitalia to the [[Cervix]] and [[Uterus]].

## Structure

### Location and Size

- Extends from **vulva (external genitalia) to [[Cervix]]**
- Approximately **7-10 cm long** (anterior wall shorter than posterior)
- Positioned between bladder/urethra (anteriorly) and rectum (posteriorly)
- Angled **posterosuperiorly** at ~45° angle to horizontal when standing

### Vaginal Fornices

- **Recesses** surrounding cervical protrusion into vagina
- **Anterior fornix**: shallow
- **Posterior fornix**: deeper (semen pools here after intercourse)
- **Lateral fornices**: on both sides

### Vaginal Wall Layers

**Mucosa** (inner lining):

- **Stratified squamous epithelium** (non-keratinised)
- No glands (keeps vagina relatively dry compared to other mucosa)
- **Transverse ridges (rugae)**: allow expansion during intercourse and childbirth
- Rich in **glycogen** (especially under estrogen influence)

**Muscularis**:

- Inner circular and outer longitudinal **[[Smooth Muscle]]** layers
- Allows considerable distension

**Adventitia** (outer layer):

- Fibrous [[Connective Tissue]]
- Merges with surrounding pelvic structures
- Attaches to pelvic walls via [[Connective Tissue]]

### Hymen

- Thin membrane partially covering vaginal opening in children
- Variable shape (annular, crescentic, septate, imperforate)
- **Normally perforated** (allows menstrual flow)
- Tears during first intercourse, childbirth, physical activity, or tampon use
- **Remnants** (hymenal caruncles) persist after tearing
- **Not a reliable indicator of virginity**

### External Opening (Introitus)

- Located in **vestibule** (area between labia minora)
- **Bartholin's glands** open on either side (secrete mucus for lubrication)
- **Skene's glands** (paraurethral glands) open near urethra

## Blood Supply

- **Vaginal artery** (branch of internal iliac artery)
- **Uterine artery** (supplies upper vagina)
- **Internal pudendal artery** (supplies lower vagina)
- **Vaginal venous plexus** drains to internal iliac [[Veins]]

## Nerve Supply

- **Upper vagina**: autonomic innervation (sympathetic and parasympathetic), relatively insensitive
- **Lower vagina**: somatic innervation via **pudendal nerve** (S2-S4), highly sensitive
- **Sensory distinction**: important for sexual response

## Vaginal Microbiome

### Normal Flora

- **Lactobacillus species** (predominant in healthy reproductive-age women)
    - L. crispatus, L. iners, L. jensenii, L. gasseri
- Produce **lactic acid** from glycogen → maintains acidic pH

### pH Regulation

- **Normal pH**: 3.8-4.5 (acidic)
- Acidity created by lactobacilli metabolising glycogen
- **Acidic environment**:
    - Inhibits pathogenic bacteria
    - Protects against infections
    - Hostile to sperm (cervical mucus neutralises)

### Factors Affecting Microbiome

- **Estrogen levels**: increase glycogen → support lactobacilli
- **Menstruation**: temporary pH increase (blood is alkaline)
- **Sexual activity**: semen is alkaline (pH ~7.2-8.0)
- **Antibiotics**: disrupt normal flora
- **Douching**: removes protective bacteria

## Function

### Copulation (Sexual Intercourse)

- Receives penis during intercourse
- **Vaginal lubrication**:
    - Plasma transudate from vaginal walls (arousal increases blood flow)
    - Bartholin's gland secretions
    - Cervical mucus
- **Distension**: vagina expands to accommodate penis

### Passageway for Menstrual Blood

- Menstrual flow exits body through vagina
- Tampon or menstrual cup use for absorption/collection

### Birth Canal

- **Dramatic expansion** during [[Labour]]
- **Can dilate to ~10 cm diameter** to allow fetal head passage
- Rugae flatten and walls stretch
- **Perineum** (area between vagina and anus) may tear or be cut (episiotomy)
- Postpartum vagina wider, less elastic (pelvic floor exercises help recovery)

### Barrier Function

- Acidic pH and normal flora prevent pathogen colonisation
- Physical barrier between external environment and sterile [[Uterus]]
- Immune cells (neutrophils, macrophages) present in vaginal fluid

## Hormonal Regulation

### Estrogen Effects

- **Proliferative**: thickens epithelium (more cell layers)
- Increases **glycogen content** of [[Epithelial cells]]
- Promotes **vaginal lubrication**
- Maintains **elasticity**
- Supports **lactobacilli** growth

### Progesterone Effects

- Less pronounced than estrogen
- Slightly thickens mucus
- Reduces lubrication

### Life Stages

**Prepubertal**:

- Thin epithelium, low glycogen
- pH neutral (~7)
- More vulnerable to infections

**Reproductive Years**:

- Thick epithelium, high glycogen
- Acidic pH, lactobacilli-dominant
- Best protection against infections

**Postmenopausal**:

- **Vaginal atrophy**: thin, dry epithelium
- Low glycogen, decreased lactobacilli
- pH rises (~5-7)
- Increased infection and irritation risk
- **Vaginal estrogen therapy** can restore health

## Clinical Relevance

### Vaginal Infections (Vaginitis)

**Bacterial Vaginosis (BV)**:

- **Dysbiosis**: loss of lactobacilli, overgrowth of anaerobes (Gardnerella vaginalis, Prevotella)
- Not sexually transmitted (but sexual activity is risk factor)
- **Symptoms**: thin, grey, fishy-smelling discharge; pH >4.5
- **Diagnosis**: Amsel criteria, Nugent score (vaginal smear)
- **Treatment**: metronidazole or clindamycin
- **Complications**: increased STI risk, preterm birth if pregnant

**Vulvovaginal Candidiasis (Yeast Infection)**:

- Fungal infection, usually **Candida albicans**
- **Risk factors**: antibiotics, [[Diabetes]], immunosuppression, pregnancy, tight clothing
- **Symptoms**: thick, white, "cottage cheese" discharge; itching, burning
- **Diagnosis**: pH normal (<4.5), microscopy shows hyphae/budding yeast
- **Treatment**: antifungal (azoles: fluconazole oral, clotrimazole topical)

**Trichomoniasis**:

- Sexually transmitted infection caused by **Trichomonas vaginalis** (protozoan)
- **Symptoms**: frothy, yellow-green discharge; itching, dysuria, "strawberry cervix"
- pH >4.5
- **Diagnosis**: microscopy (motile trichomonads), NAAT
- **Treatment**: metronidazole or tinidazole (treat partner too)

### Vaginal Atrophy (Genitourinary Syndrome of Menopause)

- Thinning, drying, inflammation of vaginal walls due to estrogen deficiency
- **Symptoms**: vaginal dryness, dyspareunia (painful sex), burning, urinary symptoms
- **Treatment**: vaginal estrogen (cream, tablet, ring), moisturisers, lubricants

### Vaginal Prolapse

- Descent of vaginal walls due to weakened pelvic floor support
- **Types**:
    - **Cystocele**: bladder bulges into anterior vaginal wall
    - **Rectocele**: rectum bulges into posterior vaginal wall
    - **Uterine prolapse**: uterus descends into/through vagina
- **Risk factors**: childbirth, aging, menopause, obesity, chronic cough/constipation
- **Symptoms**: pelvic pressure/bulge, urinary/bowel symptoms
- **Treatment**: pelvic floor exercises (Kegels), pessary, surgical repair

### Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs)

- **Chlamydia**, **gonorrhea**: often asymptomatic vaginally, cause cervicitis
- **Herpes simplex virus (HSV)**: painful vesicles/ulcers
- **Human papillomavirus (HPV)**: genital warts, cervical cancer risk
- **HIV**: can be transmitted via vaginal mucosa

### Vaginal Cancer

- Rare (1-2% of gynecological cancers)
- **Squamous cell carcinoma** most common
- Often associated with HPV
- **Symptoms**: abnormal vaginal bleeding, discharge, mass
- **Treatment**: surgery, radiation, chemotherapy

### Congenital Abnormalities

- **Vaginal agenesis**: absent vagina (Mayer-Rokitansky-Küster-Hauser syndrome)
- **Vaginal septum**: divides vagina partially or completely
- **Imperforate hymen**: blocks menstrual flow

### Fistulas

- Abnormal connections between vagina and adjacent organs
- **Vesicovaginal fistula**: vagina-bladder (urine leakage into vagina)
- **Rectovaginal fistula**: vagina-rectum (fecal leakage)
- **Causes**: childbirth trauma, surgery, radiation, infection
- **Treatment**: surgical repair

## Sexual Response

### Arousal

- Increased blood flow → vaginal transudate (lubrication)
- Vaginal expansion (tenting)
- Labia swell
- Clitoral erection (erectile tissue)

### Plateau

- Continued lubrication
- Vaginal opening narrows ("orgasmic platform")
- Uterus elevates

### Orgasm

- Rhythmic contractions of pelvic floor muscles, vagina, uterus
- Involuntary, pleasurable

### Resolution

- Blood flow returns to baseline
- Vagina relaxes
- Lubrication decreases

## Examination

### Speculum Examination

- **Bivalve speculum** inserted to visualise vaginal walls and cervix
- Assesses for discharge, lesions, inflammation
- **Pap smear** and cultures collected

### Bimanual Examination

- Two fingers in vagina, other hand on abdomen
- Palpates uterus, ovaries, cervix
- Assesses size, position, tenderness, masses

### pH Testing

- Litmus paper applied to vaginal discharge
- Normal: <4.5
- Elevated (>4.5): BV, trichomoniasis

### Wet Mount Microscopy

- Fresh vaginal discharge examined under microscope
- **Saline wet mount**: bacteria, trichomonads, clue cells (BV)
- **KOH prep**: destroys cells, reveals yeast hyphae

## Hygiene and Health

### Normal Care

- Wash external genitalia with water or mild soap
- **Avoid douching**: disrupts normal flora, increases infection risk
- Wipe front to back (prevents fecal contamination)
- Cotton underwear allows better air circulation

### Contraindicated Practices

- Douching
- Scented products (soaps, sprays)
- Prolonged pad/tampon use (change regularly)

### When to Seek Medical Care

- Abnormal discharge (colour, odour, consistency)
- Itching, burning, irritation
- Unusual bleeding
- Pain during intercourse
- Pelvic pain

## Adaptations for Function

- **Stratified squamous epithelium**: resists friction and acidic environment
- **Distensible walls**: accommodate penis, childbirth
- **Rugae**: allow expansion without tearing
- **Glycogen-rich epithelium**: supports lactobacilli, maintains acidic pH
- **Dual innervation**: autonomic (upper) for involuntary function, somatic (lower) for sensation
- **Elastic tissue**: returns to near-original size postpartum

## Summary

The vagina is a multifunctional organ serving reproductive, sexual, and excretory roles. Its unique [[Microbiome]], dominated by lactobacilli, creates an acidic environment that protects against infections. Hormonal changes throughout life (puberty, reproductive years, [[Menopause]]) significantly affect vaginal health. Understanding vaginal anatomy and physiology is essential for diagnosing and treating infections, sexual dysfunction, and obstetric complications.
