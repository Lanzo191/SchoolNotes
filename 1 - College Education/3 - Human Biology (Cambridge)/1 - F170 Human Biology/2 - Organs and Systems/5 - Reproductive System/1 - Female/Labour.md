**Labour** (parturition) is the process by which the fetus and [[Placenta]] are expelled from the [[Uterus]]. It typically occurs at **37-42 weeks gestation** and involves coordinated uterine contractions, cervical dilation, and hormonal changes.

## Stages of Labour

### Stage 1: Cervical Dilation (Longest Stage)

**Duration**: First-time mothers (~12-14 hours), subsequent births (~7-8 hours)

**Latent Phase**:

- [[Cervix]] dilates from 0 to ~4 cm
- Contractions irregular, mild (every 5-30 minutes)
- Can last hours to days
- **Effacement** (thinning): [[Cervix]] shortens from ~3 cm to paper-thin

**Active Phase**:

- [[Cervix]] dilates from ~4 to 10 cm (full dilation)
- Contractions regular, stronger, closer (every 2-5 minutes)
- Progress ~1 cm/hour (first-time mothers), faster in subsequent births
- **Transition** (8-10 cm): most intense contractions, pressure sensation

### Stage 2: Expulsion of Fetus (Pushing Stage)

**Duration**: First-time mothers (~1-2 hours), subsequent births (~30 minutes-1 hour)

- Begins at **full cervical dilation** (10 cm)
- Ends with **delivery of baby**
- **Maternal pushing efforts** coordinate with contractions
- **Fetal descent** through birth canal:
    - **Engagement**: fetal head enters pelvic inlet
    - **Descent**: downward movement through pelvis
    - **Flexion**: chin tucks to chest (smallest diameter presents)
    - **Internal rotation**: head rotates to fit pelvic dimensions
    - **Extension**: head extends as it passes under pubic symphysis
    - **External rotation (restitution)**: head rotates back to align with shoulders
    - **Expulsion**: shoulders and body delivered

**Crowning**: fetal head visible at vaginal opening (point of no return)

### Stage 3: Delivery of Placenta

**Duration**: 5-30 minutes

- Begins after **baby delivered**
- Ends with **placental expulsion**
- [[Uterus]] contracts, [[Placenta]] shears off uterine wall
- **Signs of placental separation**:
    - Gush of blood
    - Cord lengthening
    - [[Uterus]] rises, becomes firm and globular
- **[[Placenta]] examined** for completeness (all cotyledons and membranes present)
- Retained fragments → postpartum hemorrhage risk

### Stage 4: Immediate Postpartum (Recovery)

**Duration**: First 1-2 hours after delivery

- **Uterine involution** begins (contractions reduce uterine size)
- **Hemostasis**: myometrial contractions compress blood vessels ("living ligatures")
- Risk period for **postpartum hemorrhage**
- Monitoring: vital signs, uterine tone, bleeding
- **Skin-to-skin contact** and initiation of breastfeeding

## Initiation of Labour

### Hormonal Changes

**Progesterone** (pregnancy):

- Maintains uterine quiescence (inhibits contractions)
- Levels decline relative to estrogen near term

**Estrogen** (increases near term):

- Increases oxytocin receptors in myometrium
- Increases gap junctions between muscle cells (coordinated contractions)
- Promotes prostaglandin synthesis

**Oxytocin** (posterior pituitary):

- Stimulates uterine contractions
- **Positive feedback loop**: contractions → cervical stretch → more oxytocin release → stronger contractions
- Levels rise during labour
- Synthetic oxytocin (**Pitocin**) used for induction/augmentation

**Prostaglandins** (especially PGE₂ and PGF₂α):

- Promote cervical ripening (softening, dilation)
- Stimulate uterine contractions
- Increase oxytocin sensitivity
- **Synthetic prostaglandins** (misoprostol, dinoprostone) used for labour induction

**Corticotropin-Releasing Hormone (CRH)** (from [[Placenta]]):

- Increases near term
- Stimulates fetal [[Adrenal Glands]] → [[Cortisol]] production
- Fetal [[Cortisol]] promotes lung maturation and prostaglandin synthesis

### Mechanical Factors

- **Uterine stretch**: overdistension (twins, polyhydramnios) may trigger labour
- **Fetal pressure** on [[Cervix]]: stimulates oxytocin release (Ferguson reflex)
- **Fetal membranes**: prostaglandin production

### Fetal Signals

- Fetal [[Cortisol]] rise (lung maturation signal)
- Surfactant production in [[Lungs]] triggers maternal response

## Mechanisms of Labour

### Uterine Contractions

- **Rhythmic, coordinated** myometrial contractions
- Originate from **fundus** (pacemaker region), propagate downward
- Increase in **frequency, intensity, duration** as labour progresses
- **Gap junctions** between [[Smooth Muscle]] cells synchronise contractions
- **Oxytocin** and **prostaglandins** enhance contractility

### Cervical Changes

- **Effacement**: [[Cervix]] thins from 3 cm to <0.5 cm thick
- **Dilation**: cervical os opens from 0 to 10 cm
- **Softening**: collagen breakdown, increased water content
- **Position**: moves from posterior to anterior (aligns with vagina)

### Fetal Movements

- Fetus actively participates (flexion, rotation)
- Adapts to pelvic dimensions
- **Engagement**: biparietal diameter passes pelvic inlet

## Pain Management

### Non-Pharmacological

- **Breathing techniques**: controlled breathing, relaxation
- **Position changes**: walking, squatting, hands-and-knees
- **Hydrotherapy**: warm bath, shower
- **Massage, acupressure**
- **TENS** (transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation)
- **Hypnobirthing**: self-hypnosis, visualisation

### Pharmacological

**Systemic Opioids**:

- IV/IM injection (morphine, pethidine, fentanyl)
- Provides moderate pain relief, sedation
- Can affect fetal breathing (naloxone reversal if needed)

**Regional Anaesthesia**:

- **Epidural**: catheter in epidural space (L3-L4), continuous infusion of local anaesthetic + opioid
    - Excellent pain relief
    - Allows mobility (low-dose "walking epidural")
    - Risks: hypotension, prolonged 2nd stage, rare complications (headache, nerve injury)
- **Spinal anaesthesia**: single injection into subarachnoid space
    - Fast onset, complete anesthesia
    - Used for cesarean sections

**Inhaled Analgesia**:

- **Nitrous oxide** (Entonox): 50% N₂O + 50% O₂
- Self-administered
- Mild pain relief, anxiolytic

**Pudendal Block**:

- Local anaesthetic injected near pudendal nerve
- Numbs perineum for episiotomy/repair
- Minimal effect on labour

## Complications

### Prolonged Labour (Dystocia)

- **Causes**:
    - **Power**: inadequate contractions
    - **Passenger**: large fetus, malpresentation (breech, transverse)
    - **Passage**: small/abnormal pelvis
- **Management**: oxytocin augmentation, assisted delivery, cesarean

### Fetal Distress

- Inadequate oxygenation → abnormal fetal heart rate
- **Signs**: bradycardia (<110 bpm), tachycardia (>160 bpm), late decelerations, loss of variability
- **Causes**: cord compression, placental insufficiency, uterine hyperstimulation
- **Management**: maternal repositioning, oxygen, IV fluids, emergency delivery if severe

### Shoulder Dystocia

- **Anterior fetal shoulder** impacted behind maternal pubic symphysis
- **Risk factors**: large baby (macrosomia), maternal [[Diabetes]], prolonged 2nd stage
- **Complications**: brachial plexus injury (Erb's palsy), clavicle fracture, asphyxia
- **Management**: McRoberts maneuver (hyperflexion of maternal hips), suprapubic pressure, episiotomy

### Cord Prolapse

- Umbilical cord descends below presenting fetal part
- **Risk factors**: breech presentation, polyhydramnios, prematurity, artificial rupture of membranes
- **Complications**: cord compression → fetal hypoxia
- **Management**: **obstetric emergency**, immediate cesarean

### Postpartum Hemorrhage (PPH)

- Blood loss **>500 mL** (vaginal) or **>1000 mL** (cesarean)
- **Causes** ("Four T's"):
    - **Tone**: uterine atony (most common)
    - **Trauma**: perineal/cervical lacerations, uterine rupture
    - **Tissue**: retained [[Placenta]]/membranes
    - **Thrombin**: coagulopathy
- **Management**: uterine massage, oxytocin, prostaglandins, surgical repair, transfusion

### Perineal Trauma

- **Tears** (1st to 4th degree):
    - **1st degree**: skin only
    - **2nd degree**: skin + perineal muscles
    - **3rd degree**: extends to anal sphincter
    - **4th degree**: extends through rectal mucosa
- **Episiotomy**: surgical incision to enlarge vaginal opening (used selectively, not routine)
- **Repair**: suturing under local anaesthesia

## Assisted Delivery

### Vacuum Extraction (Ventouse)

- Suction cup applied to fetal head
- **Indications**: prolonged 2nd stage, maternal exhaustion, fetal distress
- **Complications**: scalp bruising, cephalohematoma (rare)

### Forceps Delivery

- Metal instruments grasp fetal head
- **Indications**: same as vacuum, also for rotation of fetal head
- **Complications**: facial bruising, rarely nerve injury

### Cesarean Section (C-Section)

- Surgical delivery through abdominal and uterine incisions
- **Indications**:
    - **Elective**: breech presentation, [[Placenta]] previa, previous classical cesarean, maternal request
    - **Emergency**: fetal distress, cord prolapse, failed labour progression, placental abruption
- **Procedure**: horizontal (bikini) or vertical incision
- **Recovery**: longer than vaginal birth (~6 weeks)
- **Risks**: infection, hemorrhage, thrombosis, future placental abnormalities (accreta)

## Fetal Monitoring During Labour

### Intermittent Auscultation

- Doppler or fetoscope
- Check fetal heart rate periodically
- Low-risk pregnancies

### Continuous Electronic Fetal Monitoring (EFM)

- **External**: [[Ultrasound]] transducer on maternal abdomen
- **Internal**: electrode attached to fetal scalp
- Continuous recording of fetal heart rate and contractions
- **Patterns**:
    - **Baseline**: 110-160 bpm (normal)
    - **Variability**: beat-to-beat fluctuations (reassuring if present)
    - **Accelerations**: transient increases (reassuring)
    - **Decelerations**:
        - **Early**: mirror contractions (head compression, benign)
        - **Variable**: abrupt drops (cord compression, monitor closely)
        - **Late**: gradual, after contraction (uteroplacental insufficiency, concerning)

### Fetal Scalp Blood Sampling

- If heart rate concerning, check fetal pH
- pH <7.20 suggests fetal acidosis (hypoxia)

## Immediate Newborn Care

### APGAR Score (1 and 5 minutes after birth)

|Sign|0|1|2|
|---|---|---|---|
|Appearance (color)|Blue/pale|Body pink, extremities blue|Completely pink|
|Pulse (heart rate)|Absent|<100 bpm|>100 bpm|
|Grimace (reflex)|No response|Grimace|Cry/sneeze|
|Activity (tone)|Limp|Some flexion|Active|
|Respiration|Absent|Weak/irregular|Strong cry|

- **Score 7-10**: normal
- **Score 4-6**: requires assistance
- **Score 0-3**: critical, requires resuscitation

### Immediate Interventions

- **Airway clearance**: suction if needed
- **Dry and warm**: prevent hypothermia
- **Stimulation**: gentle rubbing encourages breathing
- **Delayed cord clamping** (~1-3 minutes): improves iron stores
- **Skin-to-skin contact**: promotes bonding, breastfeeding, temperature regulation
- **Vitamin K injection**: prevents hemorrhagic disease of newborn
- **Eye prophylaxis**: erythromycin ointment (prevents gonococcal conjunctivitis)
- **Identification**: ID bands, footprints

## Physiological Changes After Birth

### Maternal

- **Uterine involution**: [[Uterus]] shrinks back toward normal size (~6 weeks)
- **Lochia**: vaginal discharge (blood, tissue) for several weeks
- **Breastfeeding**: prolactin stimulates milk production, oxytocin triggers milk ejection reflex
- **Hormonal shifts**: rapid drop in estrogen/progesterone → "baby blues" common (mood swings, tearfulness)

### Neonatal

- **First breath**: clears fluid from [[Lungs]], establishes air breathing
- **Circulatory changes**:
    - Ductus arteriosus closes (shunt between pulmonary artery and aorta)
    - Foramen ovale closes (shunt between atria)
    - Umbilical vessels constrict
    - Pulmonary vascular resistance drops
- **[[Thermoregulation]]**: newborn must maintain body temperature
- **Feeding**: suckling reflex, digestion begins

## Summary

Labour is a complex physiological process involving coordinated hormonal signals, mechanical forces, and maternal and fetal adaptations. Understanding the stages of labour, mechanisms of pain, potential complications, and interventions is essential for safe and supported childbirth. Modern obstetric care aims to balance natural physiological processes with medical intervention when necessary to ensure the health and safety of both mother and baby.
