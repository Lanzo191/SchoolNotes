The **ovaries** are the primary female reproductive organs responsible for producing ova (eggs) and secreting sex hormones (estrogen and progesterone). They are paired almond-shaped organs located in the pelvic cavity.X

## Structure

### Location and Size

- Located in the **pelvic cavity**, one on each side of the [[Uterus]]
- Positioned in the **ovarian fossa** on the lateral pelvic wall
- Approximately **3 cm long, 1.5 cm wide, 1 cm thick**
- Weighs approximately **5-10 grams**
- Connected to the [[Uterus]] by the **ovarian ligament**
- Suspended by the **suspensory ligament of the ovary** (containing blood vessels and nerves)

### External Anatomy

- Almond-shaped (ovoid)
- Surface covered by **germinal epithelium** (simple cuboidal epithelium)
- Appears smooth in children and young women
- Develops **surface irregularities** (scars from [[Ovulation]]) with age
- Not covered by peritoneum (unlike other pelvic organs)

### Internal Anatomy

**Outer Cortex**:

- Contains **ovarian follicles** at various stages of development
- **Stroma**: dense [[Connective Tissue]] supporting follicles
- Site of hormone production by follicle cells
- Contains primordial, primary, secondary, and mature (Graafian) follicles

**Inner Medulla**:

- Loose [[Connective Tissue]]
- Contains **blood vessels**, **lymphatic vessels**, and **nerves**
- Provides vascular support to cortex

### Ovarian Follicles

**Primordial Follicles**:

- Most numerous (approximately 400,000 at puberty)
- Consist of immature oocyte surrounded by single layer of flat follicular cells
- Most remain dormant and eventually degenerate (atresia)
- Only ~400 will ever ovulate during reproductive life

**Primary Follicles**:

- Oocyte enlarges
- Follicular cells become cuboidal (granulosa cells)
- Formation of **zona pellucida** (glycoprotein layer around oocyte)
- **Theca cells** form external layer

**Secondary (Antral) Follicles**:

- Fluid-filled cavity (**antrum**) develops
- Granulosa cells proliferate
- Oocyte surrounded by **corona radiata** (innermost granulosa cells)
- Theca layer differentiates into:
    - **Theca interna**: produces androgens (converted to estrogen by granulosa cells)
    - **Theca externa**: fibrous outer layer

**Mature (Graafian) Follicle**:

- Large (15-25 mm diameter)
- Oocyte positioned eccentrically on **cumulus oophorus** (mound of granulosa cells)
- Ready for [[Ovulation]]
- Forms just before [[Ovulation]] (~day 14 of menstrual cycle)

**Corpus Luteum**:

- Forms from ruptured follicle after [[Ovulation]]
- Yellow appearance due to lipid content
- Secretes **progesterone and estrogen**
- Maintains endometrium for potential pregnancy
- **Lifespan**: 14 days (if no pregnancy) → degenerates into corpus albicans
- If pregnancy occurs: maintained by hCG, produces progesterone until [[Placenta]] takes over (~10-12 weeks)

**Corpus Albicans**:

- Scar tissue remaining after corpus luteum degenerates
- White fibrous tissue
- Eventually absorbed into ovarian stroma

## Blood Supply

- **Ovarian artery** branches directly from abdominal aorta
- **Ovarian branch of uterine artery** (anastomoses with ovarian artery)
- **Pampiniform plexus** of [[Veins]] drains ovary
- Right ovarian vein drains to **inferior vena cava**
- Left ovarian vein drains to **left renal vein**

## Nerve Supply

- **Ovarian plexus** (autonomic nerves from renal and aortic plexuses)
- Sensory fibres detect [[Ovulation]] pain (mittelschmerz)
- Sympathetic fibres regulate blood flow and ovarian function

## Function

### Oogenesis (Egg Production)

- Production and maturation of oocytes
- Begins during fetal development (primordial germ cells → oogonia → primary oocytes)
- Primary oocytes arrest in **prophase I of [[Meiosis]] I** until puberty
- Each menstrual cycle:
    - Several follicles begin to mature
    - Usually only one reaches full maturity (dominant follicle)
    - [[Ovulation]] releases **secondary oocyte** (arrested in metaphase II)
    - [[Meiosis]] II completed only if [[Fertilisation]] occurs

### Hormone Production

**Estrogen (Estradiol)**:

- Produced mainly by **granulosa cells** of developing follicles
- Functions:
    - Stimulates endometrial proliferation ([[Follicular Phase]])
    - Develops and maintains secondary sexual characteristics
    - Promotes bone density
    - Regulates cholesterol levels
    - Affects mood and cognition
    - Inhibits FSH (negative feedback) at low-moderate levels
    - Stimulates LH surge (positive feedback) at high levels just before [[Ovulation]]

**Progesterone**:

- Produced primarily by **corpus luteum** after [[Ovulation]]
- Functions:
    - Maintains endometrium in secretory phase ([[Luteal Phase]])
    - Supports early pregnancy
    - Raises basal body temperature
    - Inhibits FSH and LH (negative feedback)
    - Reduces uterine contractility
    - Stimulates breast development

**Inhibin**:

- Produced by granulosa cells
- Selectively inhibits FSH secretion from anterior pituitary
- Part of negative feedback regulation

**Small amounts of androgens**:

- Testosterone and androstenedione produced by theca cells
- Converted to estrogen by aromatase enzyme in granulosa cells
- Excess androgens in PCOS

## Hormonal Regulation

### Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Ovarian Axis

**[[Hypothalamus]]**:

- Secretes **gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH)** in pulsatile fashion
- GnRH pulse frequency and amplitude vary across menstrual cycle

**Anterior Pituitary**:

- **FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone)**:
    - Stimulates follicle growth and maturation
    - Promotes estrogen synthesis by granulosa cells
- **LH (luteinising hormone)**:
    - **LH surge** triggers [[Ovulation]] (~36 hours before)
    - Stimulates corpus luteum formation
    - Stimulates progesterone production

**Negative Feedback**:

- Estrogen and progesterone inhibit GnRH, FSH, and LH secretion
- Inhibin selectively suppresses FSH

**Positive Feedback**:

- High estrogen levels mid-cycle trigger LH surge, causing [[Ovulation]]

## Ovarian Cycle Phases

### Follicular Phase (Days 1-14)

- FSH stimulates follicle development
- Rising estrogen from growing follicles
- Endometrium proliferates
- Ends with LH surge and [[Ovulation]]

### Ovulation (Day ~14)

- LH surge causes mature follicle to rupture
- Secondary oocyte released into peritoneal cavity
- Captured by fimbriae of fallopian tube
- Some women experience **mittelschmerz** ([[Ovulation]] pain)

### Luteal Phase (Days 15-28)

- Corpus luteum forms and secretes progesterone and estrogen
- Endometrium becomes secretory
- If no pregnancy: corpus luteum degenerates → hormone levels drop → menstruation
- If pregnancy: hCG from embryo maintains corpus luteum

## Development

### Fetal Development

- Primordial germ cells migrate to genital ridge (5-6 weeks gestation)
- Develop into **oogonia** (proliferate by [[Mitosis]])
- Oogonia differentiate into **primary oocytes** and begin [[Meiosis]] I
- Arrest in prophase I by ~7 months of gestation
- Approximately **7 million oocytes** at 5 months gestation
- **Atresia** (degeneration) begins → ~1-2 million at birth

### Childhood

- Ovaries remain inactive
- Continued atresia of oocytes

### Puberty (Menarche)

- Hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian axis activates
- Onset of menstrual cycles
- Approximately **400,000 oocytes** remain
- Usually begins age 10-15

### Reproductive Years

- Regular ovulatory cycles (usually)
- Approximately 13 cycles per year × 30-40 years = ~400-500 ovulations
- Continuous atresia of non-ovulated follicles

### Menopause

- Ovarian follicles depleted (~age 45-55)
- Cessation of [[Ovulation]] and menstruation
- Estrogen and progesterone levels drop
- FSH and LH levels rise (loss of negative feedback)

## Clinical Relevance

### Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS)

- Most common endocrine disorder in women of reproductive age (~5-10%)
- **Characteristics**:
    - Multiple small follicles (appear as cysts on [[Ultrasound]])
    - Anovulation or irregular [[Ovulation]]
    - Hyperandrogenism (excess androgens)
- **Symptoms**: irregular periods, hirsutism, acne, obesity, infertility
- **Complications**: [[Insulin]] resistance, [[Diabetes]], cardiovascular disease, endometrial cancer risk
- **Treatment**: lifestyle modification, hormonal contraceptives, metformin, fertility treatments

### Ovarian Cysts

- Fluid-filled sacs on or within ovary
- **Types**:
    - **Functional cysts**: follicular cysts, corpus luteum cysts (usually resolve spontaneously)
    - **Dermoid cysts (teratomas)**: contain various tissue types
    - **Endometriomas**: "chocolate cysts" from endometriosis
    - **Cystadenomas**: benign epithelial tumors
- **Symptoms**: pelvic pain, bloating, irregular periods, pain during intercourse
- **Complications**: rupture, torsion (ovarian twist cutting off blood supply)
- **Treatment**: observation, hormonal contraceptives, surgical removal if large or symptomatic

### Ovarian Cancer

- Seventh most common cancer in women worldwide
- Often detected late due to non-specific symptoms
- **Risk factors**: age, family history (BRCA1/BRCA2 mutations), nulliparity, early menarche, late [[Menopause]]
- **Types**:
    - **Epithelial ovarian cancer** (90%): arises from surface epithelium
    - **Germ cell tumors**: from oocytes (more common in young women)
    - **Stromal tumors**: from hormone-producing cells
- **Symptoms**: abdominal bloating, pelvic pain, early satiety, urinary symptoms (often vague)
- **Diagnosis**: pelvic examination, transvaginal [[Ultrasound]], CA-125 blood test (tumor marker), biopsy
- **Treatment**: surgery (oophorectomy, hysterectomy), chemotherapy, targeted therapy
- **Prognosis**: varies with stage; early detection improves survival significantly

### Ovarian Torsion

- Twisting of ovary on its ligaments, cutting off blood supply
- Medical emergency
- **Risk factors**: ovarian cysts, ovarian hyperstimulation, pregnancy
- **Symptoms**: sudden severe pelvic pain, nausea, vomiting
- **Diagnosis**: [[Ultrasound]] with Doppler (reduced blood flow)
- **Treatment**: emergency surgery to untwist (or remove if necrotic)

### Premature Ovarian Insufficiency (POI)

- Ovarian failure before age 40
- **Causes**: genetic (Turner syndrome, Fragile X), autoimmune, chemotherapy/radiation, idiopathic
- **Symptoms**: irregular or absent periods, infertility, menopausal symptoms
- **Treatment**: hormone replacement therapy, fertility preservation, psychological support

### Endometriosis

- Endometrial tissue grows outside [[Uterus]], often on ovaries
- Forms **endometriomas** (blood-filled cysts)
- Causes pelvic pain, especially during menstruation
- Impairs fertility

## Examination and Diagnosis

### Pelvic Examination

- Bimanual examination palpates ovaries for size, mobility, tenderness, masses
- Difficult to palpate normal ovaries (deep in pelvis)
- Abnormalities more easily detected

### Transvaginal Ultrasound

- Visualises ovarian structure, follicles, cysts, masses
- Measures ovarian volume
- Assesses blood flow with Doppler

### Hormonal Assays

- **FSH, LH, estrogen, progesterone**: assess ovarian function and menstrual cycle phase
- **Anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH)**: marker of ovarian reserve (number of remaining follicles)
- **CA-125**: tumor marker for ovarian cancer (also elevated in endometriosis, pelvic inflammatory disease)

### Ovarian Reserve Testing

- Assesses fertility potential
- **AMH**: higher levels indicate greater ovarian reserve
- **Antral follicle count (AFC)**: [[Ultrasound]] count of small follicles
- **Day 3 FSH**: elevated levels suggest diminished reserve

## Adaptations for Function

- **Follicle population** provides lifelong supply of oocytes
- **Thick cortex** protects developing follicles
- **Rich vascularisation** supports hormone production and follicle development
- **Cyclic hormone secretion** regulates endometrial preparation and [[Ovulation]] timing
- **Proximity to fallopian tube** facilitates oocyte capture after [[Ovulation]]

## Summary

The ovaries are essential for female reproduction, producing oocytes cyclically and secreting hormones that regulate the menstrual cycle, support pregnancy, and maintain secondary sexual characteristics. Ovarian function begins at puberty and continues until [[Menopause]], with approximately 400-500 ovulations occurring during a woman's reproductive lifespan. Disorders of the ovaries can significantly affect fertility, hormone balance, and overall health.
