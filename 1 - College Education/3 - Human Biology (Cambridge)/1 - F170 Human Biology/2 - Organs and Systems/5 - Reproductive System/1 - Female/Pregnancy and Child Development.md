**Pregnancy** (gestation) is the period from conception to birth, typically lasting **280 days (40 weeks)** from the last menstrual period (LMP) or **266 days (38 weeks)** from [[Fertilisation]]. It is divided into three trimesters, each marked by specific developmental milestones.

## Pre-Embryonic Period (Weeks 1-2)

### Fertilisation (Day 0)

- Occurs in **ampulla of fallopian tube**
- Sperm penetrates secondary oocyte
- **Completion of [[Meiosis]] II** → mature ovum
- **Pronuclei fusion** → diploid zygote (46 [[Chromosomes]])

### Cleavage (Days 1-4)

- **Mitotic divisions** without growth → smaller cells (blastomeres)
- Day 2: **2 cells**
- Day 3: **8-16 cells** (morula stage)
- Day 4: **Blastocyst** forms
    - **Inner cell mass** (embryoblast) → fetus
    - **Trophoblast** (outer layer) → [[Placenta]]
    - **Blastocoel** (fluid-filled cavity)

### Implantation (Days 6-12)

- Blastocyst attaches to endometrium (~day 6)
- **Trophoblast** invades endometrium
- **hCG production begins** (maintains corpus luteum)
- Completed by ~day 12
- **Decidua** forms (pregnancy-modified endometrium)

## Embryonic Period (Weeks 3-8)

### Week 3: Gastrulation

- **Three germ layers** form:
    - **Ectoderm**: nervous system, skin
    - **Mesoderm**: muscles, bones, cardiovascular system
    - **Endoderm**: digestive tract, [[Lungs]]
- **Neural tube** begins forming (brain and spinal cord)
- **Primitive heart** forms, begins beating

### Week 4

- Embryo **C-shaped**
- **Limb buds** appear
- **Eyes and ears** begin developing
- **Heart pumping blood** (visible on [[Ultrasound]])
- Size: ~4 mm

### Week 5

- **Brain** rapidly developing
- **Facial features** forming
- **Upper limbs** developing (will form earlier than lower limbs)
- Size: ~8 mm

### Week 6

- **Facial features** more distinct
- **Hands and feet** forming (webbed fingers/toes)
- **Jaw, nose, ears** developing
- **Heart** has four chambers
- **Spontaneous movements** begin (not yet felt by mother)
- Size: ~13 mm

### Week 7

- **Face** more human-like
- **Eyelids** forming
- **Limbs** lengthening
- **Fingers and toes** separating
- **Genitals** present but not yet distinguishable externally
- Size: ~18 mm

### Week 8 (End of Embryonic Period)

- **All major organs present** (though not fully developed)
- **Recognisably human**
- **External genitalia** developing (still indeterminate)
- **Spontaneous movements** more frequent
- Size: ~25 mm (1 inch)
- Weight: ~1 gram

**Critical Period**: weeks 3-8 most vulnerable to teratogens (major organ formation)

## Fetal Period (Weeks 9-Birth)

### First Trimester (Weeks 9-12)

- **Fetus** (rather than embryo) from week 9
- **Rapid growth**
- **External genitalia** distinguishable (by week 12)
- **Urine production** begins (excreted into amniotic fluid)
- **Fetal movements** increase
- **Face** fully formed
- **Fingernails** developing
- Week 12: ~6-7 cm, ~14 grams

### Second Trimester (Weeks 13-28)

**Weeks 13-16**:

- **Rapid growth**
- **Lanugo** (fine hair) covers body
- **Ossification** (bone formation) progresses
- **"Quickening"** (~18-20 weeks in first-time mothers): first felt fetal movements
- **Sex determination** via [[Ultrasound]] possible
- Week 16: ~11 cm, ~100 grams

**Weeks 17-20**:

- **Vernix caseosa** (protective waxy coating) forms
- **Brown fat** deposits ([[Thermoregulation]] after birth)
- **Myelination** of spinal cord begins
- **Senses** developing (hearing functional by ~20 weeks)
- Week 20: ~16 cm, ~300 grams

**Weeks 21-24**:

- **Lung development**: [[Alveoli]] forming, surfactant production begins
- **Eyes** can open
- **Fingerprints** established
- **Viability threshold** (~23-24 weeks): survival possible with intensive care, but high risk of complications
- Week 24: ~30 cm, ~600 grams

**Weeks 25-28**:

- **[[Lungs]]** maturing (surfactant increasing)
- **Brain** rapid development, increased complexity
- **Sleep-wake cycles** established
- **Can respond to sound, light**
- Week 28: ~37 cm, ~1000 grams

### Third Trimester (Weeks 29-40)

**Weeks 29-32**:

- **Rapid weight gain**
- **Fat deposition** (temperature regulation, energy stores)
- **[[Lungs]]** continue maturing
- **Bones** fully formed but still soft
- **Iron stores** accumulating
- Week 32: ~42 cm, ~1800 grams

**Weeks 33-36**:

- **Lanugo** shedding
- **Fingernails** reach fingertips
- **Head engaging** in pelvis (in first-time mothers)
- **Immune system** maturing (antibodies from mother)
- Week 36: ~47 cm, ~2600 grams

**Weeks 37-40 (Full Term)**:

- **Organs** fully functional (especially [[Lungs]])
- **Fat accumulation** continues (15% body weight)
- **Vernix** mostly absorbed
- **Amniotic fluid** decreases
- **Fetal movements** may decrease (less space)
- **Meconium** (first stool) accumulates in intestines
- **[[Placenta]]** shows signs of aging (calcification)
- Week 40: ~50-51 cm, ~3200-3400 grams (average)

**Post-term** (>42 weeks):

- **Placental insufficiency** risk increases
- **Oligohydramnios** (low amniotic fluid)
- **Meconium aspiration** risk
- **Induction of [[Labour]]** usually recommended

## Maternal Changes During Pregnancy

### First Trimester

- **Amenorrhea** (missed periods)
- **Nausea/vomiting** ("morning sickness"): often worst weeks 6-12
- **Breast tenderness**, enlargement
- **Fatigue**
- **Urinary frequency**
- **Mood changes**

### Second Trimester

- **"Glow"**: increased blood flow, skin changes
- **Weight gain** accelerates (~0.5 kg/week)
- **[[Uterus]]** palpable above pubic symphysis
- **Linea nigra** (dark line on abdomen), **melasma** (facial pigmentation)
- **Braxton Hicks contractions** (practice contractions, irregular, painless)
- **Nasal congestion** (increased blood volume)
- Energy levels improve ("honeymoon period")

### Third Trimester

- **Rapid uterine growth**: fundus reaches xiphoid process (~36 weeks)
- **Increased back pain, pelvic pressure**
- **Shortness of breath** ([[Diaphragm]] compressed)
- **Heartburn** (progesterone relaxes esophageal sphincter)
- **Edema** (swelling of hands, feet, ankles)
- **Braxton Hicks** more frequent
- **Colostrum** (early breast milk) may leak
- **"Lightening"** (~2-4 weeks before [[Labour]] in first-time mothers): baby drops into pelvis, breathing easier

### Cardiovascular Changes

- **Blood volume** increases 40-50% (peak ~32 weeks)
- **Cardiac output** increases 30-50%
- **Blood pressure** decreases (especially 2nd trimester)
- **Heart rate** increases (~15 bpm)
- **Supine hypotension**: [[Uterus]] compresses inferior vena cava when lying flat

### Respiratory Changes

- **Tidal volume** increases
- **Respiratory rate** slightly increases
- **Dyspnea** (shortness of breath) common

### Gastrointestinal Changes

- **Nausea/vomiting** (first trimester)
- **Constipation** (progesterone slows motility)
- **Heartburn** (relaxed esophageal sphincter)
- **Hemorrhoids** (increased pelvic pressure)

### Renal Changes

- **Glomerular filtration rate (GFR)** increases 50%
- **Urinary frequency** (first and third trimesters)
- **Glucosuria** ([[Glucose]] in urine) can occur (renal threshold lowered)

### Musculoskeletal Changes

- **Weight gain** (11-16 kg average recommended)
- **Posture changes**: increased lumbar lordosis (back arch)
- **Joint laxity** (relaxin hormone)
- **Diastasis recti**: abdominal muscle separation

### Endocrine Changes

- **hCG** (human chorionic gonadotropin): peaks ~8-10 weeks
- **Progesterone**: rises steadily (maintains pregnancy)
- **Estrogen**: rises steadily (uterine growth, breast development)
- **Relaxin**: softens ligaments (prepares pelvis for birth)
- **Prolactin**: prepares breasts for lactation

## Prenatal Care

### First Visit (Usually 8-10 weeks)

- **Medical history**: past pregnancies, medical conditions, medications
- **Physical examination**: blood pressure, weight, pelvic exam
- **[[Blood Tests]]**:
    - Blood type and Rh factor
    - Complete blood count (anemia screening)
    - Rubella [[Immunity]]
    - HIV, hepatitis B, syphilis screening
    - Optional: varicella (chickenpox) [[Immunity]]
- **Urine tests**: protein, [[Glucose]], bacteria
- **Dating [[Ultrasound]]** (8-14 weeks): confirm gestational age, detect multiples, assess viability

### Routine Prenatal Visits

- **Weeks 0-28**: every 4 weeks
- **Weeks 28-36**: every 2 weeks
- **Weeks 36-40**: weekly

**At each visit**:

- Blood pressure
- Weight
- Urine dipstick (protein, [[Glucose]])
- **Fundal height** measurement (weeks 20-36): estimated fetal size
- **Fetal heart rate** (Doppler, ~10-12 weeks onward)
- Assessment of fetal movements (after quickening)

### Important Screening Tests

**First Trimester Screen** (11-14 weeks):

- Blood test: hCG, PAPP-A (pregnancy-associated plasma protein A)
- **Nuchal translucency** [[Ultrasound]] (thickness of fluid at back of fetal neck)
- Screens for [[Down Syndrome]] (trisomy 21), trisomy 18, trisomy 13

**Second Trimester Screen / Quad Screen** (15-20 weeks):

- Blood test: AFP, hCG, estriol, inhibin A
- Screens for [[Down Syndrome]], neural tube defects (spina bifida, anencephaly)

**Non-Invasive Prenatal Testing (NIPT)** (from 10 weeks):

- Analyzes fetal [[DNA]] in maternal blood
- High accuracy for trisomies 21, 18, 13
- Can determine fetal sex

**Anomaly Scan** (18-22 weeks):

- Detailed [[Ultrasound]]
- Assesses fetal anatomy (brain, heart, kidneys, spine, limbs, face)
- Detects structural abnormalities
- Confirms placental position

**[[Glucose]] Tolerance Test** (24-28 weeks):

- Screens for gestational [[Diabetes]]
- **One-step**: 75g oral [[Glucose]], measure blood [[Glucose]] at 1 and 2 hours
- **Two-step**: 50g [[Glucose]] challenge (screening), followed by 100g oral [[Glucose]] tolerance test if abnormal

**Group B Streptococcus (GBS) Screen** (35-37 weeks):

- Vaginal/rectal swab
- If positive: IV antibiotics during [[Labour]] (prevents neonatal infection)

### Diagnostic Tests (If Indicated)

**Chorionic Villus Sampling (CVS)** (10-13 weeks):

- Sample of placental tissue
- Genetic testing (karyotype, specific genetic disorders)
- **Risk**: ~0.5-1% miscarriage

**Amniocentesis** (15-20 weeks):

- Sample of amniotic fluid
- Genetic testing, assessment of fetal lung maturity (if preterm delivery planned)
- **Risk**: ~0.1-0.3% miscarriage

## Complications of Pregnancy

### Ectopic Pregnancy

- Implantation outside [[Uterus]] (usually fallopian tube)
- **Symptoms**: abdominal pain, vaginal bleeding
- **Complications**: tubal rupture, life-threatening hemorrhage
- **Treatment**: methotrexate or surgery

### Miscarriage (Spontaneous Abortion)

- Pregnancy loss before 20 weeks
- **15-20%** of recognized pregnancies
- **Symptoms**: vaginal bleeding, cramping, tissue passage
- **Causes**: chromosomal abnormalities (most common), maternal factors, unknown

### Hyperemesis Gravidarum

- Severe, persistent nausea/vomiting
- **Dehydration, weight loss, electrolyte imbalances**
- **Treatment**: IV fluids, antiemetics, sometimes hospitalization

### Gestational Diabetes

- [[Glucose]] intolerance first recognized during pregnancy
- **Risk factors**: obesity, family history, previous gestational [[Diabetes]]
- **Complications**: macrosomia (large baby), birth trauma, neonatal hypoglycemia
- **Management**: diet, exercise, [[Insulin]] if needed

### Pre-eclampsia

- **Hypertension + proteinuria** after 20 weeks
- **Symptoms**: headache, visual changes, epigastric pain, edema
- **Complications**: eclampsia (seizures), HELLP syndrome, placental abruption, stroke
- **Treatment**: blood pressure management, delivery (only cure)

### Placental Disorders

- **[[Placenta]] previa**: [[Placenta]] covers [[Cervix]]
- **Placental abruption**: premature separation
- **Placental insufficiency**: inadequate fetal oxygenation/nutrition

### Preterm Labour

- [[Labour]] before 37 weeks
- **Causes**: infection, multiple gestation, cervical incompetence, unknown
- **Management**: tocolytics (delay [[Labour]]), corticosteroids (fetal lung maturation), antibiotics if indicated

### Intrauterine Growth Restriction (IUGR)

- Fetus smaller than expected for gestational age
- **Causes**: placental insufficiency, maternal hypertension, smoking, infection
- **Monitoring**: serial ultrasounds, Doppler studies
- **Management**: close monitoring, early delivery if severe

## Lifestyle During Pregnancy

### Recommended

- **Prenatal vitamins**: especially folic acid (400-800 mcg) to prevent neural tube defects
- **Balanced diet**: extra ~300-500 calories/day
- **Exercise**: moderate activity (walking, swimming)
- **Sleep**: adequate rest, left side sleeping position (improves placental blood flow)

### Avoid

- **Alcohol**: risk of fetal alcohol syndrome
- **Smoking**: IUGR, preterm birth, placental problems
- **Illicit drugs**: multiple risks to fetus
- **Certain medications**: teratogenic drugs (isotretinoin, warfarin, ACE inhibitors)
- **Raw/undercooked foods**: risk of listeria, toxoplasmosis
- **High-mercury fish**: swordfish, king mackerel
- **Excessive caffeine**: limit to <200 mg/day
- **Cat litter** (toxoplasmosis risk)

## Summary

Pregnancy is a dynamic process involving remarkable maternal physiological adaptations and fetal development. From [[Fertilisation]] to birth, the embryo/fetus undergoes continuous growth and organ system development, supported by the [[Placenta]] and maternal body. Prenatal care, including routine screenings and lifestyle modifications, optimizes pregnancy outcomes and detects complications early. Understanding normal pregnancy progression and potential complications is essential for promoting maternal and fetal health.
