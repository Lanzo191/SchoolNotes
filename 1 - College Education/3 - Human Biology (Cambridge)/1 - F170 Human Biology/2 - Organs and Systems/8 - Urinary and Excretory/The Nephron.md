The **Nephron** is the functional unit of the **[[Kidney]]**. Each [[Kidney]] contains approximately one million nephrons which filter the blood and produce urine.

## Structure and Process

### 1. Ultrafiltration (Bowman’s Capsule)
Occurs in the **glomerulus** (a high-pressure capillary knot).
- High hydrostatic pressure forces small molecules (water, [[Glucose]], ions, urea) out of the blood and into the **Bowman's Capsule**.
- Large molecules (blood cells, large [[Proteins]]) remain in the blood.
- The resulting fluid is called **glomerular filtrate**.

### 2. Selective Reabsorption (Proximal Convoluted Tubule - PCT)
The body takes back useful substances from the filtrate.
- **[[Glucose]]** and **[[Amino Acids]]** are 100% reabsorbed via **[[Active Transport]]**.
- Most water and mineral ions are reabsorbed here.
- The cells of the PCT have **[[Microvilli]]** to increase surface area for absorption.

### 3. Loop of Henle
Creates a low water potential in the medulla of the [[Kidney]].
- **Descending Limb**: Permeable to water (water leaves by [[Osmosis]]).
- **Ascending Limb**: Impermeable to water; actively pumps out [[Sodium]] and chloride ions.
- This creates a concentration gradient that allows for water reabsorption later in the collecting duct.

### 4. Distal Convoluted Tubule (DCT) & Collecting Duct
The final site of water reabsorption, regulated by **Hormones**.
- **ADH (Antidiuretic Hormone)**: Increases the permeability of the collecting duct walls.
- More ADH = more water reabsorbed = smaller volume of concentrated urine.

---

## Osmoregulation (Negative Feedback)
1. **Osmoreceptors** in the **Hypothalamus** detect low water potential in the blood.
2. The **Pituitary Gland** releases more **ADH**.
3. Collecting ducts become more permeable.
4. More water is reabsorbed into the blood.
5. Blood water potential returns to normal.
