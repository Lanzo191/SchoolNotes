# Nephron Function and Osmoregulation

## Overview
The **nephron** is the functional unit of the kidney, responsible for filtering blood and regulating its water and salt content.

## Key Processes
| Stage | Location | Mechanism | What is moved? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Ultrafiltration** | **Glomerulus** / **Bowman's Capsule** | High pressure forces small molecules out of blood. | Water, glucose, urea, ions, amino acids. |
| **Selective Reabsorption** | **Proximal Convoluted Tubule (PCT)** | Active transport and facilitated diffusion. | **100% of glucose** and amino acids; 85% of water/ions. |
| **Counter-Current Multiplier** | **Loop of Henle** | Salt is pumped out of the ascending limb to create a gradient. | Na+ and Cl- pumped out; water leaves the descending limb. |
| **Water Reabsorption** | **Collecting Duct** | Osmosis controlled by **ADH**. | Water is reabsorbed back into the blood if ADH is present. |

## Detailed Mechanics
- **Glomerular Filtration Rate (GFR)**: A measure of how much blood is filtered per minute.
- **The Role of ADH**: If blood is too concentrated, the pituitary releases ADH, which increases the number of **aquaporins** in the collecting duct, making it more permeable to water.
- **Microvilli**: Cells in the PCT have many microvilli to increase surface area for reabsorption.
