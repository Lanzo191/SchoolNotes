## Overview  
The **kidneys** are two bean-shaped organs located in the **posterior abdominal cavity** on either side of the spine.  
They regulate **water balance**, **ion concentrations**, **pH**, and remove **nitrogenous waste** such as urea.  
They also help control **blood pressure** and produce important **hormones**.

Blood enters through the renal artery and leaves via the renal vein.  
Urine formed in the kidneys drains through the ureters to the bladder.

## Structure  
- Outer **cortex** and inner **medulla**  
- Medulla contains **renal pyramids** whose tips (papillae) drain into:  
  - Minor calyces → major calyces → renal pelvis → ureter  
- Nephrons are the functional units of the kidney  
  - Each nephron consists of:  
    - **Bowman's capsule** containing the **glomerulus**  - Filtration
    - **Proximal convoluted tubule (PCT)**  - Closest tube after Bowman's Capsule selective reabsorption 
    - **Loop of Henle** - Reabsorption of [[Sodium]] 
    - **Distal convoluted tubule (DCT)**  - Furthest Tube
    - **Collecting duct** - Regulates Water and electrolytes interacts with ADH

### Blood Supply  
- Renal artery → afferent arteriole → glomerulus  
- Blood leaves glomerulus via **efferent arteriole**  
- Efferent arteriole forms:  
  - **Peritubular [[Capillaries]]** (around cortex nephrons)  
  - **Vasa recta** (around juxtamedullary nephrons for concentration of urine)  
- Blood then drains into the renal vein

## Key Functions  

### 1. Filtration  
- Occurs in the **glomerulus**  
- High hydrostatic pressure forces water, ions, [[Glucose]], and urea into Bowman's capsule  
- Large [[Proteins]] and blood cells remain in the bloodstream

### 2. Selective Reabsorption  
- Occurs mainly in the **PCT**  
- Reabsorbs:  
  - [[Glucose]]  
  - [[Amino Acids]]  
  - Most water  
  - Most [[Sodium]] and other ions  
- Uses co-transporter [[Proteins]] and [[Active Transport]]

### 3. Water Regulation  
- Loop of Henle creates a **salt concentration gradient** in the medulla  
- Collecting duct reabsorbs water under control of **ADH**  
  - ADH increases water permeability (aquaporins)

### 4. Excretion  
- Remaining filtrate becomes **urine** containing:  
  - Urea  
  - Excess ions  
  - Excess water  
  - Creatinine  
- Urine passes to the ureter → bladder → urethra

### 5. Hormone Production  
- **[[Erythropoietin]] (EPO):** stimulates red blood cell production  
- **Renin:** involved in blood pressure control via RAAS  
- **Activation of Vitamin D (calcitriol):** helps [[Calcium]] absorption

## Summary Table  
| Function              | Description                                        |
|----------------------|----------------------------------------------------|
| Filtration           | Removal of water/solutes from blood into nephron   |
| Selective reabsorption | Returns [[Glucose]], ions, water to blood           |
| Osmoregulation       | ADH + collecting duct adjust water levels          |
| Excretion            | Removes urea, excess ions, and water as urine      |
| Hormone production   | EPO, renin, vitamin D activation                   |

## Clinical Relevance  
- **Kidney failure:** loss of filtration → toxins build up  
- **Kidney stones:** mineral crystals block flow of urine  
- **Glomerulonephritis:** inflammation reduces filtration  
- **[[Diabetes]]:** excess [[Glucose]] overwhelms reabsorption → [[Glucose]] in urine
