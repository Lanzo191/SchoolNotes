#Biology #Homeostasis #Thermoregulation

### Thermoregulation  
Thermoregulation is the physiological process that maintains a **constant core body temperature (~37°C)** despite changes in external temperature. It ensures that enzymes involved in [[Cellular Respiration]] and metabolism function at optimum rates. Even slight deviations from 37°C can significantly reduce enzyme efficiency or cause denaturation.

### Control Centre – Hypothalamus  
The **thermoregulatory centre** in the [[Hypothalamus]] contains **thermoreceptors** that detect changes in the temperature of the blood flowing through the brain.  
It also receives input from **peripheral thermoreceptors** in the skin, which detect external temperature changes before they affect the core.

### Mechanisms When Body Is Too Hot  
- [[Vasodilation]] of arterioles  
- Increased sweating  
- Reduced metabolic rate  
- Behavioural responses (e.g. removing clothing, seeking shade)

### Mechanisms When Body Is Too Cold  
- [[Vasoconstriction]] of skin arterioles  
- Shivering (rapid contractions produce heat)  
- Increased secretion of [[Adrenaline]] and thyroxine  
- Erector pili muscles contract, trapping a layer of insulating air  
- Behavioural responses (putting on clothes, curling up)

### Importance  
Thermoregulation protects vital organs, prevents heat loss or gain, and maintains enzymatic stability. Failure leads to hypothermia or hyperthermia, which can be fatal.
