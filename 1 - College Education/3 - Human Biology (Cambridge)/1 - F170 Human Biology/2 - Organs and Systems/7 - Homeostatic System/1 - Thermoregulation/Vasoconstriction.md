#Biology #[[Thermoregulation]] #Vasoconstriction

### Vasoconstriction  
Vasoconstriction refers to the **narrowing of arterioles** supplying the capillary beds of the skin. This reduces the volume of warm blood reaching the skin surface. As a result, less heat is lost through **radiation, conduction, convection**, and **evaporation**.

### How It Happens  
- The [[Hypothalamus]] detects a decrease in core temperature.  
- Sympathetic nerve impulses trigger the **contraction of [[Smooth Muscle]]** in arteriole walls.  
- Blood is diverted away from the skin and redirected toward vital organs.  

### Physiological Purpose  
- Conserves heat in cold environments  
- Protects core temperature to keep vital organs functioning  
- Reduces the gradient for heat loss  

### Effects on the Body  
- Skin becomes pale and cool  
- Reduced sweating  
- May cause goosebumps due to erector pili activity (less effective in humans)

### Biological Significance  
Without vasoconstriction, even mild cold exposure could lead to rapid hypothermia, especially in windy or wet conditions.
