A **synapse** is the junction between two neurons (or a neuron and an effector). Signals are transmitted across the **synaptic cleft** using chemicals called **neurotransmitters**.

## The Mechanism of Transmission (Step-by-Step)

1. **[[Action Potential]] Arrives**: An electrical impulse reaches the **presynaptic knob**.
2. **[[Calcium]] Influx**: The impulse triggers **voltage-gated [[Calcium]] channels** to open. [[Calcium]] ions ($Ca^{2+}$) diffuse into the presynaptic knob.
3. **Vesicle Fusion**: The influx of [[Calcium]] causes **synaptic vesicles** (containing neurotransmitters like **Acetylcholine**) to move toward and fuse with the presynaptic membrane.
4. **[[Exocytosis]]**: Neurotransmitters are released into the **synaptic cleft**.
5. **Diffusion**: The neurotransmitters diffuse across the gap down a concentration gradient.
6. **Receptor Binding**: Neurotransmitters bind to specific **complementary receptors** on the **postsynaptic membrane**.
7. **[[Sodium]] Influx**: This binding causes **[[Sodium]] channels** to open. [[Sodium]] ions ($Na^+$) rush in, causing **depolarization** and triggering a new [[Action Potential]] in the next neuron.
8. **Inactivation**: Enzymes (e.g., **Acetylcholinesterase**) break down the neurotransmitter to stop the signal, and parts are reabsorbed into the presynaptic knob (**reuptake**).

---

## Why are Synapses Uni-directional?
1. **Neurotransmitter** is only stored in the presynaptic knob.
2. **Receptors** are only located on the postsynaptic membrane.
