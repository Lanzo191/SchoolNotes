**Definition**
**Summation** is the process where the effect of multiple neurotransmitter releases adds together to reach the threshold required to generate an [[Action Potential]]. A single impulse is often too weak to trigger a response alone.

## Types of Summation

### 1. Spatial Summation
- Occurs when **multiple pre-synaptic neurons** release neurotransmitter at the same time onto **one** post-synaptic neuron.
- The combined amount of neurotransmitter is enough to open sufficient [[Sodium]] channels to reach the threshold.
- *Analogy: Many people pushing a door at once to open it.*

### 2. Temporal Summation
- Occurs when a **single pre-synaptic neuron** releases neurotransmitter many times in **rapid succession** (high frequency).
- The neurotransmitter builds up in the cleft faster than it can be broken down, eventually reaching the threshold.
- *Analogy: One person hitting a door repeatedly until it breaks open.*

## Inhibitory Synapses
Not all synapses are excitatory. Some are **inhibitory**:
- Neurotransmitter binding causes **Hyperpolarisation** (e.g., opening Chloride channels so Cl- enters).
- This makes the potential more negative (e.g., -80mV), moving it *further* from the threshold.
- This makes it harder for an [[Action Potential]] to be generated (stops the signal).
