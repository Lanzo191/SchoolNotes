**Overview**
A cholinergic synapse uses the neurotransmitter **Acetylcholine (ACh)**. It is the most common type of synapse in the peripheral nervous system (e.g., at neuromuscular junctions).

## Mechanism of Transmission

1.  **Arrival:** The [[Action Potential]] reaches the **Pre-synaptic Knob**.
2.  **[[Calcium]] Influx:** The depolarisation causes voltage-gated **[[Calcium]] Channels** to open. [[Calcium]] ions (Ca2+) diffuse into the knob.
3.  **[[Exocytosis]]:** The influx of Ca2+ causes synaptic vesicles to fuse with the pre-synaptic membrane, releasing **Acetylcholine** into the synaptic cleft.
4.  **Diffusion:** Acetylcholine diffuses across the cleft (down its concentration gradient).
5.  **Binding:** Acetylcholine binds to specific receptor sites on **[[Sodium]] Channels** on the **Post-synaptic Membrane**.
6.  **Depolarisation:** The [[Sodium]] channels open, allowing Na+ to diffuse into the post-synaptic neuron.
7.  **[[Action Potential]]:** If the influx of Na+ reaches the threshold, a new [[Action Potential]] is generated in the post-synaptic neuron.

## Recycling (Stopping the Signal)

To prevent continuous stimulation, the signal must be stopped:

1.  **Breakdown:** The enzyme **Acetylcholinesterase (AChE)** in the cleft hydrolyses Acetylcholine into **Choline** and **Ethanoic Acid (Acetate)**.
2.  **Reuptake:** Choline and Ethanoic Acid diffuse back into the pre-synaptic knob.
3.  **Resynthesis:** [[Mitochondria]] provide [[ATP]] to recombine them into Acetylcholine, which is stored in vesicles for next time.
