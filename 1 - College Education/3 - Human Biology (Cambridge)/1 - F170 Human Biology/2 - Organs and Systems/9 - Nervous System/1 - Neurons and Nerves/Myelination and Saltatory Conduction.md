**Myelination**
Myelination refers to the insulation of an axon by the **Myelin Sheath**, which is made of lipid (fat). In the Peripheral Nervous System, this is formed by **Schwann cells** wrapping repeatedly around the axon.

## Function of Myelin
- **Electrical Insulation:** Prevents the movement of ions (Na+ and K+) across the membrane in the myelinated sections.
- **Protection:** Protects the physical integrity of the axon.

## Saltatory Conduction
"Saltatory" comes from the Latin *saltare*, meaning "to jump".

1.  In a myelinated neuron, ions can only cross the membrane at the **Nodes of Ranvier** (the gaps in the myelin).
2.  Depolarisation ([[Action Potential]]) can therefore only occur at these nodes.
3.  The electrical impulse **jumps** from one node to the next, rather than travelling along the entire length of the membrane.

### Advantages of Saltatory Conduction
*   **Speed:** Much faster transmission (up to 120 m/s) compared to unmyelinated neurons (which may be only 1 m/s).
*   **Energy Efficiency:** Less [[ATP]] is required because the [[Sodium]]-[[Potassium]] pump only needs to restore the balance at the nodes, not along the whole axon.

## Factors Affecting Speed of Transmission

1.  **Myelination:** Myelinated = Faster.
2.  **Axon Diameter:** Larger diameter = Faster (less resistance to ion flow).
3.  **Temperature:** Higher temperature = Faster (faster diffusion of ions and faster enzyme activity for respiration/[[ATP]]), up to the point of denaturation.
