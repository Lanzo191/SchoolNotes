**Overview**
A **Motor Neuron** transmits electrical impulses from the Central Nervous System (CNS) to effectors (muscles or glands). Its structure is highly specialised for the rapid transmission of signals over long distances.

## Key Structural Components

### 1. Cell Body (Soma)
- Contains the **[[Nucleus]]** and large amounts of **Rough Endoplasmic Reticulum** (RER).
- The RER is involved in the production of [[Proteins]] and neurotransmitters.

### 2. Dendrites
- Short, branched extensions from the cell body.
- They transmit electrical impulses **towards** the cell body.

### 3. Axon
- A single, long fibre that carries impulses **away** from the cell body.
- Can be very long (e.g., from the spine to the foot).

### 4. Schwann Cells
- [[Specialised cells]] that wrap themselves around the axon.
- They produce the **Myelin Sheath**.
- They also carry out phagocytosis to remove debris and play a role in nerve regeneration.

### 5. Myelin Sheath
- A fatty, insulating layer formed by Schwann cells.
- It prevents the electrical impulse from leaking out and increases the speed of transmission (see **Saltatory Conduction**).

### 6. Nodes of Ranvier
- Small gaps in the myelin sheath where the axon is exposed.
- Action potentials "jump" from one node to the next.

### 7. Axon Terminals (Synaptic Knobs)
- The branched ends of the axon.
- Contain vesicles filled with **neurotransmitters** (e.g., Acetylcholine) to transmit the signal across a synapse.
