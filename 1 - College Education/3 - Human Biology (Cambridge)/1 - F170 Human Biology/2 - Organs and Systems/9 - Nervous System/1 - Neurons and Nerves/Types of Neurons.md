**Overview**
There are three main types of neurons, each adapted to a specific role in the nervous system.

## Comparison Table

| Feature | Sensory Neuron | Relay Neuron (Interneuron) | Motor Neuron |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Function** | Transmits impulses from **Receptors** to the **CNS**. | Transmits impulses between neurons **within** the CNS (connects sensory to motor). | Transmits impulses from the **CNS** to **Effectors** (muscles/glands). |
| **Location of Cell Body** | In the **Dorsal Root Ganglion** (just outside the spinal cord). | Entirely within the **CNS** (brain/spinal cord). | Within the **CNS**, but axon extends out. |
| **Structure** | Short axon, long dendron. Cell body is in the middle of the fibre. | Short axon, short dendrites. | Long axon, short dendrites. Cell body is at one end. |
| **Myelination** | Usually myelinated. | Usually unmyelinated. | Usually myelinated. |

## Structural Differences Explained

### Sensory Neuron
- Has a long **dendron** that carries the impulse from the receptor to the cell body.
- The cell body is located off the side of the main fibre (like a "T" junction).

### Relay Neuron
- Highly branched dendrites to form complex connections with many other neurons.
- Found in the grey matter of the spinal cord.

### Motor Neuron
- Large cell body located at the end of the neuron (in the spinal cord) with a very long axon projecting out to the muscle.
