# Action Potential in Neurons

## Overview
An **action potential** is a rapid change in voltage across a membrane. It is the basis for communication in the nervous system.

## The Process
| Phase | Membrane Potential | Event |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Resting Potential** | -70mV | Na+/K+ pump maintains high Na+ outside and high K+ inside. |
| **Depolarisation** | -70mV to +40mV | Voltage-gated Na+ channels open; Na+ rushes into the cell. |
| **Repolarisation** | +40mV to -70mV | Na+ channels close; voltage-gated K+ channels open; K+ rushes out. |
| **Hyperpolarisation** | < -70mV | K+ channels are slow to close; membrane becomes more negative than resting. |
| **Refractory Period** | Returning to -70mV | Na+/K+ pump restores resting potential; no new AP can be fired. |

## Key Terms
- **Threshold**: The level of depolarisation (-55mV) needed to trigger an AP.
- **All-or-Nothing Principle**: If the threshold is reached, a full AP is fired; if not, nothing happens.
- **Saltatory Conduction**: The "jumping" of an AP between **Nodes of Ranvier** in myelinated neurons, which increases speed.
