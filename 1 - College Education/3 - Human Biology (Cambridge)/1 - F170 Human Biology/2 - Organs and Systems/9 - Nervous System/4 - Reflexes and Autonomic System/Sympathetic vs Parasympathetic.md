**Overview**
The **Autonomic Nervous System (ANS)** controls involuntary activities ([[Smooth Muscle]], [[Cardiac Muscle]], glands). It is divided into two branches that often work antagonistically.

## Comparison Table

| Feature | Sympathetic Nervous System | Parasympathetic Nervous System |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **General Function** | **"Fight or Flight"** | **"Rest and Digest"** |
| **Active During** | Stress, danger, excitement, exercise. | Relaxation, sleep, digestion. |
| **Neurotransmitter** | **[[Noradrenaline]]** (Norepinephrine) | **Acetylcholine** |
| **Effect on Heart Rate** | **Increases** heart rate and force of contraction. | **Decreases** heart rate. |
| **Effect on Breathing** | **Dilates** [[Bronchioles]] (allows more air in). | **Constricts** [[Bronchioles]]. |
| **Effect on Pupils** | **Dilates** pupils (allows more light to see danger). | **Constricts** pupils. |
| **Effect on Digestion** | **Inhibits** digestion (blood diverted to muscles). | **Stimulates** digestion and peristalsis. |
| **Effect on [[Liver]]** | Stimulates **Glycogenolysis** (releases [[Glucose]] for energy). | Stimulates **Glycogenesis** (stores [[Glucose]]). |

## Summary
The two systems work in opposition to maintain homeostasis depending on the body's needs. For example, during exercise, the sympathetic system dominates to supply oxygen and [[Glucose]] to muscles. After a meal, the parasympathetic system dominates to absorb nutrients.
