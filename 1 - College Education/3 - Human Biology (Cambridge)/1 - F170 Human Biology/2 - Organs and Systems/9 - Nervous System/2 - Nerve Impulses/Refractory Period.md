**Definition**
The **Refractory Period** is the brief time following an [[Action Potential]] during which a new [[Action Potential]] cannot be generated in that section of membrane.

## Types of Refractory Period

1.  **Absolute Refractory Period:**
    *   No new [[Action Potential]] can be generated, no matter how strong the stimulus.
    *   The voltage-gated Na+ channels are inactivated (locked shut) and cannot reopen yet.

2.  **Relative Refractory Period:**
    *   A new [[Action Potential]] can be generated, but only by a **stronger than normal** stimulus.
    *   The membrane is hyperpolarised (extra negative), so it takes a larger influx of Na+ to reach the threshold.

## Importance of the Refractory Period

It serves three critical functions:

1.  **Unidirectionality:**
    *   Ensures the impulse travels in only one direction (forward).
    *   The region behind the [[Action Potential]] is refractory, so the impulse cannot propagate backwards.

2.  **Discrete Impulses:**
    *   Ensures action potentials are separate events, not overlapping.
    *   This allows the brain to distinguish between distinct signals.

3.  **Frequency Limit:**
    *   Limits the maximum frequency of impulse transmission (how many impulses can be sent per second).
