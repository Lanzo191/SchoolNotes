**Definition**
The **Resting Potential** is the potential difference (voltage) across the membrane of a neuron when it is not transmitting an impulse. In a typical human neuron, this is approximately **-70 mV**. The inside of the cell is negatively charged relative to the outside.

## Establishing the Resting Potential

The membrane is **polarised**. This state is maintained by three main factors:

### 1. The Sodium-Potassium Pump (Active Transport)
- This transmembrane protein actively pumps ions against their concentration gradient using [[ATP]].
- For every **3 [[Sodium]] ions (Na+)** pumped **OUT**,
- **2 [[Potassium]] ions (K+)** are pumped **IN**.
- This creates an electrochemical gradient: more positive ions are outside than inside.

### 2. Differential Permeability (Facilitated Diffusion)
- The membrane has protein channels for Na+ and K+.
- It is **permeable to K+**: [[Potassium]] channels are predominantly open, allowing K+ to diffuse back **out** of the cell down its concentration gradient.
- It is **impermeable to Na+**: [[Sodium]] channels are mostly closed, so Na+ cannot diffuse back in easily.

### 3. Anions
- The cytoplasm contains large, negatively charged [[Proteins]] (anions) that cannot leave the cell, contributing to the negative internal charge.

## Summary
The combination of the Na+/K+ pump moving net positive charge out, and the "leaky" K+ channels allowing further positive charge to leave, results in the interior being negative (-70mV) relative to the exterior.
