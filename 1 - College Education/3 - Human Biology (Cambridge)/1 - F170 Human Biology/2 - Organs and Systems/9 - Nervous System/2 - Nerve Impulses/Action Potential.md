**Definition**
An **Action Potential** is a rapid change in the membrane potential that sweeps along the axon, constituting the nerve impulse. It operates on the **"All-or-Nothing" principle**: if the threshold is reached, a full response occurs; if not, nothing happens.

## Stages of an Action Potential

### 1. Depolarisation
- A stimulus triggers some [[Sodium]] channels to open.
- If the potential reaches the **Threshold Potential** (approx -55 mV), voltage-gated [[Sodium]] channels open rapidly.
- **[[Sodium]] ions (Na+) flood INTO the axon** down their electrochemical gradient.
- The inside becomes positive relative to the outside (approx **+40 mV**).

### 2. Repolarisation
- At +40 mV, the voltage-gated Na+ channels close and voltage-gated **[[Potassium]] channels open**.
- **[[Potassium]] ions (K+) diffuse OUT** of the axon down their concentration gradient.
- The membrane potential drops back down towards the negative side.

### 3. Hyperpolarisation
- The K+ channels are slow to close.
- Too many [[Potassium]] ions diffuse out, making the potential even more negative than the [[Resting Potential]] (e.g., -80 mV).
- This ensures the impulse moves in one direction (see **[[Refractory Period]]**).

### 4. Resting State
- The K+ channels close.
- The **[[Sodium]]-[[Potassium]] Pump** works to restore the correct ion balance (Na+ out, K+ in), returning the membrane to **-70 mV**.
