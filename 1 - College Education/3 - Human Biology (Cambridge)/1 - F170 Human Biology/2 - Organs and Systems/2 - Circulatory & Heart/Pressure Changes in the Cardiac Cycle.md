# Pressure Changes in the Cardiac Cycle

## Overview
The **cardiac cycle** involves a series of pressure changes that drive the flow of blood through the heart. These changes are crucial for the opening and closing of heart valves.

## The Cycle Breakdown
| Phase | Action | Pressure Changes | Valve Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Atrial Systole** | Atria contract. | Atrial pressure rises above ventricular pressure. | AV valves **open**; Semilunar valves **closed**. |
| **Ventricular Systole** | Ventricles contract. | Ventricular pressure rises sharply above atrial pressure. | AV valves **close** (S1 "lub"); Semilunar valves **open** when pressure exceeds aorta/pulmonary artery. |
| **Diastole** | Heart relaxes. | Ventricular pressure falls below aorta/pulmonary artery. | Semilunar valves **close** (S2 "dub"); atria refill with blood. |

## Key Concepts
- **Pressure Gradient**: Blood always flows from an area of **high pressure** to **low pressure**.
- **Ventricular Pressure**: Reaches its peak during systole (~120 mmHg in the left ventricle) to pump blood to the entire body.
- **Atrial Pressure**: Remains relatively low as atria only need to pump blood a short distance into the ventricles.
