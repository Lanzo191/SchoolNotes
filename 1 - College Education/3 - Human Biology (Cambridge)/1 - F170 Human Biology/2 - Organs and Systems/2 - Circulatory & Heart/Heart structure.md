### External Structure of Heart - 

- **Pericardium** – Double-walled fluid-filled sac surrounding the heart, reduces friction during beating.  
- **Endocardium** – Innermost smooth layer lining chambers and valves, prevents blood clotting and protects tissues.  
- **Myocardium** – Thick muscular middle layer responsible for contraction and relaxation of the heart.  

---

### Components / Structure - 

- 4 chambers:  
  - **Atria (right & left):** Thin-walled, receive blood.  
  - **Ventricles (right & left):** Thick-walled, pump blood (left ventricle wall is thickest for systemic circulation).  
- **Septum:** Divides left and right sides, prevents mixing of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood.  
- **Coronary arteries:** Supply heart muscle with oxygen and nutrients.  

---

### Route of Blood - 

1. Deoxygenated blood enters **right atrium** from:  
   - Vena Cava (Superior & Inferior).  
1. Moves through [[Tricuspid Valve]] → **right ventricle**.  
2. Pumped through [[Pulmonary Valve]] → **pulmonary arteries** → lungs (gas exchange).  
3. Oxygenated blood returns via Pulmonary Veins → **left atrium**.  
4. Passes through [[Bicuspid (Mitral) Valve]] → **left ventricle**.  
5. Pumped through [[Aortic Valve]] → **aorta** → systemic circulation.  

---

### Arteries & Veins - 

- **Major arteries:**  
  - **Aorta** – carries oxygenated blood from left ventricle to the body.  
  - **Pulmonary arteries** – carry deoxygenated blood from right ventricle to lungs.  
  - **Coronary arteries** – supply the heart muscle with blood.  

- **Major veins:**  
  - **Vena cava (superior & inferior):** bring deoxygenated blood back to right atrium.  
  - **Pulmonary veins:** bring oxygenated blood from lungs to left atrium.  
  - **Coronary veins:** drain deoxygenated blood from myocardium into right atrium.  

**Mnemonic for blood vessels (arteries away, veins towards):**  
 *“Arteries Away, Veins Visit”*  

### Structure of Blood Vessels - 

- **Arteries:**  
  - Thick muscular walls, elastic tissue → withstand high pressure.  
  - Narrow lumen.  
- **Veins:**  
  - Thin walls, large lumen, valves prevent backflow.  
  - Lower pressure.  
- **Capillaries:**  
  - Single cell thick wall (endothelium).  
  - Large surface area for exchange.  


### Pressure Differences - 

- **Left ventricle** → much thicker wall than right ventricle → pumps at higher pressure into systemic circulation.  
- **Right ventricle** → thinner wall, pumps at lower pressure to lungs (to avoid damaging delicate alveoli).  
### Key Adaptations - 

- **Double circulation** keeps oxygenated and deoxygenated blood separate.  
- High-pressure systemic circulation ensures rapid oxygen delivery.  
- Low-pressure pulmonary circulation protects lungs.

### Valves -
#### [[Tricuspid Valve|Tricuspid Valve]]
- Between right atrium and right ventricle.  
- Prevents backflow of blood into right atrium during ventricular contraction.  

#### [[Bicuspid (Mitral) Valve]]
- Between left atrium and left ventricle.  
- Prevents backflow into left atrium.  

#### [[Pulmonary Valve]]
- Between right ventricle and pulmonary artery.  
- Stops blood flowing back into ventricle after contraction.  

#### [[Aortic Valve]]
- Between left ventricle and aorta.  
- Stops backflow into ventricle after contraction.  

**Mnemonic for order (starting from right atrium):**  
 *“Try Pulling My Aorta”* (Tricuspid, Pulmonary, Mitral, Aortic).

### Cardiac Muscle
- Rich in **mitochondria** to meet high energy demand.  
- **Striated** (striped appearance under microscope).  
- **Branched** fibres allow coordinated contraction.  
- **Myogenic** – generates its own electrical impulses (does not rely on nervous system to beat).  
- Intercalated discs allow rapid transmission of electrical signals between cells.
