# A. Cell Biology Questions

### Q1)  What is the function of mitochondria?
**Model Answer:**  
Mitochondria carry out aerobic respiration and release ATP. This provides energy for cellular processes such as muscle contraction or active transport.

### Q2) Give two differences between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells.
**Model Answer:**  
Prokaryotes have circular DNA that is free in the cytoplasm, while eukaryotes have linear DNA enclosed in a nucleus.  
Prokaryotes lack membrane-bound organelles, while eukaryotes contain mitochondria, ER, Golgi apparatus and others.

### Q3 - Why do epithelial cells in the small intestine contain many mitochondria?
**Model Answer:**  
They need lots of ATP for active transport of glucose and amino acids during absorption.

# B. Enzymes Questions

### Q4 - Describe how temperature affects enzyme activity.
**Model Answer:**  
Increasing temperature increases the kinetic energy of molecules, causing more collisions between enzymes and substrates. Beyond the optimum, the enzyme’s active site denatures and substrate no longer fits, decreasing reaction rate.

### Q5 - Explain what is meant by an enzyme’s “active site”.
**Model Answer:**  
It is a region of the enzyme with a specific shape that is complementary to a substrate. Binding forms an enzyme–substrate complex, allowing the reaction to occur.

# C. Transport, Osmosis & Diffusion

### Q6 - Define osmosis.
**Model Answer:**  
Osmosis is the movement of water across a selectively permeable membrane from an area of high water potential to an area of low water potential.

### Q7 - Why do red blood cells burst in pure water?
**Model Answer:**  
Water enters the cell via osmosis because the cytoplasm has lower water potential. The cell lacks a cell wall, so the membrane ruptures (lysis).

# D. Tissues & Organs

### Q8 - What is the main function of nervous tissue?
**Model Answer:**  
To transmit electrical impulses rapidly around the body, allowing fast communication between receptors and effectors.

### Q9 - Why does cardiac muscle not fatigue?
**Model Answer:**  
It contains many mitochondria and a constant blood supply, providing continuous oxygen and ATP for sustained contraction.

# E. Digestive System

### Q10 - How does the structure of villi help absorption?
**Model Answer (4 marks):**  
They have a large surface area due to microvilli.  
They contain blood capillaries to maintain diffusion gradient.  
They have a thin epithelial layer for short diffusion distance.  
They contain lacteals for lipid absorption.

# F. Gas Exchange

### Q11 - Explain why alveoli are covered in capillaries.
**Model Answer:**  
Capillaries provide a rich blood supply to maintain steep concentration gradients for oxygen and carbon dioxide, ensuring rapid diffusion.

### Q12 - Describe two ways the body increases breathing rate during exercise.
**Model Answer:**  
Increased carbon dioxide concentration detected by chemoreceptors triggers faster breathing.  
Muscles require more oxygen, so the respiratory centres increase rate and depth of breathing.

# G. Circulation & Blood

### Q13 - What is the role of valves in the circulatory system?
**Model Answer:**  
Valves prevent the backflow of blood, ensuring it flows in one direction through the heart and veins.

### Q14 - Why do arteries have thick walls?
**Model Answer:**  
They must withstand and maintain high pressure generated by the heart’s contractions.

# H. Homeostasis (Thermoregulation + Osmoregulation)

### Q15 - Explain how vasodilation helps reduce body temperature.
**Model Answer:**  
Blood vessels near the skin widen, increasing blood flow to the surface. More heat is lost through radiation from the skin to the environment.

### Q16 - Describe the role of ADH in osmoregulation.
**Model Answer:**  
When blood water level is low, ADH is released from the posterior pituitary. It makes the kidney’s collecting ducts more permeable, allowing more water to be reabsorbed into the bloodstream and producing smaller volumes of concentrated urine.

---

# I. Kidney & Nephron

### Q17 - What happens during ultrafiltration?
**Model Answer:**  
High pressure forces water, ions, glucose and urea out of the blood in the glomerulus into Bowman’s capsule, forming filtrate. Large molecules like proteins remain in the blood.

### Q18 - Why do people with diabetes have glucose in their urine?
**Model Answer:**  
The proximal convoluted tubule becomes saturated and cannot reabsorb all the glucose when blood glucose levels are excessively high, so excess is excreted.

# J. Hormones & Endocrine System

### Q19 - Compare the actions of nerves and hormones.
**Model Answer:**  
Nerves cause very fast, short-lived responses through electrical impulses.  
Hormones act slower but produce longer-lasting changes by travelling through the bloodstream to target organs.

### Q20 - Explain why thyroxine is needed for growth.
**Model Answer:**  
Thyroxine controls metabolic rate; cells require sufficient metabolic activity to produce ATP, supporting growth, cell division and protein synthesis.

# K. Nervous System & Reflexes

### Q21 - Outline the pathway of a reflex arc.
**Model Answer:**  
Stimulus → receptor → sensory neuron → relay neuron (spinal cord) → motor neuron → effector → response.  
This pathway avoids the brain, making the response rapid and automatic.

### Q22 - Why is myelin important?
**Model Answer:**  
Myelin insulates the axon, allowing electrical impulses to travel faster via saltatory conduction between nodes of Ranvier.

---

# L. Immunity

### Q23 - Describe how a vaccine leads to immunity.
**Model Answer:**  
A vaccine contains antigens that stimulate a primary immune response, forming specific memory cells. On later exposure, memory cells produce a faster, stronger secondary immune response, preventing disease.

### Q24 - How do phagocytes destroy pathogens?
**Model Answer:**  
They engulf the pathogen by phagocytosis, enclose it in a vesicle, and digest it with enzymes contained in lysosomes.

# M. Genetics

### Q25 - What is the difference between mitosis and meiosis?
**Model Answer:**  
Mitosis produces two identical diploid cells for growth and repair.  
Meiosis produces four genetically different haploid gametes for sexual reproduction.

### Q26 - Explain why siblings can look different.
**Model Answer:**  
Meiosis introduces variation through independent assortment and crossing over, and fertilisation is random, meaning each child inherits a unique combination of alleles.

---

### Q27) Describe the role of the Sinoatrial Node (SAN) in the cardiac cycle.
**Model Answer:**
The **Sinoatrial Node (SAN)** acts as the heart's natural pacemaker. It generates an electrical impulse that spreads across the atria, causing **atrial systole**. This impulse is then delayed at the **Atrioventricular Node (AVN)** to ensure the ventricles fill completely before contracting.

---

### Q28) Explain the "All-or-Nothing" principle in relation to nerve impulses.
**Model Answer:**
The **All-or-Nothing** principle states that an action potential is only generated if the stimulus reaches a specific **threshold level** (usually -55mV). If the threshold is reached, a full action potential is fired at a constant size; if it is not reached, no impulse is generated at all.

---

### Q29) Outline the process of the Sliding Filament Theory during muscle contraction.
**Model Answer:**
1. **Ca2+ ions** are released from the sarcoplasmic reticulum and bind to **troponin**, moving **tropomyosin** to reveal binding sites on actin.
2. **Myosin heads** bind to actin, forming **cross-bridges**.
3. The **power stroke** occurs as myosin pulls actin filaments toward the centre of the sarcomere (M-line).
4. **ATP** binds to the myosin head, causing it to detach and "reset" for the next cycle.

# N. Extended-Response / "6–9 Mark” Style Questions

### Q30 - *Describe how the body maintains body temperature when it becomes too cold.* (6 marks)
**Model Answer:**  
Thermoreceptors in the skin and hypothalamus detect a decrease in body temperature.  
The hypothalamus triggers vasoconstriction so less blood flows near the skin surface, reducing heat loss.  
Skeletal muscles begin shivering, which increases respiration and releases heat.  
Erector pili muscles contract, causing hairs to stand up and trap an insulating layer of air.  
Metabolic rate may increase due to adrenaline release.  
Together these responses restore core temperature to normal.

### Q31 - *Explain how the kidney produces concentrated urine when blood water levels are low.* (6 marks)
**Model Answer:**  
The hypothalamus detects low water concentration and triggers the posterior pituitary to secrete ADH.  
ADH increases the permeability of the collecting ducts.  
More water is reabsorbed from the filtrate back into the blood by osmosis due to high salt concentration in the medulla.  
Less water remains in the filtrate, so urine becomes more concentrated and smaller in volume.  
This restores normal blood water balance.

### Q32 - *Describe the structure and function of the alveoli in gas exchange.* (6 marks)
**Model Answer:**  
Alveoli are tiny air sacs with thin, one-cell-thick walls providing a short diffusion distance.  
They have a large surface area due to their spherical shape and vast numbers.  
They are surrounded by capillaries which maintain steep concentration gradients.  
Moist surfaces allow oxygen to dissolve and diffuse into blood.  
Elastic fibres help alveoli recoil during breathing.  
These features allow rapid and efficient diffusion of oxygen and carbon dioxide.
