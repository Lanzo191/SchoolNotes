#Education #NervousSystem #OCR #Psychology

## Components -
1.  **[[Hypothalamus]]:** Releases **CRH** (Corticotropin-releasing hormone).
2.  **[[Pituitary Gland]]:** Releases **ACTH** (Adrenocorticotropic hormone).
3.  **[[Adrenal Glands]]:** Release **[[Cortisol]]** from the adrenal cortex.

## Functions -
- **The Stress Response:** The HPA Axis is the body's primary "slow-acting" response to long-term stress.
- **Homeostasis:** Helps regulate blood pressure, metabolism, and immune system activity during periods of pressure.
- **Negative Feedback:** High levels of [[Cortisol]] trigger the [[Hypothalamus]] to *stop* releasing CRH, preventing the response from going out of control.

## The Process (Step-by-Step) -
1.  A stressor is detected by the brain.
2.  The **[[Hypothalamus]]** secretes CRH.
3.  CRH stimulates the **[[Pituitary Gland]]** to secrete ACTH into the bloodstream.
4.  ACTH travels to the **[[Adrenal Glands]]**, triggering the release of **[[Cortisol]]**.
5.  [[Cortisol]] mobilises energy ([[Glucose]]) and suppresses non-essential functions (like digestion).

## Clinical Links -
- **Chronic Stress:** Constant HPA activation leads to high [[Cortisol]], which can damage the [[Hippocampus]] and lead to anxiety or depression.
- **Cushing’s Syndrome:** Caused by excessively high [[Cortisol]].
- **Addison’s Disease:** Caused by chronically low [[Cortisol]] levels.
