## Definition
**Brain Plasticity** refers to the brain's ability to change and adapt as a result of experience and new learning. This contradicts the old view that the brain is a static organ that stops developing after childhood.

Plasticity involves the **creation of new neural pathways** and the **alteration of existing synapses**.

---

## Types of Plasticity

### 1. Functional Plasticity
The brain's ability to move functions from a damaged area of the brain to other, undamaged areas.
*   *Example:* If a person has a stroke that damages the language centre, other areas may eventually adapt to take over some language functions (rehabilitation).

### 2. Structural Plasticity
The brain's ability to actually change its physical structure (density/size of grey matter) as a result of learning.
*   *Example:* Learning a new skill (like juggling or navigation) strengthens the connections between neurons, making that part of the brain "denser" or larger.

---

## Key Studies Supporting Plasticity

### 1. Maguire et al. (2000) - Structural Plasticity in Adults
**Study:** MRI scans of London Taxi Drivers.
**Finding:** Taxi drivers had a significantly larger **Posterior Hippocampus** than non-drivers.
**Significance:** This demonstrates **Structural Plasticity**. The intense learning of "The Knowledge" (spatial maps) physically changed the structure of their brains. It proves the adult brain is still plastic.
*   *Link:* [[Maguire et al (2000)]]

### 2. Blakemore and Cooper (1970) - Developmental Plasticity
**Study:** Kittens raised in restricted visual environments (vertical or horizontal stripes).
**Finding:** Kittens raised in a "vertical world" became physically blind to horizontal lines. Their brains lacked the neurons required to fire for horizontal orientations.
**Significance:** This demonstrates how the environment shapes the brain during a **Critical Period**. Use it or lose it-because the neurons weren't used, they were repurposed or died off.
*   *Link:* [[Blakemore and Cooper (1970)]]

---

## Mechanisms of Plasticity

1.  **Synaptic Pruning:** "Use it or lose it." Rarely used connections are deleted, while frequently used connections are strengthened. This makes the brain more efficient.
2.  **Axonal Sprouting:** Undamaged axons grow new nerve endings to reconnect neurons whose links were injured or severed.

## Factors Affecting Plasticity
*   **Age:** Plasticity is greatest in childhood (Blakemore & Cooper), but Maguire proves it still occurs in adulthood.
*   **Environment:** Enriched environments (lots of stimulation) lead to more synaptic connections.
