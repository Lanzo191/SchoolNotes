The **Cerebral Cortex** is the outer layer of the brain (the "grey matter"), responsible for higher-level processes like thought, language, and memory. It is divided into two **hemispheres** (Left and Right) and four distinct **lobes**.

---

## 1. The Frontal Lobe
*   **Location:** At the front of the brain (behind the forehead).
*   **Key Functions:** Decision making, problem-solving, planning, personality, and voluntary movement.
*   **Damage:** Can lead to changes in personality (e.g., **Phineas Gage**), loss of fine motor skills, or impulsivity.

### Key Areas in the Frontal Lobe:
*   **Prefrontal Cortex:** The very front part. Responsible for complex cognitive behavior, personality expression, and moderating social behavior.
*   **Motor Cortex:** located at the back of the frontal lobe (Precentral Gyrus). Controls **voluntary muscle movement** on the opposite (contralateral) side of the body.
*   **Broca’s Area:** Located in the **Left Frontal Lobe** only. Responsible for **speech production**. Damage causes Broca's Aphasia (slow, laborious speech).

---

## 2. The Parietal Lobe
*   **Location:** Behind the frontal lobe, on the top/back of the head.
*   **Key Functions:** Processing sensory information (touch, temperature, pain) and spatial awareness (proprioception).
*   **Damage:** Can cause "Hemispatial Neglect" (ignoring one half of the visual world) or difficulty with navigation and mathematics.

### Key Areas in the Parietal Lobe:
*   **Somatosensory Cortex:** Located at the front of the parietal lobe (Postcentral Gyrus). It receives and processes sensory information from the skin (touch, heat, pain).
    *   *The Homunculus:* A map of the body on this cortex. Sensitive areas like lips/fingers take up more space than the back/legs.

---

## 3. The Temporal Lobe
*   **Location:** On the sides of the brain (near the ears/temples).
*   **Key Functions:** Auditory processing (hearing), memory encoding, and language comprehension.
*   **Damage:** Can lead to memory loss (amnesia) or difficulty understanding spoken language.

### Key Areas in the Temporal Lobe:
*   **Auditory Cortex:** Processes sound information (pitch, volume).
*   **Wernicke’s Area:** Located in the **Left Temporal Lobe** only. Responsible for **language comprehension** (understanding speech). Damage causes Wernicke's Aphasia (fluent but meaningless "word salad").
*   **Hippocampus:** (Located deep within the medial temporal lobe). Critical for forming new **episodic memories** (see [[Maguire et al (2000)]]).
*   **Amygdala:** (Deep within the temporal lobe). Responsible for emotional responses, particularly **fear and aggression**.

---

## 4. The Occipital Lobe
*   **Location:** At the very back of the brain.
*   **Key Functions:** Visual processing.
*   **Damage:** Can cause "Cortical Blindness" (eyes work, but brain can't see) or visual hallucinations.

### Key Areas in the Occipital Lobe:
*   **Visual Cortex:** Receives input from the retina.
    *   **V1 (Primary Visual Cortex):** Basic processing (lines, edges - see [[Blakemore and Cooper (1970)]]).
    *   **V4:** Processes colour.
    *   **V5:** Processes motion.

## Connection Between Hemispheres
*   **[[Corpus Callosum]]:** The thick bundle of nerve fibres connecting the Left and Right hemispheres, allowing them to communicate. Severing this leads to "Split Brain" (see [[Sperry (1968)]]).
