## Location
- Medial [[Temporal Lobe]].
- Part of the **Limbic System**.
- Positioned anterior to the [[Hippocampus]].

## Structure
- Composed of several almond-shaped nuclei:
  - **Basolateral Complex (BLA)**: Receives sensory inputs from the cortex and thalamus.
  - **Central Nucleus (CeA)**: Primary output region; projects to the [[Hypothalamus]] and [[Brainstem]] to trigger autonomic fear responses.
  - **Medial Nucleus**: Processes olfactory stimuli influencing emotional behaviour.

## Neural Pathways
- **Fast Pathway**: [[Thalamus]] -> Amygdala (rapid, crude sensory signal for automatic, reflexive threat appraisal).
- **Slow Pathway**: [[Thalamus]] -> Sensory Cortex -> Amygdala (detailed, high-resolution evaluation of stimuli).
- **Outputs**:
  - [[Hypothalamus]] -> triggers autonomic sympathetic fight-or-flight and endocrine cascade.
  - [[Hippocampus]] -> couples emotional arousal with declarative memory consolidation.
  - [[Brainstem]] -> modulates respiratory rate, heart rate, and startle reflexes.

## Functions
- **Threat Detection**: Evaluates fear, environmental threat, and emotional salience.
- **Emotional Memory**: Encodes and consolidates emotionally charged memories via synaptic plasticity.
- **Fear Conditioning**: Acquires conditioned emotional responses to previously neutral stimuli.
- **Social Cognition**: Interprets emotional facial expressions (specifically fearful faces) and social cues.
- **Aggression Regulation**: Modulates defensive and predatory aggression.

## Neurotransmitters
- **Glutamate**: Primary excitatory neurotransmitter; mediates long-term potentiation in emotional learning.
- **GABA**: Primary inhibitory neurotransmitter; dampens amygdala hyperactivity and regulates anxiety.
- **Noradrenaline**: Released under acute stress to enhance amygdala activation and memory strength.

## Clinical Links
- **Hyperactivity**: Strongly correlated with post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), panic disorder, and specific phobias.
- **Hypoactivity**: Associated with psychopathy, antisocial personality traits, and diminished threat perception.
- **Bilateral Lesions (Kluver-Bucy Syndrome)**: Causes severe psychic blindness, loss of fear conditioning, and inability to recognize facial fear.
