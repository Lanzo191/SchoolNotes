## Location -
- Deep within the cerebral hemispheres, surrounding the [[Thalamus]].
- Includes several structures: caudate [[Nucleus]], putamen, globus pallidus, subthalamic [[Nucleus]], and the substantia nigra.

## Functions -
- **Movement Initiation:** Essential for starting voluntary muscle movements and controlling their force.
- **Inhibition:** Inhibits unwanted or competing muscle movements to ensure actions are smooth and precise.
- **Habit Formation:** Plays a key role in procedural learning (learning "how" to do things like riding a bike) and forming automatic habits.
- **Cognition:** Involved in some executive functions and emotional regulation.

## Neurotransmitters -
- **[[Dopamine]]:** Facilitates movement and reward-based learning (produced in the substantia nigra).
- **GABA:** The primary inhibitory neurotransmitter used to control motor pathways.

## Clinical Links -
- **Parkinson’s Disease:** Caused by the death of [[Dopamine]]-producing neurons in the substantia nigra. Symptoms include tremors, rigidity, and difficulty starting movements.
- **Huntington’s Disease:** A genetic disorder causing the degeneration of the basal ganglia, leading to uncontrolled, "dancing" movements (chorea).
