## Location
- Medial aspect of the [[Temporal Lobe]].
- Forms a prominent component of the **Limbic System**.
- Located directly adjacent to the [[Amygdala]].

## Structure
- Characteristic seahorse-shaped curved allocortex structure:
  - **Cornu Ammonis (CA1 - CA4)**: Principal pyramidal cell layers mediating memory circuit routing and long-term potentiation (LTP).
  - **Dentate Gyrus**: Granule cell layer; one of the few confirmed regions of persistent **adult neurogenesis** in the human brain.
  - **Subiculum**: Primary efferent output relay projecting to neocortical areas and the entorhinal cortex.

## Neural Pathways
- **Trisynaptic Circuit**:
  - Perforant path: Entorhinal cortex -> Dentate gyrus.
  - Mossy fibre pathway: Dentate gyrus -> CA3 pyramidal cells.
  - Schaffer collateral pathway: CA3 -> CA1 pyramidal cells.
- **Efferent Projections**:
  - Projections via fornix to mammillary bodies and [[Thalamus]].
  - Reciprocal connections with the [[Amygdala]] for emotional memory contextualisation.
  - Neocortical projections for long-term distributed memory storage.

## Functions
- **Long-Term Memory Encoding**: Essential for the consolidation of episodic events and semantic knowledge into durable neocortical storage.
- **Memory Consolidation**: Replays neural firing patterns during slow-wave sleep to stabilize memory traces.
- **Spatial Navigation**: Contains specialized 'place cells' that generate internal cognitive spatial maps of the physical environment.
- **Context-Dependent Retrieval**: Reconstructs environmental cues necessary for accurate recollection.

## Neurotransmitters and Hormones
- **Glutamate**: Powers NMDA receptor-dependent long-term potentiation (LTP), the cellular mechanism of learning.
- **Acetylcholine**: Modulates attention, encoding efficiency, and prevents interference between stored memories.
- **Cortisol**: High concentrations of glucocorticoid receptors make the hippocampus vulnerable to chronic stress-induced atrophy.

## Clinical Links
- **Anterograde Amnesia**: Bilateral ablation or trauma eliminates the capacity to form new declarative memories (e.g. Case Study **H.M. / Henry Molaison**).
- **Alzheimer’s Disease**: Early selective neurofibrillary tangle deposition causes profound memory loss and spatial disorientation.
- **Structural Plasticity**: **Maguire et al. (2000)** demonstrated that London licensed taxi drivers exhibit significantly increased posterior hippocampal volume correlated with navigation experience.
