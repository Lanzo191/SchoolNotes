### Description
- **Positron Emission Tomography** (PET)
- A functional imaging technique that shows **metabolic activity** in the brain.
- Often used to detect cancer or examine conditions like Alzheimer's.

### How it Works
- A patient is injected with a **radioactive tracer** (usually a form of [[Glucose]], like FDG).
- Active brain areas use more [[Glucose]] for energy.
- The tracer accumulates in these active areas.
- As the tracer decays, it emits **positrons**, which collide with electrons to produce gamma rays.
- The scanner detects these rays and produces a colour-coded image ("hot" colours like red/yellow indicate high activity; "cold" colours like blue indicate low activity).

### Strengths
- **Shows Function:** Can reveal how the brain is working, not just what it looks like.
- **Medical Diagnosis:** Valuable for detecting tumours (which have high metabolic rates) and tracking disease progression.

### Weaknesses
- **Invasive:** Requires injection of a radioactive substance.
- **Radiation Risk:** Limits the number of scans a patient can undergo.
- **Low Temporal Resolution:** Does not track rapid changes in brain activity (slower than [[FMRI]]).
- **Low Spatial Resolution:** Images are less detailed/blurrier than [[MRI]] or [[FMRI]].
- **Expensive:** Requires on-site cyclotron to produce tracers.
