**Electroencephalography (EEG)** is a non-invasive method used to record electrical activity of the brain.

## How it Works
- **Electrodes**: A series of electrodes are attached to the scalp using a conductive gel.
- **Electrical Signals**: These electrodes detect tiny electrical charges resulting from the activity of millions of **neurons** in the brain.
- **Brain Waves**: The signals are amplified and graphed as **brain waves** (e.g., Alpha, Beta, Theta, and Delta waves).

---

## Uses
1. **Sleep Research**: Identifying the different stages of sleep (e.g., REM sleep vs. Deep sleep) based on brain wave patterns.
2. **Epilepsy Diagnosis**: Detecting the abnormal "spikes" in electrical activity that characterise seizures.
3. **Cognitive Research**: Studying how the brain responds to specific stimuli (known as **Event-Related Potentials** or ERPs).

---

## Strengths and Weaknesses

| Strengths                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | Weaknesses                                                                                                                               |
| :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **High Temporal Resolution**: Can detect brain activity in real-time (to the millisecond).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | **Low Spatial Resolution**: Cannot tell exactly *where* in the brain the activity is happening, only that it is happening.               |
| **Non-Invasive and Safe**: Unlike [[PET]] scans, it does not involve radiation or injections.                                                                                                                                    | **Surface Only**: Can only detect activity in the outer layers of the brain (the cortex), not deep structures like the **Hypothalamus**. |
| **Relatively Inexpensive**: Compared to [[FMRI]] or [[MRI]], EEG equipment is cheaper and more portable. | **Artifacts**: Sensitive to interference from movement (e.g., eye blinks or muscle tension).                                             |

Sensor Placement 

Frontal Lobe - Monitoring cognition, speech, conscious control 
Temporal Lobe (side) - Linked to sound processing, memory, language 
Parietal (Upper Sides) - Touch proprioception 
Occipital (Back) - Vision and visual disturbances
