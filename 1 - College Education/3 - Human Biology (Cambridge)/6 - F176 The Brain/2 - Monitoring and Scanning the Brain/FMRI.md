### Description
- **Functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging** (fMRI)
- A brain scanning technique that measures brain activity by detecting changes associated with blood flow.
- Unlike standard [[MRI]] (which shows structure), fMRI shows **function**.

### How it Works
- Relies on the **BOLD response** (Blood Oxygenation Level Dependent).
- When a brain area is more active, it consumes more oxygen.
- Blood flow increases to the active area (haemodynamic response).
- Oxygenated haemoglobin has different magnetic qualities than deoxygenated haemoglobin.
- The fMRI scanner detects these magnetic differences to create a 3D map of activation.

### Strengths
- **Non-invasive:** Does not use radiation (unlike [[PET]]) or surgery.
- **High Spatial Resolution:** Can show detail to the millimetre, pinpointing exactly where activity is occurring.
- **Standard Procedure:** Widely used in research for mapping brain function.

### Weaknesses
- **Low Temporal Resolution:** There is a 5-second time lag (haemodynamic lag) between neuronal activity and the image on the screen.
- **Expensive:** Scanners are costly to buy and maintain.
- **Movement Issues:** Patient must stay perfectly still; even slight movement creates artefacts.
