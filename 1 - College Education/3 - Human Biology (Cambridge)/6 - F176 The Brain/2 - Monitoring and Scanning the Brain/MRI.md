### Description
- **Magnetic Resonance Imaging** (MRI)
- A technique used to produce high-quality, detailed **structural** images of the brain.
- Used to detect tumours, structural abnormalities, and extent of damage (e.g., after stroke).

### How it Works
- Uses a powerful **magnetic field** and **radio waves**.
- The magnetic field aligns the protons in the hydrogen atoms of the body's water molecules.
- Radio waves knock these protons out of alignment.
- When the radio waves stop, the protons realign and emit signals.
- Different tissues (grey matter, white matter, fluid) emit different signals, which a computer constructs into a 3D image.

### Strengths
- **High Spatial Resolution:** Provides extremely detailed images of brain structure.
- **Non-invasive:** No radiation exposure (unlike X-rays or [[PET]] scans).
- **Diagnostic Power:** Excellent for identifying physical anomalies.

### Weaknesses
- **No Functional Information:** Only shows structure, not activity/function (unlike [[FMRI]] or [[PET]]).
- **Expensive:** High cost of equipment and operation.
- **Uncomfortable:** Patients must lie in a narrow, noisy tube; problematic for claustrophobia.
- **Magnetic Safety:** Cannot be used on patients with metal implants (pacemakers, screws).
