## Overview
Titration is a **quantitative analytical technique** used to determine the concentration of a solution by reacting it with a solution of known concentration. It is based on **stoichiometry**, where the volume of titrant required to reach the reaction endpoint indicates the analyte concentration.

In biomedical science, titrations are used to analyse substances such as **acids, bases, ions, vitamins, and metabolites** in biological samples.

## Principle
A titration involves a **controlled chemical reaction** between two solutions:
- The **titrant**: solution of known concentration (in the burette).
- The **analyte**: solution of unknown concentration (in the conical flask).

An **indicator** or instrument detects the **endpoint**, where the reaction is complete.

## Procedure
1. Rinse all apparatus with the solutions to be used.  
2. Fill the burette with the titrant and record the initial volume.  
3. Pipette a measured volume of analyte into a conical flask.  
4. Add a few drops of a suitable **indicator** (e.g., phenolphthalein, methyl orange).  
5. Slowly add titrant from the burette while swirling until the **endpoint colour change** occurs.  
6. Record the final burette reading.  
7. Repeat until **concordant results** (within 0.1 cm³) are obtained.  
8. Calculate the concentration using:
   - **C₁V₁ = C₂V₂**

## Example Applications
- Determining **vitamin C (ascorbic acid)** concentration in fruit juice using iodine titration.  
- Measuring **lactic acid** in blood or [[Muscle Tissue]].  
- Determining **[[Calcium]] or magnesium ion** concentration in plasma (EDTA titration).  
- Acid–base titrations for **urine or gastric acid** analysis.

## Notes
- Read the meniscus at eye level.  
- Avoid air bubbles in the burette.  
- Perform a rough titration first to estimate the endpoint.  
- Record all readings to two decimal places.  
- Calculate mean titre from concordant results.

## Safety
- Wear eye protection and gloves.  
- Dispose of chemicals appropriately.  
- Neutralise acidic or basic waste before disposal.
