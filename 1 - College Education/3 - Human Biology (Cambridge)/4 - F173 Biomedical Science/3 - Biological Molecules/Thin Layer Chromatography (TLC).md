Thin Layer Chromatography (TLC) -  
Thin Layer Chromatography is an **analytical technique** used to **separate mixtures** of non-volatile substances, often used in biochemistry to identify [[Amino Acids]], pigments, or drugs.

### Principle -  
TLC separates compounds based on their **relative affinities** for the **stationary phase** (a thin layer of silica gel or alumina) and the **mobile phase** (a solvent).  
Different components move at different rates depending on **polarity** and **solubility**.

### Process -  
1. A thin plate coated with **silica gel** or **alumina** acts as the stationary phase.  
2. The sample is spotted near the bottom of the plate.  
3. The plate is placed upright in a solvent; as the solvent rises, it carries sample molecules upward.  
4. Compounds separate into distinct spots based on how strongly they adhere to the stationary phase.

### Rf Value -  
Each compound can be identified by its **Rf (retention factor)** value:  

$Rf = \frac{\text{Distance travelled by substance}}{\text{Distance travelled by solvent front}}$


### Applications -  
- Identification of [[Amino Acids]], lipids, or drugs.  
- Checking purity of a sample.  
- Quick, inexpensive qualitative analysis.

### Advantages -  
- Simple and fast.  
- Requires very small sample volumes.  
- Can run multiple samples simultaneously.

### Limitations -  
- Not suitable for volatile substances.  
- Qualitative rather than quantitative.
