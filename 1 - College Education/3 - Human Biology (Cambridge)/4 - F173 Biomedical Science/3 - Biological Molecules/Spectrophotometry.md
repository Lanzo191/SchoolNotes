## Overview
Spectrophotometry is an advanced form of [[Colorimetry]] that measures **light absorbance or transmission** across a range of **wavelengths** to determine the concentration and purity of substances. It is commonly used in biomedical and biochemical laboratories for **quantitative analysis** of biomolecules.

## Principle
A **spectrophotometer** emits light across a spectrum and measures how much light is absorbed by a sample at a specific wavelength.  
According to the **Beer–Lambert Law**, absorbance (A) is directly proportional to concentration (c) and path length (l):

A = ε × c × l

Different substances absorb light at characteristic wavelengths, allowing **identification and quantification**.

## Procedure
1. Turn on the spectrophotometer and allow it to warm up.  
2. Select the **wavelength** appropriate for the substance being measured.  
3. Use a **blank** to zero the instrument.  
4. Insert the sample cuvette and record the **absorbance or transmittance**.  
5. Compare absorbance values to a **standard curve** or known values.  
6. Optionally, record the **absorbance spectrum** to identify unknown compounds.

## Applications
- Determining **[[DNA]] or [[RNA]] concentration** by measuring absorbance at 260 nm.  
- Assessing **protein purity** by A260/A280 ratio.  
- Measuring **bilirubin, cholesterol, and urea** in blood plasma.  
- Monitoring **enzyme-catalysed reactions** by observing absorbance changes over time.

## Advantages
- High accuracy and sensitivity.  
- Can identify and quantify multiple compounds.  
- Useful for colourless solutions (UV range).  

## Notes
- Use matched cuvettes for reference and sample.  
- Avoid fingerprints and bubbles.  
- Ensure the sample is homogeneous and not turbid.
