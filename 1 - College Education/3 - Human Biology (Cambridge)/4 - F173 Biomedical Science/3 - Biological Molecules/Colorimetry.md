
## Overview
Colorimetry is a **quantitative analytical technique** used to determine the concentration of a coloured compound in solution by measuring the amount of light absorbed at a specific wavelength. It is widely used in biomedical science for analysing biological molecules such as [[Glucose]], [[Proteins]], and enzymes.

## Principle
The technique is based on the **Beer–Lambert Law**, which states that the amount of light absorbed by a solution is directly proportional to the concentration of the absorbing substance and the path length of the cuvette.

**Beer–Lambert Law:**  
A = ε × c × l  
Where:  
- **A** = Absorbance  
- **ε** = Molar absorptivity constant  
- **c** = Concentration of the solution  
- **l** = Path length of the cuvette (usually 1 cm)

## Procedure
1. Prepare a series of **standard solutions** of known concentrations.  
2. Select the correct **colour filter** or wavelength for the compound being tested.  
3. **Zero the colorimeter** using a blank (distilled water or reagent blank).  
4. Measure the **absorbance of each standard** and record the results.  
5. **Plot a calibration curve** of absorbance against concentration.  
6. Measure the **absorbance of the unknown sample**.  
7. Use the calibration curve to **determine its concentration**.

## Applications
- Determining **blood [[Glucose]] concentration** using the [[Glucose]] oxidase (GOD-POD) assay.  
- Measuring **protein concentration** using the Biuret or Bradford test.  
- Assessing **enzyme activity** by tracking colour change over time.  
- Estimating **haemoglobin concentration** in blood samples.

## Notes
- Always use clean cuvettes and handle them by the frosted sides.  
- Ensure consistent path length and reagent volumes.  
- Avoid bubbles and turbidity, which interfere with light transmission.
