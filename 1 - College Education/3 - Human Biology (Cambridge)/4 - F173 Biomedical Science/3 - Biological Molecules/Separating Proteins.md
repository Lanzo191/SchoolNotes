#Biology #Genetics #MolecularBiology #ProteinSeparation


[[Proteins]] are separated in the lab to **analyse their structure, function, or composition**.  
Separation is based on **size, charge, solubility, or affinity**.

### Methods of Protein Separation -  

- **[[Electrophoresis|Gel Electrophoresis]]** -  
  [[Proteins]] are denatured and coated with SDS ([[Sodium]] dodecyl sulfate) to give them a uniform negative charge.  
  They are then separated in a **polyacrylamide gel** based on **size**.  
  Smaller [[Proteins]] move faster through the gel than larger ones.

- **[[Paper Chromatography Separation Technique|Chromatography]] -**  
  [[Proteins]] can be separated based on **different properties**:  
  - **Ion Exchange Chromatography:** Separation by charge.  
  - **Gel Filtration (Size-Exclusion) Chromatography:** Separation by size.  
  - **Affinity Chromatography:** Separation by binding affinity to a specific ligand.  

- **Centrifugation -**  
  [[Proteins]] can be partially separated based on **density** by spinning samples at high speed.  
  Often used to isolate [[Proteins]] from cell fractions.

- **Isoelectric Focusing -**  
  [[Proteins]] are separated according to their **isoelectric point (pI)**, the pH at which a protein has no net charge.

### Key Points -  
- Protein separation is usually followed by **detection methods** such as **staining, Western [[Blotting]], or enzyme assays**.  
- Choosing the method depends on the **property of the protein** and the **purpose of the analysis**.  
- Often, **multiple techniques** are combined to purify a protein completely.

### Applications -  
- Studying protein structure and function.  
- Diagnosing diseases (e.g., detecting abnormal [[Proteins]] in blood).  
- Research in **biochemistry, molecular biology, and biotechnology**.  
- Preparing [[Proteins]] for **further analysis**, such as mass spectrometry or enzymatic assays.
