#Biology #Chemistry #Chromatography #AnalyticalTechniques

Paper Chromatography is a **simple separation technique** used to separate and identify components of a mixture (commonly pigments, inks, or [[Amino Acids]]).

### Principle -  
It relies on the **partition** of substances between two phases:  
- **Stationary phase:** Water molecules bound to the cellulose fibers in the paper.  
- **Mobile phase:** A solvent (e.g., ethanol or water) that moves up the paper by capillary action.  

The solutes move at different rates depending on their **solubility** in the solvent and **affinity** for the paper.

### Process -  
1. A drop of sample is placed on the chromatography paper.  
2. The bottom of the paper is placed in the solvent.  
3. The solvent travels up by capillary action, carrying components with it.  
4. Substances separate into bands or spots according to solubility and attraction.

### Rf Value -  
Each compound produces a characteristic **Rf value**:  

$Rf = \frac{\text{Distance moved by substance}}{\text{Distance moved by solvent front}}$


### Applications -  
- Separation of pigments and dyes.  
- Identification of [[Amino Acids]] in mixtures.  
- Educational and preliminary analytical uses.

### Advantages -  
- Inexpensive and easy to perform.  
- Requires minimal equipment.  
- Environmentally friendly.

### Limitations -  
- Less precise than TLC or HPLC.  
- Limited quantitative capability.  
- Slow separation process.
