HPLC is an advanced **analytical technique** used to **separate, identify, and quantify components** in complex mixtures.  
It is more precise and efficient than paper or thin layer chromatography.

### Principle -  
HPLC separates compounds based on their **interactions between a liquid mobile phase and a solid stationary phase** under **high pressure**.  
The use of pressure allows for faster flow rates, finer particles in the column, and **greater resolution**.

### Process -  
1. The sample is dissolved in a solvent and injected into the HPLC system.  
2. A **high-pressure pump** forces the mobile phase through a **chromatographic column** containing the stationary phase.  
3. Different compounds interact differently with the stationary phase and thus **elute (exit the column)** at different times - called the **retention time**.  
4. A **detector** measures the concentration of each component as it leaves the column.

### Types of HPLC -  
- **Normal-phase HPLC -** Polar stationary phase, non-polar mobile phase.  
- **Reverse-phase HPLC -** Non-polar stationary phase, polar mobile phase (most common).  

### Applications -  
- Drug testing and pharmaceutical analysis.  
- Food and beverage quality control.  
- Clinical diagnostics.  
- Environmental analysis (e.g., pollutants, toxins).

### Advantages -  
- High accuracy and reproducibility.  
- Quantitative as well as qualitative analysis.  
- Works with complex or sensitive samples.

### Limitations -  
- Expensive equipment and solvents.  
- Requires trained operators.  
- Not suitable for all sample types.
