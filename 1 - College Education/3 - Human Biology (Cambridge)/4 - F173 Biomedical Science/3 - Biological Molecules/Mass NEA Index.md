# Common Diseases and Diagnostic Tests

This note summarises common human diseases and disorders relevant to biomedical testing, including:
- What each condition is  
- How it is detected or monitored  
- Which tests are used  
- Typical normal ranges and what abnormalities indicate


## 1. Diabetes Mellitus

### Overview
A metabolic disorder where the body cannot properly regulate blood [[Glucose]] due to:
- [[Insulin]] deficiency (Type 1)
- [[Insulin]] resistance (Type 2)

### Tests Used
| Test | Normal Range | Diagnostic Indication |
|------|---------------|-----------------------|
| Fasting Blood Glucose | 3.9–5.5 mmol/L | >7.0 mmol/L = Diabetes |
| Random Glucose | <7.8 mmol/L | >11.1 mmol/L = Diabetes |
| HbA1c (Glycated Haemoglobin) | <42 mmol/mol | ≥48 mmol/mol = Diabetes |
| Urinalysis – Glucose | Negative | Positive = Hyperglycaemia |
| Urinalysis – Ketones | Negative | Positive = Ketoacidosis |

### Biomedical Basis
- Glucose cannot enter cells effectively.
- Blood sugar rises, and kidneys excrete excess glucose and ketones.



## 2. Anaemia

### Overview
A condition where the blood has too few red blood cells or insufficient haemoglobin.

### Tests Used
| Test | Normal Range | Abnormal Findings |
|------|---------------|------------------|
| Haemoglobin (Hb) | 120–160 g/L (females), 130–180 g/L (males) | Low = Anaemia |
| RBC Count | 4.2–5.4 ×10¹²/L | Low = Anaemia |
| Haematocrit | 36–50% | Low = Anaemia |
| MCV (Mean Cell Volume) | 80–100 fL | Low = Iron deficiency |
| Serum Iron | 10–30 µmol/L | Low = Iron deficiency |

### Causes
- Iron deficiency  
- Blood loss  
- Vitamin B12 or folate deficiency  
- Chronic illness  


## 3. Liver Disease (e.g. Hepatitis, Cirrhosis)

### Overview
Damage to liver cells or obstruction of bile ducts affects detoxification and metabolism.

### Tests Used
| Test | Normal Range | Abnormal Indications |
|------|---------------|----------------------|
| ALT (Alanine Transaminase) | 7–56 U/L | High = Liver cell damage |
| AST (Aspartate Transaminase) | 10–40 U/L | High = Liver/muscle damage |
| Bilirubin | <21 µmol/L | High = Jaundice, obstruction |
| Albumin | 35–50 g/L | Low = Poor liver synthesis |
| ALP (Alkaline Phosphatase) | 44–147 U/L | High = Bile duct blockage |
| Urinalysis – Bilirubin | Negative | Positive = Liver dysfunction |

### Biomedical Basis
- Hepatocyte damage causes leakage of enzymes.
- Reduced synthesis of proteins like albumin.
- Impaired bilirubin processing causes jaundice.



## 4. Kidney Disease (Renal Impairment)

### Overview
Occurs when kidneys fail to filter waste efficiently.

### Tests Used
| Test                              | Normal Range       | Abnormal Indications         |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------ | ---------------------------- |
| Urea                              | 2.5–7.1 mmol/L     | High = Impaired filtration   |
| Creatinine                        | 45–120 µmol/L      | High = Renal dysfunction     |
| eGFR (Glomerular Filtration Rate) | >90 mL/min         | <60 = Chronic kidney disease |
| Electrolytes (Na⁺, K⁺, Cl⁻)       | Na: 135–145 mmol/L | Abnormal = Fluid imbalance   |
| Urinalysis – Protein              | Negative           | Positive = Kidney damage     |
| Urinalysis – Blood                | Negative           | Positive = Glomerular damage |

### Biomedical Basis
- Glomeruli fail to remove urea and creatinine effectively.
- Protein and blood may leak into urine.



## 5. Urinary Tract Infection (UTI)

### Overview
Bacterial infection of the urinary system (bladder, urethra, kidneys).

### Tests Used
| Test                    | Normal Range | Abnormal Indications           |
| ----------------------- | ------------ | ------------------------------ |
| Urinalysis – Leukocytes | Negative     | Positive = WBC presence        |
| Urinalysis – Nitrites   | Negative     | Positive = Bacterial activity  |
| Urinalysis – pH         | 4.5–8.0      | High pH = Infection            |
| Microscopy              | Few bacteria | Many bacteria/WBCs = Infection |
| Culture                 | No growth    | Pathogen identified            |

### Biomedical Basis
- Bacteria enter and multiply in the urinary tract, causing inflammation.
- WBCs increase and nitrites appear as bacteria metabolise nitrates.



## 6. Cardiovascular Disease

### Overview
Includes conditions like atherosclerosis, coronary artery disease, and hypertension.

### Tests Used
| Test | Normal Range | Abnormal Indications |
|------|---------------|----------------------|
| Total Cholesterol | <5.0 mmol/L | High = Risk of heart disease |
| LDL Cholesterol | <3.0 mmol/L | High = Atherosclerosis |
| HDL Cholesterol | >1.0 mmol/L | Low = Increased risk |
| Triglycerides | <1.7 mmol/L | High = Metabolic syndrome |
| Blood Pressure | 120/80 mmHg | >140/90 = Hypertension |
| ECG | Normal rhythm | Abnormal waves = Heart dysfunction |

### Biomedical Basis
- High LDL and triglycerides cause plaque formation.
- Hypertension increases cardiac workload.



## 7. Infection and Inflammation

### Overview
Caused by bacteria, viruses, or autoimmune response.

### Tests Used
| Test | Normal Range | Abnormal Indications |
|------|---------------|----------------------|
| WBC Count | 4–11 ×10⁹/L | High = Infection or inflammation |
| Neutrophils | 2–7.5 ×10⁹/L | High = Bacterial infection |
| Lymphocytes | 1–4 ×10⁹/L | High = Viral infection |
| C-Reactive Protein (CRP) | <10 mg/L | High = Acute inflammation |
| ESR (Erythrocyte Sedimentation Rate) | <20 mm/hr | High = Inflammation |

### Biomedical Basis
- Immune system produces more white cells and inflammatory proteins.



## 8. Thyroid Disorders

### Overview
Abnormal hormone secretion from the thyroid gland.

### Tests Used
| Test | Normal Range | Abnormal Indications |
|------|---------------|----------------------|
| TSH (Thyroid Stimulating Hormone) | 0.4–4.0 mU/L | High = Hypothyroidism; Low = Hyperthyroidism |
| Free T4 | 10–22 pmol/L | Low = Hypothyroid; High = Hyperthyroid |

### Biomedical Basis
- Thyroid hormones regulate metabolism.
- Imbalance causes weight, mood, and energy level changes.


## 9. Dehydration and Electrolyte Imbalance

### Overview
Caused by fluid loss, poor intake, or excessive sweating.

### Tests Used
| Test | Normal Range | Abnormal Indications |
|------|---------------|----------------------|
| Sodium (Na⁺) | 135–145 mmol/L | High = Dehydration |
| Potassium (K⁺) | 3.5–5.0 mmol/L | High/Low = Cardiac risk |
| Chloride (Cl⁻) | 98–107 mmol/L | Altered = Fluid imbalance |
| Urine Colour | Pale yellow | Dark = Dehydration |
| Urine Specific Gravity | 1.005–1.030 | High = Concentrated urine |

### Biomedical Basis
- Low water levels concentrate electrolytes and cause kidney compensation.

## 10. Cancer (Overview of Detection)

### Overview
Uncontrolled cell growth forming tumours that may metastasise.

### Tests Used
| Test | Purpose |
|------|----------|
| Microscopy (Cytology/Histology) | Identifies abnormal cell morphology |
| Biopsy | Tissue diagnosis |
| Blood Tests (e.g., PSA, CA markers) | Detect tumour markers |
| Imaging (CT, MRI, Ultrasound) | Locates tumours |
| Genetic Testing (PCR) | Identifies mutations |
