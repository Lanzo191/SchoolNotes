## Overview

**Biosensors** are analytical devices that combine a **biological component** with a **physicochemical detector** to measure the presence or concentration of a biological analyte (such as [[Glucose]], urea, or hormones).

They are widely used in **biomedical laboratories**, **clinical diagnostics**, and **environmental testing** because they provide **rapid, specific, and quantitative** results.

## Basic Components of a Biosensor

| Component | Function |
|------------|-----------|
| **Biological Recognition Element (Bioreceptor)** | Detects the specific analyte; examples include enzymes, antibodies, DNA, or cells. |
| **Transducer** | Converts the biological response into an electrical signal. |
| **Signal Processor / Display** | Amplifies, interprets, and displays the result in measurable form (e.g., concentration, digital readout). |

## Principle of Operation

1. **The analyte binds to or reacts with the biological element.**
2. **A measurable physical or chemical change occurs**, such as production of electrons, pH change, or light emission.
3. **The transducer detects this change** and converts it into an electrical signal.
4. **The processor interprets the signal** and displays it as a numerical value or curve.

Example:
- Glucose reacts with **glucose oxidase**, producing hydrogen peroxide and electrons.
- The electrons generate a measurable current proportional to glucose concentration.

## Types of Biosensors

### 1. Enzymatic Biosensors
Use enzymes as bioreceptors to catalyse reactions with the analyte.
- **Example:** Glucose oxidase in blood glucose meters.
- **Detects:** Glucose, urea, cholesterol.

**Advantages:** High specificity, fast response.  
**Disadvantages:** Limited enzyme lifespan, sensitive to pH and temperature.

### 2. Immunosensors
Use antibodies to detect specific antigens or biomarkers.
- **Example:** Lateral flow pregnancy test detecting hCG.
- **Detects:** Pathogens, hormones, drugs, disease markers.

**Advantages:** Extremely specific.  
**Disadvantages:** Antibody denaturation, high cost.

### 3. DNA (Genosensors)
Use complementary DNA strands to detect genetic sequences or mutations.
- **Example:** PCR-based biosensors for infectious diseases or genetic screening.

**Advantages:** High sensitivity, useful for genetic diagnostics.  
**Disadvantages:** Complex sample preparation.

### 4. Whole-Cell Biosensors
Use living cells or microorganisms that respond to specific substances.
- **Example:** Cells that produce a fluorescent signal in presence of toxins.

**Advantages:** Can detect a wide range of compounds.  
**Disadvantages:** Slower response, complex maintenance.

### 5. Optical Biosensors
Use light-based detection (absorbance, fluorescence, or luminescence).
- **Example:** Fluorescence-based immunoassays for detecting hormones or drugs.

**Advantages:** Non-invasive, high sensitivity.  
**Disadvantages:** Requires precise instrumentation.

### 6. Electrochemical Biosensors
Measure electrical signals resulting from a biochemical reaction.

Subtypes:
- **Amperometric:** Measures current (e.g., glucose sensors).  
- **Potentiometric:** Measures voltage change (e.g., ion-selective electrodes).  
- **Conductometric:** Measures conductivity changes.

**Advantages:** Portable, fast, widely used in point-of-care diagnostics.  
**Disadvantages:** May require frequent calibration.

## Example: Glucose Biosensor

**Principle:**
1. The enzyme **glucose oxidase (GOx)** catalyses:
   - Glucose + O₂ → Gluconic acid + H₂O₂  
2. The **H₂O₂** is oxidised at an electrode.
3. The current produced is **proportional to glucose concentration**.

**Applications:**
- Blood glucose monitoring (diabetes)
- Continuous glucose monitoring devices
- Laboratory glucose assays

**Typical Range:**  
- Normal blood glucose: 3.9–5.5 mmol/L (fasting)

## Applications of Biosensors in Biomedical Science

| Field | Example Use |
|--------|--------------|
| **Clinical Diagnostics** | Blood [[Glucose]], cholesterol, hormone levels, infection markers |
| **Pharmaceutical Research** | Drug detection, metabolism studies |
| **Environmental Monitoring** | Detecting toxins, pollutants, pathogens |
| **Food Industry** | Detecting spoilage bacteria or allergens |
| **Forensic Testing** | Drug screening, [[DNA]] analysis |

## Advantages of Biosensors

- High specificity and sensitivity  
- Rapid results  
- Can detect small analyte concentrations  
- Portable and user-friendly  
- Minimal sample preparation  
- Potential for continuous monitoring (e.g., implanted [[Glucose]] sensors)

## Limitations

- Limited lifespan of biological components  
- Need for regular calibration  
- Sensitivity to temperature, pH, and contamination  
- Potential for signal interference from other compounds

## Summary

Biosensors link biological detection with electronic measurement to produce rapid, specific, and quantifiable diagnostic data.  
They play a key role in **disease diagnosis**, **monitoring**, and **research**, especially in tests like **blood [[Glucose]] monitoring**, **hormone assays**, and **pathogen detection**.
