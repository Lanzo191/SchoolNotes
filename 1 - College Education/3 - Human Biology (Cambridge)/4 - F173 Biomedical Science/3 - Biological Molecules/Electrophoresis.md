Electrophoresis -  
Electrophoresis is a laboratory technique used to **separate charged molecules**, such as **[[DNA]], [[RNA]], or [[Proteins]]**, according to their **size and electrical charge**.  
It works by applying an electric current through a gel, causing the molecules to migrate at different speeds.

### Principle -  
- Molecules with a **negative charge** (like [[DNA]]) move towards the **positive electrode (anode)**.  
- The **gel** acts like a sieve, allowing **smaller fragments** to move faster and farther than larger ones.  
- This process separates fragments into visible **bands**, which can then be analysed.

### The Process -  
1. **Preparation -**  
   A gel (usually agarose for [[DNA]] or polyacrylamide for [[Proteins]]) is poured into a tray and wells are created using a comb.  

2. **Loading Samples -**  
   [[DNA]], [[RNA]], or protein samples are mixed with a loading dye and placed into the wells.  

3. **Applying Current -**  
   An electric field is applied; molecules migrate through the gel based on their charge and size.  

4. **Separation -**  
   Smaller fragments travel faster and farther, while larger fragments move more slowly.  

5. **Visualisation -**  
   The gel is stained (often with ethidium bromide or methylene blue) so that the separated bands can be seen and measured.

### Key Points -  
- The **rate of movement** depends on molecule size, charge, and gel concentration.  
- **Smaller fragments** move **faster**; **larger fragments** move **slower**.  
- Used primarily to **separate**, **identify**, and **compare** molecular fragments.  

### Applications -  
- **[[DNA]] analysis and genetic fingerprinting** (forensics, paternity tests).  
- **Protein separation** and purification.  
- **Checking results of genetic experiments** or enzyme digestion.  
- **Determining molecular size** and verifying sample purity.

### Summary -  
Electrophoresis provides a reliable way to separate biological molecules for analysis.  
By comparing the position of bands on a gel, scientists can determine the **relative sizes and characteristics** of [[DNA]], [[RNA]], or protein samples.
