
## Purpose
Urinalysis is used to detect and monitor:
- [[Kidney]] function
- Urinary tract infections (UTIs)
- [[Diabetes]]
- [[Liver]] disease
- Dehydration
- Metabolic disorders

---

## Sample Type
- **Fresh midstream urine sample**
- Collected in a **sterile, labelled container**
- Tested **within 2 hours** of collection (or refrigerated if delayed)


---

## Physical Examination

### Aim
To observe and record **visible properties** of urine.

### Equipment
- Urine sample  
- White background or tile  
- Measuring cylinder (for volume)

### Steps
1. **Label** the sample clearly.
2. **Observe colour** in natural light.  
   - Pale yellow = normal  
   - Dark = concentrated/dehydrated  
   - Red = blood (haematuria)  
   - Brown = bile pigments  
3. **Check clarity:**  
   - Clear = normal  
   - Cloudy = infection or crystals  
4. **Note odour:**  
   - Fruity = ketones (possible diabetes)  
   - Ammonia-like = old or bacterial contamination  
5. **Record approximate volume.**

### Observations Table
| Property | Normal Range | Abnormal Indications |
|-----------|---------------|----------------------|
| Colour | Pale–amber | Dark (dehydration), red (blood) |
| Clarity | Clear | Cloudy (infection) |
| Odour | Slight | Strong/foul (infection), fruity (ketones) |

## Chemical Analysis (Dipstick Test)

### Aim
To detect specific **chemical substances** in urine using reagent strips.

### Equipment
- Urine sample  
- Reagent dipsticks  
- Timer  
- Colour chart (provided with strips)  
- Gloves

### Steps
1. Mix the urine sample gently to ensure homogeneity.  
2. Remove a dipstick from the sealed container.  
3. **Dip the strip** into the urine briefly (1–2 seconds).  
4. **Remove excess urine** by tapping the edge of the strip against the container.  
5. **Wait** for the time indicated on the packet (usually 30–60 seconds).  
6. **Compare each reagent pad** against the colour chart.  
7. **Record results** accurately in a table.

### Parameters Tested and What They Indicate
| Parameter | Normal | Abnormal | Possible Cause |
|------------|---------|-----------|----------------|
| pH | 4.5–8.0 | High or low | Diet, infection |
| Protein | Negative | Positive | Kidney disease |
| Glucose | Negative | Positive | Diabetes |
| Ketones | Negative | Positive | Diabetes, fasting |
| Blood | Negative | Positive | Infection, trauma |
| Bilirubin | Negative | Positive | Liver disease |
| Nitrites | Negative | Positive | Bacterial infection |
| Leukocytes | Negative | Positive | UTI |

### Notes
- Always use **fresh strips** and store properly.  
- Compare results promptly-delays can cause colour fading.

## Microscopic Examination

### Aim
To identify **cells, crystals, casts, and microorganisms** under a microscope.

### Equipment
- Centrifuge  
- Centrifuge tubes  
- Microscope slides and coverslips  
- Pipette  
- Light microscope

### Steps
1. Pour **10 mL** of urine into a centrifuge tube.  
2. Centrifuge for **5 minutes at 1500–2000 rpm**.  
3. Carefully **decant the supernatant**, leaving ~0.5 mL of sediment.  
4. **Resuspend** the sediment by flicking or gentle mixing.  
5. Place **one drop** of sediment on a clean slide and cover with coverslip.  
6. Examine under **low power (×10)** and then **high power (×40)**.  
7. Record observations (cells, crystals, casts, bacteria).

### Common Findings
| Microscopic Finding | Interpretation |
|----------------------|----------------|
| RBCs | Bleeding from urinary tract |
| WBCs | Infection or inflammation |
| Epithelial cells | Normal shedding |
| Bacteria | Infection |
| Crystals | Kidney stones, pH imbalance |
| Casts | Kidney tubule damage |

---

## Common Conditions Diagnosed
| Finding | Possible Condition |
|----------|--------------------|
| Glucose + Ketones | Diabetes Mellitus |
| Protein + Blood | Kidney Disease |
| Leukocytes + Nitrates | Urinary Tract Infection |
| Bilirubin | Liver Disease |
| Crystals | Kidney Stones |

---

## Health & Safety
- Treat all urine as potentially infectious.  
- Wear **gloves and eye protection**.  
- Dispose of waste in **biohazard containers**.  
- Clean equipment with **disinfectant** after use.
