#Biology #Genetics #MolecularBiology #PCR 

PCR is a laboratory technique used to **amplify [[DNA]]**, meaning it produces **millions of identical copies** of a specific [[DNA]] sequence from a small sample.  
It is essential for analysing genetic material when only tiny amounts are available.

### Principle -  
PCR mimics the natural process of [[DNA]] replication but occurs **in vitro** (outside a cell).  
It relies on cycles of heating and cooling to **separate [[DNA]] strands**, **bind primers**, and **synthesise new strands** using a **heat-stable [[DNA]] polymerase**.

### The Process -  
1. **Denaturation -**  
   The [[DNA]] sample is heated to around **95°C**, breaking hydrogen bonds and separating the two strands of [[DNA]].

2. **Annealing -**  
   The temperature is lowered to around **50–65°C**, allowing **primers** (short [[DNA]] sequences) to bind (anneal) to the complementary regions on the target [[DNA]] strands.

3. **Extension -**  
   The temperature is raised to **72°C**, the optimum for **Taq polymerase**, which extends the primers and synthesizes new [[DNA]] strands.

4. **Cycle Repetition -**  
   These three steps are repeated **around 30–40 times**, doubling the amount of [[DNA]] each cycle, resulting in **exponential amplification**.

### Key Components -  
- **Template [[DNA]]** – The original [[DNA]] sample to be copied.  
- **Primers** – Short sequences that define the start and end points of replication.  
- **Taq Polymerase** – A heat-stable enzyme that synthesizes new [[DNA]].  
- **[[Nucleotides]] (dNTPs)** – The building blocks for new [[DNA]] strands.  
- **Buffer Solution** – Maintains optimal pH and conditions for enzyme activity.

### Applications -  
- **Forensic science** – [[DNA]] profiling from tiny biological samples.  
- **Medical diagnostics** – Detecting genetic disorders or pathogens.  
- **Research** – Cloning, sequencing, and studying [[Genes]].  
- **Evolutionary biology** – Analysing ancient or degraded [[DNA]] samples.

### Summary -  
PCR is a rapid, sensitive, and efficient method to **amplify specific [[DNA]] sequences**.  
Its precision and speed have made it a cornerstone technique in **molecular biology, medicine, and forensics**.
