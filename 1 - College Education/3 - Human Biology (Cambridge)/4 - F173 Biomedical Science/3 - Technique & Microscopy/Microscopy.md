
## Types of Microscopes

### 1. Light Microscope
- Uses visible light and lenses.
- Resolution: ~200 nm.
- Magnification: up to x1500–2000.
- Can view living cells.

### 2. Transmission Electron Microscope (TEM)
- Beam of electrons through specimen.
- Very high resolution: ~0.1 nm.
- High magnification (x1,000,000+).
- 2D images, specimen must be thin, dead, stained.

### 3. Scanning Electron Microscope (SEM)
- Beam of electrons scans the surface.
- Resolution: ~20 nm.
- Magnification: x100,000+.
- 3D images of surfaces, specimen must be dead.

---

## Comparing Microscopes

| Feature       | Light | TEM | SEM |
|---------------|-------|-----|-----|
| Radiation     | Light | Electrons | Electrons |
| Resolution    | ~200 nm | ~0.1 nm | ~20 nm |
| Magnification | ~x2000 | x1,000,000+ | x100,000+ |
| Image         | Colour (with stains) | Black/white, 2D | Black/white, 3D |
| Living cells  | Yes | No | No |

## Calculations: I = A × M

- **Equation**:  
  *Image size (I) = Actual size (A) × Magnification (M)*

- Rearranged:  
  - \( A = I ÷ M \)  
  - \( M = I ÷ A \)

---

## Worked Examples

1. **An animal cell measures 50 mm on a micrograph. The magnification is ×1000. What is the actual size?**  
   - \( A = I ÷ M \)  
   - \( A = 50 mm ÷ 1000 = 0.05 mm = 50 µm \)

2. **A bacterial cell is 2 µm in actual size. On a micrograph it is 40 mm. What is the magnification?**  
   - \( M = I ÷ A \)  
   - Convert units: 40 mm = 40,000 µm.  
   - \( M = 40,000 ÷ 2 = ×20,000 \)

---

## Conversions to Remember
- 1 mm = 1000 µm  
- 1 cm = 10000 µm 
- 1 µm = 1000 nm
