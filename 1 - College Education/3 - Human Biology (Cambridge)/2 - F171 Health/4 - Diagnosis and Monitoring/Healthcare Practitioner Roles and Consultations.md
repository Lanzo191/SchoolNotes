## Healthcare Practitioner Roles and Clinical Competencies

| Practitioner | Core Clinical Responsibilities | Current Established Competencies | Emerging and Digital Skills |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **General Practitioner (GP)** | First point of community healthcare contact; diagnoses acute and chronic illnesses; coordinates multidisciplinary care. | Broad diagnostic acumen across organ systems; empathetic communication; patient-centred risk assessment. | Remote telehealth consultations; integration of clinical decision support systems and algorithmic diagnostic tools. |
| **Registered Nurse** | Direct clinical care delivery; continuous vital signs monitoring; patient education and medication administration. | Aseptic wound dressing; intravenous cannulation and fluid management; acute triage and crisis de-escalation. | Advanced electronic health record management; genomic medicine literacy; coordinating complex community care plans. |
| **Consultant Pathologist** | Macroscopic and microscopic analysis of tissue biopsies, cells, and body fluids to establish definitive diagnoses. | High-precision light microscopy; histochemical and immunohistochemical interpretation; tumour staging. | Digital pathology whole-slide image analysis; implementation of machine-learning cellular identification algorithms. |
| **Diagnostic Radiographer** | Operation of medical imaging equipment (CT, MRI, X-ray, Ultrasound); ensuring radiation safety standards. | Precise patient positioning; spatial anatomical reasoning; radiation protection (ALARA principle). | Operation of hybrid scanners (PET-CT, PET-MRI); artificial intelligence preliminary triage tools; patient anxiety alleviation. |
| **Consultant Dermatologist** | Specialized diagnosis and management of cutaneous, hair, and nail disorders; malignant melanoma excision. | Clinical dermoscopy; targeted cryotherapy; minor surgical cutaneous excisions and biopsies. | Teledermatology high-resolution image triage; AI-assisted suspicious naevus risk stratification; biologic immunotherapies. |

## Clinical Consultation Layout and Patient Experience
- The physical layout of the clinical consulting room profoundly influences patient anxiety, communication transparency, and clinical diagnostic efficacy.

| Design Element | Optimal Clinical Configuration | Rationale and Patient Benefit |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Doctor-Patient Seating** | Seating arranged at an open 90-degree or 45-degree angle without an intervening desk. | Eliminates formal psychological barriers; fosters collaborative, empathetic communication. |
| **Computer Display Placement** | Display oriented partially towards the patient. | Allows transparency; patient can view diagnostic graphs, scan results, or medical records directly, reducing anxiety. |
| **Physical Examination Area** | Modesty curtain and private changing zone separated from the primary conversation area. | Preserves patient dignity, confidentiality, and comfort during invasive physical examinations. |
| **Environmental Distractions** | Soundproofed room, controlled ambient lighting, and absence of external clinical noise. | Maintains patient confidentiality under medical data protection regulations and aids acoustic auscultation. |
