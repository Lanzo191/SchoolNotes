**Cellular Respiration** is the process by which organic molecules (primarily [[Glucose]]) are broken down to release energy in the form of **[[ATP]]** (Adenosine Triphosphate).

## 1. Aerobic Respiration (With Oxygen)
Occurs in four main stages.

### Stage 1: Glycolysis
- **Location**: Cytoplasm.
- **Process**: [[Glucose]] (6C) is split into two molecules of **Pyruvate** (3C).
- **Net Gain**: 2 [[ATP]] and 2 Reduced NAD (NADH).
- *Note: This is the only stage that occurs in both aerobic and anaerobic respiration.*

### Stage 2: The Link Reaction
- **Location**: Mitochondrial Matrix.
- **Process**: Pyruvate is decarboxylated (loses CO2) and oxidized (loses Hydrogen to NAD) to form **Acetyl Coenzyme A** (Acetyl CoA).
- **Output**: 2 Acetyl CoA, 2 CO2, and 2 NADH (per [[Glucose]]).

### Stage 3: The Krebs Cycle (Citric Acid Cycle)
- **Location**: Mitochondrial Matrix.
- **Process**: Acetyl CoA enters a cycle of reactions.
- **Output**: 4 CO2, 2 [[ATP]], 6 NADH, and 2 Reduced FAD (FADH2) (per [[Glucose]]).

### Stage 4: Oxidative Phosphorylation (The Electron Transport Chain)
- **Location**: Inner Mitochondrial Membrane (Cristae).
- **Process**: NADH and FADH2 release their electrons and protons ($H^+$). Electrons move through a chain of [[Proteins]], releasing energy to pump protons into the intermembrane space.
- **[[ATP]] Production**: Protons flow back through **[[ATP]] Synthase**, generating roughly 28-32 [[ATP]].
- **The Role of Oxygen**: Oxygen is the **final electron acceptor**, combining with electrons and protons to form **Water** ($H_2O$).

---

## 2. Anaerobic Respiration (Without Oxygen)
- **Location**: Cytoplasm only.
- **Lactate Fermentation (Humans)**: Pyruvate is converted into **Lactate** (Lactic Acid). This regenerates NAD so that Glycolysis can continue to produce a small amount of ATP (2 ATP).
- **Oxygen Debt**: After exercise, oxygen is needed to break down the accumulated lactate in the liver.
