**Adenosine triphosphate (ATP)** is the primary energy carrier in all living cells. It stores and releases energy required for biological processes and is often referred to as the “molecular unit of currency” for cellular energy.

![[Pasted image 20251203130240.png]]
## Structure
- **Adenine**: Nitrogenous base that forms part of the nucleotide.  
- **Ribose**: A five-carbon sugar that links adenine to the phosphate chain.  
- **Three phosphate groups**: Linked by high-energy **phosphoanhydride bonds**, particularly between the second and third phosphate, which store large amounts of potential energy.  

## Function
- Provides **immediate energy** for cellular activities such as muscle contraction, nerve impulse transmission, [[Active Transport]] across membranes, and biosynthesis of macromolecules.  
- Transfers a phosphate group to molecules (phosphorylation), often activating them for metabolic reactions.  
- Acts as a **coenzyme** in some enzymatic reactions.  

## ATP Cycle
- ATP is formed from **ADP (adenosine diphosphate) and inorganic phosphate (Pi)** during **[[Cellular Respiration]]** (glycolysis, Krebs cycle, oxidative phosphorylation).  
- Energy from the breakdown of [[Glucose]], fatty acids, or [[Amino Acids]] is used to reattach Pi to ADP, regenerating ATP.  
- When ATP is hydrolysed to ADP + Pi, energy is released (~30.5 kJ/mol under standard conditions) for cellular work.  
- ATP can also be hydrolysed to AMP + 2Pi in energy-intensive reactions.  

## Cellular Role
- **Mechanical work**: Provides energy for muscle contraction and [[Cytoskeleton]] movement.  
- **Transport work**: Powers [[Active Transport]], such as [[Sodium]]-[[Potassium]] pumps and [[Endocytosis]]/[[Exocytosis]].  
- **Chemical work**: Drives endergonic reactions, including the synthesis of macromolecules.  
- **Signal transduction**: ATP is a substrate for kinases in phosphorylation cascades and acts as a precursor for cyclic AMP (cAMP), a secondary messenger.  

## Clinical/Physiological Relevance
- Low ATP levels in cells can result in **cellular dysfunction and death**, as seen in ischemia or [[Mitochondrial Diseases]].  
- Overproduction or imbalance of ATP/ADP ratios affects **muscle fatigue, neuronal function, and metabolic regulation**.  
- ATP is continually regenerated; the average human body recycles its body weight equivalent of ATP each day.  

## Summary
ATP is an essential energy molecule that couples energy-releasing processes (catabolism) to energy-requiring processes (anabolism). Its **high-energy phosphate bonds, rapid turnover, and versatility** make it critical for virtually every cellular function.
