**What are Amino Acids?**

Amino acids are the building blocks of [[Proteins]], which are long chains of amino acid molecules. There are 20 different amino acids that the human body uses to build [[Proteins]], and each one has a unique set of properties and functions.

 Here's where we get them from:

1. **Diet:** We obtain some amino acids through our diet, mainly from plant-based sources like:
	* Legumes (beans, lentils, peas)
	* Nuts and seeds
	* Whole grains
	* Fruits and vegetables

2. **[[Protein Synthesis]]:** Our bodies also produce amino acids through [[Protein Synthesis]], a process where cells use [[DNA]] instructions to create [[Proteins]] from smaller molecules.

3. **Microorganisms:** Some microorganisms in our gut [[Microbiome]] can break down food sources into individual amino acids, which are then absorbed by the body.

4. **Endogenous Production:** Our bodies have the ability to produce some amino acids internally, such as:
	* Glutamine (produced in the [[Small Intestine]])
	* Asparagine (produced in the [[Liver]])

Overall, our bodies rely on a combination of dietary and endogenous sources to obtain the necessary amino acids for [[Protein Synthesis]].

**Structure**

Amino acids have four main components:

* **Alpha carbon**: The central atom in an amino acid molecule.
* **Amino group (NH2)**: A functional group attached to the alpha carbon.
* **Carboxyl group (COOH)**: A functional group attached to the alpha carbon, which is responsible for forming peptide bonds with other amino acids.
* **R-group**: A unique side chain that varies depending on the specific amino acid.

**Properties**

Amino acids have several key properties:

* **pH-dependent charge**: The charge of an amino acid can change depending on the pH of its environment.
* **Hydrogen bonding**: Amino acids can form hydrogen bonds with other molecules, including water and other amino acids.
* **Solubility**: Amino acids are generally soluble in water.

**Functions**

Amino acids have several important functions:

* **[[Protein Synthesis]]**: Amino acids are the building blocks of [[Proteins]], which perform a wide range of biological functions.
* **Metabolism**: Amino acids can be used as energy sources or converted into other molecules that can be used by the body.
* **Signaling**: Some amino acids can act as signaling molecules, transmitting information from one part of a cell to another.
