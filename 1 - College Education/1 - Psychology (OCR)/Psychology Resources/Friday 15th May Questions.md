### Question 2
**Explain how you would conduct a study using a correlational technique to investigate if there is a relationship between someone's weight and their level of extroversion. Justify your decisions. (15 marks)**
*Include: sampling technique, operationalisation of variables, ethical considerations, and control of one extraneous variable.*

**Your Answer:**
- I would use opportunity sampling outside of an environment such as a hospital/doctors office, this would allow me to collect a sample in a cost effective way where running testing is already standard behaviour this allows for less mental harm and greater protection of participants There id ask the participants to sign a consent form that details the procedure (Standing on scales to record estimation of weight) after the weight is recorded a standardised personality test (out of 20) this is then repeated with all other participants. An ethical consideration would be the disclosure of sensitive info such as weight which is why the number would be rounded/placed into groups with no names being disclosed. 

**Grade/Feedback:**
*   **Mark:** 7/15
*   **Sampling (2/3):** Good choice of opportunity sampling, but a hospital may carry "social desirability bias" or be too sensitive.
*   **Operationalisation (2/4):** Good mention of the 20-point test. Need to be more technical about the weight measurement.
*   **Ethics (3/4):** Strong focus on confidentiality and consent.
*   **Control (0/4):** You missed the requirement to describe the control of an extraneous variable (e.g., standardising the time of day or clothing).


**Model Answer:**
I would use **opportunity sampling** by recruiting participants from a busy university campus library. This is a cost-effective and efficient way to gather a large sample of people likely to have a wide range of body types, I Would then use the first 30 participants.

To **operationalise the variables**, weight would be measured in kilograms (kg) using a set of calibrated scales. Participants would remove shoes and heavy outer clothing for accuracy. Extroversion would be measured using a **standardised 20-item Likert scale questionnaire** (0 to 20), where a higher score indicates higher social outgoingness.

An **ethical consideration** would be the **protection from harm**, specifically regarding self-esteem. I would ensure **confidentiality** by assigning participant numbers rather than names. Scales would be placed behind a screen for privacy, and participants would be reminded of their **right to withdraw**.

To **control an extraneous variable**, I would ensure all testing takes place in a **standardised environment** (the same quiet library room) at a similar time of day (12pm to 2pm). This prevents variables like noise or fatigue from affecting responses, ensuring the results are a valid reflection of true extroversion levels.
