### OCR Psychology (H567) Exam Overview

The A-Level consists of three papers, each 2 hours long.

| Component | Title | Marks | Weighting |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Component 1** | Research Methods | 90 | 33.3% |
| **Component 2** | Psychological Themes through Core Studies | 105 | 33.3% |
| **Component 3** | Applied Psychology | 105 | 33.3% |

---

### Component 1: Research Methods
Focuses on the "how" of psychology. You must be able to design your own study and calculate statistics.

*   **Section A: Multiple Choice (20 Marks)**
    *   Covers definitions, sampling, ethics, and types of data.
*   **Section B: Research Design and Response (35 Marks)**
    *   You are given a scenario and must design a study (e.g., a 15-mark correlation or experiment).
    *   *Example Question:* "Design a study using a self-report method to investigate people's attitudes toward social media."
*   **Section C: Data Analysis and Interpretation (35 Marks)**
    *   Interpreting graphs, calculating mean/median/mode, and using inferential statistics (e.g., Spearman's Rho).

---

### Component 2: Core Studies
Focuses on the 20 core studies (e.g., Milgram, Loftus & Palmer, Baron-Cohen).

*   **Section A: Core Studies (36 Marks)**
    *   Short-answer questions on specific details of the studies.
    *   *Example Question:* "Describe how the AS/HFA group was recruited in the Baron-Cohen study."
*   **Section B: Areas, Perspectives, and Debates (34 Marks)**
    *   Longer answers comparing studies or linking them to debates (Nature/Nurture, etc.).
    *   *Example Question:* "Compare Milgram’s study with Bocchiaro et al. in terms of the social area."
*   **Section C: Practical Applications (35 Marks)**
    *   Applying your knowledge to a novel scenario.
    *   *Example Question:* "Using your knowledge of psychology, suggest how a primary school could use social learning theory to improve behavior."

---

### Component 3: Applied Psychology
Focuses on "Issues in Mental Health" and two optional topics (usually Criminal and Child).

*   **Section A: Issues in Mental Health (40 Marks)**
    *   Compulsory section on diagnosis, explanations, and treatments for mental disorders.
    *   *Example Question:* "Discuss the biological explanation for depression."
*   **Section B: Options (65 Marks Total - 2x Topics)**
    *   Each topic has a 10-mark "Describe" question and a 15-mark "Evaluate/Discuss" question.
    *   *Example Question (Criminal):* "Discuss the effectiveness of collection of evidence from witnesses."

### Example Questions & Model Answers

#### Q1) Identify two changes made to the original "Eyes Test" for the Baron-Cohen et al. (2001) study. (2 marks)
**Model Answer:** One change was increasing the number of response options from two to **four words** for each set of eyes. A second change was increasing the total number of items in the test from 25 to **36 pairs of eyes** to improve the test's sensitivity.

---

#### Q2) Describe the "Social Area" of psychology and explain how Milgram’s study relates to it. (10 marks)
**Model Answer:**
The **Social Area** in psychology assumes that human behavior is primarily influenced by the actual, imagined, or implied presence of others. It focuses on how the **social context** and environment (rather than internal personality traits) dictate how an individual acts, particularly through social pressures like obedience or conformity.

Milgram’s (1963) study on obedience relates to the social area as it investigated how the presence of an **authority figure** could influence individuals to perform actions that went against their moral conscience. Milgram placed participants in a prestigious environment (Yale University) where a stern experimenter in a lab coat (the social context) gave direct orders to administer electric shocks to a learner. 

The results showed that 65% of participants obeyed to the maximum 450v level, demonstrating that the situational power of the authority figure was more influential than the participants' own internal empathy. This highlights the social area's core principle: that behavior is a product of **situational forces** and our interactions within a social hierarchy.

#### Q3) Design an experiment to investigate if listening to music while studying improves memory recall. (15 marks)
*Include: Independent Variable (IV), Dependent Variable (DV), Sampling Method, and Control of one Extraneous Variable.*

**Model Answer:**
I would conduct a **laboratory experiment** using an **independent measures design**. The **Independent Variable (IV)** would be the presence of music, with two levels: listening to classical music at a fixed volume (e.g., 40dB) or studying in complete silence. The **Dependent Variable (DV)** would be the memory recall score, measured as the number of words correctly recalled from a list of 20 abstract nouns after a 10-minute study period.

I would use **volunteer sampling** by placing an advertisement on a college noticeboard. This would provide a sample of students who are already accustomed to studying and would likely be motivated to participate.

To **control an extraneous variable**, I would ensure that the **word list** is identical for both groups and that the testing takes place in the same quiet room. This ensures that any difference in recall is due to the music (the IV) and not the difficulty of the task or environmental distractions like noise.

Finally, an **ethical consideration** would be the **right to withdraw**. I would inform all participants before the study began that they could leave at any time without giving a reason, ensuring they do not feel pressured by the researcher.
