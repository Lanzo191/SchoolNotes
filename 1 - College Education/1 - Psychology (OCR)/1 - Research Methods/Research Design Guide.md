#Education #OCR #Psychology #ResearchMethods

In the OCR Psychology exam, you will often be asked a **15-mark design/planning question** (e.g., "Design a study to investigate..."). This note provides a checklist of the key areas you must cover to score high marks.

## 1. The Core Design Elements
Whenever you design a study, you must clearly operationalise these four things:
- **Design**: Is it Independent Measures, Repeated Measures, or Matched Pairs? (Explain why).
- **IV ([[Independent Variable]])**: What are you changing? Define the two (or more) conditions.
- **DV ([[Dependent Variable]])**: What are you measuring? How *exactly* will you measure it? (e.g., "a score out of 20 on a memory test").
- **Sample**: Who are your participants? How many? How will you recruit them? (e.g., Random, Opportunity, or Self-Selection).

## 2. Procedure (The "How-To")
Give enough detail so that someone else could replicate your study:
- **Environment**: Where will it take place? (Lab, Field, etc.).
- **Materials**: What specific items do you need? (e.g., word lists, stopwatches, [[Questionnaires]]).
- **Standardised Instructions**: What will you tell the participants to ensure everyone has the same experience?

## 3. Controlling Variables (Improving Validity)
You MUST mention at least two things you will do to make your study "better":
- **Eliminating [[Extraneous Variables]]**: E.g., "The temperature in the room will be kept constant at 21°C."
- **Reducing [[Demand Characteristics]]**: E.g., "A single-blind procedure will be used so participants don't know the aim."
- **Counterbalancing**: (If using Repeated Measures) "Half the group will do Condition A first, the other half will do Condition B first to avoid order effects."

## 4. Assessing Quality (Improving Reliability)
- **Test-Retest**: "I will repeat the test with the same group two weeks later to check for consistency."
- **Inter-Rater Reliability**: (If observing) "Two observers will use the same coding system, and their results will be correlated."

## 5. Ethics
Mention how you will follow at least one BPS guideline (e.g., "Participants will be fully debriefed and given the right to withdraw their data").

---

## 15-Mark Structure Template
1.  **Aims & Hypothesis**: State the aim and a one-tailed/two-tailed hypothesis.
2.  **The Design**: Identify IV, DV, and Experimental Design.
3.  **The Sample**: Identify the target population and sampling method.
4.  **The Procedure**: Describe the step-by-step process.
5.  **Controls**: Explain how you will ensure validity/reliability.
6.  **Ethics**: Briefly mention ethical considerations.
