Confounding Variables -  
A type of extraneous variable that **systematically affects the DV**, meaning you cannot tell whether the results were caused by the IV or the confound.  
Confounds **invalidate** cause-and-effect conclusions.

### Strengths -  
- Identifying them improves the reliability of results.  
- Helps researchers refine and redesign studies.

### Weaknesses -  
- If present, the experiment becomes **invalid**.  
- Hard to identify until after data analysis.  
- Often subtle (e.g., researcher tone, unequal groups).

### Examples -  
- One condition tested in the morning, the other in the evening.  
- Age differences between groups in a memory test.  
- Using different experimenters for different groups.

### Starter Questions -  
- How do confounding variables differ from EVs?  
- Give an example of a confounding variable in a caffeine study.
