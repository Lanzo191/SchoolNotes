## Definition -
**Primary data** is information that is collected **first-hand** by the researcher specifically for the purpose of the current study. It is "new" information that did not exist before the research was conducted.

## Strengths -
*   **Control:** The researcher has complete control over the procedure and the way the data is collected, ensuring it fits the research aim perfectly.
*   **Relevance:** Because it is designed for the specific study, it is likely to be highly relevant and targeted.
*   **Up-to-Date:** The data is current and reflects the behaviour or opinions of the participants at the time of the study.

## Weaknesses -
*   **Costly:** Requires significant funding for materials, participant incentives, and researcher time.
*   **Time-Consuming:** The process of designing the study, recruiting participants, and collecting/analysing the data takes a long time.
*   **Logistics:** The researcher must handle all the planning and potential problems themselves.

## Examples - 
*   **Bandura (1961):** Recorded the specific aggressive acts of children in his experiment.
*   **[[Questionnaires]]:** Results from a survey you handed out to students in your school.
*   **[[Interviews]]:** Transcripts from a discussion you led with a focus group.
