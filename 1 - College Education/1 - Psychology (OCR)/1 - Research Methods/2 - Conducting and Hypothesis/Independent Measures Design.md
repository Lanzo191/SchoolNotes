## Overview -
**Independent Measures Design** (also called Independent Groups) involves using **different participants** for each condition of the experiment. If there are two conditions, the sample is split into two separate groups.

## Key Features -
*   **One Condition Only:** Each participant only experiences **one level** of the [[Independent Variable]] (IV).
*   **Comparison:** The researcher compares the mean scores of Group A against Group B.
*   **Random Allocation:** Essential to ensure groups are as similar as possible before the task begins.

## Strengths -
*   **No Order Effects:** Because participants only do one task, they don't get better through practice or worse through boredom/fatigue.
*   **Lower [[Demand Characteristics]]:** Participants are less likely to guess the aim because they don't see the "other side" of the experiment.
*   **Saves Time:** Both groups can often be tested at the same time.

## Weaknesses -
*   **[[Participant Variables]]:** Differences between the groups (e.g., one group being naturally smarter) may affect the DV, rather than the IV.
*   **Efficiency:** Requires **twice as many participants** as a [[Repeated Measures Design]] to get the same amount of data.
