## Overview -
A **pilot study** is a **small-scale trial run** of a piece of research. It is conducted **before** the main study to check that everything works as planned.

## Why Conduct a Pilot Study? -
*   **[[Standardisation]]:** To see if the instructions are clear and easy to follow.
*   **[[Operationalisation]]:** To check if the behavioural categories (in [[Observations]]) or questions (in surveys) are effective.
*   **Timing:** To find out how long the task takes.
*   **Equipment:** To ensure any technical tools (e.g., stopwatches, software) are functioning correctly.
*   **[[Ethics]]:** To see if the task is too distressing or difficult for participants.

## Strengths -
*   **Prevents Waste:** Saves time and money by identifying major flaws before the expensive main study begins.
*   **Increases [[Validity]]:** Allows the researcher to fix confusing questions or [[Confounding Variables]].
*   **Refinement:** Helps the researcher become more confident in their procedure.

## Weaknesses -
*   **Time-Consuming:** It adds an extra stage to the research process.
*   **Biased Results:** You cannot use the data from the pilot study in the final results because the participants may have been given different instructions or the procedure was changed.

## Core Study Example -
*   **[[Bocchiaro et al]]. (2012):** Conducted **8 pilot tests** with 92 participants to ensure the cover story was believable and the procedure was standardised.
