Independent Variable (IV) -  
The variable that the researcher **manipulates** to determine its effect on the [[Dependent Variable]]. It creates the **different conditions** of the experiment.

### Strengths -  
- Allows researchers to establish **[[Cause and Effect]]**.  
- Makes comparisons between conditions clear and measurable.  
- Can be systematically controlled, improving scientific accuracy.

### Weaknesses -  
- Only used in **experimental** research - not applicable in [[Correlations]] or [[Observations]].  
- Must be **precisely operationalised**, or results become meaningless.  
- Ethical or practical limits may restrict what can be manipulated (e.g., stress levels).

### Examples -  
- Amount of caffeine given (0mg vs 100mg).  
- Type of learning strategy (flashcards vs mind-maps).  
- Hours of sleep before a reaction test.

### Starter Questions -  
- What is the IV in a study testing if music volume affects concentration?  
- Why must the IV be operationalised?
