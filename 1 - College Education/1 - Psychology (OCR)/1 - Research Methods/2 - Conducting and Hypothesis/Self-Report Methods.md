**Self-report methods** involve participants providing information about themselves, their thoughts, feelings, or behaviours. The two main types are **[[Questionnaires]]** and **[[Interviews]]**.

## 1. Questionnaires
A set of pre-written questions.
- **Closed Questions**: Fixed response (e.g., Likert scales, semantic differential scales). Produces **[[Quantitative Data]].
- **Open Questions**: Participants answer in their own words. Produces **[[Qualitative Data]]**.

### Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**: 
    - Can collect large amounts of data quickly and cheaply.
    - Highly reliable as the same questions are asked to everyone.
    - Anonymity may lead to more honest answers on sensitive topics.
- **Weaknesses**:
    - **Social Desirability Bias**: Participants may lie to look good.
    - **Response Bias**: E.g., always ticking "yes" or the middle box without reading.
    - Closed questions lack depth and detail.

---

## 2. Interviews
Face-to-face or telephone conversations.
- **Structured**: Pre-set questions asked in a fixed order.
- **Unstructured**: Informal, conversation-like; questions are guided by the participant's answers.
- **Semi-Structured**: A mix of pre-set questions and freedom to follow up.

### Strengths and Weaknesses
- **Strengths**:
    - Unstructured interviews provide high-depth qualitative data.
    - Can clarify misunderstandings during the process.
- **Weaknesses**:
    - **Interviewer Effect**: The presence or characteristics of the interviewer (gender, age) can bias responses.
    - Time-consuming and expensive to train interviewers.

---

## Key Concepts for OCR
- **Rating Scales**: E.g., Likert Scale (Strongly Agree to Strongly Disagree).
- **Social Desirability**: A major threat to the **[[Validity]]** of self-reports.
