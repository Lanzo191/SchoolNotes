Experimental Design, Research Aims, and Goals -  
Experiments are structured investigations used to determine whether changes in one variable cause changes in another.  

### Research Aim -  
The research aim is the overall purpose of the study.  
It explains **what the researcher wants to find out** in a clear, concise way.  
Example: "To investigate the effect of sleep deprivation on memory recall."

### Goals of Research -  
The goals define what the study intends to achieve:  
- **Identify [[Cause and Effect]]** between variables.  
- **Control [[Variables In Experiments|extraneous variables]]** to ensure valid results.  
- **Provide reliable and replicable findings**.  
- **Generate knowledge that can be applied** to real-world situations.  
- **Test psychological theories** and refine understanding of human behaviour.  

### Experimental Design -  
Experimental design refers to **how participants are allocated** to different conditions and how the experiment is structured:  

- **Independent Groups Design -**  
  Each participant takes part in only one condition.  
  Strength: No order effects. Weakness: Individual differences may influence results.  

- **[[Repeated Measures Design]] -**  
  The same participants take part in all conditions.  
  Strength: Controls for individual differences. Weakness: Order effects, practice, or fatigue can occur.  

- **[[Matched Pairs Design]] -**  
  Participants are paired based on relevant characteristics, then each member of a pair is assigned to a different condition.  
  Strength: Reduces individual differences. Weakness: Time-consuming, matching may not be perfect.  

### Purpose -  
- To structure the experiment so the effect of the [[Independent Variable]] on the [[Dependent Variable]] can be clearly measured.  
- To maximise control while maintaining ethical and practical feasibility.  
- To allow valid and reliable analysis of results using statistical tests.
