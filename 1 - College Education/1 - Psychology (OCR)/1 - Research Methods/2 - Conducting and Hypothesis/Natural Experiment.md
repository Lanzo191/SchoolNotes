#Education #OCR #Psychology #ResearchMethods

## Overview -
In a **natural experiment**, the researcher **does not manipulate** the [[Independent Variable]] (IV). Instead, the IV occurs naturally (e.g., a change in the law, a natural disaster, or a child's upbringing). The researcher simply takes advantage of the situation to measure the effect on the DV.

## Key Features -
*   **Naturally Occurring IV:** The change would have happened anyway, even if the researcher wasn't there.
*   **High Realism:** The events studied are usually significant and real-life.
*   **Unique Situations:** Often used to study things that would be **unethical** to manipulate in a lab.

## Strengths -
*   **Ethical:** Allows researchers to study the effects of harmful things (e.g., deprivation or trauma) without actually causing the harm themselves.
*   **High Ecological [[Validity]]:** Involves real-life changes and natural behaviours.

## Weaknesses -
*   **No Control over IV:** Since the researcher didn't start the change, they can't be sure it was the IV that caused the effect on the DV.
*   **No Random Allocation:** People are not randomly assigned to groups, so **[[Participant Variables]]** (e.g., personality) might affect the results.
*   **Rare Opportunities:** Naturally occurring events of interest to psychologists don't happen often.

## Core Study Example -
*   **Genie Wiley:** While technically a Case Study, it functions like a natural experiment on the effects of extreme isolation (the "natural" event) on language development (the DV).
