## Overview -
A **Non-Directional Hypothesis** (also called a **Two-Tailed Hypothesis**) predicts that there will be a **significant difference or relationship**, but it does **not** specify which way the results will go.

## Key Features -
*   **Vague Wording:** Uses words like: *a difference, an effect, a relationship.*
*   **Statistical Test:** Requires a **Two-Tailed test**, which looks for significance at both ends of the data distribution (both "increase" and "decrease").

## When to Use a Non-Directional Hypothesis -
*   **No Previous Research:** Use this if the topic is new or the existing research is contradictory (some say A, some say B).
*   **Unpredictability:** When you genuinely don't know what to expect.

## Example -
"There will be a **significant difference** in the stress scores (measured on a scale of 1-10) of people who live in cities compared to those who live in rural areas."

## Strengths & Weaknesses -
*   **Strength:** It is "safer" because it covers you whether the results go up or down.
*   **Weakness:** It is less precise than a directional hypothesis.
