#Education #OCR #Psychology #ResearchMethods

## Overview -
A **field experiment** is carried out in a **natural, real-world setting** (e.g., a school, hospital, or street). The researcher still deliberately manipulates the [[Independent Variable]] (IV) and measures the [[Dependent Variable]] (DV), but the environment is not controlled.

## Key Features -
*   **Real-World Setting:** Participants are usually in their own environment.
*   **Unawareness:** Participants are often unaware they are part of a study (**Covert**), reducing unnatural behaviour.
*   **Partial Control:** The researcher manipulates the IV but cannot control many [[Extraneous Variables]] (e.g., the weather or who walks by).

## Strengths -
*   **High Ecological [[Validity]]:** Results are much more likely to reflect how people behave in real life.
*   **Low [[Demand Characteristics]]:** Because people don't know they are being studied, they won't change their behaviour to "please" the researcher.

## Weaknesses -
*   **[[Extraneous Variables]]:** It is difficult to control outside factors, which can lower the **Internal [[Validity]]**.
*   **Ethical Issues:** Often involves a lack of **Informed Consent** and can cause distress to members of the public.
*   **Hard to Replicate:** Since the environment is constantly changing, it's difficult for another researcher to repeat the study exactly.

## Core Study Example -
*   **Piliavin et al. (1969):** Conducted on an NYC subway train. They manipulated the type of victim (IV) to see the effect on helping (DV).
