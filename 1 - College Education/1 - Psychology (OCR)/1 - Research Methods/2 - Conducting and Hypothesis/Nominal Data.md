## Definition - 
**Nominal data** is the lowest level of measurement. It involves placing data into separate, named categories. There is no natural order or ranking between the categories.

## Strengths -
*   **Simplicity:** Very easy to collect and classify.
*   **Objectivity:** Categories are usually clear-cut (e.g., "Yes" or "No").
*   **Frequency:** Great for counting the frequency of occurrences.

## Weaknesses -
*   **Lack of Depth:** It is the least sensitive level of data; it doesn't show "how much" or "to what extent."
*   **Limited Analysis:** You cannot calculate a mean or standard deviation. The only measure of central tendency is the **Mode**.

## Statistical Applications -
*   **Central Tendency:** Mode.
*   **Statistical Test:** **[[Chi-Square]]** (used to see if there is an association between two nominal variables).

## Examples -
*   **Gender:** Male, Female, Non-binary.
*   **Choice:** Choosing between chocolate, vanilla, or strawberry.
*   **Yes/No answers:** In a survey.
