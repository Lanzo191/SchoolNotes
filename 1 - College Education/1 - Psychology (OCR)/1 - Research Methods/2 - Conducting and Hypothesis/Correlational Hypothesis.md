## Overview -
A **Correlational Hypothesis** is used when a researcher is looking for a **relationship or link** between two **Co-Variables**, rather than a difference between groups.

## Key Features -
*   **No IV or DV:** Instead, there are two **Co-Variables**.
*   **Wording:** Uses the word **"Relationship"** or **"Correlation"** instead of "Difference."
*   **Direction:** Can be positive, negative, or non-directional.

---

## Types of Correlational Hypotheses -

### 1. Positive Correlation (Directional)
*   Predicts that as one variable increases, the other also increases.
*   *Example:* "There will be a significant **positive correlation** between the number of hours spent revising and the score achieved on a psychology exam."

### 2. Negative Correlation (Directional)
*   Predicts that as one variable increases, the other decreases.
*   *Example:* "There will be a significant **negative correlation** between the number of hours spent playing video games and the number of hours spent sleeping."

### 3. Non-Directional Correlation
*   Predicts a link but doesn't say if it's positive or negative.
*   *Example:* "There will be a **significant relationship** between a person's stress levels and their daily caffeine intake."
