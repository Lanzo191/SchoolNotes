## Overview -
The **Alternate Hypothesis** (denoted as **H₁**) is a statement that predicts there **is a real effect, difference, or relationship** between the variables being studied. It is what the researcher expects to find.

## Key Features -
*   **Predictive:** It makes a claim about the outcome of the research.
*   **Opposes the Null:** It directly contradicts the **[[Null Hypothesis]]**.
*   **Statistical Testing:** If the [[P-Value]] is low (p ≤ 0.05), we **accept** the alternate hypothesis and **reject** the null.

---

## Types of Alternate Hypotheses -

1.  **Directional (One-Tailed):** Predicts exactly which way the results will go (e.g., "increase", "faster").
2.  **Non-Directional (Two-Tailed):** Predicts there will be a change, but not which way.

## Examples -
*   **Experimental:** "There will be a significant difference in the number of words recalled by participants who listen to music compared to those who work in silence."
*   **Correlational:** "There will be a significant relationship between the amount of coffee consumed and the heart rate of participants."
