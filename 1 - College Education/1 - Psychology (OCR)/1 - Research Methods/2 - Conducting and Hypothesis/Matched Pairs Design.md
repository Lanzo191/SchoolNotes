## Overview -
**Matched Pairs Design** is a sophisticated method where participants are paired together based on **specific characteristics** (e.g., age, gender, IQ). One member of the pair is then assigned to Condition A, and the other to Condition B.

## Key Features -
*   **Matching Process:** Requires a pre-test or background check to pair people up accurately.
*   **Split Assignment:** It is an [[Independent Measures Design]], but with groups that are "artificially" made similar.

## Strengths -
*   **Reduces [[Participant Variables]]:** By matching on the most important traits (e.g., matching reading ability in a study about literacy), the researcher minimises group differences.
*   **No Order Effects:** Like independent measures, participants only do one task, so they don't get bored or practiced.
*   **Lower [[Demand Characteristics]]:** Participants only see one condition.

## Weaknesses -
*   **Time-Consuming:** The matching process takes a lot of effort and often requires a [[Pilot Study]] or pre-test.
*   **Impossible to Match Perfectly:** Even identical twins have slight differences; you can never truly eliminate all [[Participant Variables]].
*   **Loss of Data:** If one person in a pair drops out, the data for their "partner" is also useless and must be removed.
