## Definition -
**Secondary data** is information that was **collected by someone else** for a purpose other than the current study. The researcher then re-analyses this existing data to support their own work.

## Strengths -
*   **Cost-Effective:** It is often free or very cheap to access existing records compared to conducting new research.
*   **Efficient:** Saves a massive amount of time as the data collection phase is already complete.
*   **Large Scale:** Allows researchers to access data that would be impossible to collect alone (e.g., nationwide crime statistics or hospital records).

## Weaknesses -
*   **Lack of Control:** The researcher cannot be sure how the original data was collected or if there were any biases or errors.
*   **Poor Fit:** The data may not perfectly match the current research question (e.g., the categories used in the original study might be different).
*   **Outdated:** Older data might not reflect current society or behaviours.

## Examples - 
*   **Meta-Analysis:** Combining the results of many existing studies to find a general trend (e.g., Levine et al. used some existing statistics for city characteristics).
*   **Official Statistics:** Using government records on employment, crime, or health.
*   **Journal Articles:** Using the findings section of another psychologist's published work.
