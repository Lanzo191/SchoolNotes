## Overview -
The **Null Hypothesis** (denoted as **H₀**) is a statement of **no effect**. It predicts that there is no real difference or relationship between the variables, and any observed results are due to **luck or chance**.

## Key Features -
*   **Default Position:** In science, we always start by assuming the Null is true.
*   **Aim of Research:** The researcher usually aims to **reject** the Null Hypothesis.
*   **Link to P-Values:** 
    *   If p ≤ 0.05: The chance of it being luck is 5% or less. We **Reject H₀**.
    *   If p > 0.05: The chance of it being luck is too high. We **Accept H₀**.

## Examples -
*   **Experimental:** "There will be **no significant difference** in the reaction times of participants who have slept 8 hours compared to those who have slept 4 hours."
*   **Correlational:** "There will be **no significant relationship** between a person's height and their IQ score."

---

## The Importance of the Null -
Without a Null Hypothesis, researchers might see patterns in random data. It acts as a "safety check" to ensure that we only claim something is "real" if the statistics prove it is very unlikely to be a fluke.
