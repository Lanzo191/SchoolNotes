## Definition -
**Quantitative data** is information expressed in **numerical form**. It focuses on "how much" or "how many." It includes nominal frequency counts, ordinal ranks, and interval/ratio measurements.

## Strengths -
*   **Objectivity:** Results are less likely to be influenced by researcher bias.
*   **Statistical Analysis:** Allows for complex mathematical calculations (Mean, Standard Deviation) and statistical tests to determine significance.
*   **Comparisons:** Easy to compare results between different groups or conditions.
*   **Reliability:** Quantitative measures are often easier to replicate.

## Weaknesses -
*   **Lack of Depth:** Does not provide insight into *why* a person behaved in a certain way.
*   **Reductionist:** It reduces complex human experiences to simple numbers, which can be artificial.
*   **Low Mundane Realism:** The methods used to collect numbers (e.g., lab tests) often lack realism.

## Examples - 
*   **Scores:** Percentage on a memory test.
*   **Physiological:** Heart rate, brain activity levels.
*   **Frequencies:** How many times a child hit a Bobo doll.
