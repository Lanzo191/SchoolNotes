### Self-Selection / Volunteer Sampling
Self-selection sampling occurs when participants **volunteer themselves** for a study, often in response to an advertisement or call for participants. Researchers do not select the participants directly; instead, the individuals choose to take part.

#### Strengths
- Participants have **informed consent**, which ensures ethical compliance because they choose to participate.  
- It is **easy to recruit** participants quickly, reducing preparation time.  
- It is **cheap** because minimal resources are needed for recruitment.  
- Can produce a **diverse sample** if a wide audience responds voluntarily.

#### Weaknesses
- The sample can be **biased**, as only people who are motivated or interested participate, which may not represent the population.  
- It can be **ethnocentric**, with certain cultural or demographic groups being overrepresented.  
- Researchers have **no control over who participates**, which may affect the reliability and [[Validity]] of results.

#### Explanation
This method is useful for **preliminary research** or niche studies but is less suitable for studies requiring a representative sample.
