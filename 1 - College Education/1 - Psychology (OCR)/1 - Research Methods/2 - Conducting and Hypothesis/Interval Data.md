## Definition -
**Interval data** is a high-level of measurement. Like [[Ordinal Data]], it is ordered, but the **intervals between the values are equal and fixed**. However, it lacks a **true zero point** (a score of zero doesn't mean the absence of the thing being measured).

## Strengths -
*   **Precision:** Much more precise than nominal or [[Ordinal Data]] because the scale is standardised.
*   **Analysis:** Allows for the calculation of the **Mean** and **Standard Deviation**.
*   **Statistical Tests:** Suitable for powerful **Parametric Tests** (if other criteria are met).

## Weaknesses -
*   **Arbitrary Zero:** You cannot say that 40°C is "twice as hot" as 20°C because the zero point is not absolute.
*   **Narrow Scope:** Not many psychological variables are naturally interval scale.

## Examples -
*   **Temperature (Celsius/Fahrenheit):** 0°C does not mean "no heat."
*   **IQ Scores:** A person with an IQ of 100 isn't "twice as smart" as someone with an IQ of 50.
*   **Standardised Tests:** Many personality tests are treated as interval data for analysis.
