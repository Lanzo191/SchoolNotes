### Overview
Observation sampling is how a researcher chooses **when** and **what** behaviour to record during an observation. Different [[Sampling Techniques]] affect the accuracy, practicality, and representativeness of the data.

---

## Time Sampling
### What it is  
Record behaviour at **fixed time intervals** (e.g., every 30 seconds, or observe for 10 seconds each minute).

### Strengths
- Reduces observer workload.  
- Good for continuous behaviour occurring over long periods.  
- Provides an overall behavioural pattern.
- Standardised Time = Greater Reliability 

### Weaknesses
- Behaviours between intervals may be **missed**.  
- Not ideal for short, rare behaviours.
- Harder to record many behaviours as event sampling 

---

## Event Sampling
### What it is  
Record **every instance** of a specific behaviour (e.g., every aggressive act).

### Strengths
- Good for **rare** or infrequent behaviours.  
- Captures full detail of target events.
- Data is easy to analyse 

### Weaknesses
- Observer may be overwhelmed if behaviour occurs too frequently.  
- Lack of detail - only records the chosen behaviour. ignores others. 

---

## Focal Sampling
### What it is  
Choose **one individual** and record all their behaviour for a set time.

### Strengths
- Rich detail for the individual.  
- Useful when group-level sampling is impractical.

### Weaknesses
- Not representative of group behaviour.  
- Can miss group patterns or social interactions.

---

## Scan / Instantaneous Sampling
### What it is  
At set moments, **scan the whole group** and record what each person is doing at that instant.

### Strengths
- Efficient for group-level data.  
- Quick to collect and easy for large samples.

### Weaknesses
- Only records behaviour at the exact moment → lots of behaviour is missed.  
- Not good for short-duration behaviours.

---

## All-Occurrence Sampling
### What it is  
Record **all instances of all behaviours of interest** for everyone.

### Strengths
- Very complete dataset.  
- Good for detailed behavioural sequences.

### Weaknesses
- Extremely time-consuming.  
- Requires multiple observers or video.  
- Hard to manage in naturalistic settings.

---
## One–Zero Sampling
One–zero sampling records whether a behaviour occurred **at least once** during each interval.

### How It Works
- Divide the observation into equal time intervals (e.g., 10 seconds).
- For each interval:
  - Behaviour occurred at least once → record **1**.
  - Behaviour did not occur → record **0**.
- Analyse the proportion of intervals containing behaviour.

### Strengths
- Very easy and quick to record.  
- Good for behaviours that happen too rapidly to count precisely.  
- Lower cognitive load on observer.

### Weaknesses
- **Overestimates** duration - even a brief behaviour counts as a full interval.  
- **Does not measure** frequency, duration, or intensity.  
- Less precise than event sampling or continuous observation.

### When to Use It
- When behaviour is irregular or rapid.  
- When only a general pattern is needed.  
- Useful in pilot studies or for less experienced observers.

---

## Recording Formats (for context)
- **Continuous recording** – records behaviour without breaks (most accurate).  
- **Interval recording** – behaviour marked as present/absent in short intervals.  
- **Checklists / coding systems** – pre-defined behaviour categories.

### A researcher is investigating helpful behaviour arranges to watch from a closed shop till dressed as an assistant. On a day when lots of elderly people shop, they observe how shoppers react towards elderly, including patience, understanding and assistance. 

#### Outline how event sampling would be used in this situation (3)

Event sampling would be a great choice for the experiment given it records EVERY instance of a given behaviour. The researcher could mark down every instance they witness in regards to the elderly customers such as anger or if they help the customer. This creates data thats concise and easy to analyse e.g If the researcher witnesses someone letting an elderly customer ahead in the line then they could note down that event.  3/3

#### One strength and One weakness of using event sampling in this study (6 marks)

 Event sampling allows the researcher to record all instances of specific helpful or unhelpful behaviours, providing a detailed and quantitative record of how shoppers treat elderly customers. This makes it easy to identify patterns and compare behaviours.

 If too many behaviours happen at once then the researcher might miss some events, making the data incomplete or biased. Additionally, it does not capture the context or reasons behind the behaviour. 5/6

#### Extension: How would you change the experiment to use time sampling and why might it be better

The researcher could use time sampling by observing shoppers at fixed intervals (e.g., every 5 minutes) and recording which behaviours, such as helping or being impatient, occur during that time. This might be better because it reduces the chance of missing behaviours when many happen at once and makes the data easier to record and manage, while still showing general patterns of behaviour. 3/3
