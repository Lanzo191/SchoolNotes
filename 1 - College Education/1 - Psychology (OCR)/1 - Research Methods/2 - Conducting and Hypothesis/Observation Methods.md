**[[Observations]]** involve watching and recording participants' behavior. They can be conducted in controlled or natural settings.

## Types of Observation
- **Naturalistic**: Conducted in the participant's natural environment.
- **Controlled**: Conducted in a set-up environment (e.g., a lab).
- **Overt**: Participants know they are being observed.
- **Covert**: Participants are unaware they are being observed.
- **Participant**: The researcher joins the group they are studying.
- **Non-Participant**: The researcher remains separate from the group.
- Unstructured 

---

## Observation Sampling Techniques
- **Event Sampling**: Every time a specific behavior occurs, it is recorded.
- **Time Sampling**: Recording behavior at specific time intervals (e.g., every 30 seconds).

---

## Strengths and Weaknesses

| Strengths | Weaknesses |
| :--- | :--- |
| **High Validity**: Sees what people actually *do* rather than what they *say* they do. | **Ethical Issues**: Covert observations lack informed consent and invade privacy. |
| **Capture Spontaneous Behavior**: Naturalistic observations show behavior that might not happen in a lab. | **Observer Bias**: The researcher's expectations may influence what they "see" or record. |
| **Reliability**: Controlled observations with clear coding systems can be easily replicated. | **Hawthorne Effect**: If overt, participants may change their behavior because they are watched. |

---

## Improving Quality
- **Inter-Rater Reliability**: Using two or more observers and correlating their results (0.8+ coefficient).
- **Operationalised Coding Systems**: Using a clear checklist of behaviors so there is no ambiguity.
