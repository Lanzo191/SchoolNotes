### Snowball Sampling
Snowball sampling is a technique where the researcher identifies initial participants, who then **recruit other participants** they know. This continues like a “snowball” effect.

#### Strengths
- Allows research to **start with a small initial sample** and gradually expand.  
- **Inexpensive** because participants help recruit others, reducing recruitment costs.  
- **Less work for the researcher**, as recruitment is partly managed by participants.  
- Effective for **hard-to-reach or niche populations**, such as rare medical conditions or subcultures.

#### Weaknesses
- Results are **not generalisable** because participants may share similar characteristics due to social connections.  
- **Relies on social networks**, which can introduce bias if networks are homogeneous.  

#### Explanation
Snowball sampling is particularly useful for **qualitative research** and studies targeting **difficult-to-access populations**, but results must be interpreted with caution.
