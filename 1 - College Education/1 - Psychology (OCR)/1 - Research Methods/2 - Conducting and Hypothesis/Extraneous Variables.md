Extraneous Variables (EV) -  
Any variable other than the IV that **might affect the DV** if not controlled. They threaten the **[[Validity]]** of the experiment.

### Types -  
- **[[Participant Variables]]:** age, intelligence, personality.  
- **[[Situational Variables]]:** time of day, noise, temperature.  
- **Experimenter variables:** researcher characteristics or behaviour.

### Strengths -  
- Recognising EVs helps improve study design.  
- Reducing EVs increases accuracy and reliability.

### Weaknesses -  
- Impossible to control everything.  
- If overlooked, EVs can become **[[Confounding Variables]]**.  
- Harder to manage outside laboratory settings.

### Examples -  
- Fatigue affecting performance in long experiments.  
- Distractions in the testing room.  
- Prior knowledge influencing test performance.

### Starter Questions -  
- What is the difference between EVs and CVs?  
- Give an example of an EV in a memory experiment.
