## Overview -
A **Directional Hypothesis** (also called a **One-Tailed Hypothesis**) specifies the exact nature or "direction" of the expected outcome. It doesn't just say there will be a difference; it says which group will perform better or worse.

## Key Features -
*   **Specific Wording:** Uses comparative adjectives like: *higher, lower, faster, slower, more, less, positive, negative.*
*   **Statistical Test:** Requires a **One-Tailed test**, which concentrates all the "significance" at one end of the data distribution.

## When to Use a Directional Hypothesis -
*   **Existing Research:** Use this if previous studies have already shown a consistent direction of effect.
*   **Strong Theory:** If there is a very strong theoretical reason to expect one result over another.

## Example -
"Participants who drink 200mg of caffeine will recall **significantly more** words from a list of 20 than participants who drink 0mg of caffeine."

## Strengths & Weaknesses -
*   **Strength:** More precise and powerful if the direction is correct.
*   **Weakness:** If the results go the *opposite* way (e.g., caffeine makes memory worse), you must reject your hypothesis even if the difference is huge.
