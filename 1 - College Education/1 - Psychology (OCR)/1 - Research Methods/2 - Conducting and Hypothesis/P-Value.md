## Overview -  
The **p-value** (probability value) is a statistical measure that tells us **how likely the results of a study occurred by chance**. It is used to decide whether to accept or reject the **[[Null Hypothesis]]**.

## Key Rules of P-Values -  
*   **The Scale:** The p-value is always between 0 and 1.
*   **The Threshold (Significance Level):** In Psychology, the standard level is **p ≤ 0.05 (5%)**.
*   **Interpretation:** 
    *   If **p ≤ 0.05**: The results are "significant." There is a 5% or less probability that the results were due to chance. We **reject the Null** and accept the Alternative Hypothesis.
    *   If **p > 0.05**: The results are "not significant." There is a higher than 5% probability that the results occurred by chance. We **accept the Null** and reject the Alternative Hypothesis.

## Stricter Levels (p ≤ 0.01) -
Sometimes researchers use a stricter level of **1%**. This is used when the research is very important (e.g., testing a new drug) to be extra sure that the results are not a fluke.

## Error Types in Statistics -

| Error Type | What happened? | Explanation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Type I Error** | **False Positive** | You rejected the Null Hypothesis when it was actually true (you claimed a result was significant when it was just luck). |
| **Type II Error** | **False Negative** | You accepted the Null Hypothesis when it was actually false (you claimed there was no effect when there actually was one). |

*   *Mnemonic:* Type **I** is an **I**nnocent man going to jail (false positive). Type **II** is a guilty man going free (false negative).
