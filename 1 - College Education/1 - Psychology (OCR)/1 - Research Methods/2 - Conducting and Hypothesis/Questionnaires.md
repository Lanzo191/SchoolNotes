**Questionnaires** are a self-report method consisting of a set of pre-written questions. They are used to collect data about a person's thoughts, feelings, attitudes, or past behaviors.

## Open vs Closed Questions

| Feature | **Closed Questions** | **Open Questions** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Data Type** | Produces **Quantitative Data** (numerical). | Produces **Qualitative Data** (descriptive). |
| **Response** | Fixed set of responses (e.g., Yes/No). | Participants answer in their own words. |
| **Depth** | Lacks detail; "force-choice" responses. | High depth and detail; explains the "why". |
| **Analysis** | Fast and easy to represent in graphs. | Time-consuming to analyze and categorize. |

---

## Types of Closed Questions

- **Likert Scale**: Participants indicate their level of agreement with a statement.
    - *Example:* "Psychology is my favorite subject: Strongly Agree / Agree / Neutral / Disagree / Strongly Disagree."
- **Semantic Differential Scale**: Participants mark a point between two bipolar adjectives.
    - *Example:* "Rate this lesson: Boring [ ] [ ] [ ] [ ] [ ] Exciting."
- **Rating Scale**: Participants assign a numerical value to represent their opinion or feeling.
    - *Example:* "On a scale of 1 to 10, how stressed are you?"
- **Fixed Choice**: Participants must choose from a list of options.
    - *Example:* "Which of these subjects do you take? [ ] Psychology [ ] Sociology [ ] Biology."

---

## Types of Open Questions

- **Free-Response**: Participants are given a blank space to write as much as they want.
    - *Example:* "Describe your experience of school in as much detail as possible."
- **Short-Answer**: Questions that require a brief response.
    - *Example:* "What is your favorite hobby?"
- **Sentence Completion**: Participants are given the start of a sentence and must finish it.
    - *Example:* "When I think of exams, I feel..."
- **Word Association**: Participants are given a word and must write the first thing that comes to mind.
    - *Example:* "Write the first word that comes to mind for: **Stress**."

## Evaluation (Strengths & Weaknesses)

| Aspect | **Strength** | **Weakness** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sample** | **Large Samples**: Cheap/fast to distribute; increases generalizability. | **Low Response Rates**: Only certain types of people respond, creating a biased sample. |
| **Data** | **Reliability**: Standardized procedure; easy to replicate and check for consistency. | **Validity**: Social desirability bias; participants lie to look good. |
| **Processing** | **Efficiency**: Closed questions are quick to quantify and analyze statistically. | **Effort**: Open questions are time-consuming to transcribe and interpret. |
| **Interaction** | **Reduced Bias**: No interviewer effect; participants don't feel judged by a researcher. | **Misinterpretation**: No one is there to clarify questions if the participant is confused. |

---

## Key Terms for Validity
- **Social Desirability**: Changing answers to appear more "normal" or socially acceptable.
- **Response Set**: Ticking the same box (e.g., all "Agree") without reading the questions.
- **Acquiescence Bias**: A tendency to agree with statements regardless of content.
- **Leading Questions**: Questions worded in a way that suggests a specific answer.
