### Random Sampling
Random sampling is a method where **every member of the target population has an equal chance** of being selected. Selection is usually conducted using random number generators or lottery systems.

#### Strengths
- Ensures an **equal chance of selection**, reducing selection bias.  
- Facilitates the formation of **representative sample groups**, increasing generalisability.  
- Often considered the **fairest method**, as all individuals have an opportunity to participate.  
- Reduces systematic bias, which improves **internal [[Validity]]**.

#### Weaknesses
- May produce **imbalanced subgroups** by chance (e.g., uneven gender distribution).  
- Can be **expensive** and time-consuming if the population is large.  
- Requires a **complete list of the population**, which may not always be available.

#### Explanation
Random sampling is ideal for **quantitative studies** requiring statistical generalisation, but practical limitations must be considered.
