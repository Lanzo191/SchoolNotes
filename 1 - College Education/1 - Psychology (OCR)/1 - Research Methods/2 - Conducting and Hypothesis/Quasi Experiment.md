## Overview -
A **quasi experiment** is a study where the [[Independent Variable]] (IV) is based on a **naturally existing characteristic** of the participant (e.g., their gender, age, or whether they have a specific brain condition). The researcher cannot "assign" people to these groups; they already belong to them.

## Key Features -
*   **Pre-existing Groups:** The IV is a trait, not an event or a manipulated condition.
*   **Controlled Setting:** Unlike [[Natural Experiments]], quasi experiments often take place in a **Lab** environment.
*   **Comparisons:** The focus is on comparing the difference between these fixed groups (e.g., Males vs. Females).

## Strengths -
*   **Controlled:** Because they often happen in labs, they can still have high levels of [[Standardisation]].
*   **Useful:** They allow researchers to study variables that are impossible to manipulate (e.g., you cannot "give" someone autism for a study).

## Weaknesses -
*   **[[Confounding Variables]]:** Since participants weren't randomly assigned, other factors might be causing the difference. For example, if you compare older vs. younger people, their different upbringings (nurture) might be the cause, not their age.
*   **No [[Cause and Effect]]:** You can only say there is a "difference" or a "link," but you cannot strictly prove that the IV caused the DV.

## Core Study Example -
*   **Levine et al. (2001):** A cross-cultural study where the IV was the **culture/city** (pre-existing) and the DV was helping behaviour.
*   **Baron-Cohen:** Comparing people with Autism/AS to those without.
