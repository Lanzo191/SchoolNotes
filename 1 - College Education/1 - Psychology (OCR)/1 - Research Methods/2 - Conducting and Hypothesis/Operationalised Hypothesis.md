## Overview -
An **Operationalised Hypothesis** is a hypothesis that has been written with extreme precision. It clearly states exactly **how the [[Independent Variable]] (IV) is manipulated** and exactly **how the [[Dependent Variable]] (DV) is measured**.

## Why Operationalise? -
*   **[[Replicability]]:** It provides a "recipe" that other researchers can follow.
*   **Objectivity:** It removes vague terms like "smart" or "aggressive" and replaces them with numbers.

---

## The "Formula" for a Perfect Hypothesis -
A fully operationalised hypothesis should include:
1.  **Both levels of the IV** (Condition A vs. Condition B).
2.  **The specific measure of the DV** (e.g., "score on a 20-word test").
3.  **The population** (if required by the exam question).

## Examples -

*   **Vague (Bad):** "Caffeine makes memory better."
*   **Operationalised (Good):** "Participants who consume **200ml of caffeinated coffee** will recall a **significantly higher number of words from a list of 20** than participants who consume **200ml of decaffeinated water**."

*   **Vague (Bad):** "Age affects reaction time."
*   **Operationalised (Good):** "There will be a significant difference in the **time taken (in milliseconds)** to press a button in response to a light stimulus between **participants aged 18-25** and **participants aged 60-75**."
