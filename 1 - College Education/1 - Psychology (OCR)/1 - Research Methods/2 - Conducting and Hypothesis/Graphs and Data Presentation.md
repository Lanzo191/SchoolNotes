Graphs and Data Presentation -  
Used to visually represent data, making it easier to identify trends, patterns, and anomalies.  
See also [[Descriptive Statistics]] and [[Inferential Statistics]].

### Bar Chart -  
Used for **categorical (discrete)** data such as nominal or ordinal.  
Separate bars show different categories.  
The height of each bar represents frequency.  
Gaps between bars show that data is not continuous.

### Histogram -  
Used for **continuous** data (e.g., time, height).  
Bars touch because the data forms a continuous scale.  
The area of each bar represents frequency, not just the height.

### Line Graph -  
Used to show **changes over time** or **continuous variables**.  
Points connected by lines show trends clearly.

### Scattergram -  
Used to show **relationships ([[Correlations]])** between two co-variables.  
- Positive correlation: both variables increase together.  
- Negative correlation: one increases as the other decreases.  
- No correlation: no clear relationship.

### Pie Chart -  
Shows proportions or percentages of categories out of a whole.  
Useful for showing relative frequency or comparisons.

### Tables -  
Used to summarise raw data before analysis.  
Always include clear headings and units.  
Simplifies calculation of mean, range, etc.

### Purpose -  
To make complex data understandable and highlight patterns at a glance.  
Proper choice of graph ensures valid and meaningful presentation of results.
