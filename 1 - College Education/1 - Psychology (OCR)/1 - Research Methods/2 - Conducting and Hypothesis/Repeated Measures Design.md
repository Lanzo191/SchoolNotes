## Overview -
**Repeated Measures Design** involves using the **same participants** in every condition of the experiment. Each person provides two or more sets of data for comparison.

## Key Features -
*   **All Conditions:** Every participant experiences **every level** of the [[Independent Variable]] (IV).
*   **Comparison:** The researcher compares each participant's score in Condition A against their own score in Condition B.
*   **Control:** The participant acts as their own "control group."

## Strengths -
*   **No [[Participant Variables]] Differences in personality, IQ, or ability are eliminated because the same people are used in both groups.
*   **Fewer Participants:** Requires **half the number of people** compared to an [[Independent Measures Design]]

## Weaknesses -
*   **Order Effects:** 
    *   **Practice Effect:** Doing better on the second task because they learned how to do it.
    *   **Fatigue/Boredom Effect:** Doing worse on the second task because they are tired.
*   **[[Demand Characteristics]]:** Participants are more likely to guess the aim after seeing both conditions.

## How to Control Order Effects -
*   **Counterbalancing (ABBA):** Group 1 does A then B; Group 2 does B then A. This ensures any order effects are spread evenly across both conditions.
