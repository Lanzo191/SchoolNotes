## Definition -
**Ordinal data** is data that can be placed in a meaningful **order or rank** (e.g., 1st, 2nd, 3rd). However, the intervals between the ranks are not equal or measurable.

## Strengths -
*   **More Detail:** Provides more information than [[Nominal Data]] because it shows the position of participants relative to each other.
*   **Versatility:** Can be used for subjective traits like "attractiveness" or "happiness" which are hard to measure precisely.

## Weaknesses -
*   **Unequal Intervals:** The difference between 1st and 2nd place might be 1 second, but the difference between 2nd and 3rd might be 10 seconds.
*   **Subjectivity:** One person's "7/10" on a scale might be another person's "5/10."

## Statistical Applications -
*   **Central Tendency:** **Median**.
*   **Statistical Tests:** Non-parametric tests like **[[Mann-Whitney U]]**, **[[Wilcoxon T]]**, and **Spearman's Rho**.

## Examples -
*   **Ranks:** Finishing positions in a race.
*   **Rating Scales:** Likert scales (e.g., 1 = Strongly Disagree, 5 = Strongly Agree).
*   **Questionnaire scores:** A total score on a personality test.
