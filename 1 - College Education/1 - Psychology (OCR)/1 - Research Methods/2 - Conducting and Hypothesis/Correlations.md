**Correlations** are a non-experimental method used to measure the strength and direction of the relationship between two or more co-variables.

> **Note:** Unlike experiments, there is **no [[Independent Variable]] (IV) or [[Dependent Variable]] (DV)**. Instead, we refer to them as **Co-Variables**. ALSO *DO NOT USE THE WORD DIFFERENCE ONLY RELATIONSHIP*

## Types of Correlation
1.  **Positive Correlation:** As one variable increases, the other variable also increases.
    *   *Example:* Revision time and Exam score.
    *   *Graph:* Points go upwards from left to right.
2.  **Negative Correlation:** As one variable increases, the other variable decreases.
    *   *Example:* Absences from school and Exam score.
    *   *Graph:* Points go downwards from left to right.
3.  **Zero Correlation:** There is no relationship between the variables.
    *   *Example:* Shoe size and Intelligence.
    *   *Graph:* Points are scattered randomly.

---

## Correlation Coefficients
A number between **-1.0** and **+1.0** that represents the strength and direction of the relationship.

*   **+1.0**: Perfect Positive Correlation.
*   **0.0**: Zero Correlation.
*   **-1.0**: Perfect Negative Correlation.
*   **0.8**: Strong Positive.
*   **-0.3**: Weak Negative.

---

## Hypotheses in Correlations
*   **Null Hypothesis:** "There will be **no significant correlation** between [Variable A] and [Variable B]."
*   **Alternate (Experimental) Hypothesis:** "There will be a **significant correlation** between [Variable A] and [Variable B]."
    *   *Directional (One-Tailed):* Specifies the direction (e.g., "significant **positive** correlation").
    *   *Non-Directional (Two-Tailed):* Does not specify direction (e.g., "significant correlation").

---

## Strengths and Weaknesses

| Strengths | Weaknesses |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Preliminary Research:** Useful for investigating new areas to see if a link exists before setting up an experiment. | **No Causation:** Cannot establish cause and effect. Just because variables are linked doesn't mean one causes the other (Third Variable Problem). |
| **Ethics:** Allows study of variables that cannot be manipulated ethically (e.g., smoking and lung cancer). | **Non-Linear Relationships:** Fails to measure curved relationships (e.g., stress vs performance - Yerkes-Dodson). |
| **Quantitative Data:** Provides statistical data (coefficients) that is easy to analyse. | **Subjective Interpretation:** Scattergrams can be interpreted differently by different researchers if not using stats. |
