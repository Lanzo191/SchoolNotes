## Overview -
**Manipulation of Variables** is the heart of the experimental method. It involves the researcher directly and intentionally changing the **[[Independent Variable]] (IV)** to see if it causes a corresponding change in the **[[Dependent Variable]] (DV)**.

## Levels of the IV -
To manipulate a variable, the researcher must create at least two groups or conditions:
*   **Experimental Group:** Exposed to the manipulation (e.g., given caffeine).
*   **Control Group:** Not exposed, or given a neutral version (e.g., given decaf or water).
*   *Note:* Comparing these levels allows for [[Cause and Effect]]to be identified.

---

## Requirements for Successful Manipulation -

1.  **Operationalisation:** The IV must be defined in clear, measurable terms (e.g., "50mg of caffeine" rather than just "some coffee").
2.  **Internal Validity:** The manipulation must be the *only* thing that changes between groups. If other things change, the study is invalid.
3.  **Ethics:** Researchers must not manipulate variables in a way that causes undue stress or physical harm (e.g., they cannot "manipulate" someone into a car crash to study trauma).
