## Overview -
**Falsification** is a principle of the scientific method developed by **Karl Popper**. It argues that for a theory to be truly scientific, it must be worded in a way that it **can be proven wrong**.

## The Logic of Falsification -
*   Science does not try to "prove" things are 100% true. Instead, scientists try to "disprove" (falsify) their hypotheses.
*   The more a theory survives attempts to disprove it, the stronger it becomes.
*   If a theory cannot be tested or proven wrong (e.g., "everyone has an invisible invisible ghost"), it is **unscientific**.

## Application in Psychology -
*   **Freud's Psychoanalysis:** Often criticised for lacking falsifiability. For example, if you say a man has an "unconscious desire" and he denies it, Freudians might say he is just "in denial," making the theory impossible to disprove.
*   **Behaviourism:** Very falsifiable. You can test if a reward leads to a change in behaviour. If it doesn't, the theory is wrong.
