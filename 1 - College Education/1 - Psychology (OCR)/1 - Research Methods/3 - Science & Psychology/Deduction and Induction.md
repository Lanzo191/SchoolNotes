## Overview -
Deduction and Induction are two different methods of reasoning used in the scientific process. They determine whether a researcher starts with a theory or starts with the data.

---

## 1. Deduction (General to Specific) -
**Deductive reasoning** starts with a pre-existing theory or general rule and moves towards a specific prediction (hypothesis).

*   **Hypothetico-Deductive Model:** This is the standard scientific approach. You take a theory, deduce a testable hypothesis, and then gather data to see if it's correct.
*   **Process:** Theory → Hypothesis → Observation → Confirmation/Refutation.
*   *Key Strength:* High levels of objectivity and control.

## 2. Induction (Specific to General) -
**Inductive reasoning** starts with specific observations and looks for patterns to create a new, general theory.

*   **Theory Construction:** Induction is vital for building new psychological theories where none currently exist.
*   **Process:** Observation → Pattern → Tentative Hypothesis → Theory.
*   *Key Strength:* Allows for the discovery of new, complex human behaviours.

---

## Comparison -

| Feature | Deduction | Induction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Starting Point** | Theory. | Observation. |
| **Goal** | Testing a theory. | Creating a theory. |
| **Data Type** | Quantitative. | Qualitative. |
| **Logic** | "Top-Down." | "Bottom-Up." |
