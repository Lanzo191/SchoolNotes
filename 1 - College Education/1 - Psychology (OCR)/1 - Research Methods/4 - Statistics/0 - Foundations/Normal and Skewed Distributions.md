**Distributions** describe the shape of a data set when plotted on a graph. In psychology, we look at where the **Mean**, **Median**, and **Mode** sit to identify the type of distribution.

---

## 1. Normal Distribution (The Bell Curve)
In a perfect normal distribution, the data is symmetrical. 
- **The Rule**: The **Mean**, **Median**, and **Mode** are all the **same** (or very close).
- **The Shape**: A symmetrical "bell" shape.
- **Example**: IQ scores or height in a large population.

---

## 2. Positively Skewed Distribution
A positive skew happens when there are a few **extremely high scores** (outliers) that pull the "tail" of the graph to the **right**.
- **The Rule**: **Mean > Median > Mode**
    - The **Mean** is the highest because it is pulled up by those high outliers.
    - The **Mode** is the lowest because most people still have low scores.
- **The Shape**: The "hump" is on the left, and the long tail points to the **right** (positive side).
- **Example**: A very difficult exam where most people fail, but a few geniuses get 100%.

---

## 3. Negatively Skewed Distribution
A negative skew happens when there are a few **extremely low scores** (outliers) that pull the "tail" to the **left**.
- **The Rule**: **Mean < Median < Mode**
    - The **Mean** is the lowest because it is pulled down by those low outliers.
    - The **Mode** is the highest because most people got high scores.
- **The Shape**: The "hump" is on the right, and the long tail points to the **left** (negative side).
- **Example**: An easy exam where almost everyone gets 90%, but a few people get 0%.

---

## Summary Table for Exams

| Type of Distribution | **Where is the tail?** | **The Order of Averages** | **Memory Trick** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Normal** | No tail (Symmetrical) | Mean = Median = Mode | "Normal is Equal" |
| **Positively Skewed** | Points **Right** (+) | **Mean** is the Biggest | "Positive = High Mean" |
| **Negatively Skewed** | Points **Left** (-) | **Mean** is the Smallest | "Negative = Low Mean" |

## OCR Exam Question Practice
> **Q: In which type of distribution is the mean greater than both the median and the mode?**
> **A: Positively Skewed.** (Because the high outliers pull the mean up towards the positive end of the scale).
