Before choosing a test, you must know what "level" your data is.

## 1. Nominal Data (Lowest Level)
- **What it is**: Categorical data. You are just counting how many people fall into a group.
- **Example**: How many people said "Yes" vs "No"; counting how many males vs females.
- **Memory aid**: Nominal = **Named** categories.
- **Expansive Example**: A researcher investigates whether students prefer coffee, tea, or water during revision. They count the frequency for each:
    - Coffee: 15
    - Tea: 10
    - Water: 5
    - *This is nominal because you can't "rank" coffee as better than tea in a mathematical sense; they are just different names.*

## 2. Ordinal Data
- **What it is**: Data that can be put in order or ranked, but the gaps between the ranks aren't equal.
- **Example**: A race (1st, 2nd, 3rd place); a rating scale of 1-10 for "happiness".
- **Memory aid**: Ordinal = **Ordered** / Ranked.
- **Expansive Example**: A teacher ranks students on their "cooperation" in class from 1 (lowest) to 30 (highest). 
    - The difference between the 1st and 2nd student might be tiny, but the difference between the 2nd and 3rd might be huge. Because the "gaps" aren't equal, it is ordinal, not interval.

## 3. Interval Data (Highest Level)
- **What it is**: Data measured on a scale with fixed, equal units.
- **Example**: Temperature in Celsius, time in seconds, height in cm.
- **Memory aid**: Interval = **Equal Intervals** between points.
- **Expansive Example**: A psychologist measures how many seconds it takes for participants to solve a puzzle.
    - Participant A: 45 seconds
    - Participant B: 50 seconds
    - Participant C: 55 seconds
    - *The 5-second gap between A and B is exactly the same as the 5-second gap between B and C. This precision makes it [[Interval Data]].*

## Applied Scenario Practice
| Research Scenario | Level of Data | Why? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Counting how many smokers vs non-smokers in a room. | **Nominal** | Categorical groups. |
| Asking someone to rate their stress from 1-10. | **Ordinal** | Ranks, but gaps are subjective. |
| Measuring a child's height in meters. | **Interval** | Standardized, equal units. |
| A list of the top 5 most popular movies. | **Ordinal** | They are ranked in order. |
