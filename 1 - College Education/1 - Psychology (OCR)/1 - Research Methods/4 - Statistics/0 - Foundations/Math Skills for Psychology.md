Psychology exams often include simple math questions. Here is a guide on how to handle the most common ones.

---

## 1. Percentages (%)
To find a percentage of a number:
> **(Amount $\div$ Total) $\times$ 100**

- **Example**: 8 out of 40 participants got the question right. What is the percentage?
    - $(8 \div 40) = 0.2$
    - $0.2 \times 100 = \mathbf{20\%}$.

---

## 2. Fractions
To simplify a fraction, find a number that both the top and bottom can be divided by.
- **Example**: Write 15/60 as a fraction in its simplest form.
    - Both can be divided by 15.
    - $15 \div 15 = 1$
    - $60 \div 15 = 4$
    - **Answer: 1/4**.

---

## 3. Ratios (:)
Ratios show the relationship between two amounts.
- **Example**: In a group of 30, there are 20 men and 10 women. What is the ratio of men to women?
    - Write it as **20:10**.
    - Simplify by dividing both by 10.
    - **Answer: 2:1**.

---

## 4. Standard Form
Used for very large or very small numbers.
- **$1.5 \times 10^3$**: Move the decimal 3 places to the **right** $\rightarrow$ **1500**.
- **$1.5 \times 10^{-3}$**: Move the decimal 3 places to the **left** $\rightarrow$ **0.0015**.

---

## 5. Rounding & Significant Figures
- **Rounding to 2 decimal places**: Look at the 3rd decimal. If it's 5 or more, round up.
    - **Example**: Round 3.456 to 2 decimal places $\rightarrow$ **3.46**.
- **Significant Figures (sf)**: Start counting from the first non-zero number.
    - **Example**: 0.00**45**67 rounded to 2 sf $\rightarrow$ **0.0046**.

---

---

## OCR Exam Tip: Standard Deviation
You don't usually need to calculate Standard Deviation, but you MUST know what it means:
- **Low SD**: The scores are all close to the mean (consistent).
- **High SD**: The scores are spread out (inconsistent).
