**Variance** and **Standard Deviation** are measures of dispersion. They tell us how "spread out" the scores are from the average (the mean).

---

## 1. Variance
Variance is the average of the squared differences from the mean. It gives a mathematical value for the total spread of the data.

- **High Variance**: The scores are very spread out (inconsistent).
- **Low Variance**: The scores are all close to the mean (consistent).

---

## 2. Standard Deviation (SD)
Standard Deviation is the most common measure of spread in psychology. It is simply the **square root of the variance**.

### Why use SD instead of Range?
- **Range** only looks at the two most extreme scores (highest and lowest). If one person gets a weirdly high score, the range looks massive.
- **Standard Deviation** looks at *every single score* in the data set. It is a much more sensitive and accurate measure of how consistent the results are.

### What the number means:
- **Small SD**: Most participants got a score very close to the mean. The group is "homogenous" (similar).
- **Large SD**: The scores are widely spread. Some people did very well, while others did very poorly. The group is "heterogenous" (varied).

---

## Summary Comparison

| Measure | **Definition** | **Strength** | **Weakness** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Variance** | The squared spread from the mean. | Useful for further statistical calculations. | Hard to interpret because the units are squared. |
| **Standard Deviation** | The average distance from the mean. | Very accurate; uses all the data. | Can be distorted by extreme outliers. |

---

## OCR Exam Tip
You will almost never be asked to calculate these by hand in an exam. Instead, you will be given two SD numbers and asked to compare them.
- **Example**: "Group A has an SD of 2.1. Group B has an SD of 8.4. What does this tell us?"
- **Answer**: "Group A's scores are more consistent and closer to the mean. Group B's scores are much more varied and inconsistent."
