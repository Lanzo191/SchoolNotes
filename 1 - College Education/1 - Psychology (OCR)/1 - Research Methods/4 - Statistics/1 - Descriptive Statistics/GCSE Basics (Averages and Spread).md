This note covers the basic mathematical tools used to summarize data sets.

---

## 1. Measures of Central Tendency (Averages)

### The Mean
- **The Step-by-Step**:
    1. Add up every single number in the data set.
    2. Count how many numbers there are (this is your 'N').
    3. Divide the total sum by N.
- **Example**: `2, 4, 6, 8` $\rightarrow$ Sum = 20. N = 4. Mean = $20 \div 4 = \mathbf{5}$.
- **Best for**: Interval data (precise).

### The Median
- **The Step-by-Step**:
    1. Arrange the numbers in order from smallest to largest.
    2. Find the middle number.
    3. If there are two middle numbers, add them together and divide by 2.
- **Example**: `2, 3, 5, 8, 10` $\rightarrow$ Median = **5**.
- **Best for**: Ordinal data or data with outliers.

### The Mode
- **The Step-by-Step**:
    1. Count how many times each number appears.
    2. The number that appears most often is the mode.
- **Example**: `2, 5, 5, 8, 10` $\rightarrow$ Mode = **5**.
- **Best for**: Nominal data (categories).

---

## 2. Measures of Dispersion (Spread)

### The Range
- **The Step-by-Step**:
    1. Find the highest number in the set.
    2. Find the lowest number in the set.
    3. Subtract the lowest from the highest.
    4. **Psychology Rule**: Often you add 1 to the result to account for rounding errors.
- **Example**: `10, 5, 2, 8` $\rightarrow$ $10 - 2 = 8$. (Psychology range = **9**).

### Standard Deviation (SD)
- **The Simple Version**: It shows how far, on average, each score is from the mean.
- **Interpreting it**:
    - **Small SD**: The numbers are all very similar and close to the mean (consistent).
    - **Large SD**: The numbers are very different and spread out (inconsistent).
