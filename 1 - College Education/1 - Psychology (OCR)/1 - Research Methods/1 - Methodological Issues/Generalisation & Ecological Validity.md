Generalisation is applying study findings to the wider population, while ecological [[Validity]] is how realistic or representative a study’s setting and tasks are of real life.  

### Strengths -  
- High ecological [[Validity]] means findings apply to real-world behaviour.  
- High generalisability increases usefulness and relevance.  

### Weaknesses -  
- Laboratory settings often lack realism.  
- Using only certain groups (e.g. students) reduces population [[Validity]].  
- Artificial tasks may not reflect real behaviour.  

### Examples / How to Improve -  
- Conduct [[Field Experiments]] or naturalistic [[Observations]].  
- Use diverse participant samples.  
- Design tasks that mirror everyday behaviour.
