Sampling bias occurs when a sample is **not representative** of the wider population, reducing generalisability.  

### Causes -  
- Self-selection bias – participants choose to take part.  
- Volunteer bias – volunteers differ from non-volunteers (more motivated).  
- [[Opportunity Sampling]] – using whoever is available.  
- Using only certain demographics (e.g. students, one gender).  

### Strengths -  
- Easier to recruit and conduct studies quickly.  
- Acceptable in pilot or exploratory studies with clear limitations.  

### Weaknesses -  
- Findings may not generalise to the wider population.  
- May misrepresent real behaviour or opinions.  
- Reduces population [[Validity]].  

### Examples / How to Improve -  
- Use stratified or [[Random Sampling]] when possible.  
- Be cautious when generalising.  
- Acknowledge limitations clearly in reports.
