Ethical considerations ensure research is carried out responsibly and protects participants’ wellbeing.  

### Key Principles -  
- Informed consent – participants must be fully aware of the study’s purpose and their rights.  
- Right to withdraw - participants can leave at any point without penalty.  
- Confidentiality and anonymity – participant's identities and data are protected.  
- Protection from harm – researchers must avoid causing psychological or physical distress.  
- Debriefing – participants must be informed of the true nature of the study afterward.  
- Deception – only used if necessary and justified, followed by a full debrief.  

### Strengths -  
- Protects participants and maintains the integrity of psychological research.  
- Encourages public trust in psychology as a discipline.  

### Weaknesses -  
- Some ethical controls (e.g. full consent) can reduce realism or [[Validity]].  
- Balancing participant welfare and scientific value can be difficult.  

### Examples / How to Apply -  
- Obtain ethical approval before research begins.  
- Use minimal deception and always debrief.  
- Ensure all participants have the right to withdraw.  

## British Psychology Society's Code of Ethics and Conduct 
- Respect – informed consent, right to withdraw, confidentiality, Competence
- Responsibility – protection of participant, debrief
- Integrity – deception
