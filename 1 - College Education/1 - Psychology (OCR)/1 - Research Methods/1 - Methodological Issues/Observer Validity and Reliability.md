## Reliability
Reliability refers to the **consistency of a measurement or observation**. High reliability means results are repeatable under the same conditions.

### Types of Reliability
- **Test-retest reliability**: The same test produces similar results when administered at different times.  
- **Inter-rater reliability**: Two or more observers record the same behaviour independently and produce similar results.  
- **Intra-rater reliability**: The same observer records behaviour consistently across multiple [[Observations]].

### How to Improve Reliability
- Standardise procedures and instructions.  
- Train observers to use operationalised definitions.  
- Use structured coding schemes.  
- Repeat measurements to check consistency.

### Strengths of High Reliability
- Increases **trustworthiness** of data.  
- Supports replication of studies.  
- Reduces random errors in measurements.

### Weaknesses / Challenges
- Some behaviours are difficult to measure consistently.  
- Observer fatigue or environmental variations may reduce reliability.  
- Over-[[Standardisation]] can reduce ecological [[Validity]].

## Validity
Validity refers to whether a test or observation **measures what it claims to measure**. High validity ensures the results reflect the intended construct.

### Types of Validity
- **Internal validity**: Whether the study accurately measures the effect of the independent variable on the dependent variable, controlling for extraneous variables.  
- **External validity**: The extent to which findings can be generalised to other people, settings, or times.  
- **Ecological validity**: How well findings reflect real-world behaviour.  
- **Construct validity**: Whether the measure truly reflects the theoretical concept it is intended to measure.  
- **Face validity**: Whether a test appears to measure what it should at face value.  
- **Concurrent validity**: Whether the test correlates with established measures of the same construct.  

### How to Improve Validity
- Use **operationalised definitions** for behaviours or variables.  
- Control for extraneous variables in experiments.  
- Use randomisation and counterbalancing where appropriate.  
- Use triangulation: combining multiple methods or sources to measure the same concept.  
- Pilot test measures to ensure they reflect the intended construct.

### Strengths of High Validity
- Results are more meaningful and **accurate**.  
- Supports generalisation to real-world contexts.  
- Reduces risk of systematic errors and misinterpretation.

### Weaknesses / Challenges
- High control for validity can reduce naturalistic behaviour (ecological validity).  
- Some constructs (e.g., intelligence, aggression) are inherently difficult to measure.  
- Achieving perfect validity is often impossible in psychology.

## Bias
Bias occurs when systematic errors affect **observations, measurements, or conclusions**, reducing the objectivity of a study.

### Types of Bias
- **Observer / experimenter bias**: Observer expectations influence what is recorded.  
- **Participant bias**: Participants alter behaviour due to awareness of being observed (Hawthorne effect).  
- **Social desirability bias**: Participants respond in a way they think is favourable.  
- **Sampling bias**: The sample is not representative of the population.  
- **Confirmation bias**: Interpreting ambiguous data to support expectations.

### How to Reduce Bias
- **Blind or double-blind procedures** to prevent observer/participant expectations.  
- **Random sampling** to avoid unrepresentative participants.  
- **Structured coding schemes** for observations.  
- Use multiple observers to reduce observer bias.  
- Triangulate data sources to cross-check findings.

### Strengths of Controlling Bias
- Improves reliability and validity.  
- Supports accurate, objective conclusions.  
- Makes results more generalisable and trustworthy.

### Weaknesses / Challenges
- Complete elimination of bias is often impossible.  
- Some control procedures (e.g., blinding, multiple observers) require **extra resources**.  
- Attempts to reduce bias may introduce other challenges, like reduced natural behaviour.
