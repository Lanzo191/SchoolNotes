
## Overview
The **British Psychological Society (BPS)** provides a code of ethics that all researchers must follow to protect participants and the reputation of psychology.

## Core Guidelines
| Guideline                | Description                                                                                                                     |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Informed Consent**     | Participants must be told the aims, risks, and procedures so they can decide whether to take part.                              |
| **Deception**            | Researchers should avoid misleading participants. If it is necessary for the study, they must be fully **debriefed** afterward. |
| **Right to Withdraw**    | Participants can leave the study at any time, even after it has finished, and have their data deleted.                          |
| **Protection from Harm** | Participants must not experience physical or psychological distress beyond what they would in everyday life.                    |
| **Confidentiality**      | All personal information and data must remain anonymous unless consent is given to share it.                                    |
| **Debriefing**           | After the study, participants must be told the true aims and checked for any negative effects.                                  |

## Key Points
- **Presumptive Consent**: Asking a similar group of people if they would find the study acceptable (used when deception is necessary).
- **Cost-Benefit Analysis**: Weighing the value of the research against the potential harm to participants.
