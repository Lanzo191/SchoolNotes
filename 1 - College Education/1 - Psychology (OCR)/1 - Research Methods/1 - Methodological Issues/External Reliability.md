External reliability refers to the **consistency of a measure over time or across different researchers and conditions**.  
It assesses whether repeating the same test, observation, or experiment under similar conditions will produce **similar results**.

### Key Idea -  
If a psychological measure or study has high external reliability, it can be **replicated** and yield **consistent outcomes**, showing that results are not due to chance, researcher bias, or one-off factors.

### Methods of Assessing External Reliability -  

- **Test–Retest Method -**  
  The same test or measure is given to the **same participants** on **two or more occasions** (usually with a time gap).  
  If scores are highly correlated, the measure has good external reliability.  
  Example: Administering an intelligence test twice over two weeks to check for consistent results.

- **Inter-Rater Reliability -**  
  Used for **observational studies** where more than one observer records data.  
  Reliability is assessed by comparing the level of **agreement between observers**.  
  High consistency between raters indicates strong inter-rater reliability.  
  A correlation coefficient (e.g., Spearman’s rho) of **0.8 or above** is generally considered acceptable.

- **Parallel Forms Reliability -**  
  Two equivalent versions of the same test are created and given to the same group.  
  Consistent results indicate the measure’s reliability across forms.  
  (Used less often due to difficulty in making truly parallel tests.)

### Improving External Reliability -  
- Standardise testing procedures and instructions.  
- Train observers or researchers thoroughly to reduce interpretation bias.  
- Ensure environmental conditions are consistent between sessions.  
- Use objective, operationalised definitions for all behavioural categories.

### Examples -  
- A memory test should produce similar results when taken twice under the same conditions.  
- Two psychologists using the same observation schedule should record similar behaviours.

### Importance -  
High external reliability ensures that findings are **replicable and credible**.  
It demonstrates that results are not unique to one occasion or one researcher, increasing **scientific [[Validity]]** and trustworthiness of the data.
