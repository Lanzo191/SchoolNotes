**Validity** refers to the extent to which a study actually measures what it claims to measure and the extent to which findings can be applied beyond the research setting.

---

## 1. Internal Validity
Internal validity is the extent to which the results of a study are caused by the **Independent Variable (IV)** and not by **Extraneous Variables**.

### Types of Internal Validity

| Type | **Definition** | **How to Check/Improve** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Face Validity** | Whether a test *looks* like it measures what it is supposed to at first glance. | Ask an expert to review the test items. |
| **Concurrent Validity** | Comparing a new test with an already established, valid test to see if results correlate. | Give participants both tests and check if the scores match. |
| **Construct Validity** | How well the test relates to the theoretical concepts it is meant to measure. | Ensure the operational definitions of variables are accurate. |
| **Criterion Validity** | How well one measure can predict the outcome of another. | Use the test to predict future behavior (Predictive Validity). |

---

## 2. External Validity
External validity is the extent to which the findings of a study can be **generalised** to other settings, people, and times.

### Types of External Validity

| Type | **Definition** | **How to Check/Improve** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ecological Validity** | The extent to which findings from a study can be generalised to **real-life settings**. | Conduct research in natural environments (Field Experiments). |
| **Population Validity** | The extent to which findings can be generalised to the **wider population** (beyond the sample). | Use a diverse, representative sample (e.g., Random Sampling). |
| **Temporal Validity** | The extent to which findings remain true and relevant **over time**. | Replicate the study years later to see if results are consistent. |

---

## Threats to Validity

| Threat | **Type** | **Explanation** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Demand Characteristics** | Internal | Participants guess the aim and change their behavior to "help" or "hinder" the researcher. |
| **Social Desirability** | Internal | Participants lie to present themselves in a better light. |
| **Researcher Bias** | Internal | The researcher's expectations influence the data collection or interpretation. |
| **Artificial Tasks** | External | Tasks that are not "mundane realism" (like memorizing nonsense syllables) reduce ecological validity. |
| **Unrepresentative Sample** | External | Using only one group (e.g., American students) makes it hard to generalise to others. |

---

## Evaluation: Strengths & Weaknesses

| Aspect                | **Strength**                                                                            | **Weakness**                                                                               |
| :-------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Lab Experiments**   | High **Internal Validity** due to strict control over extraneous variables.             | Low **Ecological Validity** because the environment is artificial and controlled.          |
| **Field Experiments** | High **Ecological Validity** as they occur in natural settings.                         | Low **Internal Validity** because it is harder to control extraneous variables.            |
| **Self-Reports**      | Can have high **Face Validity** if questions are direct.                                | Often suffer from low validity due to **Social Desirability Bias**.                        |
| **Observations**      | High **Ecological Validity** if covert (participants don't know they're being watched). | Can suffer from **Observer Bias**, where the researcher only sees what they expect to see. |

---

## How to Improve Validity
- **Standardisation**: Use the same instructions and procedures for everyone to reduce situational variables.
- **Double-Blind Procedure**: Neither the participant nor the researcher knows the aim of the study, reducing both demand characteristics and researcher bias.
- **Random Allocation**: Ensures participant variables (like IQ or personality) are spread evenly across groups.
- **Improved Sampling**: Use random or stratified sampling to increase population validity.
- **Pilot Studies**: Run a "trial" version of the study to identify any confusing questions or instructions.
