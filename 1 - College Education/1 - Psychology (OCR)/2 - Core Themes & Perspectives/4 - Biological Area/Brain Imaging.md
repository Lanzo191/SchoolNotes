## Primary Imaging Methods Used in Psychology
- **[[FMRI]] (Functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging)**:
  - Measures blood-oxygen-level-dependent (BOLD) haemodynamic responses.
  - Used by **Casey et al. (2011)** to assess differential activation in the prefrontal cortex and ventral striatum during go/no-go delay of gratification tasks.
- **[[MRI]] (Magnetic Resonance Imaging)**:
  - Employs strong magnetic fields and radio waves to generate high-resolution structural cross-sections.
  - Used by **Maguire et al. (2000)** with VBM (Voxel-Based Morphometry) and pixel counting to measure hippocampal volume.
- **[[PET]] (Positron Emission Tomography)**:
  - Involves injecting radioactive fluorodeoxyglucose (FDG) to image metabolic glucose uptake.
  - Used in biological research (e.g. Raine et al.) to identify metabolic deficits in violent offenders.
- **[[EEG Scan]] (Electroencephalography)**:
  - Records continuous post-synaptic electrical potentials from the scalp; provides millisecond temporal resolution for sleep and event-related potential (ERP) research.
