## Biological Area Overview

The **Biological Area** assumes that all behavior has a physiological root. Our thoughts, feelings, and behaviors are determined by our **genetics**, **brain structure**, and **neurochemistry**.

### Core Assumptions
1.  **Biological Basis:** All behaviour, cognition, and emotion have a physical basis in the body (brain structure, genetics, neurochemistry, or hormones). Everything psychological is at first biological. To understand behavior, we must look at the brain, [[Genes]], and hormones.
2.  **Genetics:** [[Genes]] inherited from parents influence behaviour (e.g., intelligence, personality, mental illness).
3.  **Localisation:** Specific areas of the brain are responsible for specific functions (e.g., [[Hippocampus]] for memory).
4.  **Neurochemistry:** Neurotransmitters (like serotonin and [[Dopamine]]) and hormones regulate behaviour.
5.  **Evolution**: Behaviors have evolved over time through natural selection to help us survive and reproduce.

## Core Studies (OCR)

### 1. Regions of the Brain (Localisation)
*   **Classic: [[Sperry (1968)]]** - Investigated split-brain patients to show lateralisation of function (Left = Language, Right = Spatial).
*   **Contemporary: [[Casey et al. (2011)]]** - Investigated the neural basis of delay of gratification (marshmallow test) in the **[[Ventral Striatum]]** (reward) and **[[Inferior Frontal Gyrus]]** (control).

### 2. Brain Plasticity
*   **Classic: [[Blakemore and Cooper (1970)]]** - Showed that the visual cortex in kittens adapts to environmental stimuli (vertical/horizontal stripes), proving physiological plasticity.
*   **Contemporary: Maguire et al. (2000)** - Showed that London Taxi drivers had larger posterior **[[Hippocampus]]** volume due to spatial navigation training.

## Key Concepts

### Brain Structure
*   **[[Corpus Callosum]]:** The bridge cut in Sperry's study.
*   **[[Amygdala]]:** Involved in emotion; relevant to Casey (though Casey focused on Striatum).
*   **[[Hippocampus]]:** Memory and spatial navigation (Maguire).

### Evaluation of the Area

| Strengths | Weaknesses |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Scientific Methods:** Uses objective methods like fMRI, PET, and lab experiments (e.g., Sperry, Casey). High control and reliability. **Highly Scientific**: Uses objective, high-tech equipment like [[FMRI]], [[PET]], and [[EEG Scan]], which provides hard evidence. | **Reductionist:** Reduces complex human behaviour to simple biological parts (neurons/genes), ignoring social or cognitive factors. **Biological Reductionism**: Over-simplifies complex human behavior by reducing it to just neurons and chemicals. |
| **Useful Applications:** Leads to drug treatments (for depression/schizophrenia) and understanding of brain damage/surgery. **Practical Applications**: Led to the development of drug treatments (e.g., antidepressants) and surgery. | **Deterministic:** Suggests behaviour is determined by biology (biological determinism), ignoring free will. **Determinism**: Assumes we have no free will; if your brain is "wired" a certain way, you *must* behave that way. |
| **Strong Evidence for Nature**: Provides clear data on how biology influences traits like intelligence or aggression. | **Nature over Nurture**: Often ignores the role of the environment and upbringing in shaping behavior. |
