## Overview: The Brain in OCR A Level Psychology
In OCR Psychology, knowledge of neuroanatomy, cerebral localisation, and neural plasticity is assessed directly through the **Biological Area**, core studies, and methodological evaluations. 

All comprehensive biological and anatomical notes are maintained in the Human Biology specification under **Unit F176 (The Brain)**. Below are direct links to each anatomical structure organized by OCR syllabus themes and core studies.

---

## 1. Hemispheric Lateralisation and Split-Brain Research
- **Core Study**: Sperry (1968) Hemispheric Deconnection
- **Key Anatomical Structures**:
  - [[Corpus Callosum]]: Dense white-matter tract connecting left and right hemispheres; surgically severed (commissurotomy) in Sperry's patients to treat severe epilepsy.
  - [[Occipital Lobe]]: Houses the primary visual cortex (V1); responsible for processing contralateral visual half-fields (left visual field -> right hemisphere; right visual field -> left hemisphere).
  - [[Frontal Lobe]]: Mediates language production and motor responses based on lateralised visual perception.

---

## 2. Delay of Gratification and Neural Impulse Control
- **Core Study**: Casey et al. (2011) Neural Correlates of Delay of Gratification
- **Key Anatomical Structures**:
  - [[Prefrontal Cortex]]: Inferior frontal gyrus and ventral prefrontal regions mediating top-down executive cognitive control, self-regulation, and resisting temptation.
  - [[Inferior Frontal Gyrus]]: Shows increased fMRI activation in high delayers when resisting alluring "cool" or "hot" social cues.
  - [[Ventral Striatum]]: Deep subcortical reward hub (encompassing the nucleus accumbens) exhibiting exaggerated fMRI activation in low delayers when presented with tempting stimuli.

---

## 3. Brain Plasticity and Structural Adaptation
- **Core Studies**: Maguire et al. (2000) London Taxi Drivers; Blakemore and Cooper (1970) Visual Deprivation in Kittens
- **Key Anatomical Structures & Mechanisms**:
  - [[Hippocampus]]: Medial temporal lobe structure responsible for spatial navigation, cognitive mapping, and memory consolidation; posterior volume increases with navigational experience (Maguire).
  - [[Brain Plasticity]]: The capacity of the brain to undergo functional and structural reorganisation via synaptic pruning and axonal sprouting in response to environmental demands.
  - [[Occipital Lobe]]: Demonstrates critical period developmental plasticity; visual cortex neurons fail to develop horizontal response fields if deprived of horizontal stimulation (Blakemore and Cooper).

---

## 4. Localisation of Language and Cortical Function
- **Core Studies**: Broca (Patient Tan), Wernicke
- **Key Anatomical Structures**:
  - [[Broca’s Area]]: Posterior inferior frontal gyrus (typically left hemisphere); coordinates motor speech production.
  - [[Wernicke’s Area]]: Posterior superior temporal gyrus; mediates auditory language comprehension.
  - [[Motor Cortex]]: Precentral gyrus of the frontal lobe; controls voluntary muscular execution.
  - [[Somatosensory Cortex]]: Postcentral gyrus of the parietal lobe; processes somatic sensations (touch, pressure, pain, proprioception).

---

## 5. Emotional Processing, Stress, and Homeostasis
- **Key Anatomical Structures**:
  - [[Amygdala]]: Subcortical limbic structure mediating fear conditioning, emotional memory, and rapid threat appraisal.
  - [[Hypothalamus]]: Gathers autonomic and endocrine inputs; coordinates the sympathetic nervous system and the [[HPA Axis (Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Adrenal Axis)]].
  - [[Thalamus]]: Primary sensory relay station directing sensory signals to the cerebral cortex and amygdala.
  - [[Basal Ganglia]]: Deep grey matter nuclei regulating movement execution and procedural motor habits.
  - [[Cerebellum]]: Controls balance, motor posture, and fine coordination.
  - [[Brainstem]]: Regulates essential autonomic life-support functions (heart rate, respiration).

---

## 6. Neuroimaging Techniques in Psychological Research
- [[FMRI]]: Functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging; measures blood-oxygen-level-dependent (BOLD) signals to map neural activity during cognitive tasks (e.g. Casey et al.).
- [[MRI]]: Magnetic Resonance Imaging; provides high-resolution static structural scans of grey matter volume (e.g. Maguire et al. VBM and pixel counting).
- [[PET]]: Positron Emission Tomography; measures metabolic radioactive tracer uptake (e.g. Raine et al. brain abnormalities in murderers).
- [[EEG Scan]]: Electroencephalography; records continuous electrical field potentials across the scalp with high temporal resolution.
