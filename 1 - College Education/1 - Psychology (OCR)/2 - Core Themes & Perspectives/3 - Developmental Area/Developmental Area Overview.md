## Developmental Area Overview

**Developmental Psychology** focuses on how people change and grow over time, from birth through adulthood. It assumes that childhood is a critical period that shapes our adult behavior.

### Core Assumptions
1. **Change over Time**: People change their behavior and thinking patterns as they age due to biological maturation and experience.
2. **Impact of Childhood**: Early life experiences are more influential than later ones in determining our personality and skills.

### Strengths and Weaknesses

| Strengths | Weaknesses |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Long-Term Understanding**: Provides a complete picture of how behavior develops from childhood to adulthood. | **Ethical Issues**: Researching children is difficult as they cannot give informed consent and may be easily distressed (e.g., [[Bandura et al. (1961)]]). |
| **Practical Applications**: Influences education, parenting styles, and childcare policies. | **Sample Attrition**: Longitudinal studies (tracking the same people over years) often suffer from participants dropping out. |
| **[[Nature vs Nurture]]**: Effectively explores the interaction between innate maturation and environmental learning. | **Determinism**: Can suggest that if you have a "bad" childhood, your future is set in stone. |

### Key Themes and Studies
- **External Influences on Children's Behavior**: How do we learn from our environment?
    - *Study*: [[Bandura et al. (1961)]] (Social Learning Theory / Bobo Doll)
    - *Study*: [[Chaney et al. (2004)]] (Operant Conditioning / Funhaler)
- **Moral Development**: How do we learn right from wrong?
    - *Study*: [[Kohlberg (1968)]] (Stages of Moral Development)
    - *Study*: [[Lee et al. (1997)]] (Cultural differences in lying/truth-telling)
