## Individual Differences Area Overview

The **Individual Differences** perspective focuses on what makes us unique rather than what we have in common. It aims to understand the variations in human behavior, particularly in terms of **personality**, **intelligence**, and **mental health**.

### Core Assumptions
1. **Uniqueness**: Every person is unique, and to understand behavior, we must study the individual rather than looking for general laws.
2. **Abnormality**: We can understand "normal" behavior by studying "abnormal" behavior or people who differ from the majority.

### Strengths and Weaknesses

| Strengths | Weaknesses |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Holistic Approach**: Focuses on the whole person and their unique life story (especially in case studies). | **Low Generalisability**: Because it focuses on unique individuals or small groups, the findings may not apply to everyone else. |
| **Practical Applications**: Essential for the diagnosis and treatment of mental health disorders (e.g., Autism, OCD, Phobias). | **Subjectivity**: Some methods (like [[Freud (1909) Little Hans]]'s psychoanalysis) are based on interpretation and are not scientific. |
| **Challenge to General Laws**: Reminds psychologists that "one size does not fit all" when explaining behavior. | **Ethical Issues**: Labelling someone as "abnormal" can lead to stigma and discrimination. |

### Key Themes and Studies
- **Understanding Disorders**: How can we define and measure mental health conditions?
    - *Study*: [[Baron-Cohen et al. (2001)]] (Autism and Theory of Mind / Eyes Task)
    - *Study*: [[Freud (1909) Little Hans]] (Little Hans / Psychosexual Development)
- **Measuring Differences**: Can we quantify intelligence or personality?
    - *Study*: [[Gould (1981)]] (A Nation of Morons / Yerkes' IQ Tests)
    - *Study*: [[Hancock et al. (2011)]] (Language of Psychopaths)
