## Core Principles and Assumptions
- Focuses strictly on observable, measurable behaviour rather than internal, unobservable mental processes.
- Assumes that all behaviour is learned through interaction with the environmental context (empiricism and nurture).
- **Tabula Rasa**: Originally proposed by philosopher John Locke and adopted by John Watson; asserts that the mind is born as a blank slate and that all habits, traits, and behaviours are acquired through experience.
- The fundamental laws of learning are shared across species, justifying the application of animal research (such as rats, pigeons, and dogs) to human behaviour.

## Classical Conditioning (Pavlov)
- Learning through association, identified by Ivan Pavlov in experiments on canine salivation.
- A neutral stimulus is repeatedly paired with an unconditioned stimulus that reflexively produces an unconditioned response. Over time, the neutral stimulus becomes a conditioned stimulus that produces a conditioned response.

| Stage | Stimulus | Response |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Before Conditioning** | Unconditioned Stimulus (Food) | Unconditioned Response (Salivation) |
| **Before Conditioning** | Neutral Stimulus (Bell) | No Salivation (No relevant response) |
| **During Conditioning** | Neutral Stimulus (Bell) + Unconditioned Stimulus (Food) | Unconditioned Response (Salivation) |
| **After Conditioning** | Conditioned Stimulus (Bell) | Conditioned Response (Salivation) |

- **Stimulus Generalisation**: A conditioned response is elicited by stimuli that share perceptual similarities with the original conditioned stimulus.
- **Extinction**: The gradual weakening and eventual disappearance of a conditioned response when the conditioned stimulus is presented repeatedly in the absence of the unconditioned stimulus.
- **Spontaneous Recovery**: The sudden reappearance of an extinguished conditioned response after a period of rest or delay.

## Operant Conditioning (Skinner)
- Learning through consequences, formulated by B.F. Skinner using the Skinner Box operant chamber.
- Organisms operate on their environment; behaviour followed by desirable outcomes is strengthened, whereas behaviour followed by undesirable outcomes is weakened.

| Consequence | Operational Mechanism | Impact on Behaviour | Practical Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Positive Reinforcement** | Delivering a rewarding or pleasant stimulus immediately following a behaviour | Increases frequency of behaviour | Awarding praise or tokens to a student for completing work |
| **Negative Reinforcement** | Removing or preventing an aversive or painful stimulus following a behaviour | Increases frequency of behaviour | Taking medication to stop pain; fastening a seatbelt to silence a buzzer |
| **Positive Punishment** | Administering an unpleasant or noxious stimulus following a behaviour | Decreases frequency of behaviour | Issuing a formal detention or sanction for misconduct |
| **Negative Punishment** | Confiscating or revoking a pleasant stimulus following a behaviour | Decreases frequency of behaviour | Revoking privileges or confiscating a mobile device |

## Evaluation of the Behaviourist Perspective

| Strengths | Weaknesses |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Scientific Methodology**: Uses controlled laboratory experiments with clear operationalisation of variables, yielding high reliability and replicability. | **Environmental Determinism**: Denies free will; assumes human action is solely governed by past reinforcement histories and environmental stimuli. |
| **Real-World Applications**: Forms the foundation of clinical behaviour therapies (systematic desensitisation, token economies, applied behaviour analysis). | **Mechanistic Reductionism**: Ignores cognitive mediational processes, emotional states, and biological or genetic predispositions. |
| **Relevance to Core Studies**: Supported by studies such as Chaney et al. (Funhaler positive reinforcement improving medical compliance) and Bandura et al. (social learning theory). | **Extrapolation Issues**: Limits to generalising findings from animal subjects (rats, pigeons) directly to complex human social behaviour. |
