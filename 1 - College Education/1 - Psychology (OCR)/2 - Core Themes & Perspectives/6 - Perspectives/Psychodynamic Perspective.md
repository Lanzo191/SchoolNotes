## Core Principles and Assumptions
- Human behaviour is primarily driven by powerful unconscious psychological forces, conflicts, and desires that are beyond conscious awareness.
- Early childhood experiences (particularly during psychosexual development) exert a lasting influence on adult personality and behavioural patterns.
- Psychological conflicts between innate biological drives and societal moral standards generate anxiety, which the conscious mind manages through **defence mechanisms**.

## Tripartite Structure of Personality

| Component | Operating Principle | Developmental Timing | Role in Behaviour |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Id** | **Pleasure Principle** | Present at birth | Entirely unconscious; demands immediate gratification of primitive biological drives and impulses without moral consideration. |
| **Ego** | **Reality Principle** | Develops around age 2 | Conscious mediator; balances the impulsive demands of the Id against the moral constraints of the Superego using realistic strategies. |
| **Superego** | **Morality Principle** | Develops around age 5 (end of Phallic stage) | Internalised societal values, moral conscience, and ideals instilled by parents; punishes the Ego through guilt for wrongdoings. |

## Psychosexual Stages of Development

| Stage | Age Range | Focus of Libidinal Energy | Major Developmental Conflict | Consequence of Fixation in Adulthood |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Oral** | 0 - 18 months | Mouth (sucking, biting, swallowing) | Weaning from breast or bottle milk | Smoking, nail-biting, sarcasm, dependence, or gullibility. |
| **Anal** | 18 months - 3 years | Anus (withholding and expelling faeces) | Bowel and bladder training | **Anal-retentive** (perfectionism, obsession with order) or **Anal-expulsive** (messiness, disorganisation). |
| **Phallic** | 3 - 6 years | Genitals | **Oedipus Complex** (boys) / **Electra Complex** (girls) | Recklessness, narcissism, sexual dysfunction, or difficulty forming relationships. |
| **Latency** | 6 years - puberty | Dormant sexual feelings | Intellectual, peer, and athletic socialization | N/A (libidinal energy redirected into social and academic skill development). |
| **Genital** | Puberty - adulthood | Mature sexual interests in others | Forming adult romantic and sexual relationships | Healthy adult sexuality if previous fixations were successfully resolved. |

## Ego Defence Mechanisms
- Unconscious protective strategies deployed by the Ego to shield itself from conflict, shame, and anxiety generated by the Id and Superego:
  - **Repression**: Forcing distressing or unacceptable thoughts and traumatic memories out of conscious awareness into the unconscious mind.
  - **Denial**: Refusing to acknowledge the painful reality of an external event, diagnosis, or behaviour.
  - **Displacement**: Redirecting emotional impulses or hostility from the original threatening target onto a safer, less threatening substitute object or person.

## Evaluation of the Psychodynamic Perspective

| Strengths | Weaknesses |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Pioneering Clinical Application**: Founded modern psychotherapy and psychoanalysis ("talk therapy"), establishing that psychological trauma can be addressed through dialogue. | **Unfalsifiable and Unscientific**: Core concepts (unconscious mind, Oedipus complex, Id/Ego/Superego) cannot be empirically measured or tested, violating the criterion of falsification (Karl Popper). |
| **Influence on Psychological Theory**: Highlighted the profound role of early childhood attachment, trauma, and unconscious cognitive processing long before modern cognitive science. | **Subjective Methodology**: Relies heavily on retrospective, idiographic case studies (such as **Freud's Little Hans**) with high risk of researcher bias and lack of population generalisability. |
| **Relevance to Core Studies**: Supported by **Freud (1909)**, who analysed a young boy's phobia of horses as displaced anxiety stemming from the Oedipus complex. | **Psychic Determinism**: Suggests that all behaviour, slips of the tongue (Freudian slips), and symptoms are determined by unconscious conflicts, denying human free will. |
