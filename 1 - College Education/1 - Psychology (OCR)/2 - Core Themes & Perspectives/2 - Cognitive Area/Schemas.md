## Schemas

### Definition
Schemas are **mental frameworks** or cognitive structures that help us organize and interpret information based on our past experiences and knowledge. They are mental shortcuts that allow us to process information quickly and efficiently.

### Characteristics
-   **Organized Knowledge:** Schemas represent organized knowledge about objects, events, people, or situations.
-   **Influence Perception:** They guide our attention to relevant information and influence how we perceive new stimuli.
-   **Influence Memory:** Schemas can affect what information we encode, store, and retrieve. They help us fill in gaps in our memory but can also lead to distortions.
-   **Dynamic:** Schemas are not fixed; they can change and evolve as we gain new experiences and information.

### Types of Schemas
-   **Scripts:** Schemas about sequences of events (e.g., a restaurant script: enter, wait to be seated, order food, eat, pay, leave).
-   **Self-Schemas:** Schemas about ourselves, including our personality traits, values, and beliefs.
-   **Social Schemas/Stereotypes:** Schemas about groups of people.

### Role in Cognitive Processing
-   **Efficiency:** Schemas enable rapid processing of information by providing a framework for understanding new situations.
-   **Prediction:** They help us anticipate what is likely to happen in a given situation.
-   **Interpretation:** Schemas allow us to make sense of ambiguous information.

### Disadvantages/Biases
-   **Distortion of Memory:** Schemas can lead to memory distortions, as we might recall events in a way that is consistent with our schema, even if it didn't happen that way (e.g., [[Memory - Loftus and Palmer (1974)]] on reconstructive memory).
-   **Confirmation Bias:** We tend to pay more attention to and remember information that confirms our existing schemas.
-   **Stereotyping:** Social schemas can lead to oversimplified and often negative generalizations about groups of people.
-   **Resistance to Change:** Established schemas can be difficult to change, even in the face of contradictory evidence.

### Connection to other Concepts
-   **Reconstructive Memory:** Schemas are a key component of how memory is reconstructed.
-   **Eyewitness Testimony:** The influence of schemas can explain inaccuracies in eyewitness accounts.
-   **Problem Solving:** Existing schemas can sometimes hinder creative problem-solving (functional fixedness).
