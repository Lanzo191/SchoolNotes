## The Cognitive Area - Core Concepts Overview

## Foundational Concepts
The **Cognitive Area** compares the human mind to a computer. It assumes that **internal mental processes** (like memory, perception, and attention) can and should be studied scientifically.

### Core Assumptions
1.  **The Mind as a Computer**: Information is received (input), processed (encoding/storage), and used (output). This analogy suggests that mental processes can be studied in a systematic, objective, and scientific way, similar to how a computer processes information.
2.  **Mental Processes**: Behavior is a result of internal mental processes (like memory, perception, and attention) that cannot be observed directly but can be inferred through observable behavior and experiments.

## Defining Principles and Concepts
-   **Focus on Internal Mental Processes:** The cognitive perspective emphasizes the study of mental processes such as perception, memory, thinking, language, problem-solving, and attention. It views humans as information processors.
-   **Analogy to Computers:** Often compares the human mind to a computer, with sensory input, processing (e.g., [[Schemas]]), and output (behaviour).
-   **[[Schemas]]:** Mental frameworks that help organize and interpret information. They are built from experience and can influence how we perceive and remember things. **Schemas are crucial for cognitive processing, helping us rapidly interpret new information based on past experiences. However, they can also lead to biases, distorting our perception and memory to fit pre-existing expectations.**
-   **Models of Memory:** Such as the [[Multi-Store Model of Memory]] (Atkinson & Shiffrin) or the [[Working Memory Model]] (Baddeley & Hitch), which propose different stores and processes for memory.
-   **Experimental Methods:** Primarily uses [[Laboratory Experiments]] to objectively study internal mental processes, making inferences based on observable behavior.
