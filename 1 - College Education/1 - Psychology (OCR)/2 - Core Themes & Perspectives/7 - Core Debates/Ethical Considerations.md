## Overview
The ethical considerations debate evaluates whether psychological research priorities the safety, dignity, and rights of participants versus the scientific value and social benefit of the research findings.

## Core Concepts

| Guideline | Description | Key Issues |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Informed Consent** | Participants must be fully aware of the aims and procedures of the research before agreeing to take part. | Deception, lack of awareness (e.g. observational studies). |
| **Protection from Harm** | Participants must not experience physical or psychological distress beyond what is normal in everyday life. | Stress, anxiety, embarrassment, physical risk. |
| **Right to Withdraw** | Participants must be free to leave the study at any point without negative consequences. | Pressure to continue, payment incentives. |
| **Confidentiality** | All participant personal data must be kept private and anonymized. | Publishing details that can identify participants. |
| **Social Sensitivity** | Research that has potential social consequences or implications, either for the participants or the group they represent. | Stigmatization, political consequences, discrimination. |

## Application to OCR Core Studies

### Highly Ethical Studies
- **Grant et al. (1998)**: Followed standard ethical guidelines, using willing university students who gave informed consent and suffered no harm.
- **Chaney et al. (2004)**: Obtained parent consent before children used the Funhaler, ensuring positive reinforcement without harm.

### Ethically Challenged Studies
- **Milgram (1963)**: Deceived participants (about the shock generator and confederate), caused severe distress (seizures, trembling), and made it difficult to withdraw due to the experimenter's prods.
- **Piliavin et al. (1969)**: Conducted covert observations on a subway train without consent, participants could not withdraw from the situation, and no debriefing was possible.

### Socially Sensitive Studies
- **Gould (1982)**: Reviewed Yerkes' intelligence testing, showing how culturally biased IQ tests led to the stigmatization of certain racial groups and influenced restrictive US immigration laws.
- **Baron-Cohen et al. (2001)**: Investigated cognitive deficits in people with autism, which is socially sensitive as it deals with a clinical diagnosis.

## Evaluation

### Strengths of Conducting Ethical Research
- Protects the reputation of psychology as a profession.
- Ensures the safety and well-being of participants.
- Encourages public trust, making people more willing to participate in future research.

### Weaknesses of Strict Ethical Constraints
- Can limit the types of research that can be conducted (e.g. studying obedience under extreme pressure).
- Demand characteristics can occur if participants are fully informed, reducing validity.
- Naturalistic, covert observations are difficult to conduct ethically, but are crucial for ecological validity.
