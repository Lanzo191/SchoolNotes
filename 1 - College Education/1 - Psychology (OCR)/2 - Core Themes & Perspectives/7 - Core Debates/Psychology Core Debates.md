In the OCR specification, you are required to use these **6 core debates** to evaluate studies and areas. This is the key to scoring high marks in Component 2 and 3.

## 1. Nature vs. Nurture
- **Nature**: Behavior is the result of innate, biological factors (Genetics, brain structure).
- **Nurture**: Behavior is the result of environmental influences (upbringing, learning, culture).

## 2. Freewill vs. Determinism
- **Freewill**: Individuals have the power to make choices and are responsible for their actions.
- **Determinism**: Behavior is controlled by internal or external forces (e.g., biology or environment).
    - **Biological Determinism**: Your brain/[[Genes]] control you.
    - **Environmental Determinism**: Your upbringing/surroundings control you.

## 3. Reductionism vs. Holism
- **Reductionism**: Breaking down complex behavior into simple parts (e.g., explaining a disorder with just one chemical).
- **Holism**: Looking at the whole person and the "bigger picture" to explain behavior.

## 4. Individual vs. Situational Explanations
- **Individual**: Behavior is caused by a person's unique personality or internal traits.
- **Situational**: Behavior is caused by the environment or social context (e.g., being in a crowd).

## 5. Psychology as a Science
- **Scientific**: Uses objective, measurable, and replicable methods (e.g., Lab experiments).
- **Non-Scientific**: Uses subjective, qualitative, or non-falsifiable methods (e.g., Case studies like Freud).

## 6. Ethical Considerations
- Does the research follow BPS guidelines? (Informed consent, protection from harm, deception, etc.).
- Is the "social sensitivity" of the research considered?

---

## How to use in an Essay:
1. **Identify**: "One debate relevant to this study is..."
2. **Define**: Define the debate (e.g., "Reductionism is...").
3. **Apply**: Explain how the study fits into that debate (e.g., "Sperry is reductionist because...").
4. **Evaluate**: Give a strength/weakness of taking that side of the debate.
