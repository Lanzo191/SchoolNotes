## Overview
The holism vs reductionism debate focuses on whether human behavior is best understood by looking at the person as a whole, or by breaking complex behaviours down into simple, constituent components.

## Core Concepts

| Concept          | Definition                                                                                                             | Types / Sub-categories                                             |
| ---------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Holism**       | Explaining behavior by looking at the whole person, their experiences, and the interaction of various complex systems. | Qualitative approaches, Gestalt psychology, Humanistic psychology. |
| **Reductionism** | Explaining complex behavior by breaking it down into simple, fundamental components.                                   | Biological reductionism, environmental reductionism.               |

## Types of Reductionism
- **Biological reductionism**: Explaining behavior solely through biological variables like genetics, biochemistry, and physiology.
- **Environmental reductionism**: Explaining behavior solely through simple stimulus-response connections, ignoring cognitive or biological factors.
- **Experimental reductionism**: Isolating a single variable to study in a laboratory setting, ignoring the wider context of the behavior.

## Application to OCR Core Studies

### Biological Area
- **Sperry (1968)**: Shows **biological reductionism** by explaining cognitive abilities (speech, recognition) through specific physical hemispheres.
- **Casey et al. (2011)**: Highly **reductionist**, as it reduces the ability to resist temptation down to activity in two brain areas.

### Developmental Area
- **Chaney et al. (2004)**: Supports **environmental reductionism** by attributing compliance to positive reinforcement.
- **Kohlberg (1968)**: Somewhat more **holistic**, as it looks at qualitative moral reasoning stages across time, rather than a single chemical or behavioral response.

### Individual Differences
- **Freud (1909)**: Takes a **holistic** approach by analyzing the complex case history of Little Hans, including his family dynamics, dreams, and unconscious mind.

## Evaluation

### Strengths of the Holism Side
- Provides a more complete and realistic explanation of complex human experiences.
- Higher ecological validity, as it does not isolate behavior from real-world contexts.

### Weaknesses of the Holism Side
- Less scientific, as it is difficult to isolate and study multiple interacting variables.
- Harder to establish clear cause-and-effect relationships.
- Difficult to design specific therapies or interventions.

### Strengths of the Reductionism Side
- Highly scientific, allowing for controlled experiments and precise measurements.
- Facilitates the discovery of cause-and-effect relationships.
- Leads to practical applications, such as targeting specific neurotransmitters with drug treatments.

### Weaknesses of the Reductionism Side
- Can over-simplify behavior, ignoring the social, cognitive, and emotional factors that influence how people act.
- Lacks ecological validity by isolating variables in artificial laboratory settings.

### How do each area and core studies link to the holism – reductionism debate?

| Area                       | Link to area / perspective                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    | Link to core studies                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| :------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Social**                 | Generally **holistic** as it looks at the broader social context, group dynamics, and how the presence of others shapes behavior, rather than just looking at the individual in isolation. However, specific experimental setups can be **reductionist** by isolating single situational variables.                                           | * **Milgram / Bocchiaro:** Reductionist in how they isolate specific situational factors (e.g., authority figure, uniform) to explain obedience, ignoring individual personality traits. However, they are holistic in acknowledging that the power of the immediate social situation overrides individual morality.                                                                  |
| **Biological**             | Highly **reductionist** (biological reductionism). It explains complex human behaviors, emotions, and cognitions by breaking them down into simpler, fundamental physical components like brain structures, localized functions, neurochemistry, or genetics.                                                                                 | * **Sperry:** Reductionist as it explains behavior and consciousness strictly through the specific localized functions of the left and right hemispheres of the brain.<br><br>* **Casey et al.:** Reductionist because it attributes the ability to delay gratification to specific neural activity in the ventral striatum and prefrontal cortex.                                    |
| **Developmental**          | Can lean towards **holistic** because it considers how behaviors develop over time through an interaction of factors (nature, nurture, cognitive maturation, and environment), though specific developmental theories can isolate single mechanisms.                                                                                          | * **Lee et al.:** Relatively holistic as it evaluates how moral development is influenced by both age-related cognitive changes and contrasting cultural environments (China vs. Canada).<br><br>* **Bandura et al.:** Can be viewed as reductionist as it focuses primarily on environmental observation and imitation as the single cause for acquiring aggression.                 |
| **Individual Differences** | Tends to be **holistic** because it values the uniqueness of individuals (idiographic approach) and looks at a person's entire configuration of traits. However, psychometric testing can be **reductionist** by reducing complex human traits to a single test score.                                                                        | * **Baron-Cohen:** Reductionist because it reduces the complex experience of Autism Spectrum Disorder down to a specific cognitive deficit: a lack of an advanced 'Theory of Mind'.<br><br>* **Hancock et al.:** Reductionist in that it uses quantitative linguistic analysis software to break down psychopathic speech into specific word categories (e.g., focus on basic needs). |
| **Behaviourist**           | Strictly **reductionist** (environmental reductionism). It simplifies complex human behavior down to basic, observable stimulus-response ($S \rightarrow R$) associations and histories of reinforcement, completely ignoring internal cognitive processes or biological factors.                                                             | * **Chaney et al.:** Reductionist because it explains adherence to medical treatment strictly through the mechanisms of operant conditioning (positive reinforcement via the Funhaler).                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| **Psychodynamic**          | Primarily **holistic** because it attempts to understand the whole person by exploring past childhood experiences, a complex unconscious mind, and the interactions between the Id, Ego, and Superego. However, it is occasionally criticized as **reductionist** for explaining all behavior through basic innate sex and aggression drives. | * **Freud (Little Hans):** Holistic because it provides a highly detailed, qualitative, and comprehensive case study of a single child's unique emotional development, phobias, and family dynamics, rather than trying to measure a single isolated variable.                                                                                                                        |
