## Overview
The nature vs nurture debate focuses on the relative contributions of genetic inheritance (nature) and environmental factors (nurture) to human behavior and development.

## Core Concepts

| Concept            | Definition                                                                                                            | Key Influences                                                                       |
| ------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Nature**         | Behavior is the product of innate, biological, and genetic factors.                                                   | Genetics, brain structure, neurochemistry, evolutionary adaptation.                  |
| **Nurture**        | Behavior is the product of environmental influences and learning.                                                     | Upbringing, culture, social context, learning through association and reinforcement. |
| **Interactionism** | Behavior is developed through a complex interaction of both biological predispositions and environmental experiences. | Epigenetic, diathesis-stress model.                                                  |

## Key Arguments for Nature
- **Genetic determinism**: Certain behaviours are hardwired into our genetic code.
- **Maturation**: Human development follows a biological timetable determined by genetics.
- **Evolutionary explanations**: Behavioural traits are selected over generations because they enhance survival and reproduction.
- **Neurobiology**: Brain structure and chemical transmitters directly shape how we think and act.

## Key Arguments for Nurture
- **Tabula rasa**: The belief that humans are born as a blank slate and all behaviours are learned from scratch.
- **Social learning**: Behavior is acquired through observing and imitating models in our social environment.
- **Conditioning**: Behaviours are shaped by associations (classical conditioning) and consequences (operant conditioning).
- **Cultural variations**: Differences in behavior across cultures show that environment shapes expression.

## Application to OCR Core Studies

### Biological Area
- **Sperry (1968)**: Shows how **nature** (the corpus callosum) dictates the communication between brain hemispheres.
- **Casey et al. (2011)**: Highlights how differences in the ventral striatum and prefrontal cortex affect delay of gratification, supporting a biological explanation.

### Developmental Area
- **Bandura et al. (1961)**: Demonstrates **nurture** as children imitated aggressive behavior from adult models.
- **Chaney et al. (2004)**: Shows how positive reinforcement (operant conditioning) using the Funhaler modified behavior, supporting environmental learning.

### Social Area
- **Milgram (1963)**: Demonstrates that situational factors (the environment and authority figure) override personal moral codes, supporting the nurture side.

## Evaluation

### Strengths of the Nature Side
- Highly scientific, using objective and replicable biological measures.
- Leads to practical medical treatments, such as drug therapies for mental health disorders.

### Weaknesses of the Nature Side
- Biologically deterministic, ignoring free will and choice.
- Reductionist, as it simplifies complex behaviors down to genes or chemicals.

### Strengths of the Nurture Side
- Offers optimistic solutions, suggesting that undesirable behaviors can be unlearned.
- High practical application in education, parenting, and behavioral therapy.

### Weaknesses of the Nurture Side
- Environmentally deterministic, suggesting individuals are passive products of their environment.
- Difficult to isolate environmental variables from genetic factors in research.
