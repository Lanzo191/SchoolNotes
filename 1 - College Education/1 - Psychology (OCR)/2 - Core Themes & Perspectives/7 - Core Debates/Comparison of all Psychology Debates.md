# PEEL Paragraphs  4 Markers 

The biological area supports the nature side, whereas the social area supports the nurture side. This is because the biological area argues that behavior is determined by innate factors like genetics, brain structure, and biochemistry, while the social area claims behavior is shaped by the environment and external social context. For example, biological research often investigates how hereditary factors or specific brain regions affect behavior, suggesting we are born with these predispositions. A prime example of this is Casey et al.'s study on self-control, which found that "low delayers" who struggled to resist temptation had distinct, heightened activity in the ventral striatum (the brain's reward center), showing how innate neural architecture drives our impulses. In contrast, the social area focuses on how the immediate situation, presence of others, or cultural norms dictate how we act, implying that behavior is entirely learned through experience after birth. This is powerfully demonstrated by Milgram's classic authority experiment, where ordinary individuals delivered dangerous electric shocks to an innocent person simply due to the situational pressure of an authority figure, proving that the social context dictates human action.
