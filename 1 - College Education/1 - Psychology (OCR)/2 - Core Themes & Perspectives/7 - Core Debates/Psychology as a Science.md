## Overview
The psychology as a science debate examines the extent to which psychological research meets the rigorous criteria of a science, including objectivity, replicability, falsifiability, and control.

## Core Concepts

| Category | Definition | Key Features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Scientific** | Research that relies on objective, empirical, controlled, and replicable methods. | Controlled experiments, physiological measurements, quantitative data, hypothesis testing. |
| **Non-Scientific** | Research that relies on subjective, qualitative, or non-falsifiable interpretations. | Case studies, self-reports, qualitative observations, psychoanalysis. |

## Criteria for Science
- **Objectivity**: Observations are free from personal bias or expectations.
- **Replicability**: The study can be repeated by other researchers to see if they get the same results.
- **Falsifiability**: The theory or hypothesis can be tested and proven false.
- **Control**: Extraneous variables are kept constant to establish cause and effect.
- **Empirical evidence**: Information is gathered directly through observation or experimentation.

## Application to OCR Core Studies

### Scientific Studies
- **Casey et al. (2011)**: Used highly scientific methods (fMRI scans) in controlled laboratory conditions, providing objective and replicable biological measurements.
- **Loftus and Palmer (1974)**: Conducted controlled laboratory experiments with standardised procedures, making the study highly replicable and objective.

### Non-Scientific or Less Scientific Studies
- **Freud (1909)**: Unscientific because it is based on a single case study with subjective interpretations. The theories of the unconscious mind and psychosexual development are **unfalsifiable** (they cannot be proven wrong).
- **Kohlberg (1968)**: Relied on longitudinal self-report interviews, which can suffer from social desirability bias and subjective interpretation of moral stages.

## Evaluation

### Strengths of Scientific Psychology
- Enhances the credibility and academic status of psychology.
- Provides objective, reliable, and generalizable findings.
- Allows for the development of highly effective, evidence-based treatments.

### Weaknesses of Scientific Psychology
- Controlled laboratory experiments often lack ecological validity.
- Tends to collect quantitative data, which can lack depth and ignore the subjective experience of the individual.
- Can be reductionist, missing the wider context of human behavior.
