## Overview
The individual vs situational debate examines whether human behaviour is driven primarily by internal personal factors (disposition, genetics, cognitive traits) or external environmental factors (social pressures, authority figures, physical setting).

## Core Concepts

| Explanation Type | Theoretical Definition | Primary Causal Factors |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Individual (Dispositional)** | Behaviour is caused by unique internal characteristics, personality traits, cognitive structures, or biological makeup. | Genetics, personality, unconscious conflicts, cognitive schemas, intelligence. |
| **Situational** | Behaviour is caused by external circumstances, social roles, the immediate physical environment, or the presence of others. | Authority figures, social group pressure, physical settings, culture, systemic rules. |

## Positions of Psychological Areas and Perspectives

| Area / Perspective | Stance on Debate | Theoretical Rationale & Supporting Evidence |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Social Area** | **Situational** | Suggests behaviour is governed by social context and authority. **Milgram** proved ordinary people commit destructive acts due to situational prestige and experimenter prods. |
| **Behaviourist Perspective** | **Situational** | Rejects internal disposition (Tabula Rasa); all behaviour is acquired through environmental conditioning and reinforcement schedules (**Bandura**, **Chaney**). |
| **Biological Area** | **Individual** | Explains behaviour through unique physiological structures, neurochemistry, and genetic inheritance (**Casey et al.**, **Sperry**). |
| **Individual Differences** | **Individual** | Focuses on idiosyncratic personal traits, atypical development, and unique psychological profiles (**Freud**, **Baron-Cohen et al.**). |
| **Psychodynamic Perspective** | **Individual** | Focuses on internal psychic conflicts, unconscious drives, and personal psychosexual development (**Freud's Little Hans**). |
| **Cognitive Area** | **Interactionist (Both)** | External situational inputs are processed through internal cognitive schemas and memory systems (**Loftus & Palmer**, **Grant et al.**). |
| **Developmental Area** | **Interactionist (Both)** | Behaviour evolves through a dynamic interplay between innate biological maturation and environmental socialisation (**Bandura**, **Chaney**). |

## Application to OCR Core Studies
- **Milgram (1963)**: Demonstrates situational power; 65% of participants administered lethal 450V shocks due to authority pressure in the Yale laboratory setting.
- **Bocchiaro et al. (2012)**: Shows situational control; despite predicting high disobedience or whistleblowing, 76.5% obeyed an unethical request, while personality tests showed no significant predictive power.
- **Piliavin et al. (1969)**: Supports situational factors as the physical condition of the victim (cane vs drunk) determined helping response speed.
- **Casey et al. (2011)**: Demonstrates individual differences in cognitive control resisting alluring stimuli, linked to prefrontal cortex and ventral striatum activity.

## Evaluation of Explanations

| Perspective | Strengths | Weaknesses |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Individual Explanation** | - Recognises human uniqueness and neurodiversity.<br>- Facilitates personalised medical, educational, and therapeutic interventions. | - Risks labelling, social stigmatisation, and fatalistic diagnoses.<br>- Overlooks systemic social inequalities, trauma, and environmental drivers. |
| **Situational Explanation** | - Explains why ordinary people commit harmful acts under coercive systems.<br>- Provides actionable guidance to reform institutions, laws, and physical settings. | - Risks removing personal moral responsibility for misconduct or crimes.<br>- Difficult to study without deception, creating acute ethical dilemmas. |

## 15-Mark Essay Structure Guide
- **Introduction**:
  - Define both individual and situational explanations clearly.
  - State the overarching thesis (e.g. human behaviour is best explained through an interactionist model).
- **Main Body Paragraphs (2 - 3 PEEL Paragraphs)**:
  - **Point**: Present an area/perspective and state its position on the debate.
  - **Evidence**: Provide concrete evidence from a named core study (e.g. Milgram or Casey et al.).
  - **Explain**: Explain how the evidence supports that position and analyse the real-world implications.
  - **Counter-Argument / However**: Introduce the opposing viewpoint with contrasting evidence (e.g. although situational factors predominate in Milgram, 35% still resisted, indicating individual dispositional resilience).
- **Conclusion**:
  - Synthesise which explanation has greater psychological validity.
  - Conclude why an interactionist perspective is generally considered the most comprehensive and useful model.

## Practice Exam Questions and Model Answers

### Q1) Outline one way Milgram's study of obedience is different to Bocchiaro et al.'s study of disobedience and whistleblowing (3 marks)
**Model Answer:** One key difference is the nature of the task demanded by the authority figure. In Milgram's study, participants were instructed to administer direct, escalating physical harm to another human being through electric shocks (destructive obedience). In contrast, Bocchiaro et al.'s study investigated non-violent institutional compliance, where participants were asked to write a persuasive letter to an ethics committee to encourage students to take part in sensory deprivation. Consequently, Milgram's study involved significantly greater immediate psychological distress and physical confrontation than Bocchiaro's administrative task.

---

### Q2) Explain how Bocchiaro et al.'s study relates to the social area of psychology. Support your answer with evidence (3 marks)
**Model Answer:** The social area of psychology states that all human behaviour occurs within a social context and is heavily influenced by the actual, imagined, or implied presence of others. Bocchiaro et al.'s study directly relates to this area by investigating how an authority figure in a formal university environment exerts situational pressure over participants, causing the majority (76.5%) to obey an unethical request rather than openly disobeying (14.1%) or whistleblowing (9.4%). This confirms the social principle that immediate social settings strongly override individual personal morality.
