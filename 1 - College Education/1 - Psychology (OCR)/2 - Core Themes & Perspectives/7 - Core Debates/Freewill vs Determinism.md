## Overview
The freewill vs determinism debate concerns the extent to which humans have choice and control over their own behavior, versus the extent to which behavior is dictated by internal or external forces.

## Core Concepts

| Concept | Definition | Types / Sub-categories |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Freewill** | The capability of individuals to make active choices and have self-determination. | Humanistic psychology (personal agency). |
| **Determinism** | The belief that behavior is caused by internal or external factors outside an individual's control. | Hard determinism, soft determinism, biological, environmental, psychic. |

## Types of Determinism
- **Hard determinism**: The belief that all human behavior has a specific cause and free will is completely impossible.
- **Soft determinism**: The belief that while behavior is constrained by certain factors, individuals still have cognitive control and choice within those limits.
- **Biological determinism**: Behavior is determined by genetics, brain physiology, and evolutionary factors.
- **Environmental determinism**: Behavior is determined by external factors such as conditioning, reinforcement, and socialisation.
- **Psychic determinism**: Behavior is determined by unconscious conflicts and childhood experiences.

## Application to OCR Core Studies

### Biological Area
- **Sperry (1968)**: Demonstrates **biological determinism** as split-brain patients cannot control what information is processed by each hemisphere due to physical separation.
- **Casey et al. (2011)**: Suggests that high delayers and low delayers have genetically or physiologically determined brain differences (ventral striatum vs prefrontal cortex activity).

### Developmental Area
- **Bandura et al. (1961)**: Demonstrates **environmental determinism** since children's aggressive behavior was dictated by the model they observed.
- **Chaney et al. (2004)**: Supports **environmental determinism** as operant conditioning (the Funhaler) shaped the children's compliance.

### Cognitive Area
- **Moray (1959)**: Suggests **soft determinism** because attention is guided by both external stimuli (hearing one's own name) and internal processing.

## Evaluation

### Strengths of the Freewill Side
- High face validity, as it aligns with our everyday experience of making choices.
- Supports the legal system, which holds individuals responsible for their actions.
- Promotes positive mental health, as individuals feel in control of their lives.

### Weaknesses of the Freewill Side
- Unscientific, because it is impossible to measure or falsify the concept of free will.
- Difficult to study under controlled experimental conditions.

### Strengths of the Determinism Side
- Highly scientific, as it allows researchers to isolate cause and effect.
- Makes behavior predictable, which is useful for developing effective treatments (e.g. therapies or medication).

### Weaknesses of the Determinism Side
- Can provide an excuse for criminal or anti-social behavior.
- Ignores cognitive factors and the human capacity for self-regulation.
