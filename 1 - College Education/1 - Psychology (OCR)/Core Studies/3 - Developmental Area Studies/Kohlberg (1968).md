## Kohlberg (1968) - The Child as a Moral Philosopher

### 1. Theoretical Background
*   **Moral Development:** The process through which children develop the ability to distinguish right from wrong based on social and cultural norms.
*   **Cognitive Development:** Piaget’s theory that moral reasoning is linked to a child’s intellectual growth.
*   **Invariance:** The idea that all children must progress through the same stages of development in the same order.

### 2. Aim
To investigate how **moral reasoning** develops through a series of distinct stages from childhood into early adulthood.

### 3. Methodology
*   **Method:** Longitudinal study (following the same group over time).
*   **Design:** **Independent Measures Design** (used when comparing different age groups or cultures).
*   **Sample:** 75 American boys, aged 10 to 16 at the start, followed for 12 years until ages 22 to 28.
*   **Independent Variable (IV):** The **age** of the participants (longitudinal) and their **culture** (cross-cultural comparison).
*   **Dependent Variable (DV):** The **stage of moral reasoning** demonstrated in their responses to moral dilemmas.

### 4. Procedure
1.  Participants were interviewed every 3 years.
2.  They were presented with hypothetical **moral dilemmas** (e.g. the Heinz Dilemma, where a man must decide whether to steal a drug to save his wife).
3.  The researchers recorded the **reasoning** behind their answers, rather than just the "yes" or "no" decision.

### 5. Results (The 3 Levels)
*   **Level 1: Pre-Conventional:** Reasoning is based on **punishment and reward** (Stages 1 and 2).
*   **Level 2: Conventional:** Reasoning is based on **social approval** and maintaining **law and order** (Stages 3 and 4).
*   **Level 3: Post-Conventional:** Reasoning is based on **abstract principles** and universal human rights (Stages 5 and 6).
*   **Findings:** All boys moved through the stages in a fixed order. Not everyone reached the final stage.

### 6. Conclusion
*   Moral development occurs in a predictable, **invariant sequence** of stages.
*   Each stage represents a more advanced and logical way of thinking about justice.
*   While the speed of development may vary, the **order of stages** remains the same across cultures.

### 7.  Evaluation & Improvements

| Feature                      | Evaluation & Details                                                                                                                              |
| :--------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Research Method**          | **Longitudinal Study**: Followed the same group for 12 years, providing a clear picture of how morality changes over time.                        |
| **Design**                   | **Independent Measures**: Compared boys of different ages and cultures (e.g. Mexico, Taiwan) to test for **universality**.                        |
| **Sample**                   | **75 American Boys**: All male sample (**androcentric**), which limits the **generalisability** of the findings to females.                       |
| **Sampling Method**          | **Opportunity Sampling**: Likely selected from schools. While efficient, it lacks diversity in social class.                                      |
| **Data Type**                | **Qualitative Data**: Detailed interview responses provided a rich understanding of the participants' **moral logic**.                            |
| **Ethics**                   | **Good**: The dilemmas were hypothetical and caused no real harm. However, the 12-year commitment was significant.                                |
| **Weakness**                 | **Social Desirability Bias**: Participants may have given answers they thought were "right" rather than what they would actually do in real life. |
| **Reliability Improvements** | **Standardisation**: Every participant was presented with the same dilemmas and followed the same interview schedule.                             |
| **Validity Improvements**    | **Focus on Reasoning**: By ignoring the "yes/no" answer and focusing on the **why**, Kohlberg measured the actual process of morality.            |

### Q1) Outline one level of moral development identified by Kohlberg (2 marks)
**Model Answer:** One level is the **Pre-Conventional level**, typically seen in children aged 4 to 10. At this level, morality is based on external factors such as **avoiding punishment** or seeking personal rewards.

---

### Q2) Explain why this study is considered "androcentric" (3 marks)
**Model Answer:** The study is **androcentric** because the primary sample consisted only of **75 American boys**. By excluding girls, the findings on moral development may not be generalisable to females, who some psychologists (like Carol Gilligan) argue may develop morality based on **care** rather than justice.

---

### Q3) Discuss one strength of the longitudinal design in this research (3 marks)
**Model Answer:** One strength of the **longitudinal design** is that it allows researchers to track the **same individuals** over a long period (12 years). This effectively controls for **participant variables**, ensuring that the changes in moral reasoning observed are truly due to development rather than differences between people.
