##  Chaney et al. (2004) - The Funhaler Study

### 1. Theoretical Background
*   **Operant Conditioning:** A learning process where behavior is shaped by its consequences. **Positive reinforcement** (a reward) increases the likelihood of a behavior being repeated.
*   **Medical Compliance:** The extent to which a patient follows health advice or takes prescribed medication.
*   **Technique:** In asthma treatment, using the inhaler correctly is vital for the medication to reach the lungs.

### 2. Aim
To investigate whether an **incentive toy** (the Funhaler) would increase medical compliance and improve inhalation technique in children with asthma compared to a standard inhaler.

### 3. Methodology
*   **Method:** Field experiment.
*   **Design:** **Repeated Measures Design**.
*   **Sample:** 32 children (22 male, 10 female) aged 1.5 to 6 years with asthma.
*   **Independent Variable (IV):** The type of inhaler used (Standard Inhaler vs. **Funhaler**).
*   **Dependent Variable (DV):** The level of compliance and the success of the inhalation technique (measured via parent interviews).

### 4. Procedure
1.  Parents of children with asthma were interviewed using a questionnaire about their current use of a standard inhaler.
2.  Participants were then given the **Funhaler** to use at home for two weeks.
3.  The Funhaler included a **whistle** and a **spinning disk** that only activated if the child breathed in deeply and correctly.
4.  After two weeks, parents were interviewed again with a matched questionnaire to measure changes in compliance and attitude.

### 5. Results
*   **Compliance:** 81% of parents successfully medicated their child the previous day with the Funhaler, compared to only 59% with the standard inhaler.
*   **Technique:** Significant improvement in technique; 80% of children achieved the "4 or more breaths" required with the Funhaler compared to 50% with the standard one.
*   **Attitude:** Children showed more pleasure and less fear/resistance when using the Funhaler.

### 6. Conclusion
*   **Positive Reinforcement** can be used to significantly improve medical compliance in young children.
*   Functional toys that provide immediate rewards for the correct behavior are more effective than standard medical devices.

### 7. Detailed Evaluation & Improvements

| Feature                      | Evaluation & Details                                                                                                                           |
| :--------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Research Method**          | **Field Experiment**: High **ecological validity** as children used the devices in their own homes during their normal routine.                |
| **Design**                   | **Repeated Measures**: Every child used both inhalers, which controlled for **participant variables** like the severity of their asthma.       |
| **Sample**                   | **32 Children**: A relatively small sample size, which may limit the **generalisability** of the results to a broader population.              |
| **Sampling Method**          | **Random Sampling**: Participants were recruited from various clinics. potentially non-representative.                                         |
| **Data Type**                | **Quantitative Data**: Percentages and frequency scores allowed for a clear comparison between the two devices.                                |
| **Ethics**                   | **Very Good**: Informed consent was obtained, and the study actually improved the health outcomes of the children involved.                    |
| **Weakness**                 | **Social Desirability Bias**: Parents might have exaggerated their child's compliance to please the researchers during the interviews.         |
| **Reliability Improvements** | **Standardisation**: Both questionnaires were matched, and the Funhaler device was identical for every child, making it **replicable**.        |
| **Validity Improvements**    | **Counterbalancing**: While not explicitly mentioned, using a **repeated measures** design ensured that each child acted as their own control. |

### Q1) Define "positive reinforcement" in the context of this study (2 marks)
**Model Answer:** **Positive reinforcement** refers to the use of a reward to encourage a behavior. In this study, the **whistle** and **spinning disk** in the Funhaler acted as rewards that only activated when the child used the inhaler correctly, encouraging them to repeat the behavior.

### Q2) Explain one result related to medical compliance (3 marks)
**Model Answer:** The study found that **medical compliance** significantly increased when using the Funhaler. Specifically, **81%** of parents reported they had successfully given their child their medication the previous day using the Funhaler, compared to only **59%** when using the standard inhaler.

**Model Answer:** The study has **high ecological validity** because it was a **field experiment** conducted in the participants' own homes. Children used the inhalers as part of their normal daily routine, meaning the findings on improved compliance are likely to reflect how they would behave in real-life medical situations. weaknesses
