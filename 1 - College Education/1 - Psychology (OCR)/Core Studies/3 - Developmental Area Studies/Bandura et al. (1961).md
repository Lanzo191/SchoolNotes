## Bandura et al. (1961) - Transmission of Aggression through Imitation of Aggressive Models

### 1. Theoretical Background
*   **Social Learning Theory (SLT):** Bandura proposed that we learn not just through direct conditioning (Pavlov/Skinner), but by **observing** and **imitating** role models.
*   **Four Processes:** Attention, Retention, Reproduction, Motivation.
*   **The Blank Slate:** Behaviourists believe we are born "tabula rasa" and learn everything from the environment. Bandura was testing this nurture argument. (John Locke)

### 2. Aims & Hypotheses
**Aim:** To see if children would imitate aggression modelled by an adult, and if this imitation would occur even when the model was no longer present.
**Hypotheses:**
1.  Children observing an aggressive model will reproduce aggressive acts.
2.  Children observing a non-aggressive model will show *less* aggression than those observing no model.
3.  Children will imitate a **same-sex model** more than an opposite-sex model.
4.  **Boys** will be more predisposed to aggression than girls.

### 3. Methodology
*   **Method:** Laboratory Experiment.
*   **Design:** [[Matched Pairs Design|Matched Pairs Design]].
*   **Sample:** 72 children (36 boys, 36 girls) from Stanford University Nursery.
    *   Mean age: 52 months (4 years, 4 months).
*   **Matching:** Children were pre-rated on a 5-point scale for aggression by their nursery teacher and experimenter to ensure groups were balanced (inter-rater reliability was high: r=0.89).

### 4. Procedure (3 Stages)

#### Stage 1: Modelling (10 Minutes)
Children were taken individually to a room.
*   **Aggressive Condition (24 kids):** The adult model assembled Tinkertoys for 1 minute, then turned to a **5-foot Bobo Doll** and attacked it.
    *   **Physical Acts:** Sat on it, punched it in the nose, hit it with a mallet, threw it in the air.
    *   **Verbal Phrases:** "Sock him in the nose", "Hit him down", "Pow", "Kick him".
    *   **Non-Aggressive Phrases:** "He sure is a tough fella", "He keeps coming back for more".
*   **Non-Aggressive Condition (24 kids):** The model played quietly with Tinkertoys and ignored the Bobo Doll completely.
*   **Control Group (24 kids):** No model seen.

#### Stage 2: Aggression Arousal (2 Minutes)
*   **Purpose:** To provoke anger/frustration so that *any* aggression shown wasn't just "boredom".
*   Child was taken to a room with attractive toys (fire engine, doll set).
*   After 2 minutes, the experimenter stopped them, saying these were "her very best toys" and reserved for other children, but they could play with the toys in the *next* room.

#### Stage 3: Test for Delayed Imitation (20 Minutes)
*   Child taken to a third room with aggressive toys (mallet, peg board, dart guns, Bobo doll) and non-aggressive toys (crayons, dolls, bears).
*   **Observation:** Experimenters watched through a one-way mirror, recording behaviour every **5 seconds** (240 [[Observations]] per child).

### 5. Results (Detailed)

#### A. Imitative Aggression
*   Children in the **Aggressive Model** condition showed significantly more physical and verbal aggression than the other groups.
*   **Specific Acts:** They perfectly mimicked the novel acts (sitting on doll, using the mallet) and the phrases ("Pow!").
*   *Control/Non-Aggressive groups showed virtually NO aggression.*

#### B. Gender Differences
*   **Physical Aggression:** Boys imitated more physical aggression than girls (Mean: 25.8 for boys vs 7.2 for girls).
*   **Verbal Aggression:** No significant difference between boys and girls.
*   **Same-Sex Model:**
    *   Boys were more likely to imitate the **Male Model** (Physical aggression score: 25.8 vs 12.4 with female model).
    *   Girls were more likely to imitate the **Female Model** (Verbal aggression score: 13.7 vs 4.3 with male model).

#### C. Qualitative Comments (The "Why")
*   Children seemed to accept male aggression as "normal" but found female aggression strange.
    *   *Quote about female model:* "That's not the way for a lady to behave."
    *   *Quote about male model:* "That man is a strong fighter like Daddy."

### 6. Conclusion
*   Aggression is a **learned behaviour**, not just an innate instinct.
*   Children will imitate aggression even without a reward (Vicarious Reinforcement not strictly needed).
*   Cultural norms (gender roles) influence what we choose to imitate.

### 7. Evaluation (GRAVE)

| Area | Evaluation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Generalisability** | **Low.** Sample was from the Stanford University nursery (parents likely academics/wealthy). This is a biased sample. Also, it was only American children in the 1960s-child rearing has changed since then. |
| **Reliability** | **High.** Standardised procedure (same toys, same model script, time intervals). The **Inter-Rater Reliability** was tested (r=0.89), meaning observers agreed on what counted as "aggression." |
| **Application** | **Very Useful.** Led to the "9pm Watershed" on TV (censorship) and age ratings on video games/movies to prevent children observing violence. |
| **[[Validity]]** | **Internal:** High (Matched pairs controlled for natural aggression). **Ecological:** Low. Hitting a Bobo doll is not real aggression (it's designed to be hit). The situation is artificial-children don't usually watch an adult attack a toy then get left alone. |
| **[[Ethics]]** | **Poor.** **Protection from Harm:** Children were deliberately frustrated (toy removal) and taught aggressive behaviours they might not have known before. They might have left the study more aggressive than they entered. |

---

### 8. Detailed Evaluation & Improvements

| Feature                      | Evaluation & Details                                                                                                                                                                           |
| :--------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Research Method**          | **Laboratory Experiment**: High control over extraneous variables (toys, timing) establishing clear cause and effect. However, it lacks **ecological validity** due to the artificial setting. |
| **Design**                   | **Matched Pairs Design**: Children pre-rated for aggression and balanced across groups. This increased **internal validity** by controlling for participant variables.                         |
| **Sample**                   | **72 children** (36 boys, 36 girls) from Stanford Nursery. Gender balance is a strength, but parent demographics (academics) limit **generalisability**.                                       |
| **Sampling Method**          | **Opportunity Sampling**: Efficient but resulted in a non-representative sample of the wider population.                                                                                       |
| **Data Type**                | **Mixed Methods**: **Quantitative data** (numerical aggression scores) for comparisons and **qualitative data** (quotes) to explain the "why" (e.g. gender norms).                             |
| **Ethics**                   | **Significant Concerns**: Problems with **protection from harm** due to intentional frustration. Most critically, the study **taught aggression** to children via the models.                  |
| **Weakness**                 | **Demand Characteristics**: Some argue the Bobo doll is a "novelty" toy designed to be hit; children may have thought they were *supposed* to strike it to please the researchers.             |
| **Reliability Improvements** | **Standardisation**: Used identical rooms and scripts. **Inter-rater reliability** was ensured via independent observers (r=0.89).                                                             |
| **Validity Improvements**    | **Control**: Matched pairs controlled for baseline aggression. **One-way mirrors** were used to reduce **demand characteristics** as children didn't know they were watched.                   |

### Q1) Explain the strength of the design of this study (2 marks)
**Model Answer:** One strength of the design is the use of a **matched pairs design**. By pre-rating the children on their baseline aggression levels and matching them across conditions, Bandura ensured that natural aggression did not become a confounding variable, which increased the **internal validity** of the study.

---

### Q2) Explain one way which Bandura's study into aggression can be considered unethical (3 marks)
**Model Answer:** One way the study is unethical is the lack of **protection from harm**. Children were deliberately placed in a frustrating situation during the **aggression arousal** stage, where attractive toys were taken away to provoke them. Furthermore, the children were potentially **taught aggressive behaviours** that they may have continued to use after the study ended, leading to possible long-term psychological harm.

---

### Q3) Explain one issue with validity (3 marks)
**Model Answer:** One issue with validity is the **low ecological validity** of the study. The research took place in a highly artificial laboratory setting where children observed an adult attacking a plastic doll, which is not a common real-life experience. Consequently, hitting a **Bobo doll** (a toy designed to be hit) may not represent genuine **interpersonal aggression**, meaning the results might not generalise to how children behave in real-world social situations.
