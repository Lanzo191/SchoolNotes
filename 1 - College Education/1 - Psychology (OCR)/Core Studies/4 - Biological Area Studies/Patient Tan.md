## Overview of Patient Tan

Patient Tan, also known as Louis Victor Leborgne, was a significant case in the study of language and brain function. He became famous due to his unique speech impairment, which provided crucial insights into the localisation of language in the brain.

## Key Characteristics

### Speech Impairment

- **Limited Speech**: Tan could only say the word "Tan," despite being able to understand spoken language.
- **Comprehension**: He understood others but could not articulate coherent responses.

### Medical Examination

- **Brain Damage**: After Tan's death in 1861, Paul Broca conducted a post-mortem examination and found a lesion in the left [[Frontal Lobe]] of his brain.
- **Broca's Area**: This area is now known as Broca's area, which is critical for speech production.

## Significance in Psychology

### Contributions to Neuroscience

- **Localisation of Function**: Tan's case provided the first clear evidence that specific brain regions are responsible for specific functions, particularly language.
- **Broca's Aphasia**: Damage to [[Broca’s Area]]  leads to a condition known as Broca's aphasia, characterised by slow and effortful speech.

### Broca's Research

- **Further Studies**: Broca studied additional patients with similar speech impairments, reinforcing the idea that the left [[Frontal Lobe]] is essential for language production.
- **Brain Lateralisation**: His findings contributed to the understanding of brain lateralisation, where certain cognitive functions are localised to one hemisphere of the brain.

Tan's case remains a foundational study in psychology and neuroscience, illustrating the relationship between brain structure and language capabilities.
