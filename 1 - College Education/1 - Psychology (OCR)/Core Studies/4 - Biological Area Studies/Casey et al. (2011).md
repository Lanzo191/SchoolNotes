## Casey et al. (2011) - Neural Correlates of Delay of Gratification

### 1. Theoretical Background
*   **Delay of Gratification:** The ability to resist an immediate reward (e.g. a marshmallow) for a larger, later one. This is a key part of **impulse control**.
*   **Ventral Striatum:** A region in the brain (part of the **Basal Ganglia**) associated with rewards, desires, and "hot" processing.
*   **Prefrontal Cortex (PFC):** The region of the brain responsible for high-level "cool" processing, such as decision-making and cognitive control.

### 2. Aims
To investigate whether the ability to **delay gratification** is a stable trait from childhood to adulthood and to identify the **neural correlates** (brain regions) associated with impulse control.

### 3. Methodology
*   **Method:** Quasi-experiment and longitudinal study.
*   **Design:** **Independent Measures Design**.
*   **Sample:** 59 participants who were originally tested as children in the 1960s/70s.
*   **Independent Variable (IV):** Whether the participant was a **High Delayer** or a **Low Delayer** in childhood.
*   **Dependent Variables (DV):**
    1.  Performance on **Go/No-Go** tasks (accuracy and reaction time).
    2.  Brain activity measured by **fMRI** in Experiment 2.

### 4. Procedure
*   **Experiment 1:** Participants performed "Go/No-Go" tasks on a laptop. "Cool" cues were neutral faces, and "Hot" cues were happy or fearful faces. They had to press a button for one face (Go) and withhold for another (No-Go).
*   **Experiment 2:** 26 participants performed the "Hot" Go/No-Go task while inside an **fMRI** scanner to measure brain activity during impulse control.

### 5. Results
*   **Behavioral:** Low delayers made significantly more **false alarms** (errors) on the "Hot" No-Go tasks (e.g. failing to resist a happy face) compared to high delayers.
*   **Neural (fMRI):**
    *   **High Delayers:** Showed more activity in the **Right Inferior Frontal Gyrus** (part of the **PFC**) when withholding responses.
    *   **Low Delayers:** Showed more activity in the **Ventral Striatum** when faced with tempting ("hot") cues.

### 6. Conclusion
*   The ability to delay gratification is a **stable trait** over time.
*   Impulse control involves a balance between the **Prefrontal Cortex** (cognitive control) and the **Ventral Striatum** (reward system).

### 7. Detailed Evaluation & Improvements

| Feature | Evaluation & Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Research Method** | **Longitudinal Study**: Followed participants for 40 years, providing strong evidence that impulse control is a stable trait. |
| **Design** | **Quasi-Experiment**: Naturally occurring IV (delay ability). While realistic, it means researchers didn't manipulate the ability themselves. |
| **Sample** | **59 Participants**: Good for a longitudinal study, though 59 is a relatively small sample for **generalisability**. |
| **Sampling Method** | **Opportunity Sampling**: Participants were those from the original Mischel study who were still available and willing to participate. |
| **Data Type** | **Quantitative Data**: Accuracy scores and **fMRI** blood-oxygen levels provided highly objective and scientific data. |
| **Ethics** | **Very Good**: Informed consent was obtained, and fMRI is a non-invasive, safe procedure. |
| **Weakness** | **Low Ecological Validity**: Performing a Go/No-Go task on a screen while in an fMRI scanner is not how we resist temptation in real life. |
| **Reliability Improvements** | **Standardisation**: The Go/No-Go tasks used the same faces and timings (0.5s exposure) for every participant, ensuring high **replicability**. |
| **Validity Improvements** | **Control**: By using both "cool" and "hot" cues, Casey could prove that the difficulty for low delayers was specifically related to **tempting stimuli**. |

### Q1) Distinguish between "cool" and "hot" cues in this study (2 marks)
**Model Answer:** **"Cool" cues** were neutral faces that did not trigger an emotional response, whereas **"hot" cues** were happy or fearful faces that provided an emotional or tempting stimulus, testing the participant's **impulse control**.

---

### Q2) Describe the role of the Ventral Striatum according to Casey's findings (3 marks)
**Model Answer:** The **Ventral Striatum** is a brain region associated with reward and desire. Casey found that **low delayers** showed significantly more activity in this area when faced with "hot" cues, suggesting that their difficulty in delaying gratification is linked to a more active **reward system** in the brain.

---

### Q3) Discuss one strength of the longitudinal design used in this study (3 marks)
**Model Answer:** One strength of the **longitudinal design** is that it allows researchers to track the same individuals over a long period (40 years). This provides strong evidence that certain behaviors, like **impulse control**, are stable traits that persist from childhood into adulthood, which would not be possible with a cross-sectional study.
