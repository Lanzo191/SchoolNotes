## Blakemore and Cooper (1970) - Brain Plasticity in Kittens

### 1. Theoretical Background
*   **Brain Plasticity:** The brain's ability to change its physical structure and functional organization in response to experience and environmental stimuli.
*   **Critical Period:** A specific window in early development during which the brain is especially sensitive to environmental input.
*   **Nature vs. Nurture:** The debate over whether visual abilities are innate (**Nature**) or learned through experience (**Nurture**).

### 2. Aim
To investigate the physical and behavioral effects of a restricted visual environment (vertical or horizontal stripes) on the development of the **visual cortex** in kittens.

### 3. Methodology
*   **Method:** Laboratory Experiment.
*   **Design:** **Independent Measures Design**.
*   **Sample:** Two kittens (used for detailed neurophysiological testing) and others used for behavioral observation.
*   **Independent Variable (IV):** The orientation of the stripes in the visual environment (**Vertical** vs. **Horizontal**).
*   **Dependent Variable (DV):** Behavioral responses and the electrical activity of neurons in the **visual cortex**.

### 4. Procedure
1.  **Deprivation:** Kittens were kept in total darkness from birth until 2 weeks of age.
2.  **Visual Exposure:** From 2 weeks to 5 months, kittens spent 5 hours a day in a tall cylinder with either vertical or horizontal stripes. They wore a **ruff** (collar) to prevent them from seeing their own bodies.
3.  **Behavioral Testing:** At 5 months, they were moved to a normal room and observed for their ability to navigate and track objects.
4.  **Neurophysiological Testing:** At 7.5 months, micro-electrodes were used to measure the firing of neurons in the **Primary Visual Cortex**.

### 5. Results
*   **Behavioral:** Kittens were initially "blind" to orientations they had not been raised in. A "vertical" kitten would ignore a horizontal rod and vice versa. They also lacked **depth perception** and startle responses initially.
*   **Neural:** In the **vertical** kitten, neurons only fired in response to vertical lines. There were no neurons that responded to horizontal lines, as they had been "re-wired" or died off.
*   **Permanent Deficits:** While basic reflexes returned after 10 hours, the **orientation blindness** was permanent.

### 6. Conclusion
*   The brain is highly **plastic** during the critical period of development.
*   The environment physically changes the structure of the brain by "tuning" neurons to match the visual input received (**Nurture**).

### 7. Detailed Evaluation & Improvements

| Feature | Evaluation & Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Research Method** | **Laboratory Experiment**: High levels of control over the visual environment (dark room, striped cylinders). |
| **Design** | **Independent Measures**: Clear comparison between vertical and horizontal conditions. |
| **Sample** | **Very Small**: Only two kittens were tested neurophysiologically, which limits the **generalisability** of the findings to all kittens or humans. |
| **Sampling Method** | **Laboratory-Bred**: Kittens were raised specifically for the study, ensuring their early history was fully known and controlled. |
| **Data Type** | **Mixed Methods**: **Quantitative data** from electrode recordings and **qualitative data** from behavioral observations (e.g. bumping into legs). |
| **Ethics** | **Poor**: The kittens were subjected to long-term **sensory deprivation**, which caused permanent brain damage. This would be restricted under modern ethical guidelines. |
| **Weakness** | **Species Bias**: While mammalian brains are similar, we cannot be certain that human **visual plasticity** works in the exact same way as a kitten's. |
| **Reliability Improvements** | **Standardisation**: The dimensions of the cylinder and the duration of exposure (5 hours) were identical, making the study highly **replicable**. |
| **Validity Improvements** | **Equipment Control**: The use of a "ruff" ensured that the kittens only saw the stripes and not their own limbs, preventing **extraneous variables**. |

### Q1) Outline one behavioral effect observed in the kittens when first released (2 marks)
**Model Answer:** One behavioral effect was that the kittens were **functionally blind** to orientations they had not experienced; for example, a kitten raised in a vertical world would ignore a horizontal rod and walk directly into it.

---

### Q2) Explain how this study supports the "Nurture" side of the nature-nurture debate (3 marks)
**Model Answer:** The study supports **Nurture** because it demonstrates that the kittens' brain structure was physically altered by their **environment**. The lack of horizontal-sensitive neurons in the vertical-reared kitten proves that visual abilities are not just innate but are shaped by the sensory input received during the **critical period**.

---

### Q3) Discuss the ethical implications of using animals in this study (3 marks)
**Model Answer:** A major ethical implication is the **permanent harm** caused to the kittens, as they suffered from lifelong "orientation blindness" and structural brain changes. While the research provided valuable insight into **brain plasticity**, modern ethical standards for animal research emphasize **minimizing distress**, which was not fully met in this study.
