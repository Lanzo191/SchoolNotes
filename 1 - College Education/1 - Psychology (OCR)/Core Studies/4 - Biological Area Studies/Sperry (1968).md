## Sperry (1968) - Hemisphere Deconnection

### 1. Theoretical Background
*   **Lateralisation of Function:** The theory that each hemisphere of the brain has specialised functions (e.g. the left hemisphere for language).
*   **Contralateral Control:** The left side of the brain controls the right side of the body, and the left hemisphere receives visual input from the **right visual field (RVF)**, and vice versa.
*   **Corpus Callosum:** The bundle of nerve fibres connecting the two hemispheres. In Sperry's participants, this had been severed (**commissurotomy**) to treat severe epilepsy.

### 2. Aim
To investigate the psychological effects of hemisphere deconnection and to show that each hemisphere has its own independent stream of consciousness.

### 3. Methodology
*   **Method:** Quasi-experiment (the independent variable was naturally occurring).
*   **Design:** **Independent Measures Design**.
*   **Sample:** 11 "split-brain" patients who had undergone a commissurotomy.
*   **Independent Variable (IV):** Whether the participant had a split brain or a "normal" brain (though the study focused on the split-brain patients).
*   **Dependent Variable (DV):** Performance on a variety of visual and tactile tasks using a **tachistoscope**.

### 4. Procedure
1.  **Visual Task:** Participants focused on a central point. Images were flashed for **0.1 seconds** (too fast for eye movement) to either the **Left Visual Field (LVF)** or **Right Visual Field (RVF)**.
2.  **Tactile Task:** Participants reached under a screen to feel objects with their hands without seeing them.
3.  **Key-Ring Test:** The word "KEY" was shown to the LVF and "RING" to the RVF. Participants were asked to say what they saw and pick up the object with their left hand.

### 5. Results
*   **Visual:** Objects shown to the **RVF** (left hemisphere) could be described in speech/writing. Objects shown to the **LVF** (right hemisphere) could not be named; participants said they "saw nothing."
*   **Tactile:** If an object was placed in the **left hand** (right hemisphere), the participant could find it again with the same hand but could not describe it.
*   **Key-Ring:** Participants said "RING" but their **left hand** picked up a "KEY." They were unaware of any conflict.

### 6. Conclusion
*   The two hemispheres process information independently.
*   The **left hemisphere** is dominant for language and speech, while the **right hemisphere** is dominant for spatial tasks and non-verbal processing.

### 7. Detailed Evaluation & Improvements

| Feature                      | Evaluation & Details                                                                                                                                                                        |
| :--------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Research Method**          | **Quasi-Experiment**: High control over the visual field using a **tachistoscope**. However, the IV was naturally occurring, so **cause and effect** is harder to prove.                    |
| **Design**                   | **Independent Measures**: Compared split-brain patients to the known behavior of normal people.                                                                                             |
| **Sample**                   | **11 Patients**: A very small and unique sample. All had epilepsy, which may have affected their brain structure before the surgery, limiting **generalisability**.                         |
| **Sampling Method**          | **Opportunity Sampling**: Used patients already undergoing medical treatment. Efficient but not representative of the wider population.                                                     |
| **Data Type**                | **Qualitative Data**: Detailed descriptions of what the participants could and could not do provided rich insight into **lateralisation**.                                                  |
| **Ethics**                   | **Very Good**: The surgery was medically necessary and not performed for the study. Participants gave consent and the tasks were non-stressful.                                             |
| **Weakness**                 | **Ecological Validity**: In real life, split-brain patients move their eyes and heads, allowing information to reach both hemispheres. The deficits found are only visible in lab settings. |
| **Reliability Improvements** | **Standardisation**: The 0.1s flash time was identical for all participants, ensuring that the results were not due to eye movements.                                                       |
| **Validity Improvements**    | **Control**: By using a central fixation point and silent tactile tasks, Sperry ensured that only one hemisphere at a time was being tested.                                                |

### Q1) Explain why images were flashed for only 0.1 seconds in this study (2 marks)
**Model Answer:** Images were flashed for only **0.1 seconds** to ensure they were processed by only one **visual field**. This timing is faster than the eye can move (saccadic movement), preventing the image from being seen by both hemispheres, which is crucial for testing **lateralisation**.

---

### Q2) Outline one finding related to the "Key-Ring" test (3 marks)
**Model Answer:** When the word "KEY" was flashed to the **LVF** and "RING" to the **RVF**, participants said they saw "RING" because the left hemisphere handles speech. However, their **left hand** (controlled by the right hemisphere) picked up a key, demonstrating that the two hemispheres were processing information independently.

---

### Q3) Discuss the generalisability of Sperry's sample (3 marks)
**Model Answer:** The **generalisability** is low because the sample only consisted of **11 participants**, all of whom had severe epilepsy. Their brains may have developed differently due to years of seizures or medication, meaning the results on **lateralisation** might not accurately represent the brain function of the general "normal" population.
