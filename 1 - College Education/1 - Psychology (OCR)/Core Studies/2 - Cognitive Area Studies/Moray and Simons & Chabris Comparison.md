Moray's study focused on selective attention, where participants attended to one auditory message while ignoring others, while Simons and Chabris' study examined inattentional blindness, where participants failed to notice unexpected visual stimuli due to focused attention on a different task.

## Key Comparisons Between Moray and Simons & Chabris

### Type of Attention Investigated

| Study                                            | Type of Attention       | Description                                                                                        |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| [[Moray (1959) Auditory Attention]]              | Selective Attention     | Participants focused on one auditory message while ignoring others in a dichotic listening task.   |
| [[Simons and Chabris (1999) Visual Inattention]] | Inattentional Blindness | Participants failed to notice an unexpected visual object while concentrating on a different task. |

### Methodology

- Moray's Study: Utilised a dichotic listening task where participants repeated one message aloud while ignoring another.
- Simons and Chabris' Study: Involved watching a video of people passing a basketball and counting the passes, during which an unexpected event (a person in a gorilla suit) occurred.

### Findings

- Moray's Findings: Demonstrated that participants could accurately shadow the attended message but were largely unaware of the unattended message's content.
- Simons and Chabris' Findings: Showed that many participants did not notice the gorilla, highlighting how focused attention can lead to missing unexpected stimuli.

These studies illustrate fundamental differences in how auditory and visual attention operate, emphasising the distinct mechanisms of selective attention and inattentional blindness.
