## Loftus and Palmer (1974) - Reconstructive Memory

### 1. Theoretical Background
*   **Reconstructive Memory:** The idea that memory is not a literal record of events but an active process where we fill in gaps using **[[Schemas]]** (past knowledge and expectations).
*   **Leading Questions:** Questions that suggest a specific answer or influence the witness's response through their wording.
*   **Post-Event Information:** Information provided after an event (like a leading question) that can become integrated into the original memory trace.

### 2. Aims
To investigate whether the use of **leading questions** would affect the accuracy of an eyewitness's estimation of speed and their subsequent memory of the event.

### 3. Methodology
*   **Method:** Laboratory Experiment.
*   **Design:** **Independent Measures Design**.
*   **Sample:** 
    *   **Experiment 1:** 45 students.
    *   **Experiment 2:** 150 students.
*   **Independent Variables (IV):**
    *   **Experiment 1:** The **verb** used in the critical question (*smashed, collided, bumped, hit, contacted*).
    *   **Experiment 2:** The **verb** used (*smashed* or *hit*) or no speed question (control).
*   **Dependent Variables (DV):**
    *   **Experiment 1:** The **estimated speed** of the cars.
    *   **Experiment 2:** Whether the participant reported seeing **broken glass** one week later.

### 4. Procedure
*   **Experiment 1:** Participants watched 7 films of traffic accidents. After each, they described the accident and answered questions, including the critical speed question with the varying verb.
*   **Experiment 2:** Participants watched a 1-minute film of a car crash. They were asked about the speed with either "smashed," "hit," or no question. One week later, they were asked "Did you see any broken glass?" (There was none).

### 5. Results
*   **Experiment 1:** The verb "smashed" produced the highest speed estimate (**40.5 mph**), while "contacted" produced the lowest (**31.8 mph**).
*   **Experiment 2:** Participants in the "smashed" condition were significantly more likely to report seeing **broken glass** (32%) compared to the "hit" (14%) and control (12%) groups.
*   **Statistical Analysis:** A **Chi-Squared (χ²) test** confirmed the results of Experiment 2 were statistically significant (χ²(2) = 8.15, p < 0.05).

### 6. Conclusion
*   Leading questions can distort memory by providing **post-event information** that alters the original memory of an event.
*   Memory is **reconstructive**, and information from two sources (the original event and later information) can merge into one "memory."

### 7. Detailed Evaluation & Improvements

| Feature | Evaluation & Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Research Method** | **Laboratory Experiment**: High control over **extraneous variables** (film clips, instructions). However, it lacks **ecological validity** as watching a film is not as stressful as a real accident. |
| **Design** | **Independent Measures**: Avoids **order effects** but can be influenced by **participant variables** (natural memory ability). |
| **Sample** | **Students**: A **biased sample** as students may be more observant or have better memories than older adults, limiting **generalisability**. |
| **Sampling Method** | **Opportunity Sampling**: Efficient but unlikely to be representative of the wider population. |
| **Data Type** | **Quantitative Data**: Numerical speed estimates and frequencies of "yes/no" answers allowed for objective statistical analysis. |
| **Ethics** | **Good**: Used **deception** (hiding the true aim), but it was necessary for **validity**. Participants were not subjected to high stress. |
| **Weakness** | **Demand Characteristics**: Students may have guessed the purpose of the experiment and adjusted their speed estimates to "help" the researchers. |
| **Reliability Improvements** | **Standardisation**: All participants watched the same clips and received identical instructions, making the study highly **replicable**. |
| **Validity Improvements** | **Controls**: Precise control over the verb used and the timing of the "broken glass" question increased **internal validity**. |

### Q1) Explain the difference between the findings of Experiment 1 and Experiment 2 (2 marks)
**Model Answer:** Experiment 1 found that leading questions influenced **immediate speed estimates**, whereas Experiment 2 demonstrated that leading questions could actually **distort memory** for details (like broken glass) that did not exist, showing a more permanent change in recall.

---

### Q2) Outline one way that the study by Loftus and Palmer lacks ecological validity (3 marks)
**Model Answer:** The study lacks **ecological validity** because participants watched **film clips** of car accidents in a controlled lab setting. In a real-life situation, witnessing a crash would involve high levels of **emotional stress** and surprise, which might make the memory more vivid or more impaired compared to watching a passive video.

---

### Q3) Explain how this study links to the Cognitive Area (3 marks)
**Model Answer:** This study links to the **Cognitive Area** because it investigates the internal mental process of **memory**. It specifically explores how information is processed and stored, demonstrating that memory is **reconstructive** and can be altered by external factors like leading questions, which aligns with the cognitive view of the mind as an active information processor.
