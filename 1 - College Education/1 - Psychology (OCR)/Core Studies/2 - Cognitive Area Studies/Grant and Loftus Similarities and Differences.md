#CognitiveArea #Memory #CoreStudies #SimilaritiesAndDifferences

## Overview
Both **Loftus and Palmer (1974)** and **Grant et al. (1998)** are key studies within the **Cognitive Area**, focusing on the key theme of **Memory**. While they both explore how information is processed and recalled, they focus on different aspects of memory: **Reconstructive Memory** and **Context-Dependent Memory**.

---

## Similarities Between Studies

| Feature | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Cognitive Area** | Both investigate the core mental process of **Memory**. |
| **Research Method** | Both used **Laboratory Experiments** with high levels of control. |
| **Data Type** | Both collected **Quantitative Data** for objective statistical analysis. |
| **Experimental Design** | Both primarily used an **Independent Measures Design**. |
| **Sampling Bias** | Both relied on **Student-based samples** (narrow age range/background). |
| **Standardisation** | Both used highly **standardised procedures** to ensure reliability. |

---

## Differences Between Studies

| Feature | Loftus and Palmer (1974) | Grant et al. (1998) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Memory Focus** | **Reconstructive Memory** (Distortion) | **Context-Dependent Memory** (Retrieval) |
| **Sample Size** | **Large Sample**: 195 participants total (45 in Exp 1; 150 in Exp 2). | **Small Sample**: 40 participants total (39 analyzed). |
| **Practical Application** | **Legal System**: Eyewitness Testimony accuracy. | **Education**: Study habits and exam performance. |
| **Memory Task** | **Unintentional**: Watching films of accidents. | **Intentional**: Actively studying a 2-page article. |
| **Type of Cue** | **Internal**: Wording of the leading question. | **External**: Environmental noise or silence. |
| **Time Delay** | **Longer Delay**: One week (in Experiment 2). | **Short Delay**: 2-minute break between study and test. |
| **Ecological Validity** | **Lower**: Passive task lacks emotional impact. | **Higher**: Realistic task (studying) for the participants. |

---

## Exam Context
In **Section B** of the OCR Psychology exam, you may be asked to compare these two studies. 
*   Use **Similarities** to show how they both represent the **Cognitive Area** (e.g., both use scientific, controlled methods to study mental processes).
*   Use **Differences** to show the **breadth of the key theme** (e.g., memory is not just about being "tricked" by questions, but also about how the environment supports or hinders our ability to retrieve what we have learned).
