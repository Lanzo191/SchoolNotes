## Grant et al. (1998) - Context Dependent Memory

### 1. Theoretical Background
*   **Context-Dependent Memory:** The theory that memory is improved when the **environmental context** at the time of retrieval matches the context at the time of encoding (learning).
*   **Retrieval Cues:** Environmental factors (e.g. background noise, smells, room layout) that act as triggers to help "unlock" stored memories.
*   **Cue-Dependency:** The idea that forgetting often occurs because the necessary cues are absent, not because the memory is gone.

### 2. Aims
To investigate whether context-dependent memory effects occur for both **recall** (short answer) and **recognition** (multiple choice) using a realistic study-test task.

### 3. Methodology
*   **Method:** Laboratory Experiment.
*   **Design:** **Independent Measures Design**.
*   **Sample:** 39 participants (17 to 56 years old), recruited by 8 student experimenters.
*   **Independent Variable (IV):** Whether the study and test environments **matched** or **mismatched**.
    1.  **Silent-Silent** (Match)
    2.  **Noisy-Noisy** (Match)
    3.  **Silent-Noisy** (Mismatch)
    4.  **Noisy-Silent** (Mismatch)
*   **Dependent Variables (DV):**
    1.  **Recall score** (10-question short answer test).
    2.  **Recognition score** (16-question multiple choice test).

### 4. Procedure
1.  Participants were randomly allocated to one of the four conditions.
2.  They wore headphones while reading a 2-page article on psychoimmunology.
3.  The "Noisy" condition used a recording of a cafeteria. The "Silent" condition used headphones with no sound.
4.  After a 2-minute break, participants were tested on the article's content in either a silent or noisy environment.

### 5. Results
*   **Matching Conditions:** Participants scored significantly higher when studying and testing in the same environment (**Silent-Silent** mean: 6.7; **Noisy-Noisy** mean: 6.2 on recall).
*   **Mismatching Conditions:** Performance dropped when environments differed (**Silent-Noisy** mean: 4.6; **Noisy-Silent** mean: 5.4 on recall).
*   **Overall Noise:** There was no significant difference in performance based purely on noise; the **match** was the critical factor.

### 6. Conclusion
*   Context-dependent memory effects exist for both recall and recognition.
*   Students should study in an environment that mimics their testing conditions (usually silence) to maximize their memory performance.

### 7. Detailed Evaluation & Improvements

| Feature | Evaluation & Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Research Method** | **Laboratory Experiment**: High control over variables (e.g. 2-minute break, same article). This establishes clear **cause and effect**. |
| **Design** | **Independent Measures**: Prevents **order effects** (fatigue). However, **participant variables** (natural memory differences) could affect the results. |
| **Sample** | **39 Participants**: A fairly small sample. Since it was an **opportunity sample** of acquaintances, it may not be generalisable to the wider population. |
| **Sampling Method** | **Opportunity Sampling**: Efficient and cost-effective, but prone to **sampling bias** as participants were mainly students. |
| **Data Type** | **Quantitative Data**: Test scores provided objective, numerical data that allowed for easy statistical comparison between groups. |
| **Ethics** | **Very Good**: Informed consent was obtained, and the task was not stressful. Participants were fully debriefed at the end. |
| **Weakness** | **Ecological Validity**: While the task (reading an article) was realistic, wearing headphones in a lab is slightly artificial compared to real-world study. |
| **Reliability Improvements** | **Standardisation**: Every participant used the same recording, the same article, and the same test questions, making it highly **replicable**. |
| **Validity Improvements** | **Control**: The use of headphones for both "Silent" and "Noisy" groups ensured that any effect was due to context, not the physical sensation of wearing them. |

### Q2) Explain why the researchers used a "Silent-Silent" and a "Noisy-Noisy" condition (3 marks)
**Model Answer:** These were the **matching conditions** used to test the hypothesis of **context-dependent memory**. By having participants study and test in the same environment, the researchers could compare their scores against the **mismatching conditions** to see if a consistent environment improved recall and recognition.

---

### Q3) Discuss one strength of the data collected in this study (3 marks)
**Model Answer:** One strength is that the study collected **quantitative data** in the form of test scores for recall and recognition. This type of data is **objective** and allowed for direct statistical comparisons between the four groups, helping the researchers to clearly identify a significant difference in the matching versus mismatching contexts.
