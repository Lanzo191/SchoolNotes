## Simons and Chabris (1999) - Visual Inattention

### 1. Theoretical Background
*   **Inattentional Blindness:** The failure to notice a clearly visible but unexpected object when attention is focused on another task or object.
*   **Selective Attention:** The process of focusing on a particular object in the environment for a certain period of time while ignoring other information.
*   **Change Blindness:** A related phenomenon where people fail to notice large changes to a visual scene.

### 2. Aims
To investigate whether **inattentional blindness** occurs in a dynamic, realistic scene and to see if factors like the **difficulty of the task** or the **nature of the unexpected object** influence notice rates.

### 3. Methodology
*   **Method:** Laboratory Experiment.
*   **Design:** **Independent Measures Design**.
*   **Sample:** 228 participants (mostly undergraduate students). Data from 192 participants were used in the final analysis.
*   **Independent Variables (IV):**
    1.  **Unexpected Object:** A woman with an umbrella vs. a person in a **Gorilla** suit.
    2.  **Film Type:** Transparent (superimposed) vs. Opaque (realistic).
    3.  **Task Difficulty:** Easy (counting total passes) vs. Hard (counting bounce vs. aerial passes).
    4.  **Attended Team:** White team vs. Black team.
*   **Dependent Variable (DV):** Whether the participant noticed the unexpected object (Umbrella Woman or Gorilla).

### 4. Procedure
1.  Participants watched a 75-second video of two teams (3 players each) passing a basketball.
2.  They were told to count the passes of either the **White team** or the **Black team**.
3.  Between 44 and 48 seconds, an unexpected object (Umbrella Woman or Gorilla) walked across the screen for 5 seconds.
4.  After the video, participants were asked a series of surprise questions, including: "Did you notice anything unusual?"

### 5. Results
*   **Overall Notice Rate:** **54%** of participants noticed the unexpected object, while 46% failed to see it.
*   **Film Type:** Notice rates were much higher in the **Opaque** condition (67%) than in the **Transparent** condition (42%).
*   **Task Difficulty:** People were more likely to notice in the **Easy** condition (64%) than the **Hard** condition (45%).
*   **Nature of Object:** The **Umbrella Woman** was noticed more often (65%) than the **Gorilla** (44%).
*   **Similarity Effect:** Participants following the **Black team** were more likely to notice the **Gorilla** (which was also black) than those following the White team.

### 6. Conclusion
*   Inattentional blindness is a frequent phenomenon; people can fail to see a prominent object if their attention is engaged elsewhere.
*   We only perceive objects that we are actively attending to.
*   The likelihood of noticing depends on the **difficulty** of the primary task and the **similarity** of the object to the attended target.

### 7. Detailed Evaluation & Improvements

| Feature | Evaluation & Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Research Method** | **Laboratory Experiment**: High control over timing (video length) and standardised instructions. Establish **cause and effect**. |
| **Design** | **Independent Measures**: Prevented participants from expecting the object in a second trial. However, **participant variables** could exist. |
| **Sample** | **228 Participants**: A large sample size which increases **internal validity**. However, being mostly students limits **generalisability**. |
| **Sampling Method** | **Volunteer Sampling**: Participants were often offered candy or a small fee, potentially attracting a specific type of person. |
| **Data Type** | **Quantitative Data**: Yes/No responses on whether the object was seen provided clear, objective percentages for analysis. |
| **Ethics** | **Very Good**: No harm or distress. Participants provided consent and were fully debriefed after the surprise questions. |
| **Weakness** | **Ecological Validity**: Watching a video of a basketball game is not the same as being in a real-world environment with 3D depth and sound. |
| **Reliability Improvements** | **Standardisation**: Using the same video clips and standardized questioning for every participant makes the study highly **replicable**. |
| **Validity Improvements** | **Control**: By using both transparent and opaque films, the researchers could control for how the "visibility" of the object affected attention. |

### Q1) What is meant by the term "inattentional blindness"? (2 marks)
**Model Answer:** **Inattentional blindness** is a psychological phenomenon where an individual fails to perceive an unexpected stimulus that is in plain sight, because their **attention** is fully focused on another demanding task.

---

### Q2) Explain why participants were more likely to notice the Gorilla when they were following the Black team (3 marks)
**Model Answer:** This is due to the **similarity effect**. Because the Gorilla was black, it shared a physical characteristic with the team the participants were actively **attending** to. This shared feature made the Gorilla more likely to be processed by the brain than for those focusing on the White team.

---

### Q3) Discuss one way that task difficulty affected the results of this study (3 marks)
**Model Answer:** The study found that notice rates were significantly lower in the **"Hard" condition** (counting types of passes) compared to the "Easy" condition. This suggests that as the **cognitive load** of the primary task increases, fewer attentional resources are available to detect unexpected objects in the environment.
