## Moray (1959) - Auditory Attention

### 1. Theoretical Background
*   **The Cocktail Party Effect:** The ability to focus on one conversation while ignoring background noise, yet still being able to detect significant information (like your own name).
*   **Broadbent’s Filter Model:** The theory that we have a selective filter that blocks out unattended messages based on physical characteristics before they are processed for meaning.
*   **Dichotic Listening:** A laboratory task where two different messages are played simultaneously to each ear.
*   **Shadowing:** The task of repeating one message (the **attended** message) out loud as it is heard to ensure full focus.

### 2. Aims
To investigate what types of information can "break through" the attentional barrier of a rejected message and whether affective (personally significant) cues can penetrate the filter.

### 3. Methodology
*   **Method:** Laboratory Experiment.
*   **Design:** **Repeated Measures** (Exp 1 & 2) and **Independent Measures** (Exp 3).
*   **Sample:** Undergraduates and research workers (Exp 1 unrecorded; Exp 2: 12; Exp 3: 28).
*   **Independent Variables (IV):**
    *   **Exp 1:** Shadowed vs. rejected message.
    *   **Exp 2:** Instruction preceded by **name** vs. no name.
    *   **Exp 3:** Warning to listen for digits vs. no warning.
*   **Dependent Variables (DV):**
    *   **Exp 1:** Number of words recognized.
    *   **Exp 2:** Frequency of following instructions.
    *   **Exp 3:** Number of digits recalled.

### 4. Procedure
*   **Experiment 1:** A list of neutral words was repeated 35 times in the rejected ear while the participant shadowed a passage in the other ear.
*   **Experiment 2:** Instructions (e.g. "Stop now") were given in the rejected ear. Half were preceded by the participant's name (e.g. "John Smith, stop now").
*   **Experiment 3:** Digits were inserted into the shadowed and rejected messages. One group was told they would be tested on the digits.

### 5. Results
*   **Experiment 1:** Recognition was much lower for the **rejected list** (1.9/7) than for the **shadowed list** (4.9/7), showing a strong attentional block.
*   **Experiment 2:** Instructions were followed **20 out of 39 times** when preceded by the participant's name, but only **4 out of 36 times** without the name.
*   **Experiment 3:** No significant difference in digit recall was found, even when participants were warned to listen for them.

### 6. Conclusion
*   When attention is focused on one message, the "rejected" message is almost completely blocked.
*   **Affective cues** (like one's own name) can bypass the selective filter and reach conscious awareness.
*   Neutral material (like digits) cannot be "set" to break through the filter.

### 7. Detailed Evaluation & Improvements

| Feature | Evaluation & Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Research Method** | **Laboratory Experiment**: High levels of **standardisation** (volume matched to within 1 dB, same male speaker). Establish **cause and effect**. |
| **Design** | **Mixed Designs**: Repeated measures (Exp 1 & 2) controlled for **participant variables**. Independent measures (Exp 3) avoided **order effects**. |
| **Sample** | **Small Sample**: Experiment 2 only had 12 people. All were academics, so the sample is **biased** and has low **generalisability**. |
| **Sampling Method** | **Opportunity Sampling**: Likely recruited from the university. Convenient but potentially non-representative. |
| **Data Type** | **Quantitative Data**: Numerical scores for recognition and instructions followed allowed for objective comparison. |
| **Ethics** | **Very Good**: No distress was caused, and the tasks were ethically sound. Participants were given practice to feel comfortable. |
| **Weakness** | **Ecological Validity**: Shadowing monotone lists through headphones is an artificial task that doesn't reflect real-life "cocktail party" scenarios. |
| **Reliability Improvements** | **Standardisation**: The modification of the tape recorder to ensure independent outputs to each ear increased the **reliability** of the equipment. |
| **Validity Improvements** | **Control**: Flashing digits or words at a set rate (130-150 wpm) ensured that participants could not switch attention between ears. |

### Q1) Define the term "shadowing" as used in this study (2 marks)
**Model Answer:** **Shadowing** is a laboratory technique where a participant listens to a message through headphones and is required to **repeat it out loud** word-for-word as they hear it, ensuring their attention is fully focused on that one message.

---

### Q2) Explain why Moray used a single male speaker for all recorded messages (3 marks)
**Model Answer:** Moray used a single male speaker to ensure **standardisation** and control for **extraneous variables**. If different speakers or tones were used, participants might have found it easier to distinguish between messages based on physical pitch rather than the **attentional filter**, which would have lowered the study's **internal validity**.

---

### Q3) Outline one finding from Experiment 2 (3 marks)
**Model Answer:** One finding was that personally significant or **affective cues**, such as the participant's own name, could break through the attentional filter. Instructions preceded by a name were followed in **51%** of cases, compared to only **11%** of cases when the name was absent, showing the filter is not absolute.
