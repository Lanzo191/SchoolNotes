### 1. Theoretical Background
*   **Intelligence Testing:** The attempt to quantify human cognitive ability through standardised tests.
*   **Cultural Bias:** When a test unfairly penalises a group based on their cultural or social background rather than their innate ability.
*   **Eugenics:** A movement that advocated for the "improvement" of the human race by encouraging reproduction of those with desirable traits and restricting others.

### 2. Aim
To critique the methodology and findings of Robert Yerkes' mass intelligence testing of US Army recruits during WWI and to highlight the dangers of **scientific racism**.

### 3. Methodology
*   **Method:** Review article (Gould did not conduct a new experiment but reviewed Yerkes' data).
*   **Design:** Evaluation of the **Army Alpha**, **Army Beta**, and **Individual Examination** tests.
*   **Sample:** 1.75 million US Army recruits (the original sample being critiqued).
*   **Independent Variable (IV):** Not applicable (Review).
*   **Dependent Variable (DV):** Intelligence scores (assigned as grades A to E).

### 4. The Original Procedure (Yerkes)
1.  **Army Alpha:** A written test for literate recruits (included analogies and number sequences).
2.  **Army Beta:** A pictorial test for illiterates or those who failed the Alpha (included maze running and picture completion).
3.  **Individual Examination:** An oral test for those who failed the Beta.
*   **Gould's Critique:** In reality, many illiterates were never given the Beta test, and those who failed Beta were rarely given an Individual Examination due to time pressure.

### 5. Yerkes' Findings (Critiqued by Gould)
*   **Average Mental Age:** The average white American male had a mental age of **13**, which was just above the level of a "moron."
*   **European Immigrants:** People from Southern and Eastern Europe scored lower than those from Northern/Western Europe.
*   **Black Recruits:** Scored the lowest of all, with an average mental age of 10.4.

### 6. Conclusion
*   Yerkes' tests measured **acculturation** (schooling and cultural familiarity) rather than innate intelligence.
*   The flawed results were used to justify the **Immigration Restriction Act of 1924**, which had tragic consequences for refugees fleeing Europe later in the century.

### 7. Detailed Evaluation

| Feature | Strengths | Weaknesses |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Research Method (Review Article)** | Allows Gould to evaluate a **massive dataset** (1.75m) and identify patterns that individual researchers might miss. High historical value. | Gould was not present at the time of testing. He may have been **selective** in the data he chose to critique to support his own anti-testing stance. |
| **Design (Mass Testing)** | Yerkes' attempt to test nearly 2 million people was a feat of **standardisation** and provided a model for future large-scale psychometric testing. | The chaotic conditions of army camps meant the "scientific" design was rarely followed. Recruits were often panicked, cold, and unable to hear instructions. |
| **Sample (1.75m Recruits)** | Huge sample size provides high **statistical power** and makes the findings appear incredibly authoritative and representative of the male population. | **Androcentric Bias**: The sample consisted entirely of men. Findings about "national intelligence" completely ignored the female half of the population. |
| **Sampling Method (Opportunity)** | Gathering participants already in army camps was highly **efficient** and allowed for the largest intelligence study in history at the time. | **Sample Bias**: Army recruits are not representative of the general population (e.g., they might exclude the disabled or those in essential "home front" jobs). |
| **Data Type (Quantitative)** | Provided clear, numerical scores (Mental Age) that allowed for **rank-ordering** of individuals and groups for easy (though flawed) comparison. | **Reductionist**: Reducing human intelligence to a single number ignores the complexity of cognitive ability and masks the **cultural bias** in the questions. |
| **Ethics** | The study eventually highlighted the need for **protection from harm** and the dangers of using psychological data for political ends. | **Socially Sensitive**: The results fueled **scientific racism** and eugenics, leading to the sterilisation of thousands and the death of refugees denied entry to the US. |
| **Validity** | Yerkes attempted to use the pictorial "Beta" test to ensure **culture-fairness** for illiterate or non-English speaking recruits. | **Low Construct Validity**: The tests measured **schooling** and American culture (e.g., knowledge of brands like Crisco) rather than "innate" intelligence. |
| **Reliability** | Yerkes provided **standardised manuals** and scripts for every examiner to ensure consistency across different army camps. | **Low Inter-Rater Reliability**: Examiners were often inconsistent; some counted illiterate men as literate to speed up the process, and scoring was subjective. |

### 8. The Danger of "Scientific Racism"
Gould's primary critique is that Yerkes' work is a prime example of **scientific racism**, where flawed data is used to "prove" the inferiority of certain racial or ethnic groups.
*   **Cultural Knowledge vs. IQ**: Questions like "Crisco is a: patent medicine, disinfectant, toothpaste, food product" require familiarity with American consumer products, not raw brainpower.
*   **Institutionalised Bias**: The fact that Black recruits and recent immigrants were systematically given the wrong tests (or no test at all) shows how institutional bias can produce "objective" data that reinforces existing prejudices.
*   **Political Consequences**: The "Nation of Morons" label led to the **Immigration Restriction Act (1924)**, showing how psychological research can have devastating real-world impacts on human rights.

### Q1) Identify one problem with the administration of the Army Beta test (2 marks)
**Model Answer:** One problem was that many illiterate recruits were never actually given the **Army Beta** test. Due to overcrowding and time pressure, they were often forced to take the written **Army Alpha** test, resulting in scores of zero that did not reflect their actual intelligence.

### Q2) Explain why Gould argues that Yerkes' tests lacked internal validity (3 marks)
**Model Answer:** Gould argues that the tests measured **cultural knowledge** and schooling rather than **innate intelligence**. For example, questions about American brands (like Crisco) or sports stars required participants to be "Americanised," meaning the test was not measuring the construct it claimed to measure.

---

### Q3) Outline the "political impact" of Yerkes' findings (3 marks)
**Model Answer:** The findings were used by politicians to justify the **Immigration Restriction Act of 1924**. This law severely limited immigration from Southern and Eastern Europe based on the "scientific" evidence that these groups were intellectually inferior, which later prevented many people from escaping the Nazis.
