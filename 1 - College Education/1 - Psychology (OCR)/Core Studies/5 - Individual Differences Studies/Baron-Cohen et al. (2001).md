### 1. Theoretical Background
*   **Theory of Mind (ToM):** The cognitive ability to understand that others have beliefs, desires, and emotions that are different from our own.
*   **Autism/Asperger Syndrome (AS):** Neurodevelopment conditions characterised by difficulties in social communication and social interaction, often linked to a deficit in ToM.
*   **Social Intelligence:** The ability to accurately "read" the mental states of others through social cues, such as facial expressions.
* **Happe's Strange Stories test:** Concurrent validity  

### 2. Aim
To test whether adults with **Asperger Syndrome (AS)** or **High-Functioning Autism (HFA)** would show impairments on a revised version of the **"Eyes Test."**

### 3. Methodology
*   **Method:** Quasi-experiment.
*   **Design:** **Independent Measures Design**.k
*   **Sample:**
    *  Group 1: People with autism/AS, normal intelligence, 13 males and 3 females. Self-select samples drawn from an Ad in the National Autistic Society magazine as well as through doctors.  
    - Group 2: 50 'normal' adults (25 male and 25 female) age matched with individuals in Group 1. This was a random sample drawn from a subject panel at the University of Cambridge  
     - Group 3: People with Tourette Syndrome (TS), age matched, 8 males and 2 females. This was a self-selected sample from a clinic in London.
*   **Independent Variable (IV):** The type of participant (e.g. AS/HFA vs. "Normal").
*   **Dependent Variable (DV):** Score on the **Revised Eyes Test** (out of 36) and score on the **Autism Spectrum Quotient (AQ)**.

### 4. Procedure
1.  Participants completed the **Revised Eyes Test**. This involved looking at 36 photos of the eye region of faces and choosing one of four words that best described the person's mental state (e.g. *playful*, *comforting*, *irritated*).
2.  The words used were standardised, and a glossary was provided to ensure all participants understood the vocabulary.
3.  Participants in Group 1 (AS/HFA) also completed a gender recognition task and the AQ to measure their autistic traits.

### 5. Results
*   **Eyes Test:** The AS/HFA group scored significantly **lower** (mean 21.9) than all other groups (means ranging from 26.2 to 28.0).
*   **Gender Differences:** In the "normal" groups, females scored significantly higher than males on the Eyes Test.
*   **Correlation:** There was a significant **negative correlation** between the AQ score and the Eyes Test score, meaning as autistic traits increased, the ability to read eyes decreased.

### 6. Conclusion
*   Adults with AS or HFA have a significant deficit in the cognitive component of **Theory of Mind**, even when they have a normal or high IQ.
*   The **Revised Eyes Test** is a valid tool for identifying subtle impairments in social intelligence.

### 7. Detailed Evaluation

| Feature | Strengths | Weaknesses |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Research Method (Quasi-Experiment)** | High **internal validity** as the IV (Autism) is naturally occurring. Matching groups (e.g., IQ-matched Group 4) controls for extraneous variables. | Lack of control over the IV means we cannot definitively establish **cause and effect**. Differences in scores might be due to other factors besides ToM. |
| **Design (Independent Measures)** | Eliminates **order effects** (boredom or practice) as each participant only takes the test once. | Potential for **participant variables** (e.g., personality, eyesight, or reading ability) to affect results, though matching helps mitigate this. |
| **Sample & Generalisability** | Large number of 'normal' participants (Groups 2 & 3) provides a robust baseline for comparison. | **Androcentric Bias**: Group 1 was heavily male-biased (13m vs 3f). While AS is more common in males, the findings may not accurately reflect how females with AS perform. |
| **Sampling Method (Volunteer)** | Efficiently gathers a specific, hard-to-reach population (AS/HFA) through the National Autistic Society. | **Volunteer Bias**: Participants may be more motivated or have higher functioning abilities than the average person with AS, limiting representativeness. |
| **Data Type (Quantitative)** | Numerical scores allow for **objective comparison**, statistical analysis, and identifying clear correlations (e.g., between AQ and Eyes Test). | Lacks **qualitative depth**. It doesn't explain *why* participants chose certain words or what they were thinking during the task. |
| **Ethics** | No significant distress caused. Participants gave informed consent and the study has high **social sensitivity** value in helping the public understand Autism. | Some argue that categorising individuals based on a "deficit" (ToM) can be stigmatising for the neurodivergent community. |
| **Validity** | **Concurrent Validity**: Validated against Happe’s Strange Stories. The Revised Eyes Test reduced guessing by having 4 options instead of 2. | **Low Ecological Validity**: Static, black-and-white photos of eyes are not representative of real-life social interactions where faces are dynamic and provide vocal/contextual cues. |
| **Reliability** | **Highly Standardised**: Every participant saw the same 36 photos with the same 4 choice words, making the study easily **replicable**. | The test might measure **emotion recognition** rather than true Theory of Mind (the ability to understand complex mental states). |

### 8. The "Extreme Male Brain" Theory
	Baron-Cohen proposed that autism is an extreme version of the "male brain" (stronger at **systemising**, which is analysing rules and systems) and a deficit in the "female brain" (stronger at **empathising**).
*   **Androcentric Data**: The consistent finding that "normal" females outscore "normal" males on the Eyes Test supports the idea that the test measures a typically "female" trait (empathy/social intelligence).
*   **Bias**: Because the test was designed by a team led by a male researcher and used a predominantly male clinical sample, it has been criticised for being **androcentric**, potentially overlooking the unique way females experience and mask autistic traits.

### Q1) Outline one change made to the original Eyes Test for this 2001 study (2 marks)
**Model Answer:** One change was increasing the number of response options from two to **four words** for each pair of eyes. This reduced the chance of participants guessing the correct answer and made the test a more sensitive measure of **Theory of Mind**.

---

### Q2) Explain the "similarity" between the AS/HFA group and the IQ-matched group (3 marks)
**Model Answer:** Both groups were matched to ensure they had a **similar IQ level** (normal to high). This was done to prove that the lower scores on the Eyes Test in the AS/HFA group were due to a specific deficit in **Theory of Mind**, rather than a general lack of intelligence.

---

### Q3) Discuss the relationship between the AQ score and the Eyes Test (3 marks)
**Model Answer:** The study found a significant **negative correlation** between the two scores. This means that participants who scored higher on the **Autism Spectrum Quotient (AQ)**—indicating more autistic traits—tended to score lower on the Eyes Test, confirming that the test is a valid measure of autistic social impairment.
