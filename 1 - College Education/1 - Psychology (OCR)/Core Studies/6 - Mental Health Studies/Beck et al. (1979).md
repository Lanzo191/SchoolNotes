## Beck et al. (1979) - Cognitive Model of Depression

### 1. Theoretical Background
*   **Cognitive Approach:** Assumes mental health disorders stem from dysfunctional, irrational thinking patterns, faulty information processing, and negative schemas rather than biological flaws.
*   **Affective Disorders (Depression):** Mood disorders characterized by persistent sadness, lack of motivation, low energy, and feelings of worthlessness.
*   **Negative Automatic Thoughts (NATs):** Involuntary, negative thoughts that automatically stream through a depressed individual's mind in response to everyday events.
*   **Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT):** An evidence-based psychological treatment aimed at helping patients identify, reality-test, and restructure irrational cognitive distortions.

---

### 2. Aims
To explain the onset, maintenance, and psychological treatment of depression using a **Cognitive Model**, asserting that depression is caused by distorted thinking rather than biological disease alone.

---

### 3. Core Components of Beck's Cognitive Model

```
               Negative Self-Schemas (Acquired in childhood/trauma)
                                        │
                                        ▼
                  Stressful Life Event (Triggering Event)
                                        │
                                        ▼
                   Cognitive Errors / Distortions (NATs)
                                        │
                                        ▼
                       THE COGNITIVE TRIAD OF DEPRESSION
             ┌──────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────┐
             ▼                          ▼                          ▼
      Negative Views             Negative Views             Negative Views
      About the SELF           About the WORLD           About the FUTURE
   ("I am a failure")      ("Everyone avoids me")     ("Things will never improve")
```

#### 1. The Cognitive Triad
Depressed individuals suffer from three automatic negative views:
1.  **Negative Views About the Self:** Believing oneself to be worthless, inadequate, unlovable, or a failure.
2.  **Negative Views About the World / Current Experiences:** Believing the world presents impossible obstacles and demands that cannot be overcome.
3.  **Negative Views About the Future:** Believing that suffering and failure will continue indefinitely, creating feelings of helplessness.

#### 2. Negative Self-Schemas
*   Formed during childhood through negative experiences (e.g. parental rejection, bullying, academic criticism).
*   Stored in memory and activated during later stressful life events, causing the individual to filter all incoming information negatively.

#### 3. Cognitive Errors / Distortions
Faulty thinking habits that reinforce negative schemas:
*   **Arbitrary Inference:** Drawing negative conclusions without supporting evidence.
*   **Selective Abstraction:** Focusing exclusively on one negative detail while ignoring positive context.
*   **Overgeneralisation:** Applying a single negative outcome to all future situations.
*   **Catastrophising:** Exaggerating a minor setback into an absolute disaster.
*   **Personalisation:** Incorrectly taking personal responsibility for negative external events.

---

### 4. Therapeutic Applications (Cognitive Therapy / CBT)
*   **Patient as Scientist:** Patients are trained to treat their negative thoughts as hypotheses to be tested using empirical evidence.
*   **Disputing NATs:** The therapist challenges irrational beliefs using logical and empirical questioning.
*   **Behavioral Activation:** Encouraging depressed patients to engage in rewarding tasks and keep daily mood logs to break lethargy and social withdrawal.

---

### 5. Detailed Evaluation & Debates

| Feature / Debate | Evaluation & Analysis |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Research Method** | **Clinical & Experimental Studies**: Supported by extensive empirical research testing cognitive processing differences in depressed vs non-depressed groups. |
| **Generaliability** | **High Applicability**: The cognitive model applies widely to mild, moderate, and severe depression across diverse clinical populations. |
| **Reliability** | **High Standardisation**: Formulated standardized diagnostic tools (e.g., **Beck Depression Inventory - BDI**), ensuring consistent measurement of depressive severity. |
| **Validity** | **High Construct & Predictive Validity**: Explains both cognitive symptoms (hopelessness) and behavioral symptoms (withdrawal). Highly effective in predicting recovery. |
| **Ethics** | **Empowering / Non-Invasive**: Unlike drug therapies or ECT, CBT avoids physical side effects and empowers patients with lifelong coping strategies. |
| **Free Will vs Determinism** | Strongly supports **Free Will**: Empowers patients by teaching them that they have the cognitive control to reconstruct their thought patterns. |
| **Individual vs Situational** | **Individual Focus**: Focuses primarily on internal cognitive processing. Can be criticized for ignoring **situational** factors (e.g. living in poverty, grief, abusive environments). |
| **Reductionism vs Holism** | **Cognitive Reductionism**: Reduces complex affective disorders to irrational thoughts, potentially downplaying biological/neurochemical factors. |
| **Practical Application** | **Gold Standard Treatment**: CBT is recommended by NICE (UK) as a first-line treatment for depression, reducing relapse rates significantly compared to medication alone. |

### Q1) Describe the three components of Beck's Cognitive Triad in depression. (3 marks)
**Model Answer:** Beck's Cognitive Triad consists of three automatic negative thinking patterns found in depressed individuals: (1) **Negative views about the self** (believing oneself to be worthless or a failure), (2) **Negative views about the world** (interpreting current experiences as overwhelmingly hostile or demanding), and (3) **Negative views about the future** (believing that helplessness and suffering will never end).

---

### Q2) Explain one cognitive error identified by Beck, using an example. (2 marks)
**Model Answer:** One cognitive error is **overgeneralisation**, which occurs when an individual draws a sweeping negative conclusion from a single event. For example, if a student fails one test, they might conclude, *"I am completely stupid and will fail every exam I ever take."*

---

### Q3) Discuss the strengths and weaknesses of Beck's Cognitive Model of depression. (5 marks)
**Model Answer:** A major **strength** of Beck's model is its high **practical application**. It directly led to the development of Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT), which provides long-term, non-invasive coping mechanisms and is recommended by NICE as a primary treatment for depression. Furthermore, it supports **free will** by empowering patients to take control of their thoughts. However, a **weakness** is that the model focuses heavily on **individual** cognition while neglecting **situational factors**, such as poverty, unemployment, or trauma, which may be the genuine root cause of an individual's low mood. Additionally, it remains difficult to establish whether negative thoughts *cause* depression or are simply a *symptom* of underlying biological changes.
