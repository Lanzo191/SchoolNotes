## Bocchiaro et al. (2012) - Disobedience and Whistleblowing

### 1. Theoretical Background
*   **Whistleblowing:** The act of reporting unethical or illegal practices within an organization to a higher authority.
*   **Agentic State:** When a person follows orders, believing the authority figure is responsible for the outcome (Milgram's theory).
*   **Moral Intuition:** What people *believe* they would do in an ethical dilemma, which often differs from their actual behavior.

### 2. Aims
1.  To investigate the rates of **obedience**, **disobedience**, and **whistleblowing** in an unethical situation.
2.  To compare **predicted behaviour** with **actual behaviour**.
3.  To see if **personality traits** (dispositional factors) influence the decision to obey or whistleblow.

### 3. Methodology
*   **Method:** Laboratory study (controlled observation).
*   **Design:** **Independent measures** (one group for predictions, one for the actual task).
*   **Sample:** 149 undergraduate students from VU University in Amsterdam (Main study). 138 students (Comparison group).
*   **Independent Variable (IV):** Not manipulated (single condition).
*   **Dependent Variables (DV):**
    1.  The response of the participant (Obey, Disobey, or Whistleblow).
    2.  Personality scores (from the **HEXACO-PI-R** test).

### 4. Procedure
1.  **The Cover Story:** Participants were told about a fake study on sensory deprivation and asked to write a persuasive statement to recruit other students.
2.  **The Task:** They had to use words like "exciting" and "incredible" and were forbidden from mentioning any negative side effects.
3.  **The Choice:** Participants were left alone for 7 minutes. They could either write the statement (**obey**), refuse (**disobey**), or fill out a form to the Research Committee (**whistleblow**).
4.  **Debrief:** After the task, they were given personality and religiosity tests before being told the truth.

### 5. Results
*   **Predicted Behaviour:** 64.5% of people believed they would whistleblow, and only 3.6% thought they would obey.
*   **Actual Behaviour:** **76.5% obeyed**, 14.1% disobeyed, and only **9.4% whistleblew**.
*   **Personality:** No significant difference in personality traits (honesty, humility, extraversion) was found between those who obeyed and those who whistleblew.
*   **Religiosity:** Whistleblowers tended to have a slightly higher "faith" score.

### 6. Conclusion
*   There is a massive gap between how people think they will act (**moral intuition**) and how they **actually behave**.
*   The **situation** is a far more powerful predictor of behavior than an individual's personality.
*   Whistleblowing is an extremely difficult and rare act of resistance.

### 7. Detailed Evaluation & Improvements

| Feature | Evaluation & Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Research Method** | **Laboratory Study**: High control over the environment and timing (7 minutes). However, it lacks **ecological validity** as writing a recruitment letter is an artificial task. |
| **Design** | **Independent Measures**: Using separate groups for prediction and actual behavior avoided **demand characteristics** in the results. |
| **Sample** | **149 Students**: All from one university in Amsterdam. This is a **biased sample** and may not be generalisable to older populations or different cultures. |
| **Sampling Method** | **Volunteer Sampling**: Recruited via flyers. Volunteers might be more compliant or motivated by the incentive (€7), creating a **sampling bias**. |
| **Data Type** | **Mixed Methods**: **Quantitative data** (obedience rates) allowed for clear comparisons. **Qualitative data** from the debrief helped explain their feelings of guilt. |
| **Ethics** | **Good**: Used **moderate deception**, but it was less distressing than Milgram's study. Participants were fully debriefed and gave consent for their data to be used. |
| **Weakness** | **Lack of Diversity**: The participants were all young students. Professional adults might have responded differently to authority figures in a workplace-like setting. |
| **Reliability Improvements** | **Standardisation**: The experimenter used the same "stern" demeanor and standardized script for the cover story, ensuring high **replicability**. |
| **Validity Improvements** | **Computerised Task**: By leaving participants alone with a computer to make their choice, Bocchiaro reduced **social desirability bias** during the act of whistleblowing. |

### Q1) Distinguish between "disobedience" and "whistleblowing" in the context of this study (2 marks)
**Model Answer:** **Disobedience** is the act of refusing to follow the unethical instruction (not writing the letter), whereas **whistleblowing** is taking the extra step of reporting the unethical practice to a higher authority (filling out the Research Committee form).

---

### Q2) Explain one reason why the researchers used a comparison group in this study (3 marks)
**Model Answer:** The researchers used a **comparison group** to investigate the difference between **moral intuition** and **actual behaviour**. By asking one group to predict how they would act, they could demonstrate that people significantly overestimate their own moral courage compared to how they truly behave when under pressure.

---

### Q3) Outline one finding related to personality traits in this study (3 marks)
**Model Answer:** The study found **no significant correlation** between personality traits (such as honesty-humility or extraversion) and the likelihood of obeying or whistleblowing. This suggests that **dispositional factors** are weak predictors of behavior compared to the influence of the **situation**.
