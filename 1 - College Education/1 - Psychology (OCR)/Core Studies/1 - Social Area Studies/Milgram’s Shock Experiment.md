## Milgram (1963) - Behavioral Study of Obedience

### 1. Theoretical Background
*   **The Germans are Different Hypothesis:** The initial idea that Germans had a unique personality flaw making them more obedient, which Milgram sought to disprove by testing Americans.
*   **Agency Theory:** Milgram's theory that individuals move from an **autonomous state** (personal responsibility) to an **agentic state** (following orders as an agent of authority).
*   **Situational vs Dispositional:** The debate over whether behavior is caused by the **environment/situation** or by an individual's **internal traits**.

### 2. Aims
To investigate how obedient ordinary individuals would be to an authority figure when instructed to inflict increasingly painful electric shocks on an innocent person.

### 3. Methodology
*   **Method:** Controlled observation in a laboratory setting (often called a "pre-experiment" as there was no manipulation in the baseline).
*   **Design:** **Independent measures** (used across different variations of the study).
*   **Sample:** 40 males, aged 20 to 50, from New Haven. Recruited via newspaper ads (**volunteer sampling**). Paid $4.50.
*   **Independent Variable (IV):** Technically none in the baseline study. In variations, the IV included factors like **proximity** of the victim or the **location** of the experiment.
*   **Dependent Variable (DV):** The maximum voltage level reached by the participant (0 to 450V).

### 4. Procedure
1.  **Deception:** Participants were told the study was about the effects of **punishment on learning**.
2.  **Rigged Draw:** The participant was always the **teacher**, and a confederate (Mr. Wallace) was always the **learner**.
3.  **The Task:** The teacher read word pairs; for every error, they administered a shock, increasing by 15V each time.
4.  **The Shocks:** Ranged from 15V to 450V. A 45V sample shock was given to the teacher to establish "authenticity."
5.  **Prods:** If the teacher hesitated, the experimenter used four standardized prods (e.g. "The experiment requires that you continue").

### 5. Results
*   **Quantitative:** 100% of participants reached 300V. **65%** (26/40) went to the full 450V.
*   **Qualitative:** Participants showed extreme signs of **psychological distress**, such as sweating, trembling, stuttering, and three suffered from full-blown seizures.

### 6. Conclusion
*   Obedience is determined more by the **situation** than by individual personality.
*   Ordinary people can be coerced into performing destructive acts when under the direction of a legitimate authority figure.

### 7. Detailed Evaluation & Improvements

| Feature | Evaluation & Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Research Method** | **Laboratory Observation**: High levels of **standardisation** and control over variables. However, it lacks **ecological validity** as the task is artificial. |
| **Design** | **Independent Measures**: Used in variations to compare factors like proximity. This can be affected by **participant variables**. |
| **Sample** | **40 Males**: The sample is **androcentric** and **ethnocentric**, limiting the **generalisability** of the results to women and other cultures. |
| **Sampling Method** | **Volunteer Sampling**: May attract a specific type of person who is more compliant, creating a **sampling bias**. |
| **Data Type** | **Mixed Methods**: **Quantitative data** (voltage levels) allows for easy comparison, while **qualitative data** (stress signs) explains the impact. |
| **Ethics** | **Very Poor**: Serious breaches of **protection from harm** and the use of **deception**. However, Milgram argued it was necessary for the study's **validity**. |
| **Weakness** | **Demand Characteristics**: Critics argue participants may have guessed the shocks were fake and were simply playing along to please the experimenter. |
| **Reliability Improvements** | **Standardisation**: Every participant experienced the same shock machine, word pairs, and prods, ensuring high **replicability**. |
| **Validity Improvements** | **Deception**: By hiding the true aim, Milgram reduced **demand characteristics**, ensuring the obedience measured was genuine. |

### Q1) Explain how Milgram's study can be considered a controlled observation rather than an experiment (2 marks)
**Model Answer:** Milgram's original 1963 study is considered a **controlled observation** because there was only one condition and no **independent variable** was manipulated in the baseline version. All participants were exposed to the same authority figure and procedure to see how they would react.

---

### Q2) Outline one way that the study by Milgram was unethical (3 marks)
**Model Answer:** One way the study was unethical was the failure to provide **protection from harm**. The participants were subjected to extreme psychological stress, leading to physical symptoms like sweating and trembling, and in three cases, they experienced **uncontrollable seizures** due to the tension of the situation.

---

### Q3) Discuss the ecological validity of this study (3 marks)
**Model Answer:** The study has **low ecological validity** because both the setting (a university laboratory) and the task (administering electric shocks for memory errors) are artificial. Such a situation does not occur in everyday life, so the high levels of obedience found may not reflect how people act in **real-world** settings.
