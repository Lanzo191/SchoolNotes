## Categories of Mental Health Disorders

| Disorder Category | Clinical Definition | Core Example Condition | Defining Characteristics and Diagnostic Criteria |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Psychotic Disorder** | A severe psychological condition characterized by a fundamental loss of contact with external reality. | **Schizophrenia** | - Hallucinations (perceptions in absence of external stimuli, e.g. auditory voices)<br>- Delusions (fixed, false beliefs resistant to counter-evidence)<br>- Disorganised speech and thought derailment<br>- Impaired cognitive and occupational functioning<br>- Symptoms must persist continuously for at least 6 months. |
| **Anxiety Disorder** | Persistent, disproportionate feelings of fear, dread, and physiological arousal anticipating future threats. | **Specific Phobia** | - Immediate, irrational fear triggered by exposure to a specific stimulus or situation<br>- Active avoidance behaviours of the phobic stimulus<br>- Marked physiological distress (tachycardia, diaphoresis, hyperventilation)<br>- Symptoms must be present for 6 months or more and significantly impair daily functioning. |
| **Affective (Mood) Disorder** | Marked disturbances in emotional state and persistent disruption of normal mood regulation. | **Major Depressive Disorder** | - Persistent depressed mood and sadness lasting at least two weeks<br>- Anhedonia (loss of interest or pleasure in activities)<br>- Insomnia or hypersomnia, chronic fatigue, appetite/weight changes<br>- Feelings of worthlessness, excessive guilt, and recurrent suicidal ideation. |

## Importance of Diagnostic Categorisation
- **Accurate Treatment Selection**: Establishes appropriate clinical interventions tailored to specific aetiology (e.g. antipsychotics for schizophrenia vs SSRIs or CBT for depression).
- **Patient Education and Prognosis**: Empowers patients and families to understand symptom trajectories and implement evidence-based management strategies.
- **Potential Limitations**: Risks diagnostic labelling, social stigmatisation, and overgeneralisation that may overlook individual variation.

## Exam Practice Question and Model Answer

### Q1) Richard and Wendy are parents who have just been told their son has a psychotic disorder. How might a clinical psychologist explain to them the characteristics of a psychotic disorder? (6 marks)
**Model Answer:** A clinical psychologist would explain that a psychotic disorder is a mental health condition characterized by a loss of contact with reality, which primarily manifests through hallucinations, delusions, and disorganized thinking.

First, the psychologist would clarify that hallucinations are perceptual experiences that feel completely vivid and real to their son but occur without any external sensory stimulus. For example, he might hear voices speaking to him or see things that others cannot see. The psychologist would reassure the parents that these experiences feel entirely authentic to him, which can cause intense distress and confusion.

Second, the psychologist would explain delusions, which are fixed, false beliefs held with complete conviction despite clear contradictory evidence. For instance, their son might believe he is under constant surveillance, in imminent danger, or possesses special abilities.

Finally, the psychologist would describe disorganized thinking and speech, where thoughts may become fragmented, making it difficult for him to follow conversations or maintain daily routines. The psychologist would emphasize to Richard and Wendy that these symptoms are part of a recognized, treatable clinical illness rather than a fault in character, and that targeted therapies and medical interventions are available to support his recovery.
