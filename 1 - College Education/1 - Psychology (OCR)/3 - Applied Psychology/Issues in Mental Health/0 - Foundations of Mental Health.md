**Issues in Mental Health** is the compulsory section of Applied Psychology. It explores how we define, diagnose, and treat mental disorders.

## 1. Defining Abnormality
There are four main ways to define whether someone is "abnormal":
- **Statistical Infrequency**: Behavior that is mathematically rare (e.g., extremely high or low IQ).
- **Deviation from Social Norms**: Behavior that goes against the unwritten rules of society.
- **Failure to Function Adequately**: When a person cannot cope with the demands of everyday life (e.g., hygiene, nutrition, work).
- **Deviation from Ideal Mental Health (Jahoda)**: Looking at what is "normal" (e.g., self-esteem, autonomy) and seeing who lacks it.

## 2. Categorising Disorders
Psychologists use diagnostic manuals to identify disorders based on symptoms:
- **DSM-5**: Used primarily in the USA.
- **ICD-10/11**: Used globally and by the NHS. 
- **The Three Main Disorders (OCR Focus)**:
    1. **Affective Disorders**: (e.g., Depression) - Mood-related.
    2. **Anxiety Disorders**: (e.g., Phobias) - Fear-related.
    3. **Psychotic Disorders**: (e.g., Schizophrenia) - Loss of reality.
